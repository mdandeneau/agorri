Title: Minutes: Rye Energy Committee Regular Meeting
Fetched: 2026-11-01

Rye Energy Committee
                                         Meeting Draft Minutes
                                        October 19, 2026, 11:00 AM
                                                Town Hall


Attendance: Tom Pfau, Co-Chair; Lisa Sweet, Co-chair; Jay Mason; Silas Proft.

Approval of September 1 Minutes: A motion to approve the September 1, 2026 draft minutes was
made and seconded. The motion passed unanimously.

Renewable Energy Credit Reporting: SolarEdge has not reliably reported production from the Public
Safety Building and Recreation solar arrays to NEPOOL GIS. The Town lost January and February 2026
RECs, estimated at a few hundred dollars, because they were not recorded in time. Energy Audits
Unlimited (EAU) offered independent monitoring and reporting for $38 annually for the Public Safety
Building and $25 for Recreation, a total of $63 per year. Production readings will need to be submitted
monthly or quarterly; possible automated reporting was discussed. A motion to enroll both systems
with EAU and approve the $63 annual cost was made, seconded and approved unanimously. The cost
is expected to be paid from the Energy Revolving Fund. The Committee expects additional REC
proceeds from CPCNH.

Rye Junior High School Consumption Meter: The existing Eyedro meter at Rye Junior High School
provides electrical consumption information, but the available software does not permit the desired
15-minute analysis. The proposed $39 one-time software upgrade should help identify coincident
equipment loads behind the school’s approximately $700 to $800 monthly demand charges and test
whether staggering equipment starts could reduce peaks. After evaluating the school, the meter could
be moved to another municipal building. A motion to approve the $39 upgrade was made, seconded
and approved unanimously. The Energy Revolving Fund was suggested as the funding source.

Local Energy Solutions Conference: The Committee reviewed the $161.80 registration expense for a
third member to attend the conference in Manchester. Clean Energy New Hampshire membership
costs $250 annually and includes two conference registrations. A motion to approve the $161.80
expenditure was made, seconded and approved unanimously. The registration had already been paid.

2027 Energy Committee Budget: The proposed 2027 operating budget totals $1,500, unchanged from
the 2026 adopted budget. Members discussed previously unanticipated charges against the
Committee budget, including approximately $600 for solar direct-pay tax preparation and $60 for
Recycling Committee signs. Members requested better notice and coordination regarding spending
charged to the Committee and discussed future outreach advertising. A motion to approve the
proposed $1,500 budget was made, seconded and approved unanimously for submission through the
Town budget process.

Clean Energy New Hampshire Circuit Rider Donation: The Committee discussed Clean Energy New
Hampshire’s assistance with municipal solar grants, the Energy Revolving Fund, C-PACER and EV
charger grants. A motion to donate $250 from the current Committee budget to support the Energy
Circuit Rider program, in addition to the annual membership dues, was made, seconded and approved
unanimously. Members discussed using available funds budgeted for signs.

Future Meeting Schedule: Members discussed moving regular meetings to 6:30 to 8:00 PM to make
participation easier for working residents, with the library as a possible venue. Evening commitments
may require adjustments. The next meeting was set for Monday, November 2, 2026, at 3:30 PM at the
Rye Public Library. Items not covered at this shortened meeting may be carried forward.

NH DES Level 2 EV Charging Grant: Members reviewed the NH Department of Environmental Services
grant opportunity, discussed as providing up to 80% reimbursement for eligible installations, with a
20% applicant match and upfront financing. Sites need at least two public Level 2 chargers, year-round
access and suitable dwell time. Applications were described as being reviewed on the 15th of each
month until funding is exhausted, with completion expected by the end of 2028. Possible sites
included La Mulita’s proposed mixed-use development, Atlantic Grill, Rye Motor Inn/Southport and the
Rye Airfield area. Lisa had contacted Rye Motor Inn/Southport and will circulate her outreach note. Jay
agreed to contact Max, the La Mulita owner, about interest. Members will share factual updates, seek
interested property owners and preliminary installation estimates, and revisit progress on November 2.
ReVision Energy was identified as a possible installer; Clean Energy New Hampshire is expected to
provide an installer list. No grant application or location was approved at this meeting.

Energize Rye Solar Outreach: The solar estimator has received visits but few completed estimate
requests. Silas agreed to submit a test inquiry and to mention the program to friends. Jay authorized
posting his existing solar video, which may be updated later. Members discussed additional
homeowner videos and examples of solar and battery installations.

Adjournment: A motion to adjourn was made, seconded and approved unanimously. The meeting
adjourned at 12:03 PM.
