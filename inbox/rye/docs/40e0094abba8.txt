           2. Financial Update

Treasurer Terhune gave an update on RCC expenditures for the month of September, which
brought the total third quarter to $9,705 with year to date expenses of $36,889. The total
expenses equal 31.1% of RCC’s 2026 budget through the end of the third quarter.

The Commission reviewed the document submitted by Chair Lee that was requested by the Town
for presentation to the Select Board and Budget Committee for the upcoming budget preparation.
The request is to show total expenditures for the years 2022 through 2025 with a year to date for
2026, along with the details for the proposed 2027 budget. The worksheet also includes
percentage changes for one, three and five years. The form also shows line items with
significant increases and decreases, along with proposed capital outlays for future years and trust
fund balances.

It was noted by Treasurer Terhune that RCC does not intend to ask for a major expense increase
over the next five years other than the typical cost of living allowance.

           3. Vernal Pools

Member Suchocki reported that he has been in contact with Sarah Sullivan and Rachel Stevens
with FB Environmental. The lowest they could go with their quote for the vernal pool work is
$39,000. This is still significantly higher than GZA at $21,293. Member Suchocki pointed out
that the proposals are largely synonymous and the work is similar. In his mind, it would be
fiscally responsible to go with GZA.

The Commission agreed. They also agreed that GZA is competent to do the work. There was
some discussion about updating the Natural Resources Inventory (NRI) after the vernal pool
work is completed. The goal is to update the NRI every ten years. It was pointed out by
Member Suchocki that with GZA doing the vernal pool work, they provide the data but will not
actually do the update. The expense for the update would have to be incurred anyway.

           4. Trails Update

Vice-Chair Odryna gave an update on the trails. He noted that the Town Forest was not touched
in any way and no changes were made. When looking at putting the bog bridges back in the Jim
Raynes Forest, it was found that a building permit was never pulled for that work. The RCC will
need to go through the process, which will have to go before the ZBA because the work is
specifically within a wetland. The RCC will work through the building permit application
process with the building department. It was also noted by Vice-Chair Odryna that the RCC is
responsible for putting the bridges back into place.

Member Marttila commented that he hopes the team that took the bridges apart would be willing
to put them back. He pointed out that there are volunteers in the community, including himself,
who are willing to help. He suggested putting together a work party once this goes through the
ZBA process. He would like to avoid spending money on this project.

Member Suchocki pointed out that the proposal has to go through the NH Department of
Environmental Services (NH DES) for wetland permitting, as well.

Susan Shepcaro, 45 Recreation Road, pointed out that the original bridges were built two years
ago. The work went through the wetland permitting with DES at that time, so that permit may
still be valid.

There was some discussion about the timeframe for doing the work and how it would need to be
done.

Vice-Chair Odryna noted that he is working on updating the trail locations on the website. He
spoke to the Commission about marking the trails and what they would like to use as a standard
marker. He pointed out that roughly ten years ago, the RCC decided to use metal tags, which
have been used on a lot of the trails, except the Town Forest. The Town Forest Management
Plan specifically talks about blazes. He pointed out that tags were taken down for multiple trails
and they need to be put back.

The Commission discussed whether to use metal tags or blazes for conservation trails and other
options were discussed. It was agreed that the markings should be consistent. The RCC has set
up a new email for issues related to trails: trails@town.rye.nh.us Anyone with thoughts or
comments about the trails may send an email. There was also discussion about putting out

           5. Goss Farm Update

Member Marttila gave an update on the Goss Farm. Kvitka Farms is doing well and ending their
season. The Driftwood Garden Club submitted a proposal to extend the agreement between RCC
and the Garden Club for use of Goss Farm in May for their annual plant sale. The Garden Club
is proposing a five year agreement for use of the farm with a one year notice from RCC if the
terms of the agreement change in order for an alternative location to be secured.

Susan Dexter, 240 Garland Road, member of the Garden Club, was present at the meeting to
discuss the proposed agreement.

Member Marttila noted that he didn’t see any issues with the proposal and there have been no
problems with the Garden Club using the Farm. He pointed out that the Garden Club is using the
Farm for an agricultural use that is a benefit to the community.

There was some discussion about whether a shorter timeframe should be considered, as there
may be all new members on the Conservation Commission next year. After discussion, it was
agreed that Attorney Maher should review the agreement and the RCC will consider how many
years to make the agreement. The RCC confirmed that they are in support of the proposal and
will finalize the details at the next meeting.

Member Marttila noted that the Rye Elementary School will be visiting the Farm on October
22nd. Kvitka Farms has requested to do a workshop in November in the barn showcasing
wreaths and decorations. Kvitka Farms has also volunteered to spearhead a Christmas market at
the Farm in December.

Vice-Chair Odryna gave a brief update on the swallow shelters.

           6. Town Forest Survey Update – recorded 9/28/26

It was reported by Chair Lee that the Town Forest survey was recorded on September 28th.
Attorney Amy Manzelli has confirmed that the amended easement is in process and the RCC
should see that soon.

           7. CIP Light Update – future capital projects next five years

An email was received from the Select Board Chair inquiring about capital projects planned by
the RCC for the next five years. At this time, the RCC doesn’t anticipate any capital projects
other than what is already covered under the normal budget.
