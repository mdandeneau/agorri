    V.     SIGNAGE FOR ROADSIDE HISTORIC GRAVEYARDS

Patti Smith, Historic Family Graveyard Committee, met with HDC to talk about the work of
the Family Graveyard Committee. The Committee has cleaned several gravesites in Rye in the
past couple of years. Once the sites are cleaned, the group works on finding individuals who are
willing to maintain and take care of the sites moving forward. A list of all of Rye’s sites and
caretakers is kept by the Committee. Ms. Smith noted there are several roadside family burial
graveyards in Rye that are not identified in any way, except for maybe a stonewall or rail fence.
She has drafted a list of nineteen roadside sites, which is almost all of them. The Family
Graveyard Committee has suggested putting a sign at these sites to help identify them. A
proposed drawing of what the sign would look like was submitted to the HDC for review. The
signs with post cost approximately $120 each, so the total cost would be less than $3,000 for the
nineteen sites. The size of the sign is 18” x 18”.

Referring to the law, it was explained by Chair Herlihy that adjacent property owners do not own
the graveyard. The graveyard is public property. Ancestors have the first right of refusal to be
the caretakers. If no one comes forward, then the adjacent property owner, or someone else, can
become the official caretaker. A deed to property with a graveyard on it would refer to the
graveyard; however, the site is not part of the property.

There was further discussion about gravesites on private properties. It was agreed that further
research may be needed to see how deeds handle sites on private property. The HDC supports
the proposal for the signs but would like to see some other costs and material options. Ms. Smith
agreed to reach out to Timberline Signs.

There was discussion about having the sites marked on the town map. Rye’s maps are updated
every year. It was confirmed by the company who updates the town’s maps that this work can be
done, but it would be outside of the pricing that Rye pays for annual updating of the maps. The
cost to add the sites would be about $400.

The HDC supports the proposal for signage, as well as adding the sites to the town map.

Ms. Smith will provide the HDC with further details on costs for the sign and adding the sites to
the town map.

    VII.     TOWN FACILITIES PLAN – RHC PLAN FOR TOWN HALL

Chair Herlihy explained that the Town Hall Committee, which was a select board committee,
ended earlier this year. In 2024, the Select Board knew there was no more committee and told
the Heritage Commission to keep working on the project. He further explained that he and
Treasurer White, as members of the HDC, have been working on the Town Hall project.
Selectman Tuttle thought the plan was “putting the cart before the horse” because of the
upcoming proposal for a Town Facilities Master Plan. Chair Herlihy noted the HDC has been
working on the Town Hall and has already made progress. Representatives from Chinburg
Builders have reviewed the Town Hall and are ready to give an estimate for the work. Chair
Herlihy pointed out that the HDC has done their homework and can have an estimate for the
work within two to three weeks. The plan is to restore the Town Hall in two phases over a two
year timeframe.

Treasure White suggested including the two page proposal for the Town Hall when he presents
the proposed HDC budget to the Select Board and Budget Committee. Proposed budgets are due
to the Town by October 8th.

The HDC agreed to include the two page Town Hall proposal to the budget packet.
