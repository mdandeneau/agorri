    III.      WETLANDS/WETLAND BUFFERS

           1. 44 Causeway Road, Tax Map 2, Lot 78
              Owner/Applicant: George and Karen Sophinos
              100’ Freshwater Marsh Buffer
              Construct shed on property
              Rye Beach Village District

George Sophinos, 44 Causeway Road, presented a proposal to install a 10-by-12-foot shed at
the rear of the property, 35 feet from the wetland boundary. The shed would feature a cedar
exterior designed to complement the home and neighborhood. Because the 100-foot buffer
encompasses the entire backyard and side yard, there is no alternative location for the shed. The
shed will rest on geotextile fabric and a ¾-inch crushed-stone base extending two feet beyond its
footprint, allowing runoff to filter through the stone and into the ground.

Members of the Commission conducted a site walk prior to this meeting and reviewed the plans
submitted with the application.

Vice-Chair Odryna, who attended the site walk, stated that the proposed shed would have
minimal wetland impact, noting the site’s natural drainage and the shed’s safe placement.

No concerns were raised from other members present at the site walk.

Gary Terhune moved that the Rye Conservation Commission has no objection to the
construction of a shed on the property at 44 Causeway Road as proposed. Seconded by
Michael Bean. All in favor.

       2. 10 Causeway Road, Tax Map 2, Lot 76
          Applicant: Jason Aube, TF Moran
          Temporarily impact 17,200 S.F. of the protected shoreland to install a hybrid living
          shoreline consisting of 1-ft toe stones, clean soil fill, and extensive native plantings.
          Rye Beach Village District

Jay Aube of TF Moran presented the proposal for 10 Causeway Road that requires a variance
from the Rye Beach Village District Zoning Board of Adjustment for surface alterations within
100 feet of the marsh. The project would temporarily affect 17,200 square feet of protected
shoreland by slightly raising the property’s grade to improve resilience to future storms
following damage from a winter storm in 2024. A living shoreline with extensive native
plantings is proposed to help protect the property. It was noted that similar work at 20 Causeway
Road had been successful.

A site walk was held at 10 Causeway Road with members of the Commission.

Member Bean noted the Commission’s recommendation to retain the living boundary rather than
replace it with grass and to preserve the three maple trees in the side yard.

Vice-Chair Odryna supported the proposal, citing the success of the similar project at 20
Causeway Road and describing it as an effective solution to an ongoing problem.

The Commission raised no concerns with the proposal.

Michael Bean moved that the Rye Conservation Commission has no objection to the
construction of the living shoreline project on the property at 10 Causeway Road as
proposed. Seconded by Gary Terhune. All in favor.

       4. 734 Central Road, Tax Map 5, Lot 42
          Owner: Philip Stead
          TF Moran
          Renovate existing home with new porch, construct new ADU with pervious driveway
          & stormwater improvements
          100’ Freshwater Marsh Buffer
          Rye Beach Village District

Corey Colwell of TF Moran presented a proposal to construct an accessory dwelling unit within
100 feet of the freshwater marsh. Because the entire lot lies within the buffer, a variance from the
Rye Beach Village District will be required. The 1.9-acre property contains an existing two-story
dwelling and lies within the 100-foot buffer associated with the stream and adjacent marsh at the
rear of the lot. The proposal would retain the building footprint except for a second-story deck
over the enclosed porch, replace the paved driveway and front walkway with permeable pavers,
reconstruct the rear patio with a more pervious surface, and add stormwater-management
improvements. Post-construction lot coverage would be 7.2 percent, below the 40 percent
allowed.

During the site walk, Commission members recommended relocating and attaching the ADU to
the main structure providing an additional 15 feet of buffer, which would allow native plantings
between the ADU and the buffer. The applicant intends to revise the site plan accordingly.

It was noted that abutter Mr. McQuade has reviewed the plan and expressed his support in a
letter submitted to the RCC.

The Commission supported the proposal with the revisions as discussed with the applicant and
his team.

Gary Terhune moved that the Rye Conservation Commission has no objection to the home
renovation and construction of an ADU on the property at 734 Central Road with the
relocation of the ADU to be attached to the primary dwelling. Seconded by Vic Odryna.
All in favor.

Coordinator with the New Hampshire Department of Environmental Services, presented the
Fairhill Marsh restoration project. Under a federal grant held by NHDES, Ducks Unlimited was
contracted by the Rockingham County Conservation District and the New Hampshire Coastal

          1.   RJH 8th Grade Event at the Goss Barn

Allyson Kreycik and Joanna Spelman presented plans for a community-focused, end-of- year
celebration for the 8th grade students at Goss Farm on or around June 16, following the junior

dinner with yard games at Goss Farm. The intent is for the event to foster students’ community
awareness and appreciation for their hometown.

Chair Lee noted that the Goss Farm Barn easement requires agricultural and educational uses and
suggested incorporating agricultural education into the event somehow to meet those

There was discussion about incorporating agricultural education into the curriculum near the end
of the school year and other ways to address the education piece.

Member Marttila highlighted previous student activities at Goss Farm, including elementary
school projects plantings blueberries and the pollinator station. He encouraged future
educational partnerships with local schools.

The Commission expressed its support for the event.

Hugh lee moved that the Rye Junior High School 8th Grade Class be allowed to conduct
their educational event at the Goss Farm on or about June 16, 2027. Seconded by Michael
Bean. All in favor.

        3. Financial Update

Treasurer Terhune reported that the proposed 2027 budget of $93,000 was presented to the Select
Board and Budget Committee. The proposed budget is a 24% reduction from the 2026 budget of
$122,000. The Select Board and Budget Committee did not have any issues with the budget and
both boards voted in support. He also reported that the RCC’s year-to-date expenditures are just
under $45,000 against a budget for 2026 of $122,835. This leaves 36.7 percent remaining in the
budget; however, there will be additional expenditures before the end of they year. Some of the
anticipated expenditures include the vernal pool mapping contract, alternative swallow nesting
structure costs, and maintenance to the Seavey Acres parking lot.

Treasurer Terhune spoke about the outstanding balance owed by the RCC to the Town in the
amount of $100,000 for the $180,000 Spiewak property acquisition on Appledore Avenue, after
$80,000 was paid from the land acquisition account. An additional $50,000 is expected from
land-use change tax revenue. Under a 1995 warrant article, the Commission receives 50 percent
of each payment made when land is removed from current use, up to $10,000 per payment. Five
payments are expected this year, which will be applied to the funds owed to the Town. The
remaining funds will come from the land acquisition fund.

Airfield trails. He is not sure who, but he thanked them and is grateful they were installed
properly. He also pointed out that he filled in some areas that he thought were necessary and the
northern part of the Orange loop is now done.

           b. Trail stewardship

Chair Lee noted that the upcoming Town Newsletter will have a short paragraph about the
incentive to start a trail stewardship program. The intent is to capture the energy and enthusiasm
from the trail users to help look after the trails in town. Tim Losik has offered to help with the
program as a trail stewardship coordinator to organize the work that is done on the trails.

Tim Losik of South Road attended the meeting to discuss the trail stewardship program.
Following conversations with Vice-Chair Odryna and Chair Lee, he offered to help organize trail
maintenance, as he walks the Cedar Run and Jim Raynes trails on a daily basis. The intent is to
organize volunteers in the community who are willing to help maintain the trails and report to
RCC any issues that they may find when walking the sites.

There was discussion about prioritizing the work that needs to be done on the trails, such as
addressing safety issues as a top priority. Moving forward, the intent is to keep the trails
maintained with issues being addressed as they arise. The goal is to have several people
involved in this program who are committed to giving feedback to the Commission on the
condition of the trails and lending a hand when maintenance is needed.

Anyone interested should send their contact information and area of interest to
trails@town.rye.nh.us

Hugh Lee moved that the Conservation Commission initiate a Trails Stewardship Program
for the care and maintenance of the trails and conversation lands, with event specific
policies. Seconded by Gary Terhune. All in favor.

           d. Jim Raynes

The Commission reviewed the proposal in the amount of $13,127 from Rye Beach Landscaping
to do the bod bridge replacement in the Jim Raynes Forest.

Member Marttila expressed concern about the cost and opposed the proposal, stating that
volunteers should complete the work. He offered to assist with the project alongside other
volunteers.

Member Bean opposed reinstalling bog bridges in the wetland but agreed that volunteers should
complete the work. He does not support the proposed contract or any bridges being within the
wetland.

The Commission agreed that the bid was too high and that volunteers should complete the work.
The voters have voted to reinstate the trails, so the bridges must be put back in as they were.

Gary Terhune moved that the replacement of the bog bridges in the Jim Raynes Forest be
done by volunteers. Seconded by Joe Marttila.
Vote: 3-2 Opposed: Michael Bean and Vic Odryna
Motion passed.
