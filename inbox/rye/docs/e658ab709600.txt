DRAFT MINUTES of the RCC Meeting 9/10/26


                         RYE CONSERVATION COMMISSION
                             Thursday, September 10, 2026 – 6:30 p.m.
                                   Rye Town Hall & via Zoom


Present: Chair Shawn Joyce, Vice-Chair Victor Odryna, Secretary/Clerk Hugh Lee, Treasurer
Gary Terhune, Glenn Suchocki, Michael Bean, Joe Marttila, and Alternate Jen Madden


    I.        CALL TO ORDER AND PLEDGE OF ALLEGIANCE

Chair Joyce called the meeting to order at 6:30 p.m. and led the Pledge of Allegiance.

    II.       SEATING OF ALTERNATES & INTRODUCTIONS
              Members of the RCC introduced themselves.

    III.      WETLANDS/WETLAND BUFFERS
              None

    IV.       REGULAR BUSINESS

           1. Vernal Pool Mapping Presentations

Firms presented proposals to the Conservation Commission in response to the RFP for vernal
pool mapping for specific conservation properties with trails. The RCC met with Wetland
Scientists Tracy Tarr and Justin Sherman from GZA Environmental. They also met with
Certified Wetland Scientist Sarah Sullivan from FB Environmental, along with Rachel Stevens
from Birch Tree Conservation Consulting. The Conservation Commission agreed to take the
presentations under advisement and discuss their thoughts at an upcoming meeting.

           2. Financial Update/2027 Budget

Treasurer Terhune reviewed the previous month’s expenditures. The year-to-date expenditures
are just under 30% of the RCC’s budget for 2026. Treasurer Terhune also reviewed the proposed
budget for 2027.

Hugh Lee moved that the Conservation Commission propose a 2027 budget of $93,800 to
the Select Board and Budget Committee, with the line item breakdown as presented by
Gary Terhune. Seconded by Vic Odryna. All in favor.

           3. Swallows Update

Treasurer Terhune reported that the plan is to implement the recommendations for the alternate
structures prior to snowfall. He and Vice-Chair Odryna will be looking for help with the
structures. He noted that Pam Hunt sent an addendum to her report, which was a reaffirmation of

      4. Trails Update

Secretary/Clerk Lee noted that the trails will be open due to the recent mandate. He suggested
that each member pick a trail and go out and see what needs to be done to comply with the vote
of the public. He continued that since Whitehorse is open, the RCC should investigate the rights
of access from Washington Road down Pasture Lane to the green trail.

Hugh Lee moved that the Conservation Commission request that Eric Maher provide a
comprehensive legal analysis of what rights the Town may have over Pasture Lane for
access from Washington Road to the newly reopened Whitehorse Green Trail.
Seconded by Gary Terhune. All in favor.

Vice-Chair Odryna spoke about the new website that talks about Rye’s trails, which has been in
the works for the past two years. The website can be easily changed and modified for future
updates. A postcard about the website went out to homes in the community encouraging people
to become a user of the site. Vice-Chair Odryna noted that there are two bills that need to be
approved for this project; Southport Printing, and Graphic Detail for work on the website.

Hugh Lee moved that the Conservation Commission approve the payment to Southport
Printing in the amount of $1,907.01 for invoice number 104301 for the trails postcard
printing and mailing. Seconded by Gary Terhune. All in favor.

Hugh Lee moved that the Conservation Commission approve the payment to Graphic
Details in the amount of $2,510 for invoice number 762693 for the trails postcard design
and web development. Seconded by Vic Odryna. All in favor.

      5. Goss Farm Update

Member Marttila gave an update on the Goss Farm. The recent farmers market concept that was
held on August 26th was a success. The vendors who participated were very happy with the
turnout. Kvitka Farms and North Pole Christmas Trees were two vendors who participated in the
event. Member Marttila thanked the volunteers who worked on the event. He pointed out that
they may be working with the Library to hold another market in October or November at the
Library Common, which would have a harvest fest theme.

          6. Town Forest Survey Plan Update

Secretary/Clerk Lee reported that the survey is complete. It delineates the split of the land that
was improperly incorporated into the Town Forest that was actually cemetery land. It was
divided as set forth in a warrant article that passed a couple of years ago. The plan is complete
and ready to be recorded.

Hugh Lee moved that the Conservation Commission authorize and direct JVA Surveyors
and/or Eric Maher and/or Hugh Lee to record the completed survey plan of the Rye Town
Forest at the Rockingham County Registry of Deeds as soon as possible. Seconded by Vic
Odryna. All in favor.

          7. Airfield Development Update

Vice-Chair Odryna spoke about the conservation land at Airfield Drive and a new proposed
development that is before the Planning Board for that area. He noted that the conservation
easement allows vehicular access for certain things, which is listed in the easement. There is
also an allowance to use the “overflow parking” near the last cul-de-sac, which is a dirt parking
area. Vice-Chair Odryna pointed out that the proposed plans for the new development do not
take into account the conservation land access points as listed in the easement. He also pointed
out that it seems that thirty percent of the finished parking lot is being removed for the
development and the overflow parking will disappear as well. He is bringing this to the
Commission’s attention so they can follow the development process through the Planning Board
stages.

Resident Susan Shepcaro noted that she is a planning board member and she has brought this to
the developer’s attention, so they are aware of the issues.

The RCC will continue to follow the proposal.

    V.       OTHER BUSINESS

Vice-Chair Odryna presented the signs that he is working on posting on the conservation
properties that allow hunting. The signs say that hunting is allowed and people should wear
orange, along with the hunting dates. There is also a QR Code for further information on what’s
allowed. Hunting is allowed at Cedar Run, Seavey Acres, Airfield, Whitehorse, Marden Woods,
and Jim Raynes Forest.

             None

    VII. APPROVAL OF MINUTES
       1. August 13, 2026

Hugh Lee moved to approve the minutes of August 13, 2026, as presented. Seconded by
Michael Bean. All in favor.

    X.    PUBLIC COMMENTS

Lisa Jacobus, 39 Pine Street, commented that there are a lot of people in town who are willing
to help clean the trails and put them back together.

Vice-Chair Odryna replied that anyone wanting to help should send an email to Land Use
Assistant Kara Campbell and they will be added to the list.

Ms. Jacobus stated that she liked the comment about utilizing students for the barn swallows.
She asked if there may be an opportunity to do this with the vernal pools with regard to the data
collection. She also asked about grants that may be available for the project. Ms. Jacobus
pointed out that she had questions about the presentations that were given at this meeting. She
asked how the questions can be asked and addressed before a decision is made by the RCC. She
also asked how the vernal pool information will be used.

Chair Joyce replied that this work has been needed for many years. Had this work been done,
there may have been a different outcome for the trails, as they may have been put in places where
they shouldn’t be. It’s extremely important to map the vernal pools.

Vice-Chair Odryna pointed out that the RCC is bringing in experts who can answer the
questions. They’re helping to find places that need to be protected, not just doing mapping in
general.

Cassandra Raynes, 24 Fairhill Ave., suggested that the vernal pool mapping be done in the
areas where hunting is allowed. She also suggested it be done in areas for new developments.

Vice-Chair Odryna explained that the properties that are under conservation are far greater than
just the ones with trails. The cost would be a lot more to do all properties. That is why the
project will be focused on certain areas. He further explained that developers are required to
map wetlands and vernal pools. They come to the RCC with that information when doing their
projects and that is how those are protected.

Sally King, 535 Wallis Road, asked where the money is coming from for the vernal pool
project. She agreed that the work is very important. However, it was mentioned that the funds
are going to come from the Brown Lane Farm vernal pool fund, which is not allowed. She noted
that all the relevant documentation has been provided to the RCC that says those funds are
solely for Brown Lane Farm. She agreed to forward that information to the Rye Conservation
Commission again.

Tom King, 535 Wallis Road, pointed out that historically, many volunteers have helped
maintain the trails, Goss Farm, and other properties. A lot of times power tools and equipment
have been used. Referring to the Goss Farm, he commented that it’s been said by members of
the Commission that volunteers shouldn’t be allowed to perform “dangerous activities” such as
cleaning the Goss Barn due to liability concerns. He encouraged the RCC to examine whether
some of their other projects, such as alternative structure construction, cleaning and
modifications, which involve being on a ladder, use of power equipment for property and trail
maintenance, and hauling heavy beams to replace the bog bridges, are suitable for volunteers.
He doesn’t expect to see anybody on a ladder without straps, helmets and harnesses when
building the alternative structures. The RCC has to set the rules to protect the Town. Maybe
volunteers shouldn’t get out there to clear the trails with a chainsaw. He asked that the Town be
protected, even if there are costs to get the work done.

Suzanne Barton, 114 Clark Road, commented that the Town has become so liability focused
that it’s taken the joy out of living. She suggested a simple release of liability form for people
who want to help. Ms. Barton also spoke in support of using good quality signage for the
hunting signs.

Susan Shepcaro, 45 Recreation Road, spoke about the calls she has received from residents
about the lack of dog waste bags in the boxes at the trails. She noted that it’s relatively easy to
keep them full and is usually the responsibility of someone on the Commission.

Referring to Ms. Shepcaro’s comment, Karen Stewart, 546 Washington Road, stated that it’s
been suggested to her that it’s an intentional move by this RCC to increase the amount of dog
waste on the trails so the dogs can be eliminated from walking the trails.

Truman Cavallaro, 6 Random Road, noted his credentials as a certified wetland scientist with
an extensive background in vernal pool work. He agreed to speak further with RCC about the
vernal pool mapping project.

Bob McGrath, 6 Rands Lane, thanked each member of the RCC for their service to the Town
of Rye. For the past several months, the RCC has had to make difficult decisions on issues that
generated tremendous emotion and disagreement in the community. The RCC didn’t always
make decisions that everyone agreed with, which is the nature of public service. However, he
saw a group of people who listened to professionals, considered the information available to
them, did their jobs and stayed the course. When the criticism became personal, the Commission
didn’t quit. When people tried to distract the RCC from their responsibilities, they didn’t get
distracted. When RCC was criticized simply because people didn’t agree with the decisions,
they didn’t fire back. The Commission kept showing up and trying to do what they felt was right
for the Town of Rye. RCC’s accomplishments went far beyond the controversy that has received
so much attention. Structure and accountability has been brought to this commission. The
Commission has brought financial clarity and made responsible decisions on how the taxpayers’
dollars should be spent. Protecting Rye’s resources means not only protecting the land but the
taxpayers who fund that work. He is embarrassed by the way some members of the RCC have
been treated by fellow residents. People have the right to disagree and argue for a different
approach. However, volunteering for a town should never mean accepting personal attacks and
