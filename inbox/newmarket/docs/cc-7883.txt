          Vote:             Unanimously Approved (7-0-0).

          Angel King, Bennett Way, said she was formally putting the Council on notice that it is
          failing its oversight duties over the Town Manager under the Town Charter. She said:

              • The Council has tried to minimize a near-miss incident involving children at a bus
                stop in a construction zone by referring concerns back to the Town Manager.
              • The Town has acknowledged the near-miss to her, yet has said publicly that it
                followed all construction safety rules. She called that statement false.
              • The Town has used RSA 91-A to delay records requests rather than provide
                transparency.
              • The Council does not allow two-way dialogue with residents, yet allowed a
                school district PowerPoint she called electioneering.
              • Public Works and its contractor did not provide basic pedestrian safety, such as
                traffic control and flaggers. She said she has videos and other evidence the
                Council has not reviewed.

          She said she will escalate the matter to outside agencies if the Council does not act.

          Jennifer Dion, 2 Kimball Way, asked the Council to restore the stop sign at the bottom
          of Durrell Drive, removed during this summer's paving work. She said:

              • Her husband, Craig Dion, walks with crutches and was nearly hit by a car on
                Election Day.

                bikes and scooters. Safety should come before traffic flow.
              • Without signage, drivers turning left onto Durrell Drive and drivers turning toward
                Huckins Lane have no clear right of way.
              • Because the road turns from Durrell onto Durrell, renaming one section would

     6.    Report of the Town Manager


          a.   TM Report - November 4, 2026
               Town Manager Fournier presented the written report and added the following:

                   • Durrell Drive / Huckins Lane traffic review: Radar data from September
                     2 to October 20 shows an average speed of 17 mph. Only two vehicles
                     exceeded the 25 mph limit, at 26 and 28 mph. The radar sign will be
                     moved to other spots for comparison. Engineering is evaluating street

Town of Newmarket, NH                                                              11/4/2026

                     lights for visibility after the time change. Staff will also consider tonight's
                     public comments. He said the concerns are not falling on deaf ears.
                   • Tax rate: A preliminary tax rate has been received. The final rate is
                     expected by early next week.
                   • Housing Opportunity Planning (HOP) grant: NH Housing approved a
                     final round of about $35,000 for Community Development. The contract is
                     expected the first week of November.
                   • Community Development Block Grant (CDBG): The Town will apply for
                     up to $750,000 on behalf of the Newmarket Housing Authority for Ernest
                     A. Clark Terrace. Planned work may include sanitary sewer pipes, roofs,
                     and sidewalk and road repairs. Up to $35,000 would cover administration,
                     including a grant writer. Grant-writer proposals were due November 3, and
                     the application is due to CDFA by January 25, 2027. Federal rules require
                     public hearings, which will be scheduled.
                   • Town Hall parking lot: Phase 2 is finished. Crews found an old
                     foundation under the second phase, which added work.
                   • Radar speed signs: Seacoast Crime Stoppers will fund two more radar
                     speed signs for the Police Department, about $6,900, paying the
                     manufacturer directly.

               Council comments and questions:

                   • Councilor LaMattina: Noted the radar sign has already been moved and
                     street lighting already exists at the location. Drivers at the bottom of
                     Durrell Drive effectively must stop, and without a stop sign there is a
                     collision risk. He asked the Town to do what it can for residents of that
                     neighborhood.
                   • Councilor Blackstone: Agreed the Durrell Drive street naming is
                     confusing for non-residents, even with GPS. He supported considering a
                     rename while acknowledging the difficulty.
                   • Councilor Dornblut: Suggested a pedestrian crossing so residents of
                     Kimball Way can reach the Durrell Drive sidewalk away from the
                     intersection.
                   • Councilor McShera: Asked what the CDBG funds would be used for.
                     Town Manager Fournier said the Housing Authority, an independent
                     agency whose commissioners the Council appoints, oversees the project.
                     The Town must apply on its behalf. Councilor McShera thanked Seacoast
                     Crime Stoppers for the radar sign donation.
                   • Councilor Dornblut: Asked whether the grant writer is paid only if the
                     grant is awarded. Town Manager Fournier confirmed the writer works at
                     risk, paid from the grant, which is common practice.

              • Arts, Culture & Tourism Commission (Councilor McShera): The Commission
                requested $8,000 in the budget. That Town funding helped with the mural, the

                mosaic and the community sing, and about $40,000 more was raised from the
                community over the past year.
              • Energy & Environment Committee (Councilor LaMattina): The Committee
                met with Town and library staff on a possible solar and battery storage system. It
                would qualify the library as a critical facility during a long-term power outage.
                Grants could cover about 50% of battery costs and 30% of solar and installation
                costs, with the rest recovered through savings over about nine years. Challenges
                include the library's slate roof, possible fire code issues, upfront funding, and a
                2027 year-end completion deadline. The Committee will still develop two plans: a
                full system and batteries only. It is also exploring more EV charger sites with
                possible Volkswagen emissions settlement funding. In response to the Chair, he
                said critical facility status is what qualifies the project for the roughly 50% utility
                funding.
              • Budget Committee (Councilor Conley): Met October 26 and discussed
                whether to take a position on Question 2 for the November 3 election. No motion
                was made to take a position. The Committee set a schedule of upcoming
                meetings to review the School and Town budgets.
