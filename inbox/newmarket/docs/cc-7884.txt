     3.    Budget Review by Department


          a.   9:00 AM Recreation Department
               Proposed $388,538, up $33,703 (9%), mainly full-time salaries. The Recreation
               Director explained that two full-time staff resigned in January, citing salary and
               workload; positions were adjusted to the recent salary survey.
               Telecommunications costs dropped. The Arts, Culture & Tourism Commission
               line rises $4,000 to $8,000, mostly for the new mill worker mural on Elm Street,
               mural finalist fees and Pocket Park panel maintenance. Fireworks stay at last
               year's level ahead of the 300th anniversary.

                   • Councilor McShera: asked about salary as a factor in turnover and about
                     staff training (NHRPA, Northern New England conference, NRPA).
                   • Councilor LaMattina: asked about the Newmarket Youth Athletic
                     Association. It is a separate organization; the Town provides a grant of
                     about $21,000 and manages field reservations.
                   • No changes.

               Recreation – Revolving Fund

               Proposed $578,977, up $27,666 (5%). The fund is supported entirely by
               participant fees, not taxes. Contract instructors now sit under contractual services
               (70/30 split). Summer camp grew by 40 children to 240. Equipment purchase is
               budgeted at $45,000 for repairs and storage improvements.

                   • Councilor Dornblut: asked what makes up the $101,500 Recreation
                     Programs line (summer camp ~$22,000; Sunrise Sunset Center $34,000;
                     events ~$35,500; splash pad $8,500; teen program $1,500). The senior
                     center spent $36,000 and brought in $48,000. Councilor Dornblut

                     rather than adding line items.
                   • Councilor Blackstone: asked about fund structure. The CFO confirmed it is
                     Fund 05, a special enterprise fund owned by the Town. Fund balance is
                     near $1 million after buying 11 Creighton Street for $685,000.
                   • Councilor Glazebrook: asked about health insurance; addressed later

          b.   9:30 AM Police Department
               Proposed about $2.89 million, up $123,731 (4.5%), mainly the voter-approved
               collective bargaining agreement (~$59,000). Part-time salaries rise $13,000 for
               prosecutor fees and more accreditation manager hours. Overtime rises ~$17,000
               for training, court time and town events.

                   • Body cameras: switching from Motorola to Axon, adding about $20,000 a
                     year in lease costs. A state law change on January 1 makes most footage
                     disclosable, which will require dedicated redaction time.
                   • Councilor Conley/Councilor Dornblut/Councilor McShera: asked about
                     part-time costs, overtime and speed enforcement data (six speed signs
                     plus a trailer that records speed only).
                   • Councilor Blackstone: the Department is pursuing state accreditation
                     ($500/year) rather than CALEA ($5,000–$10,000/year).
                   • Councilor LaMattina: recent departures were personal; the Department is
                     fully staffed for the first time in over 10 years.
                   • Councilor Dornblut: online parking permits will be funded by users.
                   • No changes.


          c.   10:00 AM Environmental Services
               Water

               Proposed $1,778,074, up $24,722, funded by ratepayers. Main drivers are health
               insurance and electricity. The department has two vacancies (systems technician
               and administrative).

                   • Councilor LaMattina: land for a new water tower will come from capital
                     reserves.
                   • Solar is not cost-effective without grant aid.
                   • No changes.


               Wastewater

               Increase of 2.43%, below inflation. Part-time salaries fund two summer
               engineering interns from UNH. Sludge disposal (Turnkey, Rochester) rises under
               a five-year contract with three years left.

                   • A separate warrant article (p. 142) funds asset management through a
                     state loan that is forgiven on completion.
                   • No changes.

          d.   10:30 AM Public Works Department
               Public Works – Administration (p. 82)

               Proposed $796,940, up $40,000, mainly salaries. The department requested
               $125,000 for overtime after last year's heavy winter; the Town Manager reduced
               it to $100,000.

                   • Councilor Conley: "labor" salaries are hourly staff; "full-time" are salaried
                     administrative staff.
                   • Councilor Blackstone/Councilor McShera: the budget keeps three
                     administrative positions for a planned transition. The Director role may
                     become more administrative and the engineer a deputy director.
                   • No changes.

               Roadways & Sidewalks

               Proposed $667,900, up $33,000, mainly salt. The Town took over about 5.5
               miles of former state roads.

               The Public Works Director said the $200,000 hot top line cannot sustain the
               paving plan; the real need is about $450,000. Mix is about $100 a ton and rising.
               Grant Road, Doe Farm, Fox Circle, part of Doral and Hershey Lane were not
               completed this year.

               Councilor Blackstone moved to increase hot top to $300,000; Councilor
               Glazebrook seconded. The estimated tax impact is about $0.06 per $1,000,
               under $40 a year for a median home. Supporters cited visible benefits and rising
               future costs. Councilor Conley questioned whether the need had been quantified.
               Consensus to add $100,000 (4 in favor).

               Street Lights, Bridges, Cemeteries

                   • Street lights: up $1,500 for electricity.
                   • Bridges: up $8,000 for metal guardrails replacing wood.
                   • Cemeteries (p. 97): up $6,336, mainly salaries.
                   • No changes.

               Buildings & Grounds

               Proposed about $979,675, up $56,000, mainly utilities. Councilor LaMattina
               asked about funding to fix the Schanda Park river walkway, a Riverfront Advisory
               Committee priority. He moved to add $20,000 to Maintenance Parks (line 54300);
               seconded by Councilor Glazebrook for discussion. The Town Engineer estimated
               the cost closer to $5,000, which the Town Manager said he could find. The
               increase was not adopted.

               The Council recessed for 10 minutes.

               Vehicles

               Proposed $354,000, up $23,300.

                   • Councilor LaMattina: diesel is already running over prior-year costs. The
                     Town Manager said the budget has no contingency; shortfalls are covered
                     by holding vacancies open.
                   • Councilor Dornblut: radio maintenance (+$4,000) covers replacing old,
                     failing mobile radios.
                   • Councilor McShera: fire vehicle maintenance (+$10,000) reflects required
                     third-party pump and aerial testing.
                   • No changes.

               Solid Waste

               Proposed about $1,033,080, up $23,180, mostly contractual. A $300,000 capital
               reserve withdrawal is proposed to buy totes for automated collection expected in
               FY2028.

                   • Councilor Dornblut: asked whether collection trucks could weigh waste; to
                     be answered as implementation is planned.
                   • No changes.


          e.   11:00 AM Fire Department
               Fire & Rescue (p. 103)

               Up about $437,500. Full-time salaries of $1,173,745 include converting the fire
               chief from part-time to full-time, as recommended by the MRI study. Part-time/per
               diem was proposed at $683,000, up $233,000; last year's actual was $585,000.

               Staffing: two full-time firefighters, one per diem and the full-time chief on
               weekdays; three per diem on nights and weekends. The Town Manager said he
               would have set per diem at $500,000–$550,000. A motion to reduce it to
               $500,000 was seconded. Consensus to reduce per diem to $500,000.

                   • Councilor Blackstone: noted Fire & Rescue submitted no goals; the Town
                     Manager will request them.

               Emergency Management

               Level funded at $2,250. A grant resolution to update the emergency operations
               plan is pending this week. No changes.

               Town Council (p. 32)

               The Town Manager recommended removing the $2,912 part-time increase
               because the minute-taker vacancy will not be filled. Agreed.

               Town Manager and Finance

                   • Town Manager: up $10,616, mainly salaries and books. The Town
                     Manager's salary is split across general, water and sewer funds. Councilor
                     Blackstone asked about goals; the Town Manager said his goals are the
                     Council's goals.
                   • Finance: down $9,418 from an anticipated retirement; the position will be
                     filled at a lower rate.

               Human Resources

               Proposed $2,992,340, up $155,368 (5%). Retirement contributions are down;
               health insurance is up $170,396. The guaranteed maximum rate came in at 9%
               versus the 10% budgeted. The CFO recommended a partial reduction to keep a
               small cushion. Coverage is unaffected.

               A motion to reduce the health insurance line by $111,816 was seconded.
               Consensus to reduce. The CFO advised against reducing water and sewer
               health lines this year because new hires' plans are unknown.

               Town Clerk (p. 46)

               Down $4,949 in part-time salaries after a position moved to full-time last year.
               Election officials increase because the state primary moves to June, into
               FY2028. Equipment includes $4,800 for electronic poll books. No changes.

               Code Enforcement, Welfare, Assessing, Legal

                   • Code Enforcement: up $3,913 for merit increases, dues and training.
                   • Welfare: up $1,012; assistance is mandated by state law and town
                     guidelines. Councilor Dornblut requested a future presentation on welfare
                     operations. Councilor McShera raised staffing concerns; personnel
                     matters would go to nonpublic session.
                   • Assessing: contract level, software up.
                   • Legal: level; counsel is on retainer.
                   • No changes.

               Library

               Proposed about $662,498, up $49,037. Increases cover salary-study
               adjustments and a new $6,203 share of Siemens HVAC maintenance. Full-time
               library staff share the town health plan. No changes.

     4.   Town Manager Remaining Budget Lines
          Remaining Lines

              • Debt principal: up $787. Debt interest: down $5,376.
              • Information Technology: up about $21,000, mainly 5% software increases and
                GIS licensing. Councilor McShera confirmed the licenses are necessary.
              • Media Services: up $6,636; includes on-call PR firm JPSG, paid only when used.
              • Community Grants: level. COAST's share will rise next year.
              • Property & Liability Insurance: up $47,185.
              • Social Service Grants: up $7,350; recipients must submit audited financials.
              • Patriotic Purposes: level (Memorial Day parade).
              • No changes.

          Capital Reserve Fund Contributions (Warrant Articles)

          The Town Manager recommends $2,349,235 in capital reserve contributions; the CIP
          committee recommended $3,389,161. These are separate warrant articles, not part of
          the operating budget, and are funded from fund balance with no tax rate impact. The
          largest items are the water tower (drawn from the water fund balance) and $300,000 for
          solid waste totes.

              • Councilor LaMattina: asked to add $25,000 for Schanda Park waterfront items.
                No capital reserve fund exists for the park; creating one requires a resolution.
                The Planning Director said baseline analysis is underway and a dedicated fund
                could be part of that conversation. The presiding officer preferred waiting for a
                plan with costs. No change made.
              • Councilor Glazebrook: asked how cutting stormwater from the proposed
                $211,666 to $50,000 affects planned work. The Town Manager said
                departments will adjust once budgets are final.
              • Councilor Blackstone: raised elevated bacteria in Moonlight Brook near the
                Schanda Park dock and boat ramp, and asked about warning signs. The Town
                Engineer said the source is unconfirmed; stormwater reserve funds pay for the
                illicit discharge detection program (MS4 compliance).
              • Councilor Dornblut: asked whether the water tank, which benefits all residents
                (e.g., fire protection), should be funded only by water users. The Town Manager
                said ratepayers are the primary beneficiaries, schools pay water rates, and the
                project also uses $800,000 of water fund balance and grants.
              • Councilor LaMattina: asked about remaining fund balance. The CFO said
                contributions draw from general, water, sewer and other fund balances. Policy
                (GFOA/DRA guidance and a town ordinance) keeps unreserved fund balance at

     5.   Adjournment
          Budget Total and Adjournment

          With the workshop changes, the proposed FY2028 operating budget totals
          $19,441,428, about $198,000 below the Town Manager's proposal. The Town Manager
          estimated the tax rate impact as an increase of about $0.62 per $1,000, $0.11 less than
          his proposed budget. He had no further recommendations. The Council will vote on the
          budget Wednesday.

          Councilor LaMattina asked about the school budget increase; it has not yet been
          received.

          The meeting adjourned at approximately 11:50 AM without objection.
