Discussion and Follow Up:

   •  Review Approach Using Aerial Map and Area Segmentation

         o  A 2024 aerial map circulated by Matt Sullivan frames discussion by areas labeled 1–3
            (to be discussed first).  Areas 4–6 (East of Concord St to the river to be discussed at
            subsequent meetings).
         o  Sub-areas 1-3 identified: Manchester Street Gateway, Western Woods, Stonehouse
            and picnic areas to Concord St.

   •  Greeley Park West Side Gateway and Field Activation

         o  The Manchester St (west side) entry and adjacent field are underutilized and feel
            disconnected from the main Park. Framing design around reconnection and reimagined
            use of park land was discussed.
         o  Ideas: modest parking, swing benches, gathering areas, addressing lower-area
            flooding/drainage.
         o  Signage: gateway signs, trail map kiosks, or architectural entry features aligned with
            park history; vandalism risk and cost for kiosks noted.

   •  Manchester Street Gateway – Current Uses and Desired Changes Discussed       o  Existing elements: disc golf practice structures old and in disrepair; slated for removal
         once Roby Park is operational), frequent LARP use (members emphasized not
         displacing them), legacy fitness stations along the path (overgrown/aging/largely
         unused).
      o  Pedestrian gateway vision: prioritize pedestrian access; avoid major vehicle entry due to
         capacity and neighborhood concerns. Current on-street parking by LARP users raises
         safety issues near a blind corner.
      o  Parking concepts: explore small, structured parking near fire road entrance, possibly
         diagonal spaces with robust barriers to prevent vehicles from continuing up pedestrian
         paths. Terrain/slope and proximity to private property require caution.
      o  Conclusions: . Improve connections through West Side Woods between the west side
         and Stone House area, balancing accessibility (strollers, wheelchairs, mobility aids) with
         discouraging high-speed e-bikes/scooters.
      o  Remove unused disc golf elements; preserve pedestrian access and support informal
         groups; study limited, safe parking with strong barriers to prevent vehicle traffic beyond
         the parking area
      o  Consider path designs and signage within ordinance limits to maintain ADA usability
         while deterring unauthorized motorized traffic.

•  Woods Management and Natural Area Use

      o  Current conditions: downed trees, limited sunlight. Historical groves (~120 years old)
         are overgrown/past prime.
      o  Recommendation: engage professional forest management to assess health and
         propose interventions; consider interpretive wildlife signage and small clearings with
         benches, or minimal intervention to preserve natural character.
      o  Concerns: cost/scale and long-term tree health. General agreement to seek
         professional advice and pursue modest improvements (trails, tree management,
         connections).

•  Public Grills and Neighborhood Impact (West Side)

      o  Discussion of adding grills/party space on West Side raised mixed views and
         neighborhood concerns (noise, drinking despite rules, loud music, barrier bypassing,
         vandalism). Flooding near existing parking noted.
      o  Safety/maintenance: benches need cementing (~$5,000 each); prior homeless
         encampments on Manchester Street woods mentioned. •  Stone House Area Use and Preservation

      o  Areas surrounding the Stone House is highly uses and valued as community space for
         picnics, photos, and passive recreation. Desire to preserve Olmsted-era legacy with
         thoughtful, modest improvements.
      o  Caution against over-programming to protect aesthetics and photography appeal.
         Playground needs expert review within the master plan.
      o  Conclusion: preserve core character; pursue expert-guided upgrades for playground
         and connectivity.

•  Picnic Area Layout and Potential Pavilion

      o  Current picnic area sits back with unused space near the back road; tables/grills near
         Stone House walkway are often moved and rarely used.
      o  Proposal: consider a covered, rentable pavilion to generate revenue and support larger
         gatherings, with siting near the picnic-side walkway while preserving open play areas
         and Stone House aesthetics. Comparative examples: Lakewood, OH (Emerald
         Necklace) and Brooklyn Bridge Park.

•  Pools and Infrastructure Funding Status

      o  $5M approved for pools covers Centennial and Rotary; Stone House-area pool funding
         separate and already approved, with extensive infrastructure work due to aging
         systems. Expected opening by next summer.

•  Operations, Enforcement, and Long-Term Planning

      o  Concerns about weekend enforcement and misuse; limited personnel for monitoring.
         Include operations/enforcement funding in the master plan.
      o  Plan for increased urban density and 50–100-year horizons, echoing 1908 forethought.
         Next steps include improved mapping for the Stone House area and continued detailed
         planning.

•  Stone House Landscape and Sensory Garden Overgrowth

      o  Overgrowth on the left side of the pergola/facade at the Stone House, corresponding to
         the Sensory Garden with giant wisteria, was noted as an aesthetic concern. Overgrowth
         now blocks the symmetry of the Stone House view.  A prior plaque exists; ambiguity
         remains on National Garden Club ties.

•  Master Plan Scope and RFP Preparation

      o  The committee is compiling items for a potential RFP for a park master planning
         consultant before seeking funding from the Board of Public Works and Board of
         Aldermen.          o  Consultant scope: evaluate aesthetics, historical context, connectivity, practical usage,
            operations, enforcement, and long-term resilience.

   •  Boo Bash Planning and Volunteer Coordination

         o  Boo Bash event: 3:00–6:00 PM with a haunted house (9 high school actors), line
            management, pumpkin painting near tennis courts (supplies provided), a wooded
            hayride with decorations/actors, and character photo ops on stage (e.g., Wednesday
            Addams).
         o  Additional volunteers needed for pumpkin painting to free staff for the hayride.

   •  DPW Update and Social Media/Marketing Collaboration

         o  Reports from a DPW meeting covered involvement, master planning, and marketing
            efforts. Ideas include photo submissions in front of the Stone House.

   •  Next Meeting Rescheduling

         o  The next regular meeting conflicted with Veterans Day. November 18, 2026. Next
            meeting set for November 10, 2026.
