Mr. Minkarah welcomed the public and noted that all five members
were present, constituting a full board.

Mr. Minkarah explained the ZBA procedures, stating that the
public is invited to present cases within a fifteen-minute
timeframe. He noted that comments could be submitted for future
meetings via email to zb@nashuanh.gov or by mail to P.O. Box
2019, Nashua, NH. He identified the points of law required for
applicants to address relative to variances, explaining that an
applicant must satisfy five specific statutory criteria: that
the variance will not be contrary to the public interest, the
spirit of the ordinance is observed, substantial justice is
done, surrounding property values are not diminished, and
literal enforcement would result in an unnecessary hardship. He
outlined the order of testimony, providing applicants up to 15
minutes for presentation, followed by 5-minute periods for
speakers in favor, speakers in opposition or with questions, a
5-minute rebuttal for the applicant, and a final 5-minute
closing for a designated opposition spokesperson.

PRE-HEARING BOARD DISCUSSION: Mr. Minkarah asked Planning
Manager Sam Durfee to explain why the application was returning
to the Board. Mr. Durfee explained that the applicant previously
sought variances that differed from the specific density
variance requested on this evening's agenda. He stated that
during staff review subsequent to the applicant's submission to
the Planning Board, staff determined that a formal dimensional
density variance was required under the Land Use Code in order
for the Planning Board to take jurisdiction over a complete
application.

TESTIMONY: Anand Mahendrakar, Blue Coral Canal LLC, 20 Canal
Street, Nashua, NH. Mr. Mahendrakar stated that he was
representing Blue Coral Canal LLC, owner of 20 Canal Street. He
requested a dimensional variance from lot area and density
requirements to allow a total of six residential units on the
existing 10,308-square-foot lot. He emphasized that at a
previous ZBA hearing, the Board had already approved adding
three residential units to the building, bringing the total from
three units to six units. He clarified that tonight's
application is not a new six-unit concept, but rather seeks the
specific dimensional density relief required for the exact same
six-unit proposal. Mr. Mahendrakar stated that the property is
an existing developed residential lot with fixed boundaries, the
use will remain residential, and all parking, access, building,
and life safety requirements will be satisfied through City

                                                                             2


review. He noted that while the density formula calculates a
requirement of 52,272 square feet for six units, the proposal
causes no change to the physical building footprint. He asserted
that the underlying goals of the density ordinance—preventing
overcrowding and protecting public safety and neighborhood
character—are fully met.

In response to a question from Mr. Minkarah, Mr. Mahendrakar
confirmed that there are no changes whatsoever to the plan from
what was previously presented to the Board.

SPEAKING IN FAVOR: No one.

SPEAKING IN OPPOSITION OR WITH QUESTIONS OR CONCERNS: No one.

END OF PUBLIC HEARING, BEGINNING OF PUBLIC MEETING: Mr. Nehiley
spoke in favor of the application for the third time,
reaffirming his previous support. He noted that the building has
an irregular shape, the project will convert an unused, existing
garage, and the footprint of the structure is not changing. He
explained that creating an apartment within a strange cutout in
the building actually eliminates previously identified fire
safety concerns, making the proposal a win-win for the property
and neighborhood. He added that the lot has more than enough
parking, sits in a dense district, and the proposed small units
(between 500 and 700 square feet) are highly consistent with the
character of the surrounding area.

Mr. Lionel spoke in favor, agreeing with Mr. Nehiley. He noted
that while there was technical ambiguity regarding whether a
density variance was required, granting the dimensional relief
is appropriate.

Mr. Minkarah concurred, noting that the six-unit density is
consistent with the neighborhood, and the parcel is an unusually
deep and long double-frontage lot.

MOTION by Mr. Minkarah to approve the variance application on
behalf of the applicant as advertised.

Mr. Minkarah stated that granting the variance would not be
contrary to the public interest because the proposed six-unit
density is consistent with the established character of the
area, and there will be no injury to public health, safety, or
welfare.


                                                                             3


Mr. Minkarah stated that the spirit of the ordinance would be
observed because the proposal is consistent with the surrounding
neighborhood character, and the spirit of the ordinance is to
allow for the reasonable use of property. He noted that the
density requirement, given the physical size and layout of the
lot, is reasonable for this district.

Mr. Minkarah stated that granting the variance would do
substantial justice because it allows for the reasonable use of
the property.

Mr. Minkarah stated that the Board has no evidence indicating
that surrounding property values would be diminished.

Mr. Minkarah stated that owing to special conditions of the
property, denial of the variance would result in unnecessary
hardship because the property is an unusually large, long, and
narrow double-frontage lot for this neighborhood that can
readily accommodate the increased density. Therefore, there is
no fair and substantial relationship between the general public
purpose of the density provisions of the ordinance and their
specific application to this property, and the proposed
residential use is reasonable.

SECONDED by Mr. Lionel. MOTION TO APPROVE CARRIED UNANIMOUSLY 5-
0.

Review of Board Bylaws: Planning Manager Sam Durfee presented
proposed Bylaw amendments regarding applicant requests for
hearing postponements or continuances. Mr. Durfee explained that
recent applications seeking postponements prior to scheduled
hearings—often due to less than a full five-member board being
present—have complicated the public hearing process.

The Board discussed the proposed revisions in Section 10 and
Subsection O of the Bylaws:

   1. Newspaper Notice Requirement: Mr. Durfee noted the removal
      of the outdated requirement to publish notice of
      postponements in the newspaper six days prior to a hearing,
      stating it is logistically impossible and not required by
      state statute.
   2. Advance Request Window & Subsequent Requests: Board members
      discussed establishing clear guidelines for advance
      postponement requests. Mr. Lionel expressed concern over
      inadvertently forcing citizens to travel to City Hall
      unnecessarily for high-interest cases. Mr. Minkarah and Mr.
      Lionel suggested a two-tiered rule: an initial postponement
      request may be granted automatically by staff if received
      sufficiently in advance, but any second or subsequent
      postponement request must come before the full Board at the
      public hearing to show cause.
   3. Six Calendar Days Deadline: The Board reached a consensus
      that initial written postponement requests must be received
      by the Planning Department prior to 12:00 PM (noon) six
      calendar days preceding the scheduled public hearing. Any
      request received after that deadline, or any second
      postponement request, must be formally presented to and
      voted on by the ZBA at the hearing.

Mr. Durfee agreed to incorporate the six-calendar-day
clarification and second-request provisions into the draft
Bylaws for formal notice and action at the next meeting.
