ZONING BOARD OF ADJUSTMENT PUBLIC HEARING AND MEETING
                        Tuesday, October 13, 2026

A public hearing of the Zoning Board of Adjustment was held on
Tuesday, October 13, 2026, at 6:30 PM, in the 3rd floor
auditorium at Nashua City Hall, 229 Main Street, Nashua, NH.

Jay Minkarah, Chair, asked for a Roll Call:
Jay Minkarah, Chair
Josh Nehiley, Vice Chair
JP Boucher, Member
Steve Lionel, Member
Joseph Patry, Member

Sam Durfee, Planning Manager

Mr. Minkarah welcomed the public and introduced the members of
the Board and staff present for the evening session. He noted
that all five members were present, constituting a full board.

Mr. Minkarah explained the Board's procedures, stating that the
public is invited to present cases within a fifteen-minute
timeframe. He encouraged the public to submit comments for
future meetings via email to the Planning Department at
zb@nashuanh.gov or by mail to P.O. Box 2019, Nashua, NH, 03061.
He identified the points of law required for applicants to
address relative to variances and appeals of administrative
decisions. For a variance, Mr. Minkarah explained that the Board
must find five specific statutory criteria are met: that the
variance would not be contrary to the public interest, the
spirit of the ordinance would be observed, substantial justice
would be done, no diminution of surrounding property values
would occur, and that an unnecessary hardship exists due to
special conditions of the property. For an appeal of an
administrative decision, he explained that the Board reviews a
formal determination made by an administrative officer of the
City, in this case the Planning Manager. He outlined the order
of testimony, including the applicant’s presentation, speakers
in favor, speakers in opposition or with questions, the
applicant’s final five-minute rebuttal, and an opportunity for a
designated spokesperson from the opposition to address the Board
last if necessary.

Mr. Minkarah asked Mr. Durfee if there were any changes to the
agenda. Mr. Durfee reported that there were no changes to the
agenda order, but noted that an additional letter of testimony
had been received regarding Case No. 4 (384 Main Street).

1. Timothy Dionne (Owner & Applicant) 19 Shattuck Street (Sheet
45 Lot 129) requesting variance from Land Use Code Section 190-
17(E)(2) to exceed maximum driveway width, 24 feet permitted –
34 feet proposed for a paved driveway expansion within the front
setback. RB Zone, Ward 3.

Voting on this case:
Jay Minkarah
Josh Nehiley
JP Boucher
Steve Lionel
Joseph Patry

Timothy Dionne, 19 Shattuck Street, Nashua, NH. Mr. Dionne
stated that he is seeking a variance to expand his existing
paved driveway from 24 feet to 34 feet within the front yard
setback. He explained that space along the westward side of his
house is extremely constrained due to an existing tiered
retaining wall, making it difficult to park two vehicles side-
by-side or access the front entry steps without hugging the
vehicles. He noted that with a growing family and two children
who will eventually require vehicles, expanding the driveway
toward the side yard is necessary to provide safe off-street
parking. Mr. Dionne reported that he received an email copy of
an objection letter submitted by a rear abutter. He stated that
he believes the objection stems from an unrelated personal
grievance rather than a legitimate property impact, noting that
existing vegetation absorbs drainage. He confirmed that he has
already contacted a professional surveyor to perform a boundary
survey to establish the exact property line prior to
construction and install a fence.

Mr. Durfee noted that during staff review, a tiered retaining
wall on the property was observed to exceed four feet in
cumulative height. He advised the applicant that retaining walls
over four feet require a building permit and requested that Mr.
Dionne retroactively file for a permit and inspection with the
Building Safety Department, which Mr. Dionne agreed to do.

In response to questions from Mr. Minkarah regarding proximity,
Mr. Dionne stated that the proposed driveway expansion sits
approximately one foot inside his property line and cannot be
shifted closer to the house due to the location of the retaining
wall structure.


                                                                             2


SPEAKING IN FAVOR: No one.

SPEAKING IN OPPOSITION OR WITH QUESTIONS OR CONCERNS: No one
spoke in person.

Mr. Minkarah read a letter of concern into the record from
Steven Deschenes, 6-8 Jefferson Street, Nashua, NH. Mr.
Deschenes stated that his property directly abuts the subject
parcel to the rear and side. He expressed concern regarding the
lack of a professional survey accompanying the application,
noting the hand-drawn sketch was labeled "not to scale." He
cited concerns over stormwater runoff draining toward his parcel
due to local topography and requested that a clear drainage plan
be required. Mr. Deschenes cited uncertainty regarding the
property boundary line and requested that the Board continue the
hearing until a survey is completed, or alternatively deny the
variance.

SPEAKING IN FAVOR – REBUTTAL: Mr. Dionne reiterated that he has
already scheduled a licensed land surveyor to locate the
property stakes and confirm the boundary line before any
construction begins. He added that the driveway area will be
graded flat to prevent water from running toward neighboring
parcels.

END OF PUBLIC HEARING, BEGINNING OF PUBLIC MEETING: Mr. Boucher
spoke in favor of the application, noting that the requested 34-
foot width represents a minor four-foot expansion over the
allowed maximum. He observed during a site visit that vehicle
parking is tight on the parcel and that expanding the driveway
is a reasonable request consistent with the residential
character of Ward 3.

Mr. Nehiley stated that while he recognizes the applicant's
parking constraints, the abutter at 6-8 Jefferson Street raised
valid concerns regarding property line uncertainty and
stormwater runoff. He stated he could support approval only if
explicit stipulations were included requiring no runoff onto
neighboring land, a retroactive building permit for the
retaining wall, and a boundary survey prior to construction.

Mr. Lionel and Mr. Patry agreed that the driveway expansion is
reasonable given the site constraints. Mr. Durfee clarified that
a driveway permit from the Department of Public Works is
required, which will enforce proper pitch and drainage. Board
members agreed to include three specific stipulations to protect
the abutter.

                                                                             3


MOTION by Mr. Boucher to approve the variance application on
behalf of the owner as advertised, with stipulations.

Mr. Boucher stated that granting the variance would not be
contrary to the public interest because expanding the driveway
by four feet beyond the allowed width is consistent with the
established residential character of the neighborhood and allows
the applicant to gain necessary off-street parking.

Mr. Boucher stated that the spirit of the ordinance would be
observed because the driveway expansion accommodates required
off-street parking in a dense neighborhood setting where space
is relatively tight, without creating visual disharmony.

Mr. Boucher stated that granting the variance would do
substantial justice because it allows the homeowner to safely
park family vehicles off the street and maneuver around the
vehicles without being restricted by the narrow existing layout,
whereas denying it would impose a significant hardship with no
corresponding gain to the public.

Mr. Boucher stated that surrounding property values would not be
diminished by a paved driveway addition.

Mr. Boucher stated that owing to special conditions of the
property, denial of the variance would result in unnecessary
hardship because the existing driveway layout is constrained by
the primary home's entrance steps and an existing tiered
retaining wall, making it impossible to widen the driveway on
the opposite side. The proposed driveway expansion is a
reasonable residential use.

Stipulations of approval:

   1. Driveway construction shall be designed and pitched such
      that no stormwater runoff occurs onto neighboring
      properties.
   2. The applicant shall retroactively apply for and obtain a
      building permit from the Building Safety Department for the
      existing retaining wall.
   3. No construction work on the driveway expansion shall
      commence until a professional land survey is completed
      confirming that the driveway is located entirely on the
      applicant's property.

SECONDED by Mr. Patry. MOTION TO APPROVE CARRIED UNANIMOUSLY 5-
0.

2. Lindsey Goodykoontz (Owner & Applicant) 13 Doncaster Drive
(Sheet B Lot 1760) requesting variance from Land Use Code
Section 190-17(E)(2) to exceed maximum driveway width, 24 feet
permitted – 34 feet proposed for a paved driveway expansion
within the front setback. R9 Zone, Ward 5.

Voting on this case:
Jay Minkarah
Josh Nehiley
JP Boucher
Steve Lionel
Joseph Patry

Lindsay Lima (formerly Lindsay Goodykoontz), 13 Doncaster Drive,
Nashua, NH. Ms. Lima stated that she completed a formal name
change to Lindsay Lima. She explained that she is requesting a
variance to widen her driveway to 34 feet within the front yard
setback. She noted that the driveway is severely constrained on
both sides by existing stone retaining walls, making it
difficult to park their vehicles, open car doors, maneuver trash
barrels, or manage winter snow accumulation. She stated that the
family currently owns two personal vehicles, two work vans, and
a pop-up camper, resulting in one work van being parked on the
grass. She emphasized that the proposed expansion will wrap
slightly around the side of the house without altering or
expanding the existing curb cut along Doncaster Drive, matching
similar driveway layouts in the immediate neighborhood.

SPEAKING IN FAVOR: No one.

SPEAKING IN OPPOSITION OR WITH QUESTIONS OR CONCERNS: No one.

END OF PUBLIC HEARING, BEGINNING OF PUBLIC MEETING: Mr. Nehiley
spoke in favor of the application, noting that a site visit
confirms the severe physical constraint created by the two rock
retaining walls flanking the driveway. He emphasized that the
proposal wraps the pavement into the yard without enlarging the
curb cut, which is precisely the land planning approach the
Board encourages applicants to take.

Mr. Boucher concurred, noting that properties directly across
the street feature similar expanded driveways and that allowing
off-street parking eliminates street congestion.


                                                                             5


MOTION by Mr. Nehiley to approve the variance application for
Lindsey Goodykoontz (noting the legal name change to Lindsay
Lima per testimony), owner and applicant, as advertised.

Mr. Nehiley stated that granting the variance would not be
contrary to the public interest because the applicant is not
expanding the street curb cut, and the driveway extension merely
expands parking space further into the lot.

Mr. Nehiley stated that the spirit of the ordinance would be
observed because allowing residents to park all personal and
work vehicles safely off the public street, manage trash
receptacles, and handle winter snow removal aligns with the
intent of residential driveway regulations, and similar
driveways exist in the neighborhood.

Mr. Nehiley stated that granting the variance would do
substantial justice because it grants the family sufficient room
to open vehicle doors and walk safely between vehicles without
being pinned by the stone retaining walls, with no detriment to
the public.

Mr. Nehiley stated that the Board has no reason to believe that
surrounding property values would be diminished.

Mr. Nehiley stated that owing to special conditions of the
property, denial of the variance would result in unnecessary
hardship because the property was developed with a house set
down from street level flanked by two permanent stone retaining
walls that restrict physical movement around parked vehicles.
There is no fair and substantial relationship between the 24-
foot width limitation and its specific application to this site,
and wrapping the driveway without increasing the curb cut is a
reasonable use.

SECONDED by Mr. Lionel. MOTION TO APPROVE CARRIED UNANIMOUSLY 5-
0.


3. Suzanne P. Raisanen, Trustee (Owner & Applicant) 9 Lamb Road
(Sheet B Lot 241) requesting variances from Land Use Code
Section 190-16 Table 16-3 to 1) allow a new lot with substandard
frontage, 120 feet required – 35 feet proposed, and 2) allow a
lot with substandard width, 150 feet required – 35 feet
proposed. R40 Zone, Ward 9.

                                                                             6


Voting on this case:
Jay Minkarah
Josh Nehiley
JP Boucher
Steve Lionel
Joseph Patry

Attorney J. Brad Westgate, Welts, White & Fontaine, P.C., 29
Factory Street, Nashua, NH, representing owner and applicant
Suzanne Raisanen (present with husband Richard Raisanen).
Attorney Westgate submitted GIS zoning extracts and a proposed
subdivision plan prepared by Fieldstone Land Consultants. He
explained that the applicant seeks dimensional variances for
frontage (35 feet proposed, 120 feet required) and lot width (35
feet proposed, 150 feet required) to subdivide an existing 2.1-
acre parcel into two single-family residential lots. Proposed
Lot 1 would contain the existing single-family home on a fully
compliant 40,000-square-foot lot with required frontage and
width. Proposed Lot 2 would be a 40,000+-square-foot "flagpole"
(or pork-chop) rear lot accessed via a 35-foot wide driveway
stem extending to Lamb Road.

Attorney Westgate clarified a clerical error in the written
application, confirming the property sits in the R40 district.
He highlighted the unique physical context of the site, noting
it sits in a small, isolated pocket of R40 zoning surrounded by
R18 density, and is fully serviced by municipal water, sewer,
natural gas, and underground utilities—unlike rural R40 parcels
relying on wells and septic. He presented GIS historical data
demonstrating that five adjacent or nearby parcels along the
south side of Lamb Road were previously approved by the ZBA as
flagpole lots between 1992 and 2012. He stated that the lot
depth of over 500 feet provides ample building area and
separation between structures.

SPEAKING IN FAVOR: No one spoke in person.

Mr. Minkarah read a letter of support into the record from
Dennis and Cindy Stephens, 5 Lamb Road, Nashua, NH. Mr. and Mrs.
Stephens stated that their lot abuts the subject parcel along a
216-foot common property line. They stated they met with the
applicants and support the creation of the two 40,000+-square-
foot lots, provided that a 30-foot wide "Do Not Disturb"
landscape buffer is established on the subject lot along their
common 216-foot boundary line and recorded in the Registry of
Deeds. Attorney Westgate confirmed that the applicants fully
accept and commit to this condition.

                                                                             7


SPEAKING IN OPPOSITION OR WITH QUESTIONS OR CONCERNS: No one
spoke in person.

Mr. Minkarah noted a letter of inquiry in the Board packet from
S.M. Guisto, 12 Lamb Road, Nashua, NH. Ms. Guisto inquired
whether the proposed structure would block sightlines on Lamb
Road, whether it would be permanent, and if a new driveway cut
was required.

With Board consensus, Mr. Minkarah re-opened the public hearing
to allow Attorney Westgate to summarize his written response to
Ms. Guisto. Attorney Westgate explained that he clarified to Ms.
Guisto that no building will be constructed within 35 feet of
Lamb Road; rather, 35 feet represents the driveway stem width to
access a new permanent single-family home set deep in the rear
of the parcel. He confirmed that because the home is set far
back, no traffic sightline issues will be created along Lamb
Road.

END OF PUBLIC HEARING, BEGINNING OF PUBLIC MEETING: Mr. Patry
and Mr. Minkarah spoke in strong support of the application. Mr.
Patry noted that the parcel is exceptionally large and deep, and
subdividing it into two full-sized 40,000-square-foot lots
represents a highly reasonable use of land.

Mr. Minkarah agreed, emphasizing that the proposal maintains the
underlying R40 lot area density, preserves building spacing,
utilizes existing municipal water and sewer infrastructure, and
aligns perfectly with the established pattern of five existing
flagpole lots in this immediate pocket of Lamb Road.

MOTION by Mr. Minkarah to approve the variance applications on
behalf of the owner as advertised, with a stipulation.

Mr. Minkarah stated that granting the variances would not be
contrary to the public interest because creating a rear single-
family lot on an exceptionally deep parcel is consistent with
the established character of Lamb Road, where multiple flagpole
lots already exist, and will cause no injury to public health,
safety, or welfare.

Mr. Minkarah stated that the spirit of the ordinance would be
observed because the underlying density of the R40 district is
strictly preserved with both lots exceeding 40,000 square feet,
building separation is maintained by placing the new home far to
the rear, and driveway spacing along the roadway remains
consistent with neighboring properties.

                                                                             8


Mr. Minkarah stated that granting the variances would do
substantial justice because the benefit to the property owner of
developing a deep, underutilized parcel outweighs any potential
public injury, with no public harm identified.

Mr. Minkarah stated that surrounding property values would not
be diminished, noting that new single-family residential
construction sets back from the road will add to the city's tax
base and enhance neighborhood property values.

Mr. Minkarah stated that owing to special conditions of the
property, denial of the variance would result in unnecessary
hardship because the parcel possesses unique physical
characteristics, specifically its exceptional 500-foot depth,
full availability of municipal water and sewer utilities, and
location within an R40 pocket characterized by adjacent
historical flagpole subdivisions. There is no fair and
substantial relationship between the literal 120-foot frontage
and 150-foot width requirements and their specific application
to this deep, serviced parcel, and constructing a single-family
home on a rear lot in a single-family zone is inherently
reasonable.

Stipulation of approval:

   1. A 30-foot wide "Do Not Disturb" landscape buffer shall be
      established on the subject lot along the common 216-foot
      property line with 5 Lamb Road as depicted on the submitted
      subdivision plan, and recorded in the Hillsborough County
      Registry of Deeds.

SECONDED by Mr. Patry. MOTION TO APPROVE CARRIED UNANIMOUSLY 5-
0.


4. Wallace Holohan and Kathleen Feltus (Applicants) 384 Main
Street requesting appeal of administrative decision regarding
on-street parking by a business with an approved site plan
located at 382 Main Street. RA Zone, Ward 7.

Voting on this case: Jay Minkarah Josh Nehiley JP Boucher Steve
Lionel Joseph Patry

Wallace Holohan, 384 Main Street, Nashua, NH. Mr. Holohan stated
that he and Kathleen Feltus submitted a joint petition appealing
an administrative determination issued by Planning Manager Sam

                                                                             9


Durfee concerning employee parking associated with Main Street
Wellness Chiropractic (382 Main Street / 380 Main Street). Mr.
Holohan argued that 8 to 10 employees of the chiropractic
practice park their personal vehicles daily from 7:00 AM to 7:00
PM, Monday through Thursday, along the narrow residential
entrance of Montgomery Avenue near Main Street. He stated that
this long-term employee parking occurs despite the business
possessing a 14-space paved parking lot in the rear. He asserted
that employee parking on both sides of Montgomery Avenue
constricts travel lanes, creates traffic congestion and
sightline hazards near the Main Street intersection, delays
emergency access, and violates Land Use Code Section 190-191(B)
(Article 28). He argued that Article 28 contains mandatory
language requiring businesses to provide and utilize off-street
parking to prevent commercial parking spillover into residential
neighborhoods.

Mr. Minkarah read the subject administrative decision letter
issued by Planning Manager Sam Durfee into the record. The
decision letter stated that employees parking in legal, public
on-street parking spaces on Montgomery Avenue does not
constitute a violation of the approved site plan for 382 Main
Street. Mr. Durfee’s determination clarified that the approved
site plan complies with zoning by providing the required number
of off-street parking spaces on-site. He determined that neither
the approved site plan nor the Land Use Code restricts business
employees or members of the public from utilizing legal, public
on-street parking spaces, and noted that public parking
regulations fall under the enforcement jurisdiction of the
Parking Department and Police Department rather than zoning
enforcement.

Dr. Noah Gosling, lead chiropractor at Main Street Wellness
Chiropractic, 380 Main Street, Nashua, NH. Dr. Gosling stated
that the practice has operated at the location since 2013 and
features 14 on-site parking spaces plus one handicap space. He
stated the practice employs 10 staff members and treats 50 to 60
patients daily, many of whom are elderly or suffer from severe
spinal mobility issues requiring physical walkers. He explained
that reserving on-site parking spaces for acute pain patients is
vital for patient health and safety, particularly in winter when
public sidewalks may be icy. Dr. Gosling expressed a strong
desire to remain a good neighbor and stated he is entirely
willing to instruct his staff to park further down Montgomery
Avenue away from the Main Street intersection.


                                                                            10


David LaBossiere, representing the Salvation Army, 384 Main
Street, Nashua, NH. Mr. LaBossiere stated that the Salvation
Army operates a food pantry across the street from 9:00 AM to
12:00 PM weekdays. He expressed a desire to maintain good
neighborly relations and stated that staff actively directs food
pantry clients to park in their dedicated lot rather than
blocking street access or driveways.

SPEAKING IN FAVOR – REBUTTAL: Mr. Holohan argued that
chiropractic staff frequently re-park on Montgomery Avenue after
lunch even when rear lot spaces are open. He warned the Board
that upholding the decision sets a precedent allowing commercial
businesses to overflow into residential side streets.

END OF PUBLIC HEARING, BEGINNING OF PUBLIC MEETING: Mr. Minkarah
opened deliberations by stating that he fully supports the
Planning Manager's administrative decision. He clarified that
the ZBA's role is strictly limited to interpreting the Land Use
Code, not enacting public parking restrictions or settling
neighborhood disputes. He stated that Article 28 requires
commercial site plans to provide a specific ratio of off-street
parking spaces, which 382 Main Street satisfies. He emphasized
that unless a site plan explicitly contains a recorded condition
prohibiting employee street parking, any member of the public—
including business employees—has a legal right to park in
unrestricted public street spaces.

Mr. Lionel, Mr. Boucher, and Mr. Patry unanimously concurred
with Mr. Minkarah. Mr. Lionel stated that off-street parking
space requirements were met on the site plan, and employee use
of public street parking is not a zoning or site plan violation.

Mr. Boucher suggested that if residents face legitimate traffic
safety concerns at the corner, they can approach their Ward
Alderman to request that the Board of Aldermen enact municipal
parking ordinances, such as time-restricted parking zones.

Mr. Patry added that Section 190-191 requires off-street parking
to be provided, but contains zero statutory language regulating
or restricting who must occupy those physical spaces.

MOTION by Mr. Lionel to deny the appeal of an administrative
decision filed by Wallace Holohan and Kathleen Feltus regarding
on-street parking at 382 Main Street, and to affirm the Planning
Manager's administrative determination as correct.


                                                                            11


Mr. Lionel stated that the Board finds the Planning Manager's
administrative decision dated July 2026 to be correct under the
Land Use Code, as the approved site plan provides the required
off-street parking spaces on-site, and the code contains no
provision prohibiting employees from parking in legal public on-
street parking spaces.

SECONDED by Mr. Nehiley. MOTION TO DENY APPEAL CARRIED
UNANIMOUSLY 5-0.
