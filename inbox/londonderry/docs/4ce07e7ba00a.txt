A. CALL TO ORDER
Chair Dunn called the meeting to order at 7:00 p.m. and led the Pledge of Allegiance.
Councilor Combes requested a moment of silence in memory of Marilyn Webster Ham, who
recently passed away at the age of 97. Mrs. Ham was a long-time Londonderry resident who
founded the Londonderry Heritage/Historic District, was appointed Town Historian in 2005, and
served with her husband Chet as Grand Marshals of the 2007 Old Home Day parade.
Chair Dunn shared a letter of appreciation from Battalion Chief Bruce Howell highlighting
Londonderry Fire Rescue members. On the evening of May 19th, department members
voluntarily repaired and rebuilt a damaged roadside memorial cross and stone at Old Nashua
Road and Parmenter Road dedicated to a local teenager lost in a past crash, demonstrating
exceptional care and respect for the community.

D. PUBLIC HEARING
1. Receive public input, discuss, and approve adding alternates to the Cemetery
Trustees.
Administrative Services Director Hildonen presented Ordinance 2026-11 and Ordinance 2026-
12 pursuant to RSA 289:6 and 7, allowing the Council to appoint up to two one-year alternate
members to the three-member Cemetery Trustees board to assist with quorums, while updating
references and municipal code charge descriptions.
   Councilor Combes moved to open the public hearing. Seconded by Councilor Bouchard. Motion
   carried 5-0-0. Chair votes in the affirmative.
Chair Dunn asked for public comment; there was none.
   Councilor Combes moved to close the public hearing. Seconded by Vice Chair Faber. Motion carried
   5-0-0. Chair votes in the affirmative.
   Vice Chair Faber moved that the Londonderry Town Council hereby approves Ordinance 2026-11
   adding the Cemetery Trustees to the municipal code and correcting outdated lists, references, and
   other errors. Seconded by Councilor Combes. Motion carried 5-0-0. Chair votes in the affirmative.
   Vice Chair Faber moved that the Londonderry Town Council hereby approves Ordinance 2026-12
   describing the charge of the Cemetery Trustees. Seconded by Councilor Bouchard. Motion carried
   5-0-0. Chair votes in the affirmative.

2. Receive public input, discuss and approve the application for a Commercial /
Industrial Tax Exemption for PurposeEnergy.
Town Manager Mulholland and representatives Ari Pollock, Esq. and Mark Riley of
PurposeEnergy presented the application for a commercial/industrial tax exemption under RSA
72. The presentation detailed job creation (25-30 total positions with partner Recycle Works),
regulatory permits received, odor mitigation controls, property valuation projections ($2.5M in
taxes paid over 10 years), and a proposed residential food waste drop-off container.
    Councilor Combes moved to open the public hearing. Seconded by Vice Chair Faber. Motion carried
    5-0-0. Chair votes in the affirmative.
Public Comment:
•   Martha Smith (38 Shasta Drive): Commended her visit to the Vermont facility, questioned job
    numbers and Stonyfield waste participation.
•   Ray Breslin (3 Gary Drive): Questioned trucking volume, out-of-state waste origin, investor
    financing, and requested delaying tax relief decisions.
    Councilor Combes moved to close the public hearing. Seconded by Councilor Bouchard. Motion
    carried 5-0-0. Chair votes in the affirmative.
The Council debated exemption terms. Councilors Bouchard and Paul opposed granting tax
exemptions to commercial entities. Councilors Dunn, Faber, and Combes agreed on a modified 6-
year phase-out schedule.
    Vice Chair Faber moved that the Londonderry Town Council hereby approves a modified 6-year
    Commercial/Industrial Tax Exemption schedule beginning April 2028 (50% Year 1, 45% Year 2,
    36% Year 3, 25% Year 4, 15% Year 5, 8% Year 6). Seconded by Councilor Combes. Motion carried
    3-2-0, with Councilors Paul and Bouchard voting in opposition. Chair votes in the affirmative.
    Vice Chair Faber moved that the Londonderry Town Council hereby approves Resolution 2026-16
    regarding the Commercial/Industrial Tax Exemption for PurposeEnergy as stated, beginning April
    2028 for a 6-year period, contingent upon final documentation and signing. Seconded by Councilor
    Combes. Motion carried 3-2-0, with Councilors Paul and Bouchard voting in opposition. Chair votes
    in the affirmative.
The Council authorized Town Manager Mulholland to finalize the covenant agreement for the
residential food waste drop-off container on PurposeEnergy property with paved access, to be
presented to Council for final review.
Councilor Paul requested scheduling a future discussion to evaluate repealing or sunsetting the
Commercial/Industrial Tax Exemption ordinance.

revisions to the Londonderry Zoning Ordinance (LZO) to incorporate the 2024 ICC
International Building Codes as amended by the State of NH and recent legislative
changes.
Administrative Services Director Hildonen explained proposed revisions to sections 9.1 and 9.2 of
the zoning ordinance adopting the 2024 ICC building codes as mandated by state legislation,
removing local technical amendments while maintaining administrative permitting processes and
incorporate recent legislation affecting Building and Code Enforcement activities.
   Councilor Bouchard moved that the Londonderry Town Council hereby schedules a public hearing
   for September 21, 2026 at 7:00 p.m. in the Moose Hill Chambers of the Londonderry Town Hall
   regarding proposed revisions to section 9.1 and 9.2 of the Londonderry zoning ordinance. Seconded
   by Vice Chair Faber. Motion carried 5-0-0. Chair votes in the affirmative.

   17 to amend the Pettengill Road Tax Increment Finance District. Seconded by Councilor Combes.
   Motion carried 5-0-0. Chair votes in the affirmative.
