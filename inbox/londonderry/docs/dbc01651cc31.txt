3. Public Comment
No members of the public were present. Public comment was skipped at the opening
and closing opportunities on the agenda.


4. New Committee Business


   4.1 Planned Unit Developments – Fiscal Impact
• Town Manager Shaun Mulholland reviewed a developer-prepared fiscal impact
analysis for the proposed Brookview Hollow planned unit development (PUD). The
analysis projected approximately $7.8 million in annual revenue, $2.3 million in school
costs, and $383,000 in municipal service costs, resulting in an estimated $5.5 million
annual net positive operating impact.
• The report compared approximately 1,028 PUD units with a lower-density single-family
alternative of roughly 200 homes, estimated to generate approximately $946,000 in net
municipal value. The Town Manager explained that density can distribute road and utility
costs across a larger tax base.
• The Town Manager cautioned that the fiscal report measures operating costs and does
not fully account for road infrastructure improvements or fire and emergency response
needs. The development was described as approximately nine to ten minutes from the
nearest fire station, compared with a four-minute response benchmark cited in the
discussion.
• Traffic impacts were discussed, including already-congested intersections, the
additional burden of residential and commercial development, and the need for a fair-
share developer contribution. The Town Manager described ongoing work on an
approach to developer exactions and mitigation that can be applied consistently.
• The Committee discussed the proposed Pillsbury Road/Gilchrist Road traffic mitigation
associated with an existing development agreement. Options included a turn lane,
intersection improvements and a monetary contribution toward larger future
improvements; no Committee action was taken.
• School enrollment estimates, possible redistricting, private-road maintenance, and
long-term municipal liability if roads are accepted by the Town were discussed. The
Town Manager offered to circulate the approximately 25-page fiscal impact report.


4.2 Budget and Finance Workshop
• Members who attended a New Hampshire Municipal Association budget and finance
workshop summarized topics including the distinction between advisory and official
budget committees, municipal authority, appropriations, unanticipated revenue, revolving
and special revenue funds, and default budgets.
• The Committee discussed how reducing or eliminating an appropriation may affect later
transfers and spending authority. Members were advised that the statewide default-
budget guidance may differ from provisions in the Londonderry Town Charter.
• Workshop materials and information about a September 29 default-budget webinar
were to be shared with Committee members. Members also discussed state and federal
revenue constraints, the effect of flat aid distributions amid rising costs, and local
reliance on property taxes.
4.3 Facility Tours
  • The remaining facility visits were the Library and Fire Department. The Committee
  agreed to prioritize a tour of Fire Station 2 and the Central Fire Station at its October
  15 meeting; the third fire station would not be included.
  • The Chair will coordinate a start time, tentatively 6:30 PM, with Fire Department
  leadership. Library needs discussed included future roof, HVAC and boiler work and
  potential later-phase improvements.


  4.4 FY2028 Budget Presentation Schedule and Process
  • The Committee reviewed a proposed budget-season calendar. The joint Town
  Council/Budget Committee kickoff was planned for Saturday, November 7, with the
  Town Manager, Police, Fire, and DPW presenting. Additional department
  presentations were planned for November 10 and November 17.
  • Members requested brief, finance-focused presentations highlighting material
  changes from FY2027 to FY2028, added or removed accounts, key line-item
  increases and decreases, total dollar and percentage changes, and proposed
  warrant articles. The Committee also sought comparisons among department, Town
  Manager, Town Council, and default budget amounts where available.
  • Discussion emphasized property-tax impact rather than gross expenditures alone,
  because some expenditures are offset by specific revenue sources. The Town
  Manager noted that Town Council guidance on spending and tax impacts would
  assist department preparation and Committee review.
  • The Committee discussed reviewing warrant articles earlier in the process. Regular
  budget-season meetings were expected to begin at 6:30 PM, with approximately the
  final 30 minutes reserved for warrant article discussions. Members were encouraged
  to watch the October 5 Town Council presentation; warrant article review was
  targeted for November 10.


5. Liaison Reports


  5.1 School Board
  • The liaison reported on discussion of House Bill 1300 and possible implications of a
  tax cap for school operations and administration. Potential pressure on non-
  mandated programming, including advanced courses, athletics, extracurricular
  activities, technology, transportation and electives, was discussed.
5.2 Town Council / Town Hall
• Town Hall and Police Department roof projects, including solar work, were
discussed. The School District administrative office project was reported to be ahead
of schedule, with completion potentially earlier than originally anticipated.
• The Committee revisited legal expenses. The Town Manager indicated
approximately $475,000 in legal expenditures or commitments were being tracked,
compared with the approved $200,000 legal budget, with further invoices
outstanding. A continuation of the current pace could approach $900,000. The Town
uses multiple firms for specialized matters; the Town Manager expressed a
preference for contracted services over an in-house attorney and discussed
establishing a more realistic future legal appropriation.
• Personnel changes in the Planning Department were mentioned, including an
incoming director and a retirement. Names and effective dates should be checked
against Town records before formal approval.


5.3 Department of Public Works
• DPW budget pressures include salt and sand, fuel, contracted plowing, employee
uniforms, engineering software and solid-waste disposal. Vehicle maintenance was
reported as comparatively stable following fleet replacements.
• An owner’s project manager for the DPW facility was discussed, with approximately
$150,000 described as being covered through internal transfers rather than an
additional appropriation. The Committee also discussed longer-term approaches to
budgeting unpredictable winter maintenance.
• The cost of fire hydrant rentals and an approximately 42% rate increase were
raised, with Town management seeking further discussion with the water provider.
Trash and hazardous waste expenditures were described as potentially rising from
approximately $2.2 million to $2.8 million.
• DPW described an equipment approach to retention-pond maintenance that could
substantially reduce labor costs compared with current practices.
• The Town Manager discussed the transfer station’s estimated approximately
$250,000 annual property-tax subsidy, limited operating hours, and the possibility of
joint or privately operated services. Options under review include user fees, revised
rates and expanded service; no decision was reported.


5.4 Capital Improvement Program
• Department project presentations had concluded, and the CIP Committee was
preparing to prioritize projects. Items discussed included public safety and DPW
equipment, roads and bridges, water projects, recreation facilities, security/badging
and enterprise resource planning systems.
  5.5 Fire Department
  • Fire Station 2 work was reported to be progressing. Solar installation was
  discussed as a potential source of long-term energy savings. The Fire Department
  identified vehicle-maintenance budgeting as a persistent operating issue requiring
  alignment with actual costs.
  • Potential staffing requests included eight firefighter/EMT positions, a fire inspector
  and a training officer; service revenue associated with the Litchfield agreement may
  affect the final request.


  5.6 Police Department
  • No major operating line-item changes were reported. A significant facility project
  remains within the CIP process. The liaison was continuing to follow up on concerns
  regarding women’s locker-room capacity.


  5.7 Planning Board
  • PUD fiscal impact, traffic mitigation, and the Town’s approach to developer
  contributions were addressed during the Town Manager’s presentation under New
  Committee Business.


  5.8 Leach Library
  • The Library had reopened following interior improvements. Members discussed
  carpeting selected for durability and water resistance, outstanding rooftop
  mechanical needs, and the longer-term facility maintenance program.


6. Old Committee Business
