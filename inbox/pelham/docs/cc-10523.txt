 99    1. PSD  Budget/District Overview FY27/28
100           Chair Michael Carter opened the discussion by noting that the Board had received the Pelham
101           School District budget files the previous week and thanking the district for providing additional
102           tools. He invited Mr. Greg Smith, chair of the Pelham School Board, to offer opening remarks.
103           Mr. Smith introduced himself and acknowledged that some of his prepared remarks had already
104           been covered in the chair's announcements. He noted that there were some new faces present,
105           including returning veterans and at least one new member. He identified Dr. Chip McGee as the
106           school superintendent, Dr. Sarah Marandos as the assistant superintendent, and Brian Krueger
107           as the new director of finance, who had joined the district from Southern New Hampshire
108           University on June 1, replacing retiring Finance Director Deb Mahoney. Mr. Smith explained that
109           the Budget Committee had new  responsibilities this year, as it was now accountable for both the
110           operating budget and the default budget. He noted that the district had made available several
111           tools, including a default budget calculator and a budget in Excel format, and stated that he had
112           an announcement  to share once the presentation concluded.
113           Dr. McGee  indicated he was ready to proceed and noted he had a brief slideshow to present
114           before diving into the elementary school, which was the focus of the session. He explained that
115           the slideshow would provide an overview for the community and the Budget Committee. Dr.
116           McGee  stated that the district's focus was on students and staff. He described Pelham High
117           School as serving grades nine through twelve with Division III athletics, noting that the class of
118           2025 had achieved a 79 percent college-bound rate. Pelham Memorial School served grades six
119           through eight and had experienced some traffic due to a Halloween event. The school offered a
120           full range of athletic and activity programs, including family and consumer science, STEAM,
121           band, and Spanish. The elementary school included a preschool program serving pre-kindergarten
122                       through fifth grade, with experienced staff and supports in place for academics and
123           student behavior.
124           Dr. McGee  outlined the school board's goals. For student connection, the board had established
125           a goal of ensuring every student had a connection not just to a trusted adult in the building but
126           also to friend groups and engagement in activities within or outside school. For mathematics, the
127           board aimed to increase the number of proficient students by twelve points; the district had
128           achieved eight of those twelve points and looked forward to moving the remaining four. For
129           reading, the goal was also to increase proficiency; the district had improved thirteen percentage
130           points the previous year, exceeding the ten-point goal, and the board had asked the district to
131           revise the goal to place it in the top 25 percent of peer districts once state data on peer districts
132           was finalized. For staff retention, the goal was to make the district the best place to work,
133           measured  by a 90 percent retention rate. The district had met that goal for teaching staff as

       PELHAM BUDGET COMMITTEE  — REGULAR MEETING —                                            Page 3 of 8
       THURSDAY,OCTOBER  15, 2026134           originally described, and the board had expanded it to include all staff, whether teachers,
135           instructional assistants, administrators, or office support.
136           Mr. Smith interjected to highlight a key soundbite: the intent was to be in the top 25 percent in
137           math and reading compared  to peer districts while remaining in the bottom 25 percent for cost
138           per student.
139           Dr. McGee  discussed enrollment trends. He explained that the district reported enrollment
140           differently for preschool than in prior years. Looking at district enrollment for kindergarten through
141           grade twelve only, the district had 1,489 students the previous year, 1,488 in the current October
142           1 count, and was projecting 1,485 for the next year. The headline was that enrollment appeared
143           to be stabilizing, with individual grades ranging between 100 and 140 students, though the
144           typical range was now between 100 and 120 per grade. Dr. McGee presented data on cost per
145           pupil, noting that over the last fifteen years, Pelham had remained below the state average. The
146           district was currently $2,487 per student less than the state average in fiscal year 2025, the most
147           recent year presented.
148           Dr. McGee  addressed the question of what the district was doing to save money and highlighted
149           three significant initiatives. First, the district had decided to outsource custodial services,
150           effective November 6 of the current year. This decision would result in avoiding $47,000 in costs
151           in the current fiscal year. The limiting factor in savings was the board's decision to offer current
152           custodians positions in the new organization at the same wage rate. Of the 16 positions
153           proposed for shifting, all were moved to contracted service, and instead of recruiting, budgeting,
154           and providing benefits for those positions, the district now had a contract with a 4 percent annual
155           increase in overall cost. Dr. McGee acknowledged this had been a difficult process with regard to
156           the people affected but important from a fiscal perspective. Second, the district was
157           implementing a capital investment plan, an idea that had come directly from the Budget
158           Committee. The goal was to smooth capital spending over time. The Capital Improvement Plan
159           Committee  had recommended,  though not mandatorily, that the district look at capital reserves
160           for long-term projects, including the major air conditioning project coming for the elementary
161           school's first four classrooms, gym, and a few other spaces. That project would not appear in the
162           operating budget but would be presented as a separate warrant article. Third, the district had
163           already established a special education reserve. The previous year, the district had removed
164           approximately $400,000 from the operating budget and had asked voters to approve placing
165           some  unassigned fund balance into the capital reserve; voters had approved $168,000, which
166           topped off the reserve fund to approximately $400,000. This was an investment fund that varied
167           in value. The board had continued this approach, and under this model, if an unanticipated
168           catastrophically large special education expense arose, the district would use the capital reserve
169           rather than budgeting for it annually and returning it if unused.
170           Mr. Smith added a comment, noting that the slide titled "What are we doing to save money?" was
171           not comprehensive and that the district was doing other things that would be discussed further.
172           Dr. McGee  discussed staffing, explaining that the details were in the budget document and that
173           he would now focus on the budget being presented. He identified targeted additions in green and
174           proposed reductions and reallocations in yellow. The district was proposing a new first-grade
175           classroom teacher, as 126 kindergarteners had enrolled and the district would prefer seven
176           classrooms instead of the six currently budgeted. The district wanted to support its mathematics
177           improvement  goal by adding an hourly tutor position, both at the elementary school and at
178           Memorial School, both part-time to avoid triggering health insurance and New Hampshire
179           retirement benefits. For special education, the district was budgeting a registered behavioral
180           technician for behavioral supports and proposing a part-time special education secretary at the
181           elementary school and one splitting time between the middle and high school. The district
