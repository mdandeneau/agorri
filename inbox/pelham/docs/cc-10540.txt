51    2. Public Re-Hearing  – Board of Health- Request for Waiver of Well Ordinance —  Board of Health
52           The Board of Health reconvened to reconsider Andrew McNiff's request for a waiver of the well
53           ordinance to allow a shared well between two nonconforming lots. Mr. McNiff explained that he
54           had obtained a yield test showing the existing well produces ten gallons per minute, exceeding
55           the town's threshold of eight gallons per minute. He stated that while he could legally place a
56           new well on his property using a well reduction form from the state, doing so would position it
57           only twenty feet from his neighbor's septic system, whereas the shared well would be sixty feet
58           away. The board engaged  in lengthy discussion about the nature of hardship, the public health
59           implications of shared versus separate wells, and the procedural questions surrounding approval
60           of a nonconforming lot. Selectmen Danielle Masse Quinn raised several concerns, including that
61           the board lacked authority to create a use for a property that is not currently an approved
62           buildable lot, and that approving a waiver would not adequately protect future homeowners or
63           comply with the New Hampshire Safe Drinking Water Act. She also noted that once the waiver
64           was granted and the house built, the well ordinance would no longer apply to subsequent
65           owners, leaving them unprotected. Health and Environmental Officer Dena Hoffman
66           acknowledged  the difficulty of the situation as a "catch 22," explaining that while shallow septic
67           systems posed theoretical contamination risks, well water from hundreds of feet below typically
68           benefited from soil filtration. Chair Croteau expressed support for one well over two, but
69           acknowledged  the board's concerns. The board ultimately proceeded to a recorded vote on
70           whether to deny the waiver request.

 86    3. Right to Know  Policy Revision and Approval
 87           Town  Administrator Joseph Roark presented a revised Right to Know policy that had been
 88           developed in collaboration with the town's legal counsel and Selectmen Brett Gagnon, who
 89           brought expertise and concerns to multiple conference calls with the attorney. The most
 90           significant change from the existing policy involves how requests are submitted and fulfilled.
 91           Previously, requesters were required to submit requests in person at town offices, a requirement
 92           intended to limit requests from out-of-state and international data miners. The new policy allows
 93           requests to be made remotely via email or telephone, but requires that all records be collected in
 94           person at the Selectmen's office, paid for in advance, and provided either as hard copies or on
 95           factory-sealed USB drives rather than via email or cloud-based services. Roark emphasized that
 96           the town has experienced a substantial increase in Right to Know requests, receiving nine in the
 97           past five days alone, two of which required more than thirty days to complete. Several additional
 98           provisions were incorporated into the policy based on Selectmen Gagnon's input. These include
 99           allowing verbal requests when requesters decline to put requests in writing, enabling the town to
100           suggest reasonable modifications to broad requests to reduce costs and effort, changing the
101           daily receipt cutoff from four o'clock to five o'clock, requiring prompt notification of changes to
102           estimated production dates, establishing itemized estimates for electronic communications
103           exceeding two hundred fifty records, and clarifying that town officials and employees must
104           search personal devices and accounts used for town business. Selectmen Gagnon noted the
105           policy allows requesters to photograph records rather than purchase copies, and Roark
106           explained the prohibition on mailing records helps ensure advance payment and serves as a
107           guardrail against out-of-state data mining operations. Selectmen Danielle Masse Quinn sought
108           assurance that the revised policy remained legally defensible, and Roark confirmed that all
109           language was written by town legal counsel and represented a customized version of the
110           attorney's boilerplate policy addressing specific town concerns. Vice Chair Charlene Takesian
111           confirmed the policy could be revised in the future if unanticipated issues arose.

112       Motion  by Selectmen  Brett Gagnon  to Approve the latest Right to Know policy for the Town of
113       Pelham  as recommended    by town legal. Seconded  by Vice Chair Charlene Takesian. Carried
114       (5–0–0).

145    Discussion
146    Town Administrator Joseph Roark reported that the state legislature recently mandated that the budget
147    committee draft a default budget for the town, a complicated process with limited legislative guidance.
148    Roark explained that he, Tammy Penny, Mike Carter, and Ken Woonton have begun developing a plan
149    to implement this requirement. The budget committee chairman Mike Carter researched training options
150    and found that the New Hampshire Municipal Association (NHMA) is willing to conduct a training session
151    on default budgets, including what should and should not be included in them. Roark recommended that
152    the Board of Selectmen approve a $700 transfer from the emergency management budget to the budget
153    committee's budget line to cover the training cost, noting that the budget committee currently has only $1
154    budgeted. He confirmed that Fire Chief Dan Newman approved the transfer as the Emergencey
155    Management  Coordinator. Selectmen Brett Gagnon and Danielle Masse Quinn asked clarifying
156    questions about the training format and the budget committee members' receptiveness to the training.
157    Roark clarified that the instruction would be delivered via Teams or Zoom with a live instructor, and that
158    the session would be recorded for members unable to attend live. Selectmen Masse Quinn expressed
159    hope that committee members would actively embrace the training given the $700 cost. Gagnon noted
160    that while the budget committee members are passionate and well-intentioned, they would likely still rely
161    on Tammy  Penny's guidance given the complexity and time constraints involved. Vice Chair Charlene
162    Takesian asked whether the default budget mandate could be fulfilled in the town office; Roark confirmed
163    the mandate is binding but indicated that Penny would continue to assist by preparing preliminary
164    frameworks for the committee to review and modify.

165    Motion by Selectmen  Danielle Masse Quinn  to Approve  a $700 line item transfer from the
166    Emergency  Management    budget to the Budget Committee  budget  line item for the purpose of
167    NHMA  default budget training. Seconded  by Selectmen  Brett Gagnon. Carried (5–0–0).
