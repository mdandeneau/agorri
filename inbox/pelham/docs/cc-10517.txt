            DISCUSSION

            Mr. Wing began the Board’s review of the five variance criteria and combined the public-interest and spirit-of-
            the-ordinance criteria. He explained that the proposal should not adversely affect public health, safety, or
            welfare and should remain consistent with the character of the neighborhood. Ms. Dube found that
            constructing a replacement shed was a customary residential use and noted that the Conservation
            Commission supported the proposed location. Mr. Hamilton agreed that the Commission’s review and
            favorable recommendation supported a finding that the proposal was consistent with the public interest and
            the spirit of the ordinance. Mr. Wing further noted that the proposed shed represented only a modest
            increase over the existing shed and that the proposed deck had been designed to remain outside the
            Wetlands Conservation District.

            In considering substantial justice, Mr. Wing stated that denying the variance would provide no identifiable
            public benefit while preventing the applicants from replacing a shed that had reached the end of its useful
            life. Ms. Dube also observed that retaining the existing shed in its current location would continue to restrict
            the narrow passage between the house and the fence. Mr. Wing and Mr. Welch agreed with Ms. Dube. The
            Board therefore concluded that the applicants’ loss from denial would not be outweighed by any
            corresponding gain to the public.

            Regarding surrounding property values, Mr. Wing found no reason to conclude that replacing the existing
            shed with a newer shed would diminish neighboring property values. Mr. Welch agreed with this finding.
            For the hardship criteria, Ms. Dube found that nearly half of the property was located within the Wetlands
            Conservation District and that the remaining area was already occupied or constrained by the house,
            proposed deck, driveway, fence, and access to the lake. She determined that alternative shed locations
            would either obstruct lake access or occupy the limited passage between the house and fence. The Board
            concluded that the lot’s small size, limited available area, and existing improvements created special
            conditions that made strict compliance with the ordinance impractical.

            frontage, lot size, setbacks, and the Wetland Conservation District requirements. The properties are located
            near the end of Grace Road, along a portion described as a private way.

            Attorney Groff stated that, at the prior hearing, the Board requested a Site Walk and a presentation to the
            Conservation Commission. Board members had since viewed the existing conditions during the Site Walk,

            Attorney Groff explained that the applicant was seeking conditional variance approval before undertaking the
            expense of complete engineering plans. The Conservation Commission, however, believed that additional
            plan detail was necessary before it could vote or provide recommendations. Mr. Groff stated that the

            Attorney Groff then summarized the Commission’s concerns, and the revisions incorporated into the updated

            proposal extended slightly closer to the pond, while the updated footprint had been shifted toward the
            roadway as far as practicable. The plan also identified possible locations for additional shoreline plantings.

            Attorney Groff proposed reducing impervious surfaces by removing the existing paved driveway, boat-ramp
            area, concrete features, and certain pavers. He explained that those areas would be replaced, where
            feasible, with grass or a combination of grass and permeable pavers. He noted that a Conservation
            Commission member preferred grass to pavers, particularly on the downslope area, but that the final
            treatment would be refined through further review.

            Attorney Groff stated that the revised concept included a stormwater infiltration chamber beneath the
            driveway. He explained that the roof of the proposed dwelling would slope toward the roadway rather than
            the pond, and roof runoff would be directed to the infiltration system. He added that a second, smaller
            drainage feature could be added to capture runoff from the opposite side of the property.

            Attorney Groff further explained that the properties currently contain two septic systems and two wells. He
            stated that the proposal would retire both existing septic systems and install one new septic system for the
            combined property. He explained that one existing well would remain in service, and the other would be
            abandoned.

            Attorney Groff reiterated that the applicant did not wish to incur the full cost of engineering until there was
            reasonable assurance that the project could proceed. He requested conditional approval for the setback,

            Commission for its review and final recommendation.

            Ms. Dube discussed the existing artificial turf and if it was an impervious or previous surface. Mr. Chisholm
            explained that the turf was installed over four inches of compacted three-eighths-inch crushed stone and a

            Mr. Stanvick asked for the total of the existing and proposed impervious surfaces. Mr. Chisholm calculated
            that the existing 2,792 square feet of patios and driveways, combined with 2,700 square feet of roof area,

            Mr. Stanvick noted that Shoreland Protection Requirements apply when impervious coverage exceeds 30
            percent and may require plantings extending toward the pond. Mr. Chisholm confirmed that all required
            permits would be sought from the New Hampshire Department of Environmental Services.

            Mr. Stanvick expressed concern that the proposal was a major development supported primarily by a
            conceptual sketch rather than a complete engineered plan. He compared the proposal with a prior Wetland
            Conservation District matter involving a storage shed for which full engineering plans had been provided. He
            stated that many of the applicant’s drainage and site-improvement representations were prospective, and
            that the Board did not yet have a definitive plan of record.

            Mr. Chisholm responded that he had retained a septic designer and had already invested time and money in
            the project. He explained that the revised house location reflected the Conservation Commission’s request to
            move the structure farther from the pond. He stated that the change provided approximately 22 feet of
            clearance near the proposed leach field, which he represented as the minimum necessary clearance. He

            With the approved conditions in mind, the Board reviewed the five variance criteria. Regarding public interest

            Regarding substantial justice, Mr. Wing stated that denial would prevent the applicant from replacing and
            improving the two existing homes, and could identify no corresponding public benefit from denial. The Board
            concluded that the private loss to the applicant would not be outweighed by any gain to the public.

            DISCUSSION
            During deliberation, Mr. Wing stated that the variance request was limited to the frontage requirement. He
            agreed with Attorney Allard that a duplex is a permitted use in the residential district and stated that the
            permitted use itself should not serve as a basis for denying the requested frontage relief. The Board
            therefore focused its review on the request to allow the southerly lot to have 100 feet of frontage where 200
            feet is required.
            Mr. Wing stated that, if the application were approved, the conditions imposed with the June 8, 2026 variance
            approval should be added to this new approval. Ms. Dube stated that there were suggested conditions
            included with the current application and read them as follows.

                     • This approval shall apply to the proposed duplex development on the southerly lot substantially
                       as depicted on the revised subdivision plan submitted with this application.
                     • The conditions imposed under the June 8, 2026, variance approval, Case ZO2026-00009, shall
                       remain in effect, including the removal of the existing park model trailer and associated
                       improvements in accordance with the Planning Board approved phasing plan.
                     • The applicant shall obtain Planning Board Subdivision Approval prior to recording any
                       subdivision plan or creating any new lot.

            The Board reviewed the five variance criteria. Regarding the public interest and the spirit of the ordinance,
            the Board found that the proposal would remain consistent with the residential character of the area and

                                                  Page 18 of 20

            would not adversely affect public health, safety, or welfare. Ms. Dube noted that other multifamily dwellings
            existed nearby, including properties to the north and near the transfer station, and therefore concluded that
            the proposed duplex would not introduce an unfamiliar use into the area. Mr. Wing discussed roadway safety
            and the purpose of the minimum frontage requirement. He stated that the 200-foot frontage standard helps
            provide adequate sight distance for vehicles entering and leaving a property. He observed that the relevant
            section of Windham Road was relatively flat and provided good sight lines in both directions. Ms. Dube also
            noted that three driveways currently served the parcel and that the proposal would reduce and reorganize
            the access points, including one driveway serving the southerly lot’s 100 feet of frontage. The Board
            therefore found that the requested frontage relief would not create an unreasonable safety concern.

            Regarding substantial justice, the Board found no identifiable public benefit from denying the application. Mr.
            Wing noted that the property already contained two residential uses, with separate mailing addresses and
            septic systems, although the existing dwellings currently shared one well. He concluded that denying the
            opportunity to replace the park model trailer with a permitted duplex would impose a loss on the applicant
            without a corresponding public gain and would therefore be unjust. Mr. Welch agreed with Mr. Wing.

            Regarding surrounding property values, Mr. Wing acknowledged the concerns expressed by abutters but
            noted that a duplex is a permitted use in the district. Mr. Welch stated that replacing the existing park model
            trailer with a new two-family dwelling located farther from the road and partially screened by vegetation could
            improve the property and potentially benefit surrounding property values. Ms. Dube agreed with Mr.
            Welch. Mr. Wing also noted that the proposal would remain subject to Planning Board review, which could
            address site-design issues. The Board found that surrounding property values would not be diminished.

            Regarding hardship, Mr. Wing noted that the southerly lot met the applicable requirements for lot area and
            setbacks and required relief only from the frontage standard. He stated that obtaining additional frontage
            would require affecting neighboring or northerly property and that no practical opportunity existed to gain
            frontage to the south. Mr. Welch identified the property’s long-standing treatment as two separate residential
            areas, including separate mailing addresses and multiple driveways, as a distinguishing condition. Members
            concluded that the proposal would not materially change or intensify the established residential use of the
            property and that the existing lot configuration supported the hardship criteria.
