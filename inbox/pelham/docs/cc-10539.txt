32    rose, and burning bush. She detailed the threat posed by Tree of Heaven, an aggressive invasive
33    species native to China that can grow to 60 feet and produces thousands of seeds, noting its association
34    with the Spotted Lanternfly, an emerging insect pest recently confirmed in Salem, New Hampshire.

37    articles with the forestry committee as managers, rather than being voted as a town forest.

39    lanternflies on Tree of Heaven specimens near the parking lot and on wild grapevines near the beach.

102    2. Al Steward: Town  Center Trail Project Update — Al Steward
103           Al Steward presented a proposal for the Pelham Senior Health Pathway, a fully funded trail
104           project designed to provide accessible recreational opportunities for seniors and residents of all
105           ages and fitness levels in the town center. The pathway would establish a walkway system
106           beginning at the Hobbs parking lot, crossing in front of Pelham Funeral Home, connecting
107           through Lions Park behind the police station and library, and extending through town woodlands
108           with various loop options ranging to approximately a mile and a half in length. Steward explained
109           that the project has received widespread community support and has been presented to multiple
110           town boards and committees, including the Conservation Commission, Forestry Committee,
111           Trails Subcommittee, and Pelham Council on Aging. The Trails Subcommittee has agreed to
112           assume  primary responsibility for construction. Most of the trail would utilize existing paths and
113           natural clearings, minimizing required construction work through the wooded areas. The Board
114           discussed several considerations during the presentation. Selectmen Jaie Bergeron inquired
115           about the trail's proximity to private property lines, and Steward confirmed that the woodland
116           section would be fifty to sixty-seven feet wide, keeping the trail well away from abutters' property.
117           Town  Administrator Joseph Roark raised concerns about securing a formal easement from
118           James  O'Donnell, the owner of Pelham Funeral Home, and requested clear signage to direct
119           users away from the police station area. Selectmen Brett Gagnon asked whether existing
120           sidewalk connections would require maintenance or improvement, particularly along Sawmill
121           Road  and Cemetery Road. Steward  indicated the project would work with existing conditions but
122           welcomed  any assistance from the highway department. Chair Jason Croteau and others praised
123           the initiative as a valuable community resource consistent with the town's mission.

190    7. Tammy   Penny,  Finance Director/ Asst Town Administrator- Fund  Balance Update  and
191       Potential Uses —  Tammy   Penny, Finance
192           Assistant Town Administrator/Finance Director Tammy Penny reported on the town's fund
193           balance position and explained that she was unable to provide a final tax rate estimate because
194           the Department of Revenue Administration review and school coordination had not yet been
195           completed. Penny clarified that unassigned fund balance is an accounting measure rather than
196           actual cash in a bank account, serving as available resources to help the town pay bills between
197           the two annual tax collection periods. The town ended 2025 with $10,094,008.51 in unassigned
198           fund balance, representing 15.69 percent of total obligations. She noted that the New Hampshire
199           Government  Finance Officers Association recommends maintaining between 8 and 17 percent,
200           and that Pelham operates effectively at 15 percent. The fund balance decreased from 2024 to
201           2025 due to planned uses including $500,000 for fire apparatus capital reserve, approximately
202           $437,000 for roundabout project funding, and $500,000 for tax rate setting. Penny projected
203           2026 unassigned fund balance would remain around 15.43 percent if the previously voted
204           $400,000 fire capital reserve deposit and up to $500,000 in fund balance for tax rate offset were
205           utilized. Selectmen Brett Gagnon asked for a visual representation showing the relationship
206           between cash flow and expenses to better illustrate how close the town comes to being unable to
207           pay bills. Town Administrator Joseph Roark explained that previous administrations had used
208           substantial fund balance amounts—sometimes  up to $2 million—to maintain lower tax rates
209           while dipping into the protective reserve level. Chair Jason Croteau and Roark emphasized that
210           maintaining adequate fund balance is critical to avoid becoming dependent on tax anticipation
211           notes or borrowing from banks, which creates financial instability and poorly serves taxpayers.
212           Gagnon  expressed concern about the perception of unassigned fund balance as a "slush fund"
213           similar to criticism the school had faced. Penny responded that unassigned fund balance cannot
214           be spent without board approval or a vote and is therefore not a slush fund. The discussion at
215           length covered the importance of maintaining a prudent reserve level while balancing tax rate
216           stability for residents.
