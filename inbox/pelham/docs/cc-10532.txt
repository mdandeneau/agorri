   5)    PL2026-00025 - Map 15 & 22 - Lots 8-86, 8-88, and 8-89 - Edward N Herbert Associates, Inc. (applicant) / Brookwood Realty
         Group, LLC. (owner) - Tina Avenue (off) - The applicant is seeking to consolidate and subdivide the referenced lots into 22
         residential lots. The proposed subdivision will require the construction of a roadway through the Wetland Conservation District
         (WCD) buffer to provide access to the proposed residential lots. Accordingly, the applicant is requesting a Special Permit for the
          proposed intrusion into the WCD buffer to facilitate roadway construction.

            Ms. Masse Quinn read the abutters into the record.

            Shane Gendron, representing the applicant and property owner, explained that the proposed project had
            been presented conceptually to the Board the previous year. The project was originally approved in 2006 but
            had not moved forward until now. He stated that a lot-line revision had been granted in the spring to address
            frontage requirements. The three existing lots would be combined and then subdivided into 22 lots, all of
            which would meet the zoning requirements.

            Mr. Gendron explained that the proposed development included connecting Tina Avenue to Balcom Road
                                                   Page 2 of 7
 PLANNING BOARD MINUTES                                                               MEETING DATE: 10/19/2026

            and creating a subdivision road, Pembroike Court, off the new connection. The project would be completed in
            two phases. The first phase would involve connecting Tina Avenue to Balcom Road and constructing the
            subdivision road. He stated that the road was relatively flat and that drainage would be directed to Lots 8-86-
            15 and 8-86-13. Two filtration ponds would collect, treat, and release stormwater. Although engineering
            review had not yet occurred, no waivers had been identified as necessary at that time. He explained that a
            special permit was being requested because a culvert associated with the road would be located within
            wetlands. The proposed road, drainage improvements, and flood storage would also be located within the
            buffer zone. Mr. Gendron added that the proposal incorporated the Board’s recommendations from the
            previous meeting.

            Ms. Masse Quinn asked which lots contained the wetland crossing. Mr. Gendron identified Lots 8-86-5, 8-86-
            6, 8-86-13, and 8-86-14.

            Mr. Doherty requested to see the plan showing Tina Avenue connecting to Balcom Road and the location of
            Pembroike Court. He noted that the State was expected to prohibit municipalities from limiting the number of
            cul-de-sacs beginning in April. He suggested eliminating Pembroike Court and making Tina Avenue a cul-de-
            sac rather than connecting it to Balcom Road. He asked Mr. Gendron whether that option was feasible.

            Mr. Gendron stated that the Fire Department had requested a connection but acknowledged that eliminating
            it was possible. He explained that the decision would depend on the Highway Safety Commission. Mr.
            Lamoureux clarified the location of the proposed connection and the State’s anticipated decision.

            Mr. Doherty stated that eliminating the connection to Balcom Road could prevent disruption to the existing
            neighborhood, particularly if the cul-de-sac could be extended. Ms. Takesian disagreed, emphasizing that the
            Highway Safety Commission’s recommendation carried considerable weight and that a through road was
            generally safer than a cul-de-sac. She explained that a connection would distribute traffic in two directions,
            whereas a cul-de-sac could force traffic through one subdivision and create dead-end conditions on both
            Tina Avenue and Balcom Road.

            Ms. Paquin asked where the retention ponds would be located because of her concerns about water. Mr.
            Gendron identified the proposed ponds on Lots 14 and 15. He clarified that they were infiltration ponds and
            explained that the soil was very sandy, making it suitable for drainage, although seasonally high water tables
            were present. The ponds were designed to allow water to infiltrate into the ground, with outlets to retain water
            during periods of heavy rainfall.

            Ms. Takesian stated that she had driven along the roads under discussion and noted that a house was
            located at the end of Tina Avenue. She expressed concern that, if a cul-de-sac were created, headlights from
            passing vehicles could shine directly toward the house.

            Mr. Bilapka stated that he did not support connecting the road to Balcom Road because drivers would likely
            use the connection as a shortcut. He suggested keeping the connection closed to general traffic while
            providing emergency access for fire apparatus.

            Ms. Masse Quinn stated that she lived on a cul-de-sac and had no concerns about the lack of a second
            outlet, provided fire trucks could turn around. She noted that the decision depended partly on the abutters’
            preferences and recalled that the abutters had previously opposed a connection to Balcom Road. With the
            anticipated change in State law allowing cul-de-sacs, she believed the Board should consider the
            neighborhood’s concerns.

            Mr. Gendron stated that he had no preference regarding whether the road would connect to Balcom Road.
            He explained that eliminating the connection could result in a roadway approximately 4,000 feet long.
            Emergency access could still be provided as long as no frontage was lost. He stated that he was seeking
            direction from the Board.

            Ms. Paquin asked whether the owners intended to reduce the number of lots. Mr. Gendron replied that they
            did not. He reiterated that the Fire Department wanted access but that the Board would make the final

                                                   Page 3 of 7

            decision.

            Ms. Masse Quinn asked how the road would be oriented if the connection were eliminated. Mr. Gendron
            explained that a curve would be added to the road and the lot would be retained. Ms. Masse Quinn then
            asked where the septic system would be located if emergency access were provided. Mr. Gendron stated
            that sufficient room was available. Mr. Doherty asked whether the septic system was located within the
            building envelope, and Mr. Gendron confirmed that it was. Mr. Doherty also asked whether the drainage
            easement was within the building envelope, noting that part of the building envelope was located in the
            floodplain.

            A motion was made by Danielle Masse Quinn and seconded by Bruce Bilapka to accept Case PL2026-
            00025, Map 15 and 22, Lots 8-86, 8-88, and 8-89, Edward N. Herbert Associates, Inc. (applicant) and
            Brookwood Realty Group, LLC (owner), Tina Avenue (off), for consideration. The motion passed by a vote of
            7-0-0. Those voting in favor were Timothy Doherty, Danielle Masse Quinn, John Spottiswood, Charlene
            Takesian, Jennifer Paquin, Bruce Bilapka, and Scott Sawtelle.

            Mr. Doherty opened the discussion to the public.

            Susan Hayes of 15 Balcom Road asked how the additional traffic generated by the 22 proposed homes
            would access the surrounding roads. She described Balcom Road as very narrow and stated that vehicles
            sometimes drove onto her lawn because two cars could not pass each other. Although she acknowledged
            that property owners had the right to develop their land, she questioned how the safety of existing residents
            would be protected. She explained that she had initially intended to address concerns about wetlands but
            considered the safety of children and elderly residents using the narrow roads to be a more pressing issue.

            Trina Grant of 17 Tina Avenue asked how the development would affect existing wells and water tables. She
            also expressed concern about the additional traffic entering and leaving Tina Avenue as a result of the 22
            new homes. Mr. Lamoureux asked whether she supported connecting Tina Avenue to Balcom Road. Ms.
            Grant replied that a connection might help protect the neighborhoods but questioned how anyone could
            predict where the additional traffic would travel.

            Lynne Miller of 33 Balcom Road stated that she had lived in her home since the early 1980s and had
            purchased it after being told that the property in question could not be developed. She reported that her yard
            had been sinking since 2006. Mr. Doherty asked whether her property was directly in front of the proposed
            road connection.

            Ms. Miller stated that water was her greatest concern. She reported that the first floor of her home had
            flooded despite never having experienced water intrusion before. She also described a sinking driveway, a
            pool deck that had pulled away from the house, and a persistently wet yard. She was concerned that the
            development would introduce additional water onto the property. She also anticipated increased traffic at the
            end of Balcom Road, particularly around 4:30 a.m., and questioned whether drivers would use Tina Avenue
            instead. She noted that ambulances had been using the existing circular route for years and expressed
            concern that the proposed roadway would negatively affect the neighborhood. She added that water already
            pooled in certain areas and that the development could reduce property values. She explained that residents
            had purchased homes on Balcom Road because it was a private road.

            Mr. Doherty asked whether water was being directed to Lot 13. Mr. Gendron explained that water from Tina
            Avenue would be directed to Lot 14, while water could not be discharged onto Balcom Road because of its
            age and condition. Water would therefore be directed to Lot 13. He stated that stormwater plans were subject
            to review by State engineers and that all stormwater had to be managed on the development lots. He also
            explained that the negative slope from Balcom Road directed water toward Lot 13 and that water from
            Pembroike Court would also drain to Lot 13. Mr. Gendron noted that the area was zoned for business use
            and stated that the proposed single-family homes represented a favorable use of the property. He believed
            the new homes could improve property values, although he acknowledged that traffic would increase.

            David Barangine of 1 Balcom Road stated that approximately 40 homes were located on Balcom Road and
            asked how many homes were on Tina Avenue. Mr. Doherty questioned why he was asking the Board to

                                                   Page 4 of 7

            provide the number of homes. Mr. Barangine explained that, if Balcom Road had approximately 40 homes
            and Tina Avenue had 15, adding the proposed 22 homes would bring the number of homes associated with
            Tina Avenue closer to that of Balcom Road. He believed this would balance the traffic between the two
            roads. He stated that most traffic would travel toward Salem and that exiting Balcom Road during rush hour
            was difficult. He believed Tina Avenue offered better visibility because of the traffic signal in that area. He
            supported emergency access but opposed a general road connection.

            Mr. Lamoureux noted that visibility at Balcom Road was a concern and stated that using Tina Avenue would
            be safer because of the traffic signal. Ms. Paquin added that construction on Main Street was expected to
            begin soon and would contribute to traffic congestion.

            Mr. Doherty stated that a study should be conducted to assess the potential for accidents near Balcom Road
            and determine how traffic would be diverted as a result of the proposed development. Mr. Bilapka suggested
            that the State might consider installing traffic signals. He opposed directing additional traffic onto Balcom
            Road, stating that the risk of accidents could increase, particularly with other development occurring farther
            up the street.

            Mr. Gendron emphasized that good planning included connectivity and that a road connection would provide
            drivers with another way to leave the subdivision. He reiterated that the development could proceed under
            either configuration and that he wanted direction from the Board before revisiting the proposal with the
            Highway Safety Commission.

            Mr. Barangine agreed that connectivity was important but emphasized that safety needed to be considered.
            He stated that an emergency access road could provide an alternative without creating a public through road.
            Mr. Doherty noted that many cul-de-sacs existed throughout the Town and State without additional access
            points.

            Pat Gendron of 579 Bridge Street stated that he wanted an easement to pass through Lot 8-85-1. Ms. Masse
            Quinn asked Shane Gendron whether the proposal involved connecting the apartments. Mr. Gendron replied
            that it did not. Pat Gendron clarified that he was seeking access through the wetlands for logging and tree
            cutting, based on previous discussions with the property owner. He also stated that the property line should
            be reinforced because vehicles had been parking on the leach field.

            Shane Gendron stated that he would speak with the owners about the easement and signage. Ms. Masse
            Quinn asked what type of easement was being requested. Mr. Gendron explained that no easement
            currently existed but suggested that a 10-foot-wide area on either side could potentially be designated for
            that purpose. Ms. Masse Quinn asked Pat Gendron to clarify what he wanted to access, and he reiterated
            that he needed access for logging and tree cutting and had an understanding with the owner granting him
            permission. Ms. Takesian asked whether Lots 8-85 and 8-85-1 were entirely wetlands. Pat Gendron
            confirmed that the area was extensive. Ms. Takesian advised him to discuss the matter directly with the
            property owner because it was not a matter for the Board. Ms. Masse Quinn reiterated that no easement
            currently existed on the property. Mr. Doherty noted that an easement already existed from Balcom Road to
            provide access to the brook.

            Alisha Tario of 8 Balcom Road stated that safety was her primary concern. She supported fire access but
            opposed allowing general public traffic through the neighborhood. She explained that the neighborhood's
            walkability contributed to its quality of life and that many residents who walked in the area were older adults.
            She was concerned that additional traffic would increase the danger to pedestrians. She asked what the
            developer could do to assist affected neighbors and what measures would be taken to address their
            concerns. Mr. Doherty responded that Mr. Gendron's responsibility was to represent his client rather than to
            ensure that all neighbors were satisfied. Ms. Hayes added that the Girl Scout camp was very busy during the
            summer, making traffic safety particularly concerning.

            Kevin Cash of 8 Tina Avenue expressed concern about the water supply, noting that a neighbor had run out
            of water a few weeks earlier. Mr. Spottiswood clarified that the apartments were connected to public water
            rather than private wells. Mr. Cash asked whether the proposed homes could also be connected to public
            water, expressing concern that 22 additional homes using private wells could affect his well and asking how

                                                   Page 5 of 7

            he could be assured that it would not run dry. Mr. Bilapka stated that extensive testing of water drawdown
            and water quality would be required before any foundations were poured.

            Mr. Doherty closed the public discussion.

            Mr. Lamoureux asked how testing could determine whether an existing well might run dry. Mr. Gendron
            stated that the existing wells had good water quality and explained that the number of homes that could be
            connected to public water did not justify the cost of installing the necessary infrastructure. Mr. Lamoureux
            asked whether individual wells would be tested for drawdown. Mr. Thomas noted that adjacent homes on
            Sherman had been tested and monitored simultaneously. Ms. Takesian clarified that the Sherman project
            involved a community well. Mr. Gendron explained that the Sherman project had received approval from the
            New Hampshire Department of Environmental Services (DES), whereas the current proposal involved
            individual wells.

            Mr. Doherty asked the Board how it wished to address the road connectivity issue. Ms. Paquin supported
            emergency access but opposed a through road. Ms. Takesian requested that the proposal be referred to the
            Highway Safety

            Commission because State requirements were changing. Ms. Lacey agreed that Tina Avenue and Balcom
            Road should not be connected. Mr. Sawtelle also opposed the connection. Mr. Lamoureux agreed with Ms.
            Takesian that the matter should be reviewed by the Highway Safety Commission.

            Ms. Masse Quinn asked whether the proposal had already been reviewed by the Highway Safety
            Commission. Mr. Gendron confirmed that an earlier version had been reviewed but explained that the current
            plan was new and would need to be resubmitted. He requested permission to return to the Board to discuss
            the road connection separately. Ms. Masse Quinn stated that she opposed the through road but wanted the
            Board to consult with Fire and Safety officials before making a decision. Mr. Gendron noted that the cul-de-
            sac at the end of Pembroike Court was 80 feet in diameter and would accommodate fire apparatus.

            Mr. Bilapka reiterated his support for emergency access but opposition to connecting the roads for general
            traffic. Mr. Doherty noted that, beginning in April, emergency access could no longer be mandated under the
            anticipated State requirements. Mr. Thomas stated that he did not support a road connection and believed
            the matter should be reviewed by the Highway Safety Commission, with the decision based on its impact on
            existing residents. Mr. Spottiswood stated that, although he did not live on Balcom Road and could not speak
            directly for its residents, he would personally prefer to have an alternative way out. However, he respected
            the views of the abutters who would be directly affected.

            Ms. Takesian observed that many of the arguments against connecting Balcom Road could also be made
            against routing all traffic through Tina Avenue. She stated that preventing a connection would force all traffic
            in one direction rather than allowing it to be divided between two routes.

            Mr. Doherty reopened the public discussion.

            Pat Gendron stated that an existing stub road off Tina Avenue was used by trucks to travel beneath the
            power lines. He suggested that this access road could serve as the primary entrance to the subdivision,
            avoiding new connections to both Tina Avenue and Balcom Road. He showed Shane Gendron the location
            on the plans.

            Shane Gendron stated that using the existing access road could raise concerns with the State regarding curb
            cuts and the proximity of a road to an existing road along Route 38. Pat Gendron responded that the State
            had installed the existing access road, so a new curb cut should not be necessary. Ms. Takesian and Ms.
            Masse Quinn both stated that the suggestion was worth considering.

            Mr. Gendron noted that a right-of-way issue might arise. Pat Gendron explained that the owner of the
            property involved in the current application also owned the land containing the access road, so obtaining
            permission from a separate owner should not be necessary. Mr. Doherty asked Shane Gendron to raise the
            alternative access proposal with the Highway Safety Commission. Pat Gendron then showed the route on

                                                   Page 6 of 7

            the plans, explaining that the access road connected to Tina Avenue, then Rita Avenue, and ultimately Route
            38.

            Mr. Gendron asked when the next Highway Safety Commission meeting would be held. Ms. Zagorski stated
            that it was scheduled for October 29 and asked whether he intended to appear before the Conservation
            Commission first. Mr. Gendron confirmed that he did but explained that he needed the Board's direction
            regarding the road as soon as possible. He requested to be placed on the agenda for the next Planning
            Board meeting.

            Mr. Gendron stated that he would investigate whether the existing access road could be used and determine
            the feasibility of the alternative with the Department of Transportation (DOT).
