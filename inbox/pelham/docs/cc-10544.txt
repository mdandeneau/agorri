   2)    PL2026-00025 - Map 15 & 22 - Lots 8-86, 8-88, and 8-89 - Edward N Herbert Associates, Inc. (applicant) / Brookwood
         Realty Group, LLC. (owner) - Tina Avenue (off) - The applicant is seeking to consolidate and subdivide the referenced lots
         into 22 residential lots.

            The Committee reviewed the proposed 22-lot residential subdivision submitted by Edward N. Herbert
            Associates, Inc., on behalf of Brookwood Realty Group, LLC. Shayne Gendron of Edward N. Herbert
            Associates, Inc., presented the proposal, and Roger Ploof was identified as the property owner. Mr. Gendron
            explained that a subdivision had previously been approved in 2006 but was never recorded. The applicant
            subsequently completed a lot line adjustment and returned with a revised 22-lot subdivision plan. The
            Planning Board requested input from the Highway Safety Committee regarding whether the proposed
            development should include a public connection to Balcom Road, an emergency-access-only connection, or
            no connection.

            Mr. Gendron explained that the original proposal included a connection between Tina Avenue and Balcom
            Road, with approximately 900 feet of roadway terminating in a cul-de-sac. He noted that some abutters had
            expressed opposition to a connection to Balcom Road and that concerns regarding water quantity had also
            been raised. The Planning Board had been leaning toward not connecting the two roads. Mr. Gendron
            presented a revised plan that would avoid crossing the wetlands, thereby reducing construction costs and
            avoiding wetland impacts and associated mitigation requirements. The revised proposal would extend Tina
            Avenue approximately 800 feet and terminate in a cul-de-sac, with no connection through to Balcom Road.
            He explained that avoiding the wetland crossing would provide significant cost savings to the applicant and
            reduce environmental impacts. The revised plan would retain a connection to Balcom Road serving eight

            lots, rather than allowing all 22 lots to use the connection. Mr. Gendron stated that the revised roadway
            would be a straightforward 900-foot road ending in a cul-de-sac and would not require waivers.

            Ms. Susan Hayes introduced herself as an abutter to the proposed subdivision and expressed concern
            regarding the safety of vehicles entering and exiting Balcom Road. She stated that it was difficult to imagine
            the additional traffic attempting to exit Balcom Road and that the increased traffic would be detrimental to the
            neighborhood. She indicated that she was not opposed to a development but felt that allowing eight homes
            to access Balcom Road, rather than 22 homes using it as a cut-through, would be preferable.

            Chief Perriello noted that the 65-unit apartment complex under construction nearby had raised traffic
            concerns and that she had been working with the State to obtain a new traffic study once the complex was
            occupied. She stated that the goal would be to determine whether the speed limit in the area could be
            reduced. Ms. Hayes thanked Chief Perriello for the update and added that traffic on Route 38 also increases
            when the Girl Scout camp is in session.

            Ms. Beauregard asked whether proposed Lot 13 met the required buildable lot size, and Mr. Gendron
            confirmed that it did. Chief Perriello asked where 15 Tina Avenue was located in relation to the proposed
            plan. Mr. Gendron identified its location on the plan and explained that the existing home at 15 Tina Avenue
            would be demolished and a new home constructed as part of the development.

            Fire Chief Newman asked about the existing length of Tina Avenue, and Mr. Gendron confirmed that it was
            approximately 1,250 feet.

            Mr. Gendron noted that the property is located in the B-5 zoning district, where a more concentrated
            development could be pursued. However, he felt that a conventional 22-lot subdivision would be appropriate
            for the property. He also explained that the Planning Board had previously discussed using the power line
            easement for the roadway, but the utility company had indicated that this would not be permitted.

            Fire Chief Newman discussed the anticipated change in State law effective April 1, 2027, regarding municipal
            authority to limit the length of cul-de-sacs. He stated that, under the anticipated change, there would be no
            basis to prevent the proposed roadway from being constructed if it met the applicable requirements. Mr.
            Hoffman stated that he preferred the revised plan because, if a culvert issue occurred, residents would still
            be able to enter and exit the neighborhood. Fire Chief Newman acknowledged that the length of the cul-de-
            sac raised a public safety concern but stated that there would be nothing to prevent the applicant from
            constructing the road at the proposed length. Ms. Beauregard noted that a waiver would be required under
            the current requirements for a roadway of this length.

            Mr. Gendron stated that stormwater management would be addressed through appropriate engineering and
            that the system would be designed to handle the proposed development. He added that, from an
            environmental standpoint, avoiding the wetland impacts would be preferable. The revised plan would not
            include an island within the cul-de-sac. Mr. Hoffman stated that he generally preferred islands because they
            assist with winter maintenance and reduce the need for vehicle backup alarms during late-night operations.
            Mr. Gendron agreed that they could design the cul-de-sacs with an island. Chief Perriello stated that she
            preferred a through connection for emergency response purposes but was not opposed to the revised plan,
            noting that the proposed roadway would not extend substantially farther than 15 Tina Avenue. Mr. Hoffman
            also expressed a preference for the revised plan, stating that avoiding the wetland crossing was beneficial.
            Ms. Beauregard agreed that the revised plan was preferable, particularly because it would avoid wetland
            impacts and limit the number of lots accessing Balcom Road.

            The Committee discussed its recommendation to the Planning Board. Fire Chief Newman stated that the
            primary disadvantage of the revised plan was the lack of full roadway connectivity, which could limit access
            in the event of an emergency. However, he noted that the proposed roadway would meet the applicable
            minimum Fire Code requirements and that, although connectivity would be safer from a public safety
            standpoint, the proposed modifications were acceptable. He stated that the Fire Department could support
            the revised plan, although it would not be the preferred option, and that the required approvals would be
            provided if the proposal met all applicable requirements. Fire Chief Newman indicated that he would prepare
            a letter on behalf of the Highway Safety Committee to the Planning Board. Ms. Beauregard stated that the
            Committee generally agreed that roadway connectivity was preferable but that the revised plan was

   3)    PL2026-00023 & PL2026-00024 – Map 35, Lot 351-65 – R.J. McCarthy Development (Owner/Applicant) – 56 Bridge
         Street – Previously reviewed by the Highway Safety Committee on September 24, 2026, for Subdivision and Site Plan
         review. The applicant/owner has requested continued discussion regarding the proposed street names.

            Ms. Beauregard explained that Mr. McCarthy requested a change to the previously approved street name,
            Founder’s Lane, and would prefer Paddock Lane instead. Paddock Lane is the proposed name for the Town
            roadway that extends from Bridge Street and ends in a cul-de-sac. Workshop Way would remain the
            proposed name for the private roadway leading into the northern parcel for the commercial development.

            Motion:  To accept the proposed name Paddock Lane for the proposed Town road ending in a cul-de-sac.
            Motion By: Anne Perriello / Second By: Daniel Newman Vote: (4-0-0) The motion passed.
            In Favor: 4 — Anne Perriello, Daniel Newman, Jennifer Beauregard, James Hoffman
