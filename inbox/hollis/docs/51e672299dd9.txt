21   REPORTS
22   Schools – L. Flamino presented highlights from the SAU Government Board’s last meeting.
23   Fiscal Year 2027’s adopted budget has been finalized at $2,544,642. Fiscal Year 2028’s proposed budget
24   is $2,595,347, which is an increase of 1.99% over last year. The increase is due to a 4% salary increase
25   and an adjusted snow removal budget line. Decreases in are seen in NHRS, custodial & health insurance
26   budget lines.
27   This proposed increase was based on the August Consumer Price Index (CPI) of 3.9%. C. Hyde noted the
28   September CPI is traditionally used. L. Flamino stated the SAU subcommittee and the Governing Board
29   would review the September CPI before the December public hearing and make any necessary
30   adjustments.
31   L. Flamino also reviewed the Hollis School District’s year-end revenue expenditure report. Adjustments
32   were made to allow funds to be used for tax relief. Special education costs and miscellaneous
33   encumbrances contributed to a surplus of $465,000. Revenues also increased. After funding the
34   Maintenance Trust Fund and SAU Building Trust, the remaining fund balance available to reduce FY2026
35   taxes is $333,781.
36   C. Hyde asked about the audit. L. Flamino stated that finalized findings would not be available until
37   spring. Preliminary reports have been submitted to & accepted by the DOE. Reports can now be
38   submitted to the DRA for tax rate calculation.
39   R. Zack reviewed enrollment numbers from the latest HEC Hollis Renovation & Expansion Committee,
40   HPS totals 374 student and HUES 302.
41   The community survey received 370 responses, with feedback focusing primarily on classroom sizes, the
42   Farley building, and the Hollis pharmacy. HREC requested that Banwell Architects reduce the scope of
43   the proposed project and provide two alternative expansion options, estimated at $5 million and $10
44   million.
45   C. Hyde asked whether the revised project scope would be reflected in the Capital Improvements Plan
46   (CIP). M. Kost explained that the septic system had been removed from the building project scope and

56   Town – T. Whalen reported that the transfer station project has been completed and the Still Good table
57   has reopened. One of the new compactors was delivered in the incorrect model due to a manufacturer
58   error. A temporary workaround is being developed until the correct compactor arrives. The project was
59   completed under budget.
60   T. Whalen also reported that all road paving projects scheduled for the year have been completed.
61   D. Padykula stated that approximately 75% of the 2026 budget had been expended. Several unanticipated
62   expenses had recently arisen:
63       • Public Works overtime: Expenditures have exceeded the budget.
64       • Road salt: Costs have already exceeded the budget, and additional supplies will be needed before
65            year-end.
66       • Fire Department vehicle maintenance: Major repairs have pushed the vehicle maintenance budget
67            line over budget.
68       • Police Department septic project: Significant issues were encountered, increasing the original
69            $80,000 estimate to approximately double that amount due to deteriorating pipes.
70       • Vehicle fuel: Costs remain a concern for the remainder of the year.
71   D. Padykula reported that 98% of taxes had been collected. The Town hopes to receive the tax rate from
72   the DRA by the end of the month. Updated financial statements are expected to be available the following
73   week.
74   The salt shed, which was included in the CIP, has been completed. The solar panels at the Department of
75   Public Works have been installed but still need to be connected.
76   M. Harris asked about the status of the structural analysis of Town Hall. T. Whalen explained that two
77   issues are being considered: structural integrity and Americans with Disabilities Act (ADA) compliance.
78   The structural analysis has been completed, and some previously recommended work remains
79   outstanding, including items identified as urgent. The Select Board is planning a warrant article to fund an
80   engineering firm's on-site evaluation of the building. An ADA study is forthcoming.
81   C. Hyde questioned whether Town Hall would adequately meet the Town's future needs. He asked
82   whether the land purchase listed in the CIP for a shared Police and Fire Safety Center could also
83   accommodate Town Hall and the Town Clerk's office as part of a municipal complex.
84   T. Whalen stated that the Select Board has discussed the possibility of a municipal complex but noted that
85   finding a suitable parcel of land near the center of town has been challenging.
86   M. Kost thanked D. Padykula and L. Flamino for compiling CIP information for the Capital Expenditure
87   Advisory Committee (CEAC). He noted that, under its charter, the CEAC is intended to understand
88   proposed projects rather than challenge them. He also stated that the CEAC does not have the authority to
89   determine how CIP items are incorporated into or funded through the budget. M. Kost encouraged the
90   leadership of the Town, Cooperative School District (COOP), and Hollis School District (HSD) to work
91   collaboratively to make CIP information accessible to taxpayers.

 94   D. Mann discussed HB 1300, an act establishing a school district local tax cap question for the 2026 and
 95   2028 state general elections and imposing related limitations on school district central office
 96   administrative expenses.
 97   The bill has passed and will appear on the November ballot. An informational public hearing is scheduled
 98   for October 21, 2026, following the 6:00 p.m. COOP School Board meeting in the high school
 99   auditorium.
100   The bill establishes a cap on local school district tax assessments and a limit on SAU central office
101   spending. The language and formula used to calculate the cap are outlined in RSA 32:5-i. D. Mann noted
102   that the question will appear on both the 2026 and 2028 ballots, regardless of the outcome in 2026.
103   The COOP Budget Committee formally voted not to recommend the bill. D. Mann will represent the
104   COOP Budget Committee at the October 21 public hearing.
105   D. Mann asked the Hollis Budget Committee whether it wished to recommend a representative for the
106   public hearing and whether it wanted to take a position on HB 1300 before or after the hearing.
107   M. Kost asked why the COOP Budget Committee had voted against the bill. D. Mann explained that the
108   COOP already uses an inflation-based model when developing its budget. She also noted that previous
109   spending cap proposals, including votes and petition warrant articles, had either been defeated or passed
110   over. Additionally, HB 1300 is being considered outside the normal budget cycle, and many details
111   remain uncertain.
112   M. Kost asked whether the SAU administration budget had ever increased by 6%. D. Mann stated that the
113   COOP had never approached a 6% increase, even when bond costs were included.
114   M. Harris asked whether the Hollis Budget Committee wished to take a position on HB 1300. Members
115   discussed the lack of available data at this time.
116   C. Hyde moved that the Budget Committee not recommend HB 1300. M. Leavitt seconded the motion.
117   Motion passed unanimously, 6-0-0.
118   C. Hyde recommended T. Gehan to represent the Hollis Budget Committee at the October 21st public
119   hearing on HB 1300. M. Kost seconded the motion.
120   Motion passed unanimously, 6-0-0.
