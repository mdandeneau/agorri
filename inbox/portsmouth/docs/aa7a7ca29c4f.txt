This month’s safety message is as follows:
The Portsmouth Fire Department ty and the NFPA would like to remind
you: As you start preparing your holiday schedule and organizing that
large family feast, remember, by following a few simple safety tips you can
enjoy time with your loved ones and keep yourself and your family safer
from fire.

Thanksgiving Fire Statistics
       • Thanksgiving is the peak day for home cooking fires with more
          than three times the daily average for such incidents. Christmas
          Day and Christmas Eve ranked second and third, with both
          having nearly twice the daily average.
       • Unattended cooking was by far the leading contributing factor
          in cooking fires and fire deaths.
       • Cooking causes half (51%) of all reported home fires and nearly
          two of every five (35%) home fire injuries, and it is a leading
          cause of home fire deaths (16%).
       • On Thanksgiving Day alone, an estimated 1,139 home cooking
          fires were reported to U.S fire departments in 2024, reflecting
          321 percent of the daily average.
       • Structure fires occurring on Thanksgiving Day - 80% are due
          to cooking. And those cooking fires account for 35% of the
          civilian injuries sustained in structure fires that occurred on
          Thanksgiving. These are annual estimates for the period 2020 -
          2024 (no civilian deaths had occurred in cooking fires on
          Thanksgiving for these years)
Source: NFPA Research Top Safety Tips
       • Stay in the kitchen when you are cooking on the stove top so you
          can keep an eye on the food.
       • Stay in the home when cooking your turkey, and check on it
          frequently.
       • Keep children away from the stove. The stove will be hot and
          kids should stay three feet away.

           •   Make sure kids stay away from hot food and liquids. The steam
               or splash from vegetables, gravy or coffee could cause serious

           •   Keep knives out of the reach of children.

Deputy Chief Egan reported (26-132) on EMS & Training/Education and
Department Activities in the month of October.

October Training & Education
During October, the EMS & Training Division coordinated and delivered four
department-wide training initiatives focused on patient care, responder safety,
specialized emergency response, and implementation of updated medical
protocols. Each training program reached 60 personnel across the department's
four shifts.

           October Training Summary
           Training Program                 Classroom Hours   Personnel Contact Hours
           Sensory Backpacks                4                 60
           Human Trafficking Awareness      8                 120
           Unitil Utility Safety            8                 120
           Medication Protocols             4                 60
           Total                            24                360
           October Training Accomplishments

• 4 department-wide training initiatives completed
• 16 individual training sessions conducted across four shifts
• 60 personnel reached through each training initiative
• 24 cumulative classroom delivery hours
• 360 personnel continuing education contact hours
These figures represent training delivery and educational impact. They do not
include the additional administrative time required for scheduling, curriculum
development, outside agency coordination, preparation, or training
documentation.

     1. Sensory Backpack Training
Implemented training on the use of sensory backpacks designed to assist
firefighters and EMS personnel when treating autistic individuals and children
and adults with developmental disabilities.

3. Unitil Gas & Electrical Emergency Response Training
Coordinated specialized first responder training with Unitil's gas and electrical
operations personnel. Topics included recognizing electrical hazards, natural
gas emergencies, leak detection, scene safety, situational awareness, and
coordination with utility crews during emergency incidents.

•   Course duration: 2 hours
•   Sessions: 4

•   Classroom delivery: 8 hours
•   Personnel education: 120 contact hours
