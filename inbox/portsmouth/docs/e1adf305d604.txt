   8. 11. E. Planning Board Recommendation Regarding Licenses and a Sidewalk Easement
      for 361 Hanover Street

City Manager Conard introduced the scope of the item, reiterating that the vote would accept
and record a Sidewalk Easement, a Landscape License, and a Pole License from 361 Hanover
Steam Factory, LLC for 361 Hanover Street.

Moving into discussion, Councilor Bagley proposed splitting the item into two pieces: a motion
to accept and record a Sidewalk Easement, followed by a separate motion to a Landscape
License and a Pole License. Mayor McEachern supported hearing from staff and discussing
before dividing the motion.

Deputy City Attorney McCourt explained details of the easement and the two licenses.
Councilor Cook looked to clarify whether the Sidewalk Easement could be treated
independently from the Landscape and Pole Licenses, to which Deputy City Attorney McCourt
confirmed. Assistant Mayor Kelley and Mayor McEachern inquired on why the developer opted
for underground wires and if it was required. Public Works Director Peter Rice stated he

couldn’t confirm all details on behalf of Eversource, but Eversource had previously cited their
case for burying wires as safety and longevity concerns.

Assistant Mayor Kelley asked why public comment was not allowed at the Planning Board, to
which Deputy City Attorney McCourt explained there’s no specific rule that referrals from the
City Council to the Planning Board come with a public hearing. Councilor Cook questioned the
positioning of the transformer, to which Director Rice explained that it’s possible to move a
transformer under certain parameters and understood concerns of sight distance and
aesthetics. Further questions from Councilor Cook explored details of the transformer, ability
to conceal wires, and differences between pole-mounted and ground-mounted transformers.

Councilor Bagley proposed once again splitting the item into two separate motions, but Mayor
McEachern requested holding off splitting any motions until all three facets of the item have
been heard.

Deputy City Attorney McCourt explained the details of the Landscape License and subsequent
responsibility of the developer to maintain the existing retaining wall for a period of fifty years.
Councilor Bagley had questions regarding sizing of the transformer, to which Director Rice did
not have specifics. Councilor Tabor questioned Deputy City Attorney McCourt on public access
to the land, to which Deputy City Attorney McCourt confirmed crossing developer-owned land
would be required to access the space. Further questions ensued regarding integrity of the
retaining wall and its relativity to new construction. Assistant Mayor Kelley and Councilor Cook
noted that it would be helpful to have more information on the green space sizing and logistics,
liability concerns, as well as information from Eversource justifying size and placement of the
proposed ground-mounted transformer. Councilor Bagley also noted the need for more
information and whether the raised green space is actually a win for the City.

Further comments from Mayor McEachern, Councilor Blalock, Councilor Cook, and Councilor
Bagley discussed retaining wall viability and details of the transformer’s placement and long-
term purpose.

Councilor Bagley moved to divide the question into two parts, seconded by Councilor Cook.

On a unanimous vote, motion passed.

Councilor Bagley moved to authorize the City Manager to accept and record a Sidewalk
Easement from 361 Hanover Steam Factory, LLC for 361 Hanover Street in a form acceptable
to the City Attorney, subject to the recommendations in the enclosed memorandum from
Assistant Planning Director Stith, seconded by Councilor Hopkins.

On a unanimous vote, motion passed.

Discussion ensued on how to incorporate the Pole and Landscape Licenses into a work
session, as well as whether a transformer on public land is intended to benefit the public.

   9. 10. A. Third and Final Reading to Amend Chapter 10, Zoning Ordinance, which will
      remove most parking regulations from the Zoning Ordinance to the Site Plan Review
      Regulations (except one and two-family dwellings)

Councilor Tabor moved to pass third and final reading as presented, seconded by Councilor
Blalock.

Councilor Blalock looked to Councilor Moreau for greater detail on the motion, with Councilor
Moreau explaining that removals create more condition-based assessments. Councilor Bagley
commented this was a good first step. Councilor Tabor noted this has also been a goal of the
Housing Committee.

Following a roll call vote, the motion passed 9-0.

   10. 10. B. Adoption of Resolution #15-2026 authorizing a Bond Issue and/or Notes of the
      City under the Municipal Finance Act of up to Six Hundred Thousand Dollars
      ($600,000) for costs related to the purchase of fire apparatus

City Manager Conard commented the Resolution was considered at the August 3, 2026, City
Council meeting as a Public Hearing with a presentation. City Council action was tabled
pending the outcome of the City's application to the FEMA Port Security Grant Program. The
City’s request was not successful, and that Public Hearing was held on August 3rd, explaining
why Public Hearing was not noticed for tonight’s meeting.

Councilor Bagley moved to adopt Resolution #15-2026 as presented, second by Councilor
Moreau.

Councilor Blalock commented he would be voting against the item, citing that $600,000 is too
much to spend on a boat based on the current resources and agencies available. Councilor
Moreau proposed adjusting the motion to read “fire apparatus emergency boat” but was made
aware that changing the motion would be discouraged by bond council. Councilor Bagley
expressed his support based on recent rescue calls, citing a need for adequate equipment.

Councilor Hopkins echoed support and rationale. Councilor Cook noted it’s critical to have a
boat that serves the needs of our first responders, especially for conducting CPR. Councilor
Blalock replied that CPR can be done anywhere.

Mayor McEachern invited Fire Commissioner Gamester to comment on the importance of the
apparatus. Commissioner Gamester noted the importance of providing the proper apparatus
for hazardous conditions, as the present boat is no longer suitable.

Councilor Tabor moved to suspend the rules and offer the ability for anyone in the public to
speak to for or against the bond resolution in front of the Council, seconded by Councilor
Blalock.

On a unanimous vote, motion passed.

There were two speakers: Erik Anderson reiterated that assembling a regional water response
team would not place the entire burden on Portsmouth, sharing responsibility and equipment
with neighboring communities. Anderson encouraged exploring more funding sources. Susan
Sterry commented that there isn’t a centralized plan or coordination when an event happens
along the river, and that it would be wise to act now.

Councilor Bagley mentioned that he wished the City had acted sooner on procurement
following recent emergency calls on the water. Mayor McEachern thanked Fire Chief McQuillen
for his efforts submitting a grant application, thanked members of the public for reaching out
to their elected officials, and echoed points made around needing a regionalization of
resources

Following a roll call vote, the motion passed 8-1, with Councilor Blalock voting opposed.

   12. 11. G. Planning Board Recommendation on Cornwall Street

City Manager Conard directed the City Council to the memorandum from Assistant Planning
Director Peter Stith regarding the Planning Board’s October 15, 2026, vote regarding a portion
of Cornwall Street. As noted in the memorandum, the Planning Board voted unanimously to
recommend that the City Council approve the discontinuance of the identified portion of
Cornwall Street, subject to the recording of a municipal utility easement over the existing sewer
line and requesting a voluntary lot merger to align the parcel boundaries with the configuration
shown on the City’s tax map.

Councilor Tabor moved to schedule a public hearing on the discontinuance of the identified
portion of Cornwall Street at the November 16, 2026, City Council Meeting, seconded by
Councilor Blalock.

Councilor Moreau provided context to the item, noting questions regarding an access point on
a previous Planning Board application and supported the street’s discontinuation.

On a unanimous vote, motion passed.

   13. 11. I. Planning Board Recommendation on 0 Elm Street

City Manager Conard directed the City Council to a memorandum from Assistant Planning
Director Peter Stith regarding the Planning Board’s October 15, 2026, vote regarding property
located at 0 Elm Street—also noting two possible motions: move to authorize issuance of a
building permit, or move to deny the request. City Manager Conard also reminded Councilors
that Council approve does not constitute development approval and does not guarantee the
parcel is buildable under the City’s land use regulations.

Councilor Cook and Councilor Moreau agreed letting this process move forward would be
advantageous, especially considering future use of adjacent City-owned lots. Councilor Tabor
raised a question of road maintenance by the property owners, which Deputy City Attorney
McCourt explained maintenance of the private continuation of the road would be up to the
property owners. Councilor Bagley asked for clarification on the Council’s role in this decision.
Deputy City Attorney McCourt explained that it’s in the City’s interest to approve the parcel from
a public policy perspective, with Mayor McEachern adding that if approved, the City will not
intervene on the basis that the road was never initially fully developed. Councilor Blalock also

expressed support for approval. Councilor Flynn inquired on which adjacent lots the City owns,
to which Deputy City Attorney McCourt provided tax map and parcel numbers.

Councilor Flynn moved to authorize the issuance of a building permit for 0 Elm Street
contingent on the applicant obtaining all other required variances, approvals, and permits,
and on construction of a driveway adequate for emergency vehicle access as approved by the
Department of Public Works and the Fire Department; this authorization confers no private
right of access over Forrest Street or any other private property, seconded by Councilor
Hopkins.

On a unanimous vote, motion passed.

   14. 11. A. Request for Solid Waste Ordinance Work Session

City Manager Conard noted the review of the City’s ordinance related to the collection of solid
waste, yard waste, and recycling in preparation for the Department of Public Work’s transition
to the collection of solid waste via an automated side loader; and that the ordinance should
reflect future changes.

Councilor Flynn moved to schedule a Solid Waste Ordinance Work Session on December
7, 2026, at 6:00 PM to allow staff to provide additional details on solid waste collection
challenges and receive City Council and public input, second by Councilor Hopkins.

Councilor Hopkins requested a furthering education component into the work session,
explaining variability of recyclable materials. Mayor McEachern also added that the work
session would be an opportunity to further explain the staggered roll-out plan of new toters
and collection. Councilor Bagley noted this matter has been discussed for years and it appears
it’s finally moving forward.

On a unanimous vote, motion passed.

            (PFAS) requirements, the City is commencing design of a new treatment facility

            require additional sewer capacity for sections of sewer mains including the
            section of Borthwick Avenue. City staff recognize that upgrading the section of

            additional sewer capacity to support commercial and housing development. In
