Rolling Stock Work Session
                            Eileen Dondero Foley Council Chambers
                             Monday, October 26, 2026, at 7:00 PM


City Council Present:       Mayor McEachern, Assistant Mayor Kelley, Councilors Cook, Tabor,
                            Blalock, Bagley, Moreau, Flynn, and Hopkins

City Staff Present:         Karen Conard, City Manager; Susan Morrell, City Attorney; Michael
                            Tucker, Deputy Finance Director; Nathan Lunney, Deputy City
                            Manager – Finance and Administration

At 6:21 PM, Mayor McEachern called the work session to order.

Mayor McEachern introduced the topic for the work session. City Manager Conard remarked
that this work session highlights the decision-making and fine details behind the City’s Rolling
Stock program, noting this work session is a first on the matter.

Deputy City Manager Nathan Lunney began the presentation. The presentation demonstrated
what assets the City owns, how they are managed, purchasing challenges and funding sources,
as well as takeaways based on the current fleet and anticipatory needs down the line.

Councilor Hopkins inquired on the number of electric light-duty vehicles in the City’s fleet and
whether there is a policy in place to incorporate more electric vehicles going forward.
Councilor Blalock asked to hear from Ken Linchey, Portsmouth Schools Director of Buildings &
Grounds/ Maintenance, on the rollout of the district’s new autonomous field paint striper.
Linchey noted a drastic cut in labor needed to paint the fields, reallocating staff to more
pressing projects. Councilor Cook echoed Councilor Hopkins’ inquiry into the City’s
implementation of electric vehicles. Councilor Cook then asked if and how the City is weighing
vehicle maintenance costs against replacement costs. Peter Rice, Director of Public Works,
noted that these considerations are regularly being evaluated by the scenes.

Further discussion and questions ensued, primarily around pros and cons of fleet electrification,
commending consistent preventative maintenance across assets, and the impact of the City’s
new side-loading garbage truck on Solid Waste staff. Mayor McEachern noted it would be helpful
to break down rolling stock data based on vehicle class and use, verifying costs with other
municipalities. He also requested more data on the repair and lifecycle costs of electric vehicles,
as well as information from other municipalities with electric vehicles in their fleet.

There were two public speakers: Petra Huda had suggestions on increased vehicle pooling, as
well as posing whether outsourcing street sweeping would be cost-effective; Esther Kennedy
had questions on the quality of assets the City chooses to purchase, as well as comments on
the new Solid Waste Toter rollout.

At 7:05 PM, Mayor McEachern closed the work session.
