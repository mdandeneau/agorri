1. Amend Article 2 - Zoning Map, to add a new Section 2.3 regarding Flexible Sizing Dwelling Units. The
   purpose of the new section is stated therein, along with applicability, the methods of calculation, the need
   to meet all parking, health and safety requirements, and the pertinence of any other applicable
   requirements for residential uses to the underlying zoning district. This new ordinance encourages
   diversity in housing types and sizes, consistent with the Town of Hampton Master Plan, without
   increasing the size of developments.

Mr. Bachand reviewed the amendment.

Purpose: To encourage diversity in housing types and sizes, consistent with the Hampton Master
Plan, without increasing the size of developments.
Background: This amendment was prepared as part of Hampton’s Housing Opportunity Planning
(HOP) grant efforts. Several meetings have been held with the Planning Board and the Rockingham
Planning Commission (RPC), leading up to this public hearing, including a Public Engagement
Session held on September 16th.
This innovative concept can help to add more attainably priced dwelling units without significant
impacts on infrastructure or traffic. This approach has been used in other communities such as
Scarborough, ME. While Hampton could be an early adopter in NH, the concept has been tested
elsewhere.
One important consideration is that the applicable minimum dwelling size requirements still apply
(i.e., minimum square footage per dwelling unit, maximum number of dwelling units per structure).
Applicability:
The Flexible Sizing Dwelling Units Ordinance may be used in the following districts:
RB       RCS       B         BS       BS1       POR               TC-H, TC-N, TC-S           G
It may also be used in combination with the Liberty Lane Overlay, a Multi-Family Dwelling
proposal, a Manufactured Home Park, or Elderly Housing Standards.
This amendment may NOT be used with a Cottage Court Ordinance or with Accessory Dwelling
units.
Calculations:
Not more than 1 bedroom       AND      Not more than 750 SF         =   0.5 dwelling unit
Not more than 2 bedrooms      AND      Note more than 1,250 SF      =   0.75 dwelling unit
3 bedrooms or more            OR       More than 1,250 SF           =   1.0 dwelling unit
Each dwelling unit developed under this ordinance must meet all parking, health and safety
requirements, and any other applicable requirements for residential uses as the underlying zoning
district.

2. Amend Article 4 - Overlay Districts, Section 4.3 - Floodplain Management Ordinance by adding new
   definitions of “Compensatory Flood Storage” and “Hydraulic Reach” under Section 4.3.2, and adding a
   new Section 4.3.7.7 regarding Compensatory Flood Storage. The purpose of the new section is stated
   therein, along with general standards, specific standards for location, hydraulic connectivity, elevation,
   design and stability, wetland resources, timing for completion, and recording of instruments at the
   Registry of Deeds. Submission requirements are also provided. This new higher floodplain management
   standard is intended to prevent an increase in flood hazards resulting from the placement of fill or
   structures within the floodplain by requiring the replacement of lost flood storage capacity.

Mr. Bachand and Dr. Ritt (Coastal Resilience Coordinator) reviewed the amendment.

Purpose: To prevent an increase in flood hazards resulting from the placement of fill or structures
within the floodplain by requiring the replacement of lost flood storage capacity.

Background: In March 2024, a rewritten Floodplain Management Ordinance was adopted. This
included three higher floodplain management standards involving critical facilities, the elevation of
utilities, and sea level rise design flood elevation (i.e., enhanced freeboard). The menu of higher
floodplain management standards prepared at that time included six additional standards for future
consideration, including Compensatory Flood Storage.

Compensatory Flood Storage was identified by the Coastal Hazards Adaptation Team (CHAT) as a
priority to pursue for the 2027 Town Meeting, which led to the subsequent discussions with the
Planning Board.

While the continued adoption of higher floodplain management standards will make Hampton safer
and more resilient to increasing flooding events, such actions will have the added benefit of
enhancing the Town’s profile when it becomes eligible to apply for participation in the FEMA
Community Rating System (CRS).

Mr. Snetsky asked if the language concerning deed restrictions had been incorporated. Dr. Ritt
stated that yes, after legal review, that language had been incorporated into the wording ordinance.

Ms. Mullen asked if this would only apply to larger projects? Dr. Ritt discussed types of projects
that could trip the ordinance:
    • Yard or lawn regrading for drainage
    • Driveways and parking built on base materials
    • Sheds, garages, and utility pads on filled grade
    • Raised or mounded septic systems
    • Additions and rebuilds on a filled pad
    • Raised slabs on beach-corridor redevelopment
    • Road raising, culvert approaches, seawall backfill

Dr. Ritt reviewed Hydraulic Reach for the Board:
A discrete, hydraulically connected segment of a tidal wetland, salt marsh, or coastal water system,
including tidal rivers and their tributary creeks, tidal channels, and coastal lagoons, within which
tidal flow, storage, and exchange are functionally interrelated such that a change in flood storage
capacity at any point within the segment measurably affects water surface elevations, tidal prism, or
flow conveyance throughout that segment. A hydraulic reach is bounded by physical or hydraulic
controls, such as a tidal inlet, culvert, bridge, causeway, or other restriction, or a natural divide in
tidal influence, that limit or alter the propagation of tidal flow and storage effects beyond that
boundary.

3. Amend Article 2 - Zoning Map, to add a new Section 2.4 regarding Pervious Hardscapes. This new
   section primarily relocates existing standards from Article 1 - General Provisions, Section 1.5
   Definitions (under “Impervious Surface” and “Pervious Surface”), but also introduces a statement of
   purpose and intent, and includes modest changes to standards involving decks and pervious patios. The
   relocated and enhanced language is intended to offer greater clarity and predictability in determining
   what qualifies as a pervious surface or an impervious surface.

Mr. Bachand and Ms. Hagan (Conservation Coordinator) reviewed the amendment.

Purpose: To relocate and enhance existing ordinance language to offer greater clarity and
predictability in determining what qualifies as a pervious surface or an impervious surface.

What is Proposed: The existing standards have been removed from the definitions and relocated to
a new and more appropriate location (new Section 2.4.). A revised definition of “impervious
surface” consolidates existing examples, without changing the intent, by adding the following
language: “…compacted gravel or crushed stone, and any hardscape that is not designed in
accordance with zoning requirements and industry standards for pervious hardscapes.”

A revised definition of “pervious surface” adds properly “designed” (not just installed and
maintained) for pervious hardscape systems, while also referencing to the new Section 2.4.

Under the new Section 2.4:
A “Purpose and Intent” statement is included. Standards under “Design, Installation, and
Maintenance” are relocated from the existing definition of pervious surface. Standards under
“Decks and Pervious Patios” are mostly relocated from the existing definitions of impervious
surface, with some exceptions:

Pervious patios and decks that are 6 feet or less above the ground and located within the
Groundwater Protection District (GPD) or the Wetland Conservation District (WCD), and are
constructed to be pervious according to the standards, shall only be calculated as pervious up to a
maximum of 160 square feet, any portion exceeding 160 square feet shall be calculated as
impervious surface.

This language introduces the WCD, it is no longer limited to the GPD.

The Building Inspector, or designee, may choose to inspect a pervious hardscape system to deem it
impervious should it be determined that the system has not been maintained or has been
compromised as to no longer infiltrate water effectively.

4. Amend Article 4 - Overlay Districts, Section 4.1 - Wetlands Conservation District by amending Section
   4.1.3. Allowable Uses through modifications to Section 4.1.3.A.8 (a, b, and c) regarding the replacement
   or repair of an existing structure. The amendments are intended to improve the existing vague language,
   while adding that the provision applies to Town Wetland Permits that are not more than five years old, or
   if greater than five years old, are supported by a wetland delineation not more than five years old
   showing the wetlands in the same location or no closer to the proposed work.

Purpose: To improve existing vague procedural language about the repair and replacement of
existing structures, while further outlining when a new Wetlands Permit will (or will not) become
required.

Background: This section of the WCD Ordinance has been a source of confusion and has led to
occasional conflicts about whether a Town Wetlands Permit is required.

When is a Town Wetlands Permit NOT Required:
If the structure has an approved Town Wetlands Permit that aligns with all aspects of the proposed
replacement or repair.

If a Town Building Permit (if one was required) was received, with construction done in
compliance with all applicable regulatory requirements at the time of the original application.

The Town Wetlands Permit is not more than five years old
OR
If the permit is greater than five years old is supported by a wetland delineation not more than five
years old showing the wetlands in the same location or no closer to the proposed work.

There is no increase in, or encroachment of, temporary and/or permanent impacts beyond what was
originally approved.

The work is in compliance with Town ordinances and regulations in place at the time of
replacement or repair.

There is discretion to require a new Town Wetlands Permit:

A new Town Wetlands Permit can be required if the Building Inspector finds inconsistency with the
purposes of the WCD Ordinance.

5. Amend Article 7 - Procedures, Section 7.2 Construction Provisions to amend Section 7.2.5 pertaining to
   Commercial Facilities. Specifically, the language added to Section 7.2.5.1.2 clarifies that outdoor pop-up
   retail merchants (such as craft fairs and farmers markets) may be permitted by the Building Inspector
   provided that such display area does not negatively impact the flow of traffic on the site, public access,
   or surrounding uses.

Mr. Bachand reviewed the amendment.

Purpose: To further clarify ordinance language that was adopted in 2026 for commercial facilities.

Background: Since the adoption of the amended commercial facilities language at the March 2026
Town Meeting, questions have been raised about the applicability of the section to the outdoor pop-
up retail events such as craft fairs and farmers markets proposed to be held on private property.

Current Zoning Language: An outdoor display area, used for the limited seasonal sale (60 days
or less) of Christmas trees, flowers, pumpkins, and other similar seasonal products, may be
permitted by the Building Inspector provided that such display area does not negatively impact the
flow of traffic on the site, pubic access, or surrounding uses.

Proposed Zoning Language: Adds “…as well as pop-up retail merchants (such as craft fairs and
farmers markets)” to the current language with no other changes proposed.

This section only applies in zoning districts which allow commercial (retail) uses. The Building
Inspector still needs to approve all proposals, including determining that the pop-up event will not
negatively impact the flow of traffic on site, public access, or surrounding uses.

  6. Amend Article 1 - General Provisions, Section 1.5 Definitions to delete the existing definition of
     “Frontage” and add new definitions of “Contiguous” and “Frontage”. The new definitions are
     collectively intended to clarify how lot frontage is measured, including for corner lots.

  Mr. Bachand reviewed the amendment.

  Purpose: To clarify and codify how lot frontage is measured.

  Background: The current definition of frontage is outdated (last amended in 1996) and is unclear
  as to how this should be measured for corner lots.

  New Definitions:
  Frontage
     • Linear distance along the front lot line;
     • Adjoins a Class V or better highway;
     • Between the points of intersection with the side lot lines;
     • Corner lot frontage is the total length along the roadway which is the legal address of the
        subject property.
  Contiguous:
     • A continuous unbroken line that forms a single connected path, regardless of it geometric
        shape.
