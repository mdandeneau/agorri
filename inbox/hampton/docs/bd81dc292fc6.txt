      2. Default Budget Workshop
  Chair Galanis: The default budget process is interesting, informative, also confusing; the Budget
  Committee develops the default budget and it is calculated according to state law; starting with the
  most recently approved operating budget (the 2025 budget); what is included are only contractual
  obligations approved by voters; what is not in the default budget are one-time expenditures from
  the prior budget, eliminated positions; open positions that legally exist (Police Part-time) even if
  unfilled stay in the default. The Finance Director will attend the budget discussion meetings to
  answer any questions. The School Board (SAU 90), not the Budget Committee, is responsible for
  the SAU administrative and default budgets and once the budgets are presented at the public
  hearing, the budgets cannot be changed.
  Sel. Beriont: The School Board must prepare four different budgets: a proposed budget, a default
  budget, a budget if HB 1300 passes, and a budget if HB 1300 does not pass; that requires a lot of
  extra work and planning.
  Chair Galanis: Ms. Curtis has already calculated what a 6% cap would look like and has identified
  possible areas for cuts if the 6% cap is approved.
  Ms. Rega: The ballot language is unclear; it appears the 6% cap includes Winnacunnet and SAU
  90’s administrations.
  Discussion. No one knows how the 6% will be managed across multiple communities if one town
  votes yes and another votes no; the new default-budget process does not make things easier for the
  Select Board, the Select Board still has to review and forward the proposed budget, and the Budget
  Committee develops the default budget and holds the public hearing; overall, the new system
  creates more work for everyone, not less.

         5. Budget Process and Schedule Hampton Municipal Budget Committee 2026/2027 Schedule
     Chair Galanis: The Select Board has begun their review of each department’s budget; a draft
     proposal of the budget is on the website Draft-2027-Budget-Proposal. The budget review will
     start once the Select Board is finished and sends the proposed budget to us. The schedule for
     reviewing the budget is as follows: November 17th is the budget presentation of the Tax Collector,
     Town Clerk, and Librarian; December 1st Public Works; December 3rd Police and Fire; if time
     allows, other departments will also be covered during those two meetings, December 8th final
     review. December 10th SAU 90’s budget; December 15th default budget; January 5th default budget
     and any warrant articles; January 12th Warrant Articles; January 14th Public hearing, snow date is
     January 19th, January 30th is the Deliberative session. SAU 90’s Deliberative Session is to be
     determined. In February, the committee will receive the Hampton Beach Village District operating
     budget. Budget books are expected on November 6th or 9th.
     Discussion. Only contractual items that are voter-approved are included in the default budget; for
     non-contractual items the town must find the money elsewhere to cover the costs; concern that the
     default budget will be in operatable to operate; it is enticing to some residents to support the default
     because it appears to reduce taxes; the budget process has been hampered in such a way that it will
     negatively impact town services; multiple years of approving default budgets will strain staffing,
     reduce service levels, and make it harder for departments to function, especially with reduced state
     and federal funding; despite that town employees consistently work to maintain high-quality
     services; delaying necessary investment only leads to bigger, more expensive problems later. The
     purpose of adding the full-time positions of the Grant Administrator and Public Information
     Officer’s is to bring in more revenue to the town to offset the property tax rate.
