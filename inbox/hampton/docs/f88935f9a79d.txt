Chair Galanis: The committee’s responsibilities are to take a thoughtful look at the proposed 2027
Operating Budget, understand the assumptions behind the numbers, make adjustments as
warranted, that the resources and anticipated revenues are carefully considered; under RSA 32 the
committee is responsible for preparing the operating budget that is presented to the voters.
 II.      Appointments [-1:16]
       1. Jamie Sullivan, Town Manager and Kristi Pulliam, Finance Director - 2027 Budget
          Overview
TM Sullivan: The budget review process begins early summer with the department heads,
reviewing what their needs are; the Select Board gave guidance to develop a budget that is under
Municipal Cost Index (MCI), similar to the Consumer Price Index (CPI); the MCI measures what
it costs to provide municipal services, the MCI in July was 4.91 % and 6% in September. The
proposed budget submitted by Select Board is $39,218,620. The default budget numbers are not
final until the Committee approves them. Dir. Pulliam: The budget submitted by Select Board is
$39,218,620; representing an increase of 3.89% or $1,470,052 over the 2026 budget; the starting
point for the 2027 Default Budget is estimated to be $37,593,458; the September CPI-U is 3.8%
and the MCI is 6%; explained the proposed budget breakdown section by section; wages and
employee benefits are the largest percentage of the budget.
       2. Cheryl Hildreth, Town Clerk -2027 Town Clerk Budget [-1:09]
TC Hildreth: Very little changes from last year; staff development (Town Clerk and Deputy
certification), office supplies (dog licenses, resident and transfer station decals, paper, envelopes,
copier supplies), contractual wages, the office is at full staff.
TC Hildreth answered the questions from the members about the budget cost increases; 12,225
registered voters, 9,033 ballots were cast in the General Election; election costs have increased
(staffing, poll pads, new voting machines).
       3. Vivian Considine, Tax Collector - 2027 Tax Collector Budget [-0:53]
TC Considine: The budget is small, five-line items; Deputy Clerk wages are contractual, Tax lien
instruments are lower, the lien went down by 27%; supplies and expenses are up due to the
software cost increase.

TC Hildreth: We are planning to submit potential warrant article to increase Town Clerk and Tax
Collector salaries, either combined or separately, working on the language.
       4. Amanda Reynolds Cooper - 2027 Library Budget [-0:49]
Dir. Reynolds-Cooper presented the library budget, Focusing on the staff, merit pay line has
increased, health care increased, technology costs are increasing, library part-time wages are lower
than wages at McDonalds; 7 full-time and 9 part-time employees; the library’s health care cost is
included in the town’s cost; maintenance costs for the building are increasing; answered the
questions asked by the committee.
The committee engaged in a lengthy discussion on budget decisions; how reductions affect
services going forward; certain services (resident stickers, use of the Transfer Station) should be
charged for; Operations and Maintenance factors should be considered in decisions, maintenance
issues pushed off will cost more in the end.
Presentation of the spreadsheet reflecting activity percentage changes between budgets from 2020
to the proposed 2027 to help guide the committee to focus their questions; budget reductions and
increases; balancing budget line items expenditures in a bottom-line budget; the 2026 operating
budget will come in at about 3% under budget at the end of the year.

  V.      New Business [-0:17]
Chair Galanis: Mr. Ladd will no longer serve on the committee as the HBVD representative as he
is resigning from the HBVD; will reach out to the Chairman for who will be the representative.
The committee will begin its Tuesday and Thursday’s meeting schedule, the next meeting is
December 1st, following with December 3rd, 8th, and the 15th.
Ms. St. Jeanne: SAU 90. Question 2 passed, and has two parts, first part it capped Administrative
costs at 6%, the second part capped the entirety of the tax levy for the school at the rate of inflation
plus a complicated formula. The administrative budget is less than 4%, that is not affected; for the
tax levy cap portion, the school had to cut $800,000 from the proposed $1.66 million dollar budget,
resulting in the transportation (busing service) cost will be a warrant article, cuts made to
professional development, student extracurricular activities, and services the School is not legally
bound to provide. Part of the legislation is that the SAU 90 budget is separate from the rest of the
school’s budget, two public hearings will be held for the two budgets. Question on whether an
override can occur at Deliberative Session has been asked, legal confirmation has not been
received. Collective Bargaining Agreements (CBAs) are coming up next year and to pay those
contractual obligations will take away funds from programs and services.
