III. Review of the Committee's Charge from the Select Board
The committee discussed its goals, priorities, and timeline, organized around the following
questions.
A. Is this a legitimate gap in municipal government? Members noted the need to take some of
the pressure off Hampton Beach as the town's primary economic driver and to improve the Town
Center in alignment with the Master Plan.
B. Do neighboring communities perform this function? Exeter, Dover, Portsmouth, and
Newmarket were cited as examples. Members noted differences in geography, resources, and
assets between those communities and Hampton. Members also noted historic precedent:
Hampton once had a thriving small business community and downtown.
C. What would be the value of a dedicated individual, committee, or consultant? The
committee discussed the value of having someone responsible for Hampton's economic
development, representing the interests of the town as established by Town Meeting, the Select
Board, the Planning Board, and the Master Plan, and serving as the point person for local
businesses, prospective businesses, and developers. Benefits identified:
   •    Clarity from the town on what is required to establish a small business in Hampton and
        what resources or assistance are available
   •    Shifting the tax burden away from residential taxpayers and expanding the tax base
D. What resources does the town currently have?
   •    The 2025 Town of Hampton–Plan NH Charrette and Recommendations
   •    Rockingham Planning Commission (RPC)
E. Do residents see this as a responsibility of the town? Members asked whether economic
development is a necessary function of town government and which other groups currently
perform this work. Chris Parker, Deputy City Manager of Dover, was cited as an example: he
also oversees economic development and helps communicate it as a priority to the community.
Members also reviewed the lay of the land for development activity in Hampton that occurs apart
from the Select Board and town governance.
F. Draft recommendation The committee discussed whether to recommend establishing an
economic development role or position in town government. As discussed, the role would:
   •    Ideally be filled by a candidate with prior economic development experience in New
        Hampshire or New England
   •    Be funded at a level to be determined
   •    Attract a wider variety of businesses in alignment with the Master Plan
   •    Work with current small business owners throughout Hampton to identify successes and
        challenges
   •    Interface with commercial property owners and town government on strategic
        development
   •    Improve coordination between downtown and the beach
   Challenges identified: perceptions that the town already has too many positions, questions
   about establishing timeline for evaluating performance, concern about funding the position

IV. UNH Extension Community Business Engagement (CBE) Program
The committee reviewed Hampton's pending application to the UNH Extension Community
Business Engagement (CBE) program. If accepted, the program would include:
   1.   Assessment and goal-setting
   2.   Electronic survey
   3.   Business outreach (volunteer-led interviews and focus groups)
   4.   Data analysis and report-out with actionable recommendations
   5.   Action plan development
   6.   Six-month follow-up to review progress and sustain momentum
