-   Standard Stipulations:
    1. The applicant shall work with the Conservation Coordinator to select and plant approximately 50 ft of
       plantings along the existing retaining wall.
    2. Install Wetlands Conservation District markers at the wetland buffer edge on the front corners of the
       house. The markers must be permanently affixed to a structure such as a dwelling, fence or a post
       cemented into the ground. Wetland markers can be purchased at the Hampton Planning Office.
    3. Permeable surface driveway, parking area, or patio shall be photo-documented during construction and
       submitted to the Conservation Coordinator prior to the final inspection. Permeable hardscapes shall be
       maintained as permeable. (§ 1.5)
    4. Decks 6’ high or less shall not be enclosed above. (§ 4.1.3.7 C) Decks below 6’ can be enclosed with
       anything that allows for the flow of water.
    5. The application of fertilizer, pesticides, insecticides, or herbicides is prohibited in the Wetland
       Conservation District. However, the application of limestone is permitted. (§ 4.1.4.2.5)
    6. Storage of grass clippings, yard waste, wood or snow is not permitted in the wetland or its buffer. (§
       4.1.4.2.6)
    7. All proposed plantings shall have at least 75% success after two (2) growing seasons. Any plants that do
       not survive shall be replanted or replaced with another suitable plant species.
    8. The Conservation Commission encourages the implementation of Best Management Practices for
       properties that are adjacent to or contain salt marsh. Those include:
        a. Add or enhance infiltration features such as properly installed permeable driveways to absorb and
           filter stormwater before it reaches the marsh.
        b. Add or enhance a natural barrier to keep people and pets out of the marsh while protecting sensitive
           marsh species, habitats and water quality.
        c. Relocate fire pits inland from the marsh edge or buffer to protect plants and prevent erosion.
        d. Limit access paths to or through the marsh to protect the marsh vegetation.
        e. Pick up litter in or near the marsh to keep the marsh safe wildlife.
        f.   Store watercraft (and other outdoor gear and equipment) away from the marsh to prevent damage and
             reduce erosion.
        g. Establish a “no mow” line at the wetland buffer edge to protect wildlife and water quality. This
           practice may also reduce flood risks. Landscaping rocks or a native vegetative buffer provide a
           reminder of where to stop mowing.
        h. Create rain gardens to soak up rainwater and reduce pollutants entering nearby wetlands.
        i.   For more information about these items and being a good steward of marsh land, please
             visit: https://www.des.nh.gov/water/coastal-waters/caring-for-our-marshes

           b. RCCD estimate for invasive removal
       -   There are two small isolated patches of invasive plants (Island Path parking lot and Plaice Cove entrance).
           The Rockingham County Conservation District (RCCD) went on site to estimate the costs of treatment for the
           two sites.
               o Island Path parking lot:
                         RCCD recommended hand-cutting the isolated Phragmites patch before a targeted herbicide
                            treatment in late summer 2027.
                         The site is also slated for restoration planting in spring 2027.
                         Excavation could require separate permitting. B. Hagan explained that extensive Phragmites
                            stands are difficult to control because they spread by rhizomes.
                         J. Diener asked whether the stand could be dug out. B. Hagan will ask RCCD.
               o Plaice Cove entrance:
                         A patch along the post-and-rope fence to the right of the walkway contains beach rose,
                            bittersweet, black swallowwort, and poison ivy.
                         B. Hagan proposed treating the patch and waiting six to eight weeks for dieback before Town
                            staff remove the plant material, since poison ivy makes volunteer removal difficult.
                         Clearing the patch would make room for additional dune plantings.
                         Construction at the entrance was expected to finish within about three weeks.
       -   B. Hagan: RCCD would handle herbicide permitting. The two sites are suitable for targeted treatment because
           the patches are small and isolated.
       -   Motion: J. Diener moved to authorize contracting with RCCD for invasive plant treatment at the Island Path
           parking lot and the Plaice Cove entrance for $663. B. Powell seconded. Vote: 7-0.
