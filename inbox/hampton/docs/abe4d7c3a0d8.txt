 PUBLIC HEARING FOR ZONING ORDINANCE AMENDMENTS
 1. Required Zoning Ordinance Amendments for Compliance with 2026 Changes to the New Hampshire
    Revised Statutes Annotated (RSA’s):

 Mr. Bachand explained where the 2027 Zoning Amendments can be found on the Town website. He noted
 that the amendments under consideration tonight have been shared with the Zoning Board of Adjustment and
 reviewed by the Building Inspector and the Town’s legal counsel.

 Mr. Bachand explained that the #1 a. – e. amendments were not selected by the Town, these are changes
 mandated at the State level and they are required to be placed on the ballot for the Town’s Zoning ordinance
 to be updated.
     a. Amend Article 1 - General Provisions, Section 1.5 Definitions (“Attached Accessory Dwelling Unit”
        and “Detached Accessory Dwelling Unit”) and Article 6 - Specific Use Regulations, Section 6.1
        Accessory Dwelling Units to Single-Family Dwellings (Section 6.1.5) to comply with changes in
        State law as prescribed in NH RSA 674:71-72 regarding Accessory Dwelling Units.

 Purpose: To comply with recent NH RSA (RSA 674:71-72) changes regarding Accessory Dwelling Units
 (ADU). This was brought forward under HB 1079 and went into effect on August 31, 2026.

 What Has Changed: Any legal pre-existing structures built on or before July 1, 2025, that do not conform
 with current zoning requirements for setbacks and/or lot coverage, shall be permitted to be converted into an
 ADU by right. Any structure built after July 1, 2025 and approved to be converted into an ADU will be
 vested and receive an exemption from any subsequent changes to zoning ordinances.

What Must We Change in Zoning:
Definitions – Attached ADU and Detached ADU definitions are simplified.

For Existing Structures Built On or Before to July 1, 2025: If not conforming with current dimensional
requirements for setbacks and lot coverage, the Building Inspector determines eligibility for placement of an
ADU within the existing structure, based on whether:
     The existing structure qualifies as pre-existing non-conforming structure under State law
     OR
     A prior zoning approval or determination confirmed that the structure was exempt from current setback
     and lot coverage requirements.

For Existing Structures Built After July 1, 2025: Conformance with current dimensional requirements for
setbacks and lot coverage is required, but the ADU and structure are protected under State law from future
zoning changes.

Additional Changes: The language now clarifies that an existing structure converted to an ADU does not
constitute a change of use. It is stated that if a structure is pre-existing non-conforming, it shall retain that
status.

    b. Amend Article 7 - Procedures, Section 7.2 Construction Provisions (Sections 7.2.2.4 and 7.2.2.5) and
       Article 8 - Administration and Enforcement, Section 8.1 Board of Adjustment (Section 8.1.3) to
       comply with changes in State law as prescribed in NH RSA 155-A regarding Building and Fire Code
       Provisions.

Purpose: To comply with recent NH RSA (RSA 155-A) changes regarding Building and Fire Code
Provisions brought forth as HB 244 and went into effect July 1, 2026.

What Has Changed: The change repeals building and fire code related statutes in the Planning and Zoning
Chapters of the RSAs and recodifies them in RSA 155-A. There were also new statutory amendments, which
make it clear that local ordinances, rules, or regulations that amend, override, or deviate from the State
Building Code are prohibited unless specifically permitted by statute.

What Must We Change in Zoning: All new buildings, building components, and structures shall comply with
both the State Building Code and the State Fire Code. To the extent that there is any conflict between the
State Building Code and the State Fire Code, the code creating the greater degree of life safety shall take

precedence, subject to the review provisions contained in RSA 155-A:10. In the event of local disagreement,
conflict resolution shall proceed in accordance with RSA 155-A:2, II.

The administration and local enforcement mechanism for the State Building Code shall be managed by the
Building Inspector pursuant to RSA 155-A. In accordance with RSA 155-A:3, the Zoning Board of
Adjustment (ZBA) shall act as the local Building Code Board of Appeals, with its jurisdiction strictly limited
to those local matters permitted under RSA 155-A:3-d. The ZBA’s authority to hear building code appeals is
no longer exclusive. The Board can still hear appeals within its jurisdiction per statute. All other appeals
regarding the State Building Code are directed to the State Building Code Review Board.

    c. Amend Article 1 - General Provisions, Section 1.5 Definitions (new definition of “Charter School”)
       and Article 5 - General Use Regulations, Section 5.2 Use Definitions and Requirements (Sections
       5.2.7 and 5.2.43) to comply with changes in State law as prescribed in NH RSA 194-B:3 and 674:54
       regarding Charter Schools.

Purpose: To comply with recent NH RSA (Ra 194-B:3 and 674:54) changes regarding Charter Schools
brought forth as HB 1093 and went into effect August 4, 2026.

What Has Changed: Charter Schools now have the same rights as public schools under State law, including
treatment as a governmental land use. They are provided with the same exemption from having to comply
with local zoning as other governmental land uses.

What Must We Change in Zoning: Charter Schools have been added to the same use category as public
schools, libraries, and municipal uses. The section on private schools has been amended to specifically
exclude Charter School to avoid misinterpretation.

    d. Amend Article 5 - General Use Regulations, Section 5.2 Use Definitions and Requirements (Sections
       5.2.13, 5.2.14, 5.2.15, and 5.2.16) to comply with changes in State law as prescribed in NH RSA
       674:16 and 674:81 regarding Child Care Centers.

Purpose: To comply with recent NH RSA (RSA 674:16 and 674:81) changes regarding Child Care Centers
brought forth under HB 1195 which took effect July 1, 2026.

What Has Changed: The Family Day Care / Group Day Care (residential) language previously adopted was
revised under this change. Such home-based child care must be allowed by right in the primary or an existing
accessory structure but are not allowed in both structures, it must be one or the other. Child care centers on
commercially zoned land must be allowed by right (without Site Pan review) if the licensed capacity of the
child care center is 30 children or fewer. If the licensed capacity is greater than 30 children, Site Plan review
may continue to be required. State child care program licensing rules (He-C 4002) still apply

What Must We Change in Zoning: Section 5.2 has been amended to note that the currently permitted use can
be located in the primary dwelling or an existing accessory structure, but not both. The home must still be the
primary residence of the provider.

    e. Amend Article 2 - Zoning Map, Section 2.2 Dimensional Requirements (Modify Section 2.2.9);
       Amend Article 3 - Zoning Districts, Section 3.2 Town Center District (Modify Sections 3.2.3.1,
       3.2.3.20, and 3.2.4); Amend Article 4 - Overlay Districts, Section 4.1 Wetland Conservation District
       (Modify Section 4.1.7.C.4); Amend Article 6 - Specific Use Regulations, Section 6.8 Multi-Family
       Dwellings (Modify Sections 6.8.1 and 6.8.2, and add a new Section 6.8.3). These amendments are
       set forth to comply with changes in State law as prescribed in NH RSA 674:16 and 674:80 regarding
       Multi-Family Residential Development on Commercially Zoned Land.

Purpose: To comply with recent NH RSA (RSA 674:16 and 674:80) changes regarding multi-family
development on commercially zoned land brought forth under HB 1010 and HB 1588 and effective July 1,
2026.

What Has Changed: Municipalities shall allow multi-family dwelling units (defined as 3 or more units)
where adequate infrastructure is present, on commercially-zoned land. This means all zoning districts that
allow commercial uses except if the zoning district permits industrial and manufacturing uses with potential

effects incompatible with residential use (dust, glare, vibration, air, noise, odor, or transportation impacts),
multi-family dwelling units are not required to be permitted. Municipalities cannot impose any requirements
beyond what is permitted for commercial uses. These include, but are not limited to, frontage, setbacks,
height requirements, Site Plan regulations, and provisions for adequate infrastructure. Specific dimensional
requirements cannot be different from those which apply to permitted commercial uses. Municipalities may
not require a Special Exception or Conditional Use Permit for multi-family dwellings on land in zones that
permit commercial uses, but can still require Site Plan review.

What Must We Change in Zoning: Anything dimensional that applies specifically to multi-family dwellings
has been deleted. The language from the RSA has been inserted. In the TC-H district, multi-family dwellings
above non-residential use are now permitted (with Site Plan review). A Conditional Use Permit is no longer
required. The 12-foot dwelling structure setback from the Wetlands Conservation District is deleted from the
General and Industrial Zones. The Industrial zone does not allow multi-family residential uses, but it is
underlying to the Liberty Lane Overlay District, which does allow commercial and multi-family residential
uses.

Mr. Bachand explained that a correction to the amendment does need to be made regarding the RB Zone,
because this is a substantive change, a second public hearing for this amendment will need to be held.

2. Amend Article 3 - Zoning Districts, Section 3.2 Town Center District through modifications to Section
   3.2.3 Permitted Uses in the Town Center-Historic District and Section 3.2.6 Permitted Uses in the Town
   Center-South and Town Center-North Districts. The substantive changes involve prohibiting auto dealers
   and motor vehicle repair and service stations under Section 3.2.6 (these uses are already prohibited under
   Section 3.2.3), and requiring that uses with drive-up windows (which are only permitted under Section
   3.2.6) contain their vehicle stacking within the subject property. Additional text adjustments are included
   for the purpose of clarifying and refining existing uses. These changes are intended to facilitate future
   improvements in the Town Center District, consistent with the Town of Hampton Master Plan and
   recommendations from the Plan NH Community Design Charrette.

Purpose: To facilitate future improvements in the Town Center District consistent with the Town of Hampton
Master Plan and recommendations from the Plan NH Community Design Charrette

Automotive uses such as auto dealers and motor vehicle repair and service stations would become prohibited
in the TC-N/S districts. These uses are already prohibited in the TC-H district. Legally existing uses will be
grandfathered and may continue to operate. Automotive repair/service shops and gas stations are already
prohibited on properties located within the Groundwater Protection District.

3. Amend Article 7 - Procedures, Section 7.1 Building Permits and Inspection to add a new Section 7.1.3.1
   to require notification to the Building Department of any non-residential ownership changes, use
   changes, expansions of use, changes in occupancy use, and any other changes that may impact any
   applicable building code, fire code, or life safety code even when such changes do not require permits or
   other review. The Building Inspector, Health Inspector, and Fire Chief, or their designee(s) shall have the
   right to inspect the property consistent with applicable federal, state, and local laws to ensure
   compliance. This amendment is intended to ensure that non-residential operations in the Town of
   Hampton maintain safe and sanitary conditions, which is essential to protecting the public interest.

Purpose: To ensure that non-residential operations in the Town of Hampton maintain safe and sanitary
conditions, which is essential to protecting the public interest.

This amendment was requested by the Building and Fire Department and applies only to non-residential
uses. New Section 7.1.3.1 would required the Building Department to be notified (within 10 days) of any
ownership changes, uses changes, expansions of non-residential uses, changes in occupancy, and any other
changes that may impact any applicable building code, fire code, or life safety event when such changes do
not require permits or other review. The Building Department will maintain a Changes to Non-Residential
Use form for this purpose. The Building Inspector, Health Inspector, and Fire Chief (or their designee(s))
would have the right to inspect the subject property consistent with applicable federal, state, and local laws to
ensure compliance with all applicable laws, regulations, and codes. The Building Department would issue a
Certificate of Inspection upon satisfactory completion of the inspection or review.
