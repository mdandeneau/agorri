The meeting/tour started at 9:30 a.m. at the Hampton Fire Department 140 Winnacunnet Rd.
The Committee met with Chief McMahon and Deputy Chief Nathan Denio. The discussion
initially focused on fire station maintenance needs at both the main station and the beach sub-
station. Chief McMahon spoke to his budget, noting that 88% is allocated to personnel costs,
with the remaining 12% covering all other expenses, including, but not limited to, building
maintenance, training, computer software, utilities, and fuel. He discussed the challenges of
making necessary building repairs during a default budget year. The department is currently fully
staffed, although four staff members are out for various reasons. The cost of training tuition and
class fees has increased. The overtime budget is underfunded, as the department must ensure
adequate coverage regardless of the reason for an employee’s absence, including sick leave,
vacation, military obligations, and workers’ compensation.

A question was asked about when a new ambulance will be required and the expected lifespan of
an ambulance. The cost of ambulances comes out of the EMS fund, which is funded through
insurance billing when an ambulance and its team provide services. Ambulance purchases are
staggered. The next purchase is targeted for 2027, with delivery expected in 2028. The lifespan
of an ambulance is approximately eight years.

The ladder truck that voters approved this year has been ordered and came in below expectations
in terms of cost. It is expected to be delivered in 2028.

The Committee toured the EMS training lab, where staff practice and improve their skills in
providing advanced lifesaving procedures.

The tour ended at 10:35 a.m.
