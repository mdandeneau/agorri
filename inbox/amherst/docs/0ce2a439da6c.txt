Title: Public Works Department –            Department: Public Works
Promotion of Wayne Titus to Heavy
Equipment Operator
Meeting Date: October 5, 2026               Staff Contact: Eric Slosek

BACKGROUND INFORMATION:
Wayne Titus was hired by the Department of Public Works in January 2026 as a
Laborer/Driver and has proven to be a tremendous asset to the Department. Wayne
came to Amherst with extensive municipal public works experience, including prior
service as both a Heavy Equipment Operator and a Public Works Director.

Since joining the Department, Wayne has demonstrated the experience, judgment, and
operational ability necessary to perform work beyond the typical duties of his current
Laborer/Driver classification. Most recently, Wayne has regularly operated the Town's
grader, which is classified as heavy equipment, and has also operated the excavator
when our current Heavy Equipment Operator has been unavailable.

Maintaining several qualified Heavy Equipment Operators is an important operational
goal for the Department. Additional qualified coverage provides continuity of services
during employee absences and allows the Department to undertake multiple projects
requiring specialized heavy-equipment experience at the same time.

Wayne is currently classified at Grade 4, Step 9, earning $28.49 per hour, or
$59,259.20 annually.

Staff recommends promotion to Heavy Equipment Operator, Grade 11, Step 2, at
$30.75 per hour, or $63,960.00 annually. This represents an annual wage increase of
$4,700.80.

Recent employee resignations, together with the recently approved reclassification plan
for the Mechanic position, provide sufficient capacity within the existing DPW wage
budget to accommodate this promotion without increasing currently budgeted wage
expenditures.

BUDGET IMPACT:
(Include general ledger account numbers)
Fully funded in FY27 budget.

POLICY IMPLICATIONS:


                                                                                         Page 4 of 17
DEPARTMENT HEAD RECOMMENDATION:
Reccommend that the Board of Selectmen approve the promotion of Wayne Titus from
Laborer/Driver to Heavy Equipment Operator at Grade 11, Step 2, $30.75 per hour
($63,960.00 annually), effective retroactively to August 31, 2026.

SUGGESTED MOTION:
Motion to approve the promotion of Wayne Titus to the position of Heavy Equipment
Operator at Grade 11, Step 2, at a rate of $30.75 per hour, or $63,960.00 annually,
effective retroactively to August 31, 2026.

TOWN ADMINISTRATOR RECOMMENDATION:
Town Administration recommends that the Board of Selectmen approve the promotion
of Wayne Titus to Heavy Equipment Operator within the Department of Public Works.
The addition of another Heavy Equipment Operator will enhance the Department’s
operational capacity and provide greater flexibility in the assignment of personnel and
equipment. This added capacity will improve the Department’s ability to respond
efficiently to road maintenance needs, storm events, emergency operations, and other
essential public works services.

Mr. Titus has demonstrated the skills and experience necessary to assume the
responsibilities of the position, and Town Administration is confident that he will
continue to be a valuable contributor to the Department in his new role.

The additional cost associated with Mr. Titus’s promotion can be fully accommodated
within the existing FY27 DPW wage budget. Recent employee resignations, together
with savings resulting from the approved reclassification of the Mechanic position,
provide sufficient budgetary capacity to fund the promotion. Accordingly, no increase in
currently budgeted wage expenditures is required.

Title: Public Works Department - Plowing   Department: Public Works
Contract Approval
Meeting Date: October 5, 2026              Staff Contact: Eric Slosek

BACKGROUND INFORMATION:
Purpose
To request Board approval to enter into a one-year agreement with Knott’s Land Care,
LLC for snow and ice management services on DPW Plow Route 5 for the 2026–2027
winter season.

Background
The Public Works Department currently has an employee out on an extended absence
who is not expected to return during the upcoming winter season. To maintain
adequate plow-route coverage and ensure continuity of winter operations, DPW
solicited a proposal from Knott’s Land Care to provide plowing and treatment services
for Route 5.

Knott’s Land Care has proposed a seasonal cost of $48,000 for the 2026–2027 winter
season. The proposal includes plowing and treatment of Route 5, with road salt
supplied by the Public Works Department. Although the proposal provides pricing for
three winter seasons, staff is seeking authorization only for the 2026–2027 season.

Cost Comparison
During the 2025–2026 winter season, Route 5 required 428 total labor/equipment
hours, consisting of:

    • 128.5 regular-time hours
    • 299.5 overtime hours
    • 428 total hours

Using actual labor expenses and the applicable FEMA equipment rates, the Town’s
estimated labor and equipment cost for Route 5 was $45,853.09, or approximately
$107.13 per hour.

Applying the same 428 hours to the proposed Knott’s contract results in an equivalent
cost of approximately $112.15 per hour. While the contracted hourly equivalent is
modestly higher than the direct labor and FEMA equipment calculation, this comparison
does not capture several additional costs associated with providing the route with Town
personnel and equipment.


                                                                                        Page 9 of 17
Most significantly, health insurance for a single employee plan is approximately $4,400
for the four-month winter period. When this expense is included, the Town’s
comparable cost increases to approximately $52,400, or $122.43 per hour, exceeding
the effective hourly cost of the proposed contract.

Additionally, diesel fuel prices are currently at historically high levels, exceeding $6.30
per gallon. These recent increases are not fully reflected in the FEMA equipment rates
used in the comparison and further increase the actual cost of operating Town
equipment.

The current market for contracted snow-plowing services also supports the
reasonableness of the proposal. In 2023, NHDOT increased its contracted plowing
rates by approximately 15 percent in an effort to better compete with municipalities
reportedly paying approximately $115 to $150 per hour for comparable driver, truck,
and equipment services. Despite those adjustments, shortages of available contracted
plowing assistance have remained an ongoing challenge.

Title: Public Works Department - Walkway Department: Public Works
Shoveling Contract
Meeting Date: October 5, 2026            Staff Contact: Eric Slosek

BACKGROUND INFORMATION:
The Public Works Department is seeking approval to enter into a new three-year
agreement with our existing winter maintenance contractor, Knott’s Land Care, LLC, for
seasonal snow and ice management of building walkways.

The proposed agreement provides walkway clearing and treatment at both Fire
Department locations, the Police Station, Library, and Town Hall. The contract runs
from November 15th through April 15th of each winter season and includes seasonal
walkway clearing, treatment, installation/removal of snow stakes, and repair of plow-
related damage. The Department will continue to supply the deicing materials used for
walkway treatment.

This service has become an emerging operational need during winter weather events.
The Public Works Department frequently does not have sufficient seasonal staffing
available to provide consistent building walkway coverage while simultaneously
maintaining highway operations. In many instances, highway personnel are required to
shovel municipal building walkways after completing their plowing assignments,
extending already lengthy storm shifts. At other times, supervisory and management
staff are required to fill the gap.

Historically, the Public Works Department has relied in part on seasonal winter
employees for this work. These positions are often filled by college students or other
temporary employees whose availability does not always align with the timing and
duration of winter storms. Contracting this work will provide more reliable coverage
while allowing highway personnel and managers to remain focused on their primary
winter maintenance responsibilities.

Knott's Walkway Shoveling Proposal

2027–2028       $14,420
2028–2029       $14,855

The proposed increases are approximately 3% annually.

Although the first-year contract value is $14,000, the estimated net budget impact is
approximately $6,500. A substantial portion of the contract expense can be offset by
wages currently budgeted for seasonal winter assistance, which would no longer be
required upon approval of this contract. Accordingly, this initiative represents an
opportunity to redirect existing resources toward a more dependable contracted service
rather than relying on seasonal labor whose availability can be inconsistent.

Staff believes the agreement will improve winter operations by ensuring predictable
coverage of municipal building walkways, reducing the need to extend highway
employees' storm assignments, often paid for with overtime wages, and allowing
supervisory staff to remain focused on managing broader winter operations.

POLICY IMPLICATIONS:

DEPARTMENT HEAD RECOMMENDATION:
Staff recommends that the Board of Selectmen approve the three-year seasonal snow
and ice management agreement with Knott’s Land Care, LLC, beginning with the 2026–
2027 winter season.

Agreement with Knott’s Land Care, LLC, in the amount of $14,000 for the 2026–2027
winter season, $14,420 for the 2027–2028 winter season, and $14,855 for the 2028–
2029 winter season, and to authorize the Director of Public Works to execute the
agreement on behalf of the Town.

TOWN ADMINISTRATOR RECOMMENDATION:
Town Administration recommends that the Board of Selectmen approve the proposed
contract as presented.
