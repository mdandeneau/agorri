181          7.7.    Fire and EMS Department - Update On Third Ambulance Status
182
183   Fire Chief Waldron stated that the purpose of this agenda item is to review the continued need
184   for a third ambulance and seek Board approval to maintain a three-ambulance fleet with a 12-
185   year replacement cycle. The Fire and EMS Department recommends approval to support
186   response capacity during vehicle maintenance, repairs, and replacement. In May 2023, the Board
187   authorized the department to retain its 2011 Ford F-450 ambulance when a replacement entered
188   service, creating a fleet of two front-line ambulances and one reserve. The Board requested a
189   follow-up review at that time. The accompanying staff report provides that review and the basis
190   for the department’s recommendation. The Town needs to have two ambulances available at all
191   times and, thus, a third for resiliency. Call volume continues to increase. The second ambulance
192   currently goes out approximately every other day. A three ambulance will cost $110,500 per
193   year, versus $90,000 for a two ambulance fleet. However, without a third ambulance, there could
194   be approximately $27,000 worth of missed revenue in a year. A third ambulance also allows the
195   Town to honor its contract to provide services to Mont Vernon. That contract has increased from
196   $17,000 per year to $90,000 per year.
197
198   Selectman D’Angelo stated that the presentation seems to want the Board to move to a three
199   ambulance fleet as a policy instead of an experiment, to keep ambulance 3 for another two years,
200   and to go to a 12-year replacement schedule for three ambulances. The Town has always
201   replaced ambulances every six years. He asked how many times the Town has had none of its
202   three ambulances available. Captain Bowler stated that, since having three ambulances in Town,
203   the Town has not ever been without all three for any one time. Selectman D’Angelo stated that
204   he is not sure keeping A3 around until 2028 is a good idea due to its age and the lead times to
205   purchase apparatuses. Chief Waldron stated that he is confident in keeping the ambulance around
206   at this time. If the ambulance needed to be decommissioned, it could be discussed at that time
207   whether this should occur before or after the arrival of a new ambulance.
208
209   Selectman D’Angelo noted that changing the assumptions between a two and three ambulance
210   fleet changes the financials. There is no reason to believe that the Town would go to a
211   replacement schedule of less than 12 years, whether a two or three ambulance fleet. Chief
212   Waldron stated that the Town has a 32,000 mile/year fleet, whether across three or two vehicles.
213   Three vehicles allow this to be further spread out. With a two ambulance fleet, he would
214   recommend a ten year lifespan replacement.
215
216   Selectman D’Angelo stated that the amount received to deploy one of the ambulances to the high
217   school football game should cover the cost for someone to sit in the ambulance, but not the
218   transport fee. Chief Waldron stated that no one has yet been transported from a football game.
219   Selectman Grella noted the importance of having an ambulance available during school football
220   games. Chief Waldron stated that the third ambulance has been used during high heat events and
221   also large events such as the German Christmas Market. A third ambulance is a necessary tool
222   for the community.
223

      BOARD OF SELECTMEN MEETING MINUTES 2026.09.28                                                     5
224   Selectman D’Angelo stated that ten years ago there were half as many calls, but two ambulances
225   seemed appropriate. The doubling of calls could be a good reason for a third ambulance. The
226   argument that a third ambulance is needed because the other two may be on the road may not be
227   as convincing. There is time to phase in a three ambulance rotation, but he cannot be convinced
228   into keeping A3 until it is 17 years old.

253
254   Chief Waldron stated that this agenda item requests that the Board approve the purchase of a new
255   ambulance. This ambulance is expected to arrive in 2028. This request will be made regardless
256   of ambulance fleet size. The ideal lifespan per ambulance would be 12 years at the time of
257   retirement for a three ambulance fleet. This purchase will replace a 2011 ambulance, which will
258   be 17 years old at the time of replacement. This is the first ambulance purchased since the Fire

265   and the ambulance from Surgarloaf Fire Rescue. At current prices, the overall cost is $393,877.
266   The request is for approval to spend up to $415,000 on this purchase, with the difference as a
267   contingency for unforeseen expenses and price fluctuations. There is an 18 month build time for
268   this vehicle.

280          7.9.    Fire and EMS Department — Authorization to Purchase PFAS Free Personal
281                  Protective Equipment (PPE)
282
283   Fired Chief Waldron stated that in March 2026, voters approved Article 38, providing $185,000
284   to purchase PFAS-free PPE for the Fire and EMS Department. The request is for authorization to
285   make the first phase of this gear purchase. This first phase will include the purchase of 26 sets of
286   PFAS-free turnout gear (coat and pants) and 56 PFAS-free hoods. The total cost of Phase 1 is
287   $115,977.36. This pricing reflects Massachusetts state contract pricing and thus is exempt from
288   competitive bid under Town policy. This purchase will ensure every firefighter has at least one
289   set of new, PFAS-free gear. This will also eliminate all gear past its allowable 10-year lifespan
290   under nationally recognized guidelines and provide several members with a second set of PFAS-
291   free gear. The department intends to purchase additional gear and accessories in Phase 2, which
292   is anticipated before the end of the year. The intention is to have gear on a five year replacement
293   schedule.

331   4. Souhegan Cooperative School District KF Policy Amendment - The Souhegan Cooperative
332   School District has proposed changes to Policy KF, Use of School Buildings and Facilities (see
333   attached). The proposed revision would remove the Town Recreation Department’s current
334   second priority for use of school facilities. Town recreation departments, Town-affiliated
335   organizations, and groups primarily serving local youth would retain a general preference but
336   would no longer have a defined place in the scheduling order. The Superintendent or designee
337   would have greater discretion to consider factors such as space availability, scheduling needs,
338   frequency of use, the nature of the event, and an organization’s history of responsible facility
339   use. For Amherst Recreation, the change could make it less certain when school facilities will be
340   available for programs.
341
342   The School Board held its first reading of the proposal on September 17th and is accepting public
343   comments through its next scheduled meeting in October. The School Board Chair
344   acknowledged Town Administration’s communication that the Board of Selectmen would review
345   the proposal and likely provide formal comments. Town Administration is seeking Board of
346   Selectmen’s guidance on the proposed changes and the comments Town Administration should
347   submit to the School District. The Amherst School District does not plan to adopt this policy at
348   this time.
