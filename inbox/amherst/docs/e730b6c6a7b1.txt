4. Souhegan Cooperative School District KF Policy Amendment - The Souhegan
Cooperative School District has proposed changes to Policy KF, Use of School
Buildings and Facilities (see attached). The proposed revision would remove the Town
Recreation Department’s current second priority for use of school facilities. Town
recreation departments, Town-affiliated organizations, and groups primarily serving
local youth would retain a general preference but would no longer have a defined place

to consider factors such as space availability, scheduling needs, frequency of use, the

Town Administration is seeking Board of Selectmen’s guidance on the proposed

 Policy KF: Use of School Buildings and Facilities
 Original Adopted Date: 06/07/2007 | Last Revised Date: 04/02/2026 | Last Reviewed Date: 04/02/2026

It is the School Board’s desire that the citizens of our town enjoy the use of school facilities. It is the board’s intent
that such use should take place with proper regard to all applicable statutes and ordinances, and that such use should
take place with respect to the preservation of the facilities for school use. The Superintendent will establish
administrative procedures and regulations to implement this policy (see SAU 39 Facilities Use Procedure Manual).
When not in use for school purposes, school facilities may be used by community groups and organizations, as well
as other non-community-based groups, provided that an application process has been completed and is approved.
The application process will include the submission of proof of insurance.

School-related activities and organizations with direct school affiliation shall receive first consideration for the use of
school facilities. In considering all other requests, the District will generally give preference to Town Recreation
Departments, organizations with direct Town affiliation, and organizations that primarily serve the youth of the
community. These categories are intended to guide scheduling decisions and are not an absolute order of
precedence. In administering facility use, the Superintendent or designee may also consider factors such as the
nature of the event, availability of appropriate space, scheduling logistics, frequency of use, and the District’s
established relationships with organizations that have demonstrated a history of responsible facility use. No priority
category guarantees approval or exclusive access to facilities. First priority for the use of all school facilities is
granted to the schools for school activities and organizations with direct school affiliation. Second priority for the use
of school facilities is granted to the Town(s) Recreation Department. Third priority is granted to organizations with
direct Town affiliation. Fourth priority is granted to those activities and organizations that serve the youth of the
community. All other proper requests shall be granted on the basis of availability.
No person, group, or organization has any vested right to use the school facilities. Approval may be denied due to
lack of appropriate space, if a previous activity by the applicant resulted in a violation of any aspect of any School
Board policy or guidelines, or if the facility is to be used for an unlawful purpose.
Authorization for the use of school facilities shall be considered neither an endorsement nor approval of the activity,
group, organization, or the purposes they may represent.
The Board supports community use of its facilities in a manner that reflects and benefits the communities of Amherst
and Mont Vernon. In accordance with the SAU 39 Facilities Use Procedure Manual, the Board may waive rental fees
for eligible entities. Any entity that charges a participation fee and seeks a facility fee waiver must charge the same
fee to residents and students of the communities of Amherst and Mont Vernon. Compliance with this requirement
shall be a part of the facilities use approval process.

Organizations connected with and promoting recognized school functions will not incur any facility use fees but
personnel and/or equipment fees may be assessed. The School District should not incur additional costs due to the
community use of school facilities.
Whenever a community group is permitted to use a school facility, at least one district employee must be on hand,
unless the Superintendent or designee determines the school employee’s presence is not necessary.
When schools are closed because of inclement weather, the facilities will be closed for all use. The schools do not
assume the responsibility of contacting groups when such closures occur. The Superintendent or designee may waive
this requirement.

The Superintendent or designee, on the basis of this policy and its accompanying regulations, shall approve all rentals
of school facilities. The Superintendent must approve any special requests or exceptions to this policy and/or
regulations.
It shall be the responsibility of any user of a school facility to leave the school facility in a satisfactory condition. All
users of school facilities agree, as a condition of use, to pay the cost of any damages to the facilities and school
equipment other than damage caused by normal wear and tear.

BACKGROUND INFORMATION:
Elderly, Disabled, and Blind Exemption Recommendation for FY28
The purpose of this agenda item is to present the Town Assessing Consultant’s
recommendation to review and increase the elderly, disabled, and blind exemption
limits and to seek Board of Selectmen direction on placing a special warrant article on
the 2027 Town Warrant. The limits have not increased since 2025. In light of the 2026
revaluation, the proposed review would help maintain the relative value of these
exemptions for eligible residents.

The average increase of assessed value for the 2026 revaluation was 47%. Therefore,
I recommend adjusting the exemption amounts to a number close to that. The old and
recommended amounts are as follows:

Ages 65-74, was 104,120, the new amount will be 153,000.

Ages 75-79, was 156,180, the new amount will be 230,000.

Ages 80 up, was 206,870, the new amount will be 304,000.

Blind, was 50,690, the new amount will be 74,500

Disabled Exemption, was 89,050, the new amount will be 131,000.

Recommended Income Limits for the Elderly and Disabled Tax Exemptions: The Social
Security increases for 2027 is expected to be 3.5%, 2026 - 2.8%, totaling 6.3% (since
the last increase in 2025 per Article 36).

Single was $57,600, the new amount will be $61,200
Married was $77,700, the new amount will be $82,600

The purpose of this agenda item is to seek Board of Selectmen approval to award
engineering services for the Safe Streets and Roads for All (SS4A) Village Streets
Safety Improvements Project to BETA Group, Inc., and to approve the negotiated Base
Phase scope of services, design engineering fee of $294,315.33, and related

federal Local Public Agency qualifications-based selection process. The Town
subsequently negotiated the scope and fee, and the U.S. Department of Transportation

design work begins.

project at six Village-area intersections. Services include data collection and field review,
alternatives analysis, conceptual and preliminary design, environmental and historic-resource
coordination, National Environmental Policy Act (NEPA) documentation, and community
engagement and outreach. Final design, right-of-way plans, bidding, and construction services will
be addressed in future phases.

Executive Summary
Amherst Fire & EMS currently operates a three-ambulance fleet: two units in daily front-line service and one
reserve/spare unit. This document consolidates fleet age and mileage data, national replacement standards,
downtime history, event-coverage demand, and the department mechanic's assessment to support retaining the
third ambulance.
The core finding: total annual EMS call mileage (about 32,000 miles) does not change based on fleet size.
Removing the third ambulance does not reduce demand — it concentrates the same wear onto two vehicles,
shortening their service life from the current 15–17 years down to roughly 10–12 years, while removing the
operational buffer that has twice left the town with zero available ambulances.


1. Incident Volume by Town (FY26)
From July 1, 2025 to June 30, 2026, AFEMS responded to 1,224 incidents. The breakdown by town illustrates
both the regional scope of the department's coverage area and the weight of its Mont Vernon EMS contract:

Mont Vernon alone accounts for more than 1 in 9 runs — second only to Amherst itself — underscoring the
significance of the town's EMS contract with AFEMS (see Section 2). The 1,224 annual incidents also average to
about 3.35 calls/day, consistent with the 3.4 calls/day figure used throughout this report.


2. Mont Vernon EMS Contract
Amherst is under contract to provide ambulance service to the Town of Mont Vernon. Beginning in 2027, Mont
Vernon will pay $90,000 per year under this contract. For many years, this contract was billed at $17,000 per

the core capability a third ambulance protects. Losing that resiliency would directly limit the department's
capacity to honor its growing commitment to Mont Vernon.

11. Ambulance Accidents and Resulting Downtime
Beyond routine mechanical failures, a serious ambulance accident presents a distinct and more severe risk to fleet
capacity. Repairs following a significant collision can take the unit out of service for months; if the vehicle is
damaged beyond repair, replacement can take 1–2 years.
    ●   Accident repair timeline: months out of service
    ●   Accident replacement timeline: 1–2 years out of service
If the town were operating on only a single ambulance during that period, the department would have no way to
perform even routine fleet maintenance — oil changes, tires, brake jobs — without shutting down EMS response
entirely, since taking the town's only remaining ambulance out of service for that maintenance would leave zero
ambulances available.
Operating on a single ambulance for an extended period would:
    ●   Compromise the department's ability to respond to residents' medical emergencies
    ●   Require extended response times while waiting for mutual aid from Milford, Merrimack, Bedford, and
        Hollis
    ●   Compromise the department's ability to fulfill its EMS contract with the Town of Mont Vernon

    ●   Consideration of placing a second ambulance at Station 2 to strengthen operational readiness
    ●   Consideration of possible future staffing models to sustain mission-ready coverage

BACKGROUND INFORMATION:
In March 2026, voters approved Article 38, providing $185,000 to purchase PFAS-free
PPE for the Fire and EMS Department. We are requesting authorization to make the
first phase of this gear purchase. This first phase will include the purchase of 26 sets of
PFAS-free turnout gear (coat and pants) and 56 PFAS-free hoods. The total cost of
phase 1 is $115,977.36. This pricing reflects Massachusetts state contract pricing and
thus is exempt from competitive bid under Town policy.

This purchase will ensure every firefighter has at least one set of new, PFAS-free gear.
This will also eliminate all gear past its allowable 10-year lifespan under nationally
recognized guidelines and provide several members with a second set of PFAS-free
gear. The department intends to purchase additional gear and accessories in phase 2,
which is anticipated before the end of the year.

44                Land Services and Arene Berry, Recreation Director

46   Chairman Pray recused herself from this item.

48   Sam Foisie, Meridian Land Services, presented the revised plans for the future development of
49   the Buck Meadow Recreation and Conservation Area. The proposed project includes the
50   construction of two full-size athletic fields, four pickleball courts, a basketball court, associated
51   parking and access/egress improvements, and ADA-compliant pathways around the perimeter of
52   the proposed improvements. The revised plans incorporate the major site features and design
53   elements necessary to advance the project for formal review and approval by the New Hampshire
54   Department of Environmental Services (NHDES) and New Hampshire Department of
55   Transportation (NHDOT). The plan shows a total of 210 parking spaces, with 68 paved and 64
56   unpaved, and 78 overflow grass parking spaces. The fields will not be lit. Additional design
57   details related to landscaping, screening, and sound mitigation are still being developed and will
58   be incorporated into the plans at a later stage. These elements will take into consideration the
59   questions, concerns, and input received from abutters as the project continues through the design
60   and permitting process. The purpose of the presentation is to provide the Board and public with
61   an opportunity to review and provide input on the current design and for the Board to consider
62   approval of the plans so the Town may proceed with formal submissions to NH Department of
63   Environmental Services (NHDES) and NH Department of Transportation (NHDOT). Following
64   receipt of the necessary state approvals, the Town will be positioned to submit its formal
65   application for Land and Water Conservation Fund (LWCF) grant funding to support the project.
66
67   In response to a question from Selectman Dokmo, Mr. Foisie stated that he believes NH DOT
68   will want to access from the site onto Route 101A to remain as a right turn only. Arene Berry,
69   Recreation Director, stated that if the access way becomes backed up during tournaments, there
70   could be a police detail.
71
72   Selectman Coughlin asked about the emergency access area. Mr. Foisie explained that there will
73   be a gate internal to the site at the emergency access way. There is already a gate in place at
74   Stearns Road. There will also be gates to block the overflow parking area when it is not in use, in
75   hopes of preserving the grass.

78   condos and if there will be visible standing water. Mr. Foisie stated that these are all designed as
79   infiltration basins. This plan complies with the Town ordinances to the maximum extent
80   possible. The detention area is approximately 120’ away from the existing septic system for the
81   condos.
