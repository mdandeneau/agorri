36      •   CASE #: PZ20063-091526 – Kevin and Claudine Curran (Owners), Ken Clinton –
37          Arago Land Consultants (Applicant), Red Gate Lane, PIN #: 008- 094-000.
38          Conditional Use Permit. To depict the requirement improvements (temporary and
39          permanent) to replace the failed bridge within the Red Gate Lane right of way. Zoned
40          Residential/Rural.
41
42   Ken Clinton, acting as agent for Kevin and Claudine Curran, explained that two weeks ago, a
43   Conditional Use Permit (CUP) application was submitted to the Planning Board. A site walk was
44   then held. The request is for the Commission’s support for the CUP application and a DES

45   Wetlands application which will be submitted shortly. He reviewed photos of the previous
46   damage to the bridge, as well as an area of scour on the abutments. Emergency authorization
47   from DES was given to remove the structure and abutments, and stabilize the area, but not to
48   proceed with replacement. This was only to secure the area from collapsing into the brook.
49   Demolition of the bridge occurred and the area was stabilized. Erosion control measures were
50   installed. The material under the north bank appears to be rubble from where the abutment was
51   peeled back as part of the current approvals and permitting. Once all of the permitting is in place,
52   the rest of the material will be removed and cleaned up. A laydown/construction area adjacent to
53   the bridge replacement area is needed in order for tractor trailers to access the site to bring in box
54   culvert sections, which will then be placed by crane. The bridge is proposed at a 24’ width due to
55   a request from the Fire Department. DES plans to fast-track the project in hopes of the bridge
56   being completed before the winter. The expected lifespan of the new bridge could be
57   approximately 75 years. Within the 100’ buffer of the wetlands, there will be some of the bridge
58   area, an expanded turnout area of Red Gate Lane, and a small stormwater basin. Six or eight
59   trees may be replanted in an area that currently has some stump shavings. Some stones/boulders
60   that were previously part of the railroad abutments downstream are proposed to be repurposed to
61   help control traffic, as this is not a public road. In the southwest corner, a permanent wetland
62   impact of 188 s.f. is proposed; in the northwest corner, a permanent wetland impact of 162 s.f.;
63   and in the northeast corner, a permanent impact of 18 s.f. is proposed. This total 368 s.f. of
64   permanent wetland impacts. The temporary wetland impacts are expected to be approximately
65   550 s.f. This area will be reclaimed once construction is complete. There will be a gain in stream
66   bed function to help offset these impacts, including the flow from approximately 19’ at the top
67   end of the abutments, to as low as 12’ along the brook path, to a 32’ span and a 21’ opening at
68   the bottom. There will be infill scour protection/rip rap as part of the project. On the wing walls
69   of the inlet side, there will be rip rap for future scour protection.
70
71   There was discussion regarding seeding some of the disturbed areas to help with erosion. Ken
72   Clinton stated that doing so in the spring would be an appropriate recommendation of approval.
73   In the short-term, through the winter, wood chips are proposed to help stabilize the area.
74
75   Rob Clemens asked if the sandbags for the coffer dams will be removed post-construction. Ken
76   Clinton stated they will. Rob Clemens asked about the abutters. Ken Clinton stated that there
77   have been discussions with them, and they seem supportive of the project. Laura Taylor noted
78   that there have been sales in the area and so discussions may need to be reconsidered. Ken
79   Clinton stated that, per the RSA, notification deals with the information that the Town has on
80   file, but he will check this with the Town.
81
82   Jared Hardner stated that the project trades some buffer impact in the laydown area and
83   turnaround for improved hydraulic function and ecological connectivity of the waterway. This
84   seems appropriate. Ken Clinton noted that the laydown area shape and size have to occur for the
85   equipment needed for the project.
86
87   The Commission agreed to draft a letter recommending support of the project.
