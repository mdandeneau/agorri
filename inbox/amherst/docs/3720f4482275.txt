35      •   County Road Culvert Replacement– Fieldstone (Chad Branon)
36
37   Chuck Ritchie, P.E., Fieldstone Land Consultants, explained that the proposal is for a culvert
38   replacement application to the State for an existing 12” corrugated metal pipe under County
39   Road. This is located about 2/3 of a mile past the gravel portion of the road, where the developer
40   is upgrading the roads and conducting off-site improvements. The proposal is to replace this
41   culvert with a 24” reinforced concrete pipe in a similar location. The inlet to the pipe will be
42   shifted 10’ over and the outlet is in the same location. The length of the pipe is being reduced by
43   1’ to one from a 46’ to a 45’ linear foot pipe. Umm, The slope is currently pitched upwards by
44   1/10 of a foot. The new pipe is proposed to be installed at 0.44% slope, which would result in the

61      •   CASE #: PZ20075-101326 – 24 BR Partners, LLC (Owner and Applicant), 24 Brook
62          Road, PIN #: 010-026-000. Subdivision Application. To depict an 18-unit Planned
63          Residential Development subdivision. Zoned Northern Rural.
64      •   CASE #: PZ20076-101326 – 24 BR Partners, LLC (Owner and Applicant), 24 Brook
65          Road, PIN #: 010-026-000. Conditional Use Permit. To depict an 18-unit Planned
66          Residential Development subdivision. Zoned Northern Rural.
67
68   Sam Foisie, Meridian Land Services, explained that this property is zoned Northern Rural which
69   has minimum lot sizes of five acres and a requirement of 300’ of frontage. Instead of a
70   conventional subdivision, the applicant is seeking to construct a Planned Residential
71   Development (PRD) subdivision to consolidate the development on the 20 acres closest to Brook
72   Road instead of using the entire site. Approximately 25 years ago, this site was proposed for a
73   38-unit condominium project, with mostly two-bedroom units. Those approvals have obviously
74   lapsed. It was back before the Planning Board in 2019 under the Integrated Innovative Housing
75   Ordinance (IIHO) and the developer at that time was. granted up-to the same number of units.
76   That permit has lapsed as well. The current proposal is being submitted under the existing
77   Amherst regulations. The applicant held a conceptual meeting with the Planning Board. The
78   intention is to not have to survey the entirety of the property again and instead use the previous,
79   consultants’ data for information about the wetlands and the steep slopes. This was supplemented
80   with up-to-date LiDAR information. Test pits were performed by soil scientists and buffer
81   determinations were made by wetland scientists as well. Tere were some slight differences in the
82   wetland determinations from the previous consultants, mainly for one internal wetland area. The
83   width of this wetland was flagged by the scientists as being a bit wider than what was previously
84   found. An additional pocket of wetland was also discovered. The majority of the wetlands on the
85   site have associated 100’ buffers because they are associated with Joe English Brook. There are
86   also some finger tributaries that drain into it. There is an existing culvert that separates wetlands
87   A and D. The proposal looks to maintain the same 100’ buffer for wetland D instead of reducing
88   it to a 50’ buffer. There are some existing impacts that were not permitted, likely due to past use

 89   of the property. There has been some rip rap laid in the brook to allow for heavy equipment to
 90   cross it and some culvert pipes that cross wetlands A and D. Also, a culvert pipe that crossed
 91   wetlands F and E. The proposed design will utilize those crossings and permit them through the
 92   process. The applicant will also mitigate for the rip rap in the brook.
 93
 94   Mr. Foisie explained that a road was cut into the site for logging purposes and there is a beaver
 95   dam upstream of the property. There is an existing cabin located in between Joe English Brook
 96   and Brook Road which is proposed to be removed. This will allow that buffer to revert back to a
 97   more natural state. In terms of determining a proposed density for the property, the applicant had
 98   to conduct a yield plan. All of the proposed roads meet the curvature requirements and the slope
 99   requirements. This allows for 14 lots to be created, all with more than five acres of total net tract
100   area. Most of the lots have an average of seven acres total gross area. The density count includes
101   14 base units from the yield plan. In applying the 25% density bonus, this brings the unit count to
102   a total 17.5, which rounds to 18. An erosion control plan was also submitted. The plans show a
103   road coming in from Brook Road, crossing Joe English Brook, with a 40’ bridge proposed to
104   span the brook, without any permanent wetland impacts. The DPW has stated that they prefer
105   concrete structures. The applicant will meet with NH DES to discuss the wetland impacts that
106   occur from this. Unfortunately, part of the wetland will be impacted, as this is the only alignment
107   for the road in order to fit the house lots outside of the 100’ wetland buffers. The project also
108   impacts a small pocket wetland by approximately 300 s.f. to fit in the road and allow for usable
109   yard areas for three houses on the left hand side of the road and four houses along the right hand
110   side. At the rear of the site, the applicant plans to utilize an existing wetland crossing and
111   improve it to a reinforced concrete pipe (RCP) to allow for two lots in the rear. The applicant
112   will utilize the same wetland crossing to get to an existing well.
113
114   Mr. Foisie stated that the applicant met last week with the Fire Chief. Originally a fire cistern
115   was proposed along Brook Road, outside of the development, as there is an existing well within
116   the wetland area with good capacity. It was then proposed in an alternative location at the
117   entrance of the subdivision, but the Fire Department described that the fire cistern needs to be
118   within 800’ of the furthest house and so it is now proposed at an intersection of the two streets.
119   This has not yet been designed but will be a 30,000 gallon fire cistern made up of three 10,000
120   gallon fiberglass tanks laid down with concrete blocks. There will be a parallel parking spot for a
121   fire truck with a stanchion pipe to hook up to the cistern. The applicant is also proposing to move
122   one of the houses to the other side of the well in order to create two estate lots within the
123   subdivision that will be much larger than the rest. The applicant is proposing a pretreatment
124   system for the septics which will shrink the footprint down to about 25% of what is currently
125   shown on the plans, allowing for two of the homes to be four-bedrooms instead three. Each lot is
126   being served by community septics, but each lot will have its own well. There will not be a
127   community water system proposed.
128
129   Rob Clemens asked why a community water system is no longer being proposed, as this was
130   seen in previous design iterations for this site. Mr. Foisie explained that drilling and maintaining
131   a community water system is more expensive compared to drilling multiple individual wells that
132   are owned and maintained by individual property owners. There will be overlap of the protected

133   well radii, which is allowed by the State. The water quality has not yet been tested for PFAS.
134   Jared Hardner explained that when the Commission spoke with Pennichuck a few years ago,
135   their recommendation was that, in areas of new subdivisions, community wells should be
136   considered based on the treatment options they provide. As the landscape densifies and more
137   contaminants are introduced, this is a way to have a reliable source of clean water for new
138   developments. Rob Clemens agreed that this was discussed by the Planning Board during the
139   previous application for this site in 2019.
140
141   Mr. Foisie explained that the internal roads are currently proposed to be Town roads, though
142   there is a caveat to this, as there is a private sewer. This will need to be worked through with
143   DPW. In terms of mitigation, the rip rap from the logging access will be removed from the site.
144   The culverts are proposed to be improved to RCP pipes. Also, a PRD is required to have 40%
145   open space, and the applicant is proposing to have 79%. Doubling the open space is a tradeoff
146   for the buffer impacts and additional four units of bonus density proposed. This will be
147   permanently preserved open space either via an easement or deed restriction. There is no public
148   access anticipated to the open space easement area at this time.
149

153   The Commission discussed hearing from a consultant regarding if the proposed buffer
