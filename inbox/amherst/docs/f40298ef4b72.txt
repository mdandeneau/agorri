                                         Proclamation
                          National First Responders Day — October 28, 2026

WHEREAS, National First Responders Day is observed each year on October 28 to honor the men and women who
answer the call when residents are facing emergencies, disasters, and moments of crisis; and
WHEREAS, the members of the Amherst Police Department stand watch over our community every hour of every day,
protecting lives and property, keeping our roads safe, and serving residents with professionalism, integrity, and
compassion; and
WHEREAS, the members of Amherst Fire and EMS, including career, per-diem, and paid-on-call personnel, respond to
fires, medical emergencies, motor vehicle crashes, rescues, and hazardous conditions throughout our community, often
leaving their families, jobs, and homes at a moment’s notice to help neighbors in need; and
WHEREAS, the employees of the Amherst Department of Public Works are essential partners in public safety, clearing
roads through storms, responding to downed trees and washouts, maintaining critical infrastructure, and working
alongside police and fire personnel to keep emergency routes open and the community functioning; and
WHEREAS, the dispatchers of Amherst Communications, along with support staff and mutual aid partners who work
behind the scenes, are vital links in the chain of response that delivers help quickly and effectively; and
WHEREAS, these dedicated public servants routinely place the safety of others ahead of their own, accept physical and
emotional risks as part of their duties, and carry the weight of difficult calls long after the emergency has ended; and
WHEREAS, the families of our first responders share in that sacrifice through missed holidays, interrupted meals,
sleepless nights, and the quiet worry that accompanies every call; and
WHEREAS, the Town of Amherst is fortunate to be served by people whose courage, skill, and commitment reflect the
very best of our community;
NOW, THEREFORE, BE IT PROCLAIMED that the Amherst Board of Selectmen does hereby recognize October
28, 2026, as First Responders Day in the Town of Amherst, and extends its deepest gratitude to the members of the
Amherst Police Department, Amherst Fire and EMS, Amherst Communications, and the Amherst Department of Public
Works for their service, sacrifice, and steadfast dedication to the safety and well-being of our residents.
BE IT FURTHER PROCLAIMED that the Board encourages all residents to take a moment to thank the first
responders who serve our community, and to remember those who have given their lives in the line of duty.

Overview
The town established the Amherst Conservation Commission (ACC) in 1968
under Chapter 36-A of the New Hampshire revised statutes "for the proper
utilization and protection of the natural resources and for the protection of
watershed resources" in the Town of Amherst.


The ACC manages ~3,000 acres of natural open space (13% of Amherst).


                                                                                Page 4 of 76
Page 5 of 76
Staffing
ACC has no paid staff – most work is performed by volunteers. Outside of regular
meetings, each commissioner and alternate commissioner dedicates time to
specific activities such as participation in the planning board, property acquisition,
building/maintaining trails and infrastructure, supervising forestry and grasslands
management (including habitat enhancement and invasive species control),
communications, and finance/administration.
The ACC relies on the Office of Community Development and DPW to support some
functions and can draw upon the expertise of the Nashua Regional Planning
Commission for support with GIS.
ACC hires specialized contractors for forestry, invasive species management, and
analysis.
                                                                                         Page 6 of 76
Funding Sources
The ACC is supported through several revenue sources:
•     An annual allocation from the Town Budget ($25,650 in the 2027 fiscal year)
•     Payments of the Land Use Change Tax (LUCT) when undeveloped private
      properties are developed for residential or commercial use
•     Profits from forestry on town land
•     Grants and donations
•     Warrant articles for special purposes such as acquisition of land

Current Conditions
• Maintaining Amherst’s water resources and preserving the semi-rural nature of the town are the highest
  priorities of town residents.
• Significant changes may be occurring to the local environment due to development, invasive species, climate
  change, and other pressures.
• We have incomplete knowledge needed for management. We must conduct more analysis where feasible,
  manage uncertainty, and approach our work with a certain amount of experimentation.
• The timelines for observing and measuring results from our actions can be years or decades long, so we need
  to be innovative and flexible in how we assess progress over the shorter-term.
• ACC must coordinate and collaborate with other town entities to achieve our goals.
• Conservation of natural resources and biodiversity requires management on public and private lands
  throughout Amherst.

Staffing Needs – Patrol Division
  The demands on a patrol officer have steadily increased. Calls for
  service are more complex, community expectations are higher,
  and administrative requirements such as documentation, training,
  and compliance continue to expand.
  For operational efficiency, it is imperative that we evaluate our
  current staffing levels and determine whether they remain
  appropriate for the demands of both today and the future. Meeting
  New Hampshire and national standards for officer-to-population
  ratios is not only a matter of workload balance, but also of officer
  wellness, retention, and the department’s ability to maintain high
  levels of service to the community


                                                                         Page 37 of 76
Staffing Standards

There is no single, universally adopted standard for the
number of police officers per 1,000 residents in the United
States, but the ratio is often used as a benchmark when
evaluating staffing levels. Nationally, the Bureau of Justice
Statistics has historically reported an average of 2.1 to 2.4
sworn officers per 1,000 residents for communities of
similar size, though figures vary depending on population
density, crime rates, and community expectations.


New Hampshire staffing levels vary town to town. Recent
analyses — including staffing studies conducted in Dover
and other municipalities — suggest that the practical average
in New Hampshire today trends close to 2 officers per 1,000
residents, aligning with national norms.
