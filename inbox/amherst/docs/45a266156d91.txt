27   Red Gate Lane Bridge Update – Ken Clinton
28
29   Ken Clinton, acting as agent for Kevin and Claudine Curran, explained that this discussion is a
30   precursor to a Conditional Use Permit (CUP) application that has been filed with the Planning
31   Board. This came before the Commission on August 12th in an informal sense, for the
32   Commission to hear the landowner's standpoint. The emergency authorization from DES to
33   perform the demolition work of the bridge came in that same day. The limits of the emergency
34   work are to make sure that the bridge and abutments do not fall into the brook, therefore causing
35   more environmental harm than necessary. The bridge deck had been removed, but the three
36   beams, especially the one that splintered, were all in place to help make sure that the abutments
37   were stable until the equipment was in place to remove everything. The coordination of this
38   activity has been ongoing. Chappell Farms Logging was hired to handle the tree removal that
39   was necessary for the bridge demo and for access from Route 101 through Red Gate Lane. The
40   trees are so close together in some sections that a box truck cannot get through. Trees were
41   removed for the demo per the emergency authorization, for access from Route 101 to the site,
42   and for a lay down area to allow for a place to park, materials, and a staging area.

44   Ken Clinton stated that he had a meeting with department heads last week to describe the project.
45   The original bridge proposed had a total width of about 12’. It was felt this may be too narrow
46   and 16’ was considered instead, which would allow for a 14’ passing open lane. In conversations
47   with the department heads, particularly the Fire Chief, they were seeking to meet National Fire
48   Protection Association (NFPA) standards. This requires a 20’ unobstructed lane and his clients
49   were asked to consider this. This has two ramifications. One is permitting, particularly at the
50   DES level, and a second being cost. While the recent bridge that failed was inadequate on most,
51   if not all, levels, the necessity for a bridge with a 20’ wide unobstructed travel lane is entirely
52   due to safety. There is no desire to develop the property to any extent at this time. The Town,
53   particularly Town Administrator Daley, offered a letter of support for the DES application that
54   was received today. The commitment was to consider the requested width. However, it goes to
55   DES permitting as well as cost. The proposal is, at minimum, greater than what was there, both
56   in width and carrying capacity/truck weight limit. He is not in a position to say that his clients
57   will pursue the full width that the Town would like but that they are considering it as an option.
58
59   Ken Clinton stated that he has a meeting with the DES Wetlands Bureau tomorrow. The team
60   will discuss what is reasonable and what the Town’s request is. The proposal is for three 2’ wide
61   span sections that come in 4’ bits. The Town’s proposal would lead to additional sections of
62   width required to meet the NFPA standard. The deck had been removed already. The beams are
63   still there. That demo work is scheduled to be completed tomorrow and Friday, just prior to the
64   deadline that was established by the emergency authorization of the 11th. The hope is to complete
65   the work by Thanksgiving Day.
66
67   In terms of the complaint that was received, Ken Clinton explained that he offered to look at this
68   on site. He has not received anything to do with the complaint in terms of complete written
69   documentation, pictures, or video. He has also not received anything about the DES response to a
70   complaint. He met with Steve Lutz for a brief meeting on site. He also has more than one trail
71   camera on the site. There are people who have ignored the caution tape, barricades, and jersey
72   barriers, which is shocking as this could be a dangerous site for many and it will become more
73   dangerous during construction. Additional site controls like construction fences will need to be
74   implemented. They are doing everything possible to keep the site safe aside from having
75   someone posted there 24/7. He asked that the Commission treat the recently submitted CUP as
76   current and active in order to hold a site walk prior to the next meeting on the 23rd. There can
77   then be more substantive discussion about why there will be certain permanent and temporary
78   impacts within the buffer. The lay down area, which is absolutely necessary, is proposed to be
79   reclaimed. He will identify the square footage of impacts, erosion control locations, etc. for
80   further discussion at the next meeting. The CUP will be heard by the Planning Board on October
81   7th.
82
83   In response to a question from Rob Clemens, Ken Clinton stated that, technically, the proposal is
84   a 32’ span open box culvert. It will have wing walls associated with it. The design of the footings
85   and the wing walls will be better known after the demo is complete.

 93   Other Business
 94      • Pennichuck Watershed Update
 95
 96   Rob Clemens stated that there have been a number of development proposals through the
 97   Planning Board that deal with the Aquifer Conservation District, including the Pennichuck
 98   Watershed. Part of the requirements of the Zoning Ordinance for review of the proposals is for
 99   Pennichuck to weigh in. He had some conversations with people at Pennichuck. George Bower,
100   a Town resident, is the Chair of the Pennichuck Board. The intention is for Pennichuck to present
101   to the Commission regarding their activities with regard to the watershed, protection of the
102   watershed, and the current distribution systems for getting water back to Amherst. Members of
103   the Board of Selectmen, Planning Board, and other interested bodies or entities in Town will be
104   invited to hear the presentation. The targeted date for the presentation is October 28th.
