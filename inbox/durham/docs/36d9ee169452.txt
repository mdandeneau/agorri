3. Housing working paper (Chair Belshaw)
Purpose. A Town Council member asked this past spring for a review of the Commission’s
housing discussions. The Chair called it a “working paper review” and said that when the
Commission spends a lot of time on an issue, it makes sense to put it on paper.
Council’s limits (Gooze). Many of the paper’s ideas are outside the Council’s purview, and
there is little local money, especially for item #6. The Council can look at ADUs and transitional
housing and can bring the issue to people’s attention.
Awareness and a regional approach. Perkins-Howland said item #1, raising awareness,
matters most and that a regional approach is needed through Strafford Regional Planning.
Gooze noted a regional push for workforce housing. Selig said Michael Lehrman represents
Durham at regional meetings. Hirsch Mayer and Schwartz supported a regional group, since
members aren’t professionals in this area.
Integrated services (Schwartz). Housing should be tied to social services, employment
support and mental health services.
Town Administrator’s perspective (Selig).
   • People in need ideally stay near services, since they may lack transportation.
   • Building a facility could make Durham a “magnet” for a population it lacks the services
      and tax base to support.
   • Most local public assistance goes to people in short-term hardship, such as an elderly
      resident who can’t pay for heat or electricity, or someone who lost a job.
Budget figures (Selig). The public assistance budget is $20,000–$25,000. The town
contributed $14,000 in 2026 to organizations such as Casa, Ready Rides, Haven and Meals on
Wheels, listed on the town website. Fuel assistance comes through a State of NH program, with
temporary local help if a resident qualifies.
Other points raised.
   • Gooze distinguished transient homelessness from people hit by rising costs and said
       Durham might act as a facilitator or liaison.
   • Perkins-Howland noted there is no county commission on homelessness, so people are
       referred to CAP or Strafford Regional Planning Commission.
   • Hirsch Mayer suggested mining the Durham community, including churches and
       organizations, for people who care and can help.
   • Belshaw noted transitional housing in Northwood, and Perkins-Howland said certain
       populations (those in recovery, pregnant people) have it around the seacoast.
   •   Belshaw asked whether the town would view a transitional housing project in a rural
       section more favorably.
Open question. Perkins-Howland asked whether this is a working document or a summary and
what the Commission will do with it. The group did not resolve this beyond sending it to the
Council and the Friday updates.
