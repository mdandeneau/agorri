whether Gail Jablonski was still the town's business manager, noting that Leslie Schwartz

vouchers to landlords for "those out of college." She stated it is illegal to offer vouchers for, or
discriminate against, a certain group, and that such a program must be available to everyone. Ms.
Schwartz suggested framing it as incentives and noted that lower apartment rents generally
would benefit the target population without being exclusive to it. Chair Belshaw stated the
wording was left over from the first draft and that he would correct it. Chair Belshaw noted the
absences of Kathy and Deborah Hirsch Mayer and expressed uncertainty about having a quorum.
Mr. Selig noted that three of the five members were present and mentioned ex-officio status,
leading Chair Belshaw to conclude a quorum was present.

Chair Belshaw officially opened the meeting and stated that the meeting was not being recorded.

IV. Discussion of the working paper by Chair Belshaw summarizing housing
topic/discussions for review and comment, in preparation for forwarding the same to the
Town Council, as was requested this past spring.

Chair Belshaw asked that the meeting end no later than 4:30 PM because some members had to
leave. He distributed a second draft of a working paper titled "Basic Human Right for Shelter,"
which he hoped to present at a December Town Council meeting, noting the Council had
requested it in the spring.

Chair Belshaw read the full draft aloud. The document outlined forms of homelessness,
contributing causes such as unemployment and mental illness, and affected populations including
students, young adults, and older people. It detailed the town's role as a referral source through
Ms. Jablonski, with Mr. Selig reporting about eight to 10 persons seeking assistance per year,
representing a significant increase in need. The draft covered regional coordination with the
Community Action Partnership of Strafford County and area shelters, the role of police and
schools, and the lack of temporary shelters or rooming houses in Durham. It highlighted specific
barriers for young adults, citing a cost of $1,000 per bed for a university student, high income
requirements from landlords, and average rent costs in Strafford County. The draft also posed
questions regarding zoning, multi-generational housing, transitional housing, tax credits,
vouchers, and the Commission encouraging town advocacy at the regional and state levels.

Ms. Schwartz praised the amount of work in the draft and suggested the housing crisis might be
the main focus, with homelessness as a subset, or that the two should be woven together more
convincingly. She anticipated questions about what the phrase "hiding from authority" meant in
the context of the document. She also asked whether the phrase "one size fits all fails" implied
the Council was being asked to solve the issue.

Ms. Perkins-Howland thanked Chair Belshaw and requested clarification on the phrase "a
process in this work unique to the individual," which Ms. Schwartz suggested changing to "on a
case-by-case basis." Ms. Perkins-Howland objected to the phrase "modicum efforts" regarding
school professionals, stating many make huge efforts for unhoused students, and proposed
alternative wording which Chair Belshaw accepted. She also questioned a phrase linking the
$1,000 per bed cost to a college education; Chair Belshaw clarified he meant room and board
and agreed to change it. Ms. Perkins-Howland requested an electronic Word version of the
document to use track changes, and Chair Belshaw agreed to post it. Mr. Selig asked to be listed
among the membership at the end of the document.

Speaking as a Councilor, Mr. Gooze stated that most Councilors would listen but favored a much
shorter piece stating there is a housing problem and people cannot afford it. He expressed that the
town would not build a housing center or set up a process for eight to 10 people a year given its
budget, noting larger towns have more money and a larger tax base. He cited local real estate
prices to argue the town cannot control market rates and did not think the town could do more
than it currently does. Regarding Accessory Dwelling Units (ADUs), he noted the town passed

an ordinance requiring one side to be owner-occupied to avoid student rentals and allow multi-
generational living. Ms. Perkins-Howland questioned why other towns are more able to address
these issues, and Mr. Gooze reiterated they have larger tax bases and that Durham is stretched
thin. Chair Belshaw responded that his intent was to report the situation rather than demand
action from the town, leaving the Council to choose, and offered to make the paper stronger and
more readable. Mr. Gooze suggested a concise bullet-point format covering the problem, current
actions, and feasible solutions.

Ms. Schwartz suggested the document might work better as an awareness piece, explaining that
prices are market-driven and that making the Council aware could lead to considering these
issues alongside others for constructive solutions. Ms. Perkins-Howland agreed with presenting
the problem and noted that neighboring communities absorb a bigger problem, describing the
attitude of "it's their problem, not ours" as a NIMBY attitude. She recounted a successful housing
program in Rochester that initially faced neighborhood opposition but eventually gained
community support, stating she saw no reason Durham could not have something similar. Mr.
Selig noted people would likely have initial concerns and recalled a similar proposal on Mill
Road that neighbors fought and killed years ago. Mr. Gooze described himself as "more of a
NIMBY guy" and supported the questions on the second page of the draft as being presented
without judgment.

Mr. Selig discussed the wider housing backdrop, including expanded ADU opportunities, a
Planning Board analysis of downtown-adjacent properties, revisiting the conservation
subdivision ordinance, and the Council recently approving five stories in a portion of downtown.
Chair Belshaw asked to meet with Mr. Selig to include this information in the document. Mr.
Gooze noted he voted against the five-story approval due to architectural design concerns. Mr.
Selig commented that people often talk out of both sides of their mouths regarding housing,
finding prices unattainable while sellers want maximum value.

Ms. Hirsch Mayer arrived at the meeting, apologized for her lateness, and asked about
developments in Newington at the Fox Run Mall. Mr. Gooze and Ms. Schwartz noted the
residential portion was removed in favor of commercial developments. Ms. Hirsch Mayer asked
about the affordability of apartments near the Hampton Inn, which Mr. Gooze stated was
unknown. Mr. Gooze asked whether Durham's ordinances require a percentage of workforce
housing. Mr. Selig noted an incentive for 55-and-older housing but was unsure about workforce
housing. Chair Belshaw summarized the action points, stating he would convert the paper into a
shortened bullet-point document, post it for member edits, and aim for a December Town
Council meeting.

V. De-brief of the October 27 Mount Kearsage Museum Education in honor of Indigenous
Peoples Day at Durham Public Library.

The Commission debriefed the October 27 Mount Kearsage Museum education program in
honor of Indigenous Peoples Day at the Durham Public Library. Ms. Perkins-Howland found the
program good but questioned the value of continuing such events, noting attendance was mostly
older people. Chair Belshaw found it valuable but missed a human rights element, noting there
was no discussion of human rights and that New Hampshire is the only state that does not

VI. Discussion on new topics going forwards
Discussing new topics going forward, Ms. Perkins-Howland proposed the town explore buying
land to create an educational Indigenous walk honoring the Indigenous people who lived in
Durham, clarifying she meant educational recognition rather than financial payments. Chair
Belshaw suggested it as a topic for the next meeting. Ms. Hirsch Mayer cited models from
Germany and groups like Widen the Circle that focus on reparations through work and
educational reminders. Mr. Gooze suggested the historic house at Wagon Hill as a place for an
Indigenous display. Mr. Selig noted the plan there includes changing historical displays, which
currently focus on the Black experience but could be developed around the Indigenous
experience. Ms. Schwartz suggested working from the land acknowledgement to create markers.

Approval of the September 28, 2026, minutes was not taken up during the meeting.

IX. Adjourn
Chair Belshaw called for a motion to adjourn. Following the call for adjournment, an informal
post-meeting conversation took place. Mr. Gooze apologized for his critique of the housing
document, which Chair Belshaw welcomed as a positive editing experience. Chair Belshaw and
Mr. Selig agreed that Chair Belshaw should speak with Town Planner Michael Behrendt
regarding the document changes. Chair Belshaw recapped the meeting's earlier discussions for
Ms. Hirsch Mayer, noting he would reduce the draft to bullet points and present it to the Council
in December. Ms. Hirsch Mayer suggested the housing crisis could be the topic sentence and
raised concerns about the lack of Zoom access and microphone use in the room. The members
also briefly discussed the use of AI, with Ms. Hirsch Mayer favoring volunteers to build
community and Chair Belshaw noting AI's value for research. The meeting then formally
concluded with the adjournment vote.

Jay Gooze: Motion to adjourn; Second: Todd Selig
The meeting adjourned at approximately 4:51 PM.
