V. Public Comments

Katherine Ashcraft of Stephen's Way addressed the Commission regarding the proposed project
at 121 Technology Drive. Ms. Ashcraft emphasized the importance of the Oyster River for
ecosystem functions and as a drinking water supply. She expressed concern about the project's
potential impacts on wetlands, hydrology, stormwater runoff, sediment, nutrients, and salt, and
urged the Commission to closely examine any variances or construction beyond the permitted
size.

Dana Joya of Pendexter Road also spoke about the 121 Technology Drive project, stating that
the proposal seems problematic and counterintuitive. Ms. Joya noted that the town is currently
removing a dam to improve the river while potentially approving a project with waivers that
could negatively impact the drinking water. Ms. Joya further questioned the need to approve
additional parking when sufficient parking already exists on the site.

VII. 121 Technology Drive – Light Industry Buildings

Sean Smith and Bridget Souza from R.J. Kelly presented details on the stormwater management
system for the 121 Technology Drive project. Ms. Souza explained the proposed treatment train,
which directs runoff through deep sump catch basins into four ponds, consisting of three gravel
wetlands and one detention pond with a sediment forebay. The system is designed to hold up to a
100-year storm and treat the first flush of runoff. Ms. Lightbody asked about the maintenance
requirements for the system. Ms. Souza outlined the operation and maintenance plan, which
includes quarterly check-ins for the first two years and biannual checks thereafter. Town Planner
Michael Behrendt noted that the town's third-party engineering firm, Apex, is reviewing the
stormwater report and expects to provide feedback in two to three weeks. Chair Trueblood asked
if outflow from the gravel wetlands would be monitored for salinity and nutrients. Ms. Souza and
Mr. Smith explained that gravel wetlands have proven removal rates and that point-discharge
monitoring is typically only required when discharging directly into a sensitive water body,
whereas this site discharges into a vast wetland buffer.

Brendan Quigley from Gove Environmental discussed the buffer impacts associated with the 121
Technology Drive project, noting that 65 percent of the impacts are on the southern edge
associated with the gravel wetland, which serves a water quality function. Ms. Lightbody raised
concerns about the northern wetland potentially being dewatered by the grading of the parking
lot. Mr. Quigley and Mr. Smith explained that the northern wetland sits on impermeable soils
with a perched water table, meaning it is fed by surface water rather than groundwater, so cutting
the slope below it will not drain the wetland. Ms. Lightbody requested to see the test pit data to
confirm this assessment, and Mr. Smith agreed to provide the recent geotechnical findings. The
Commission noted it would make its formal recommendation to the Planning Board at the
October meeting.

VIII. 2 Sunnyside Drive – Erosion Control within Buffer

Homeowner Derek Hubbard and Mark West, a wetland scientist, presented a plan to address
severe erosion along a steep slope next to Mr. Hubbard's driveway on Sunnyside Drive. They
explained that runoff from the road is washing out the slope and proposed regrading the area to a
gentler grade, adding loam, and planting native shrubs and an erosion control seed mix. A portion
of the work falls within the 75-foot wetland buffer. Mr. West argued that the project represents a
net increase in protection for the buffer as it will stabilize the soil and manage runoff before it
reaches the wetland.

Ms. Lightbody asked if the applicant would consider reducing the mowed lawn area in the buffer.
Mr. Hubbard explained that the area consists mostly of roots and woodchips rather than actively
mowed lawn, and he avoids cutting near the wetlands to protect them. The Commission reached
a consensus that the project qualifies as a permitted non-structural conservation activity to
stabilize erosion. Planner Behrendt stated he would administratively approve the activity based
on the Commission's consensus.

IX. Sweet Trail Rest Area

Joanne Glode, Erin Norton, and Kylie Thomason from The Nature Conservancy presented a
Permitted Use B application to create an observation area off the Sweet Trail at Lubberland
Creek Preserve. The proposal includes a 10-foot radius area with a picnic table and an
interpretive panel overlooking a beaver pond. The goal is to make the first half mile of the trail
more accessible for wheelchairs and strollers by removing rocks and creating a firm gravel
surface.

Ms. Lightbody asked why the resting area was not located on the already-disturbed Old
Stagecoach Road. Ms. Norton explained that the old road sits lower and would require
significantly more vegetation pruning to achieve a view of the wetland. Mr. Nachilly and Vice
Chair Slepian expressed strong support for the project, noting the public demand for resting
benches. Chair Trueblood asked how trash from the picnic table would be managed. The
applicants assured the Commission that they regularly monitor the preserve and that walk-in sites
typically experience less littering than roadside sites.

X. Churchill Rink and Community Center Renovation Project.
Bill Page, Ice Rink Manager, Chris Rice from TF Moran, and Rachel Gasowski, Recreation
Director, presented a conceptual design to renovate and expand the town-owned Churchill Rink.
The plan utilizes the existing A-frame structure but adds expansions to the front and back to
include a new lobby, bathrooms, lace-up rooms, team locker rooms, a dedicated Zamboni room,
and a multi-purpose community room. The ice surface would be slightly shortened to
accommodate bleachers.

Mr. Rice discussed the environmental constraints, noting that the building expansion will
encroach further into the 75-foot wetland buffer. To minimize impacts, the applicants propose
building retaining walls rather than grading out into the buffer. Chair Trueblood asked how roof
runoff would be handled to protect the buffer. Mr. Rice stated they intend to capture the runoff
and route it to underground chamber systems or a stabilized outfall to ensure it does not sheet
flow down the slope into the wetland. Mr. Nachilly asked about integrating the existing trail
system across the street and potentially adding a pump track. Ms. Gasowski noted that relocating
the playground across the street will help activate those trails. The Commission expressed
general support for the concept, noting no immediate issues regarding the buffer, provided a
robust stormwater management plan is presented during the formal design phase.
