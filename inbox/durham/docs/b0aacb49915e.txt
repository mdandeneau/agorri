 9   VI. Public Comments
10   During the time for public comments, Chair Brickner-Wood noted there was no one present in
11   the room and the meeting was no longer broadcast live. Mr. Janosz asked why the meeting was
12   not live, and Chair Brickner-Wood and Carden Welsh explained that the change was a budget-
13   saving measure due to staffing and costs.
14   VII. New Town Planner. Introduction to Maddie Harris, new Durham Town Planner and
15   staff for the Historic District/Heritage Commission. HDC/HC
16   Chair Brickner-Wood officially welcomed Maddie Harris as the new Town Planner and
17   presented her with gifts. Ms. Harris shared her background in community planning in New
18   Hampshire and answered questions from Mr. Welsh and Aaron Wolfson-Slepian about her
19   experience and expectations. The Commission then bid farewell to Mr. Behrendt, as this was his
20   final meeting after 14 and a half years of service. Members including Mr. Welsh, Ms. LaRocca,
21   and Chair Brickner-Wood shared tributes and thanked Mr. Behrendt for his guidance. Mr.
22   Behrendt shared remarks on his career and his appreciation for the town of Durham.
23   XIII. Other Business
24   Ms. Hitchcock presented a video and background information regarding proposed federal
25   changes to Section 106 of the National Historic Preservation Act under Title III of the Bipartisan
26   American Affordability and Jobs Act (BAJA). She explained that the changes would make
27   community input optional rather than required. The Commission discussed contacting
28   representatives to oppose the alteration. Mr. Behrendt and Chair Brickner-Wood discussed the
29   bill's sponsors, and Ms. Hitchcock noted that tribal nations were upset by the lack of
30   consultation. Chair Brickner-Wood drafted a motion to formalize the Commission's opposition.
31
32   Carden Welsh: Motion that the Historic District/Heritage Commission oppose the
33   alteration of the Section 106 process in the current bill and in any future bills, executive
34   orders or policies, as it has done measurable good across the country including in Durham
35   (used for the dam), and strongly urge its representatives and senators to oppose it as well.;
36   Second: Charlotte Hitchcock; Vote: 6-0-0 — PASSED

22   IX. Project Updates. HC

23   B. Proposed Plaque for the Grange
24   Ms. Singer proposed transferring funds for a historical marker at the Grange. Mr. Behrendt
25   expressed concern about a potential shortfall for the Village Cemetery plaque, but Chair
26   Brickner-Wood suggested non-town funds could be found if needed.
27
28   Carolyn Singer: Motion that the Durham Historic District Commission/Heritage
29   Commission approve the transfer of $500 from its annual budget to the Durham Historical
30   and Cultural Resource Fund as its contribution towards the Grange historical marker.;
31   Second: Aaron Wolfson-Slepian; Vote: 6-0-0 — PASSED
32   A. Purchase of Plaque for Village Cemetery
33   Mr. Behrendt provided an update on the Village Cemetery plaque, noting that production had
34   started with an eight-week lead time. The plaque will be a vertical bronze sign on a black steel
35   post, to be installed by the Department of Public Works.
36
37   Mr. Behrendt distributed rough draft text for a proposed plaque for the Grange. Ms. Singer
38   suggested focusing more on the history and architecture of the building. Ms. Hitchcock and Ms.
39   Singer discussed the problematic history of General John Sullivan, and Chair Brickner-Wood
40   agreed he had no relation to the Grange. Members debated whether to include references to Peter
41   Murphy, the Freedom Cafe, and the building's relocation. Ms. LaRocca and Ms. Hitchcock

 1   suggested making the text shorter. Members agreed to email comments to staff, who will consult
 2   with Ms. Mackie and return with revised text in December.
 3   C. Signs for placement on private buildings
 4   Ms. Hitchcock presented mock-ups for signs for the Ballard House, owned by Mark Henderson.
 5   She noted her research showed Moses Emerson bought the property in 1751 and offered naming
 6   options, including the Emerson-Ballard House. Mr. Janosz expressed concerns about the height
 7   and readability of existing town signs. Mr. Welsh suggested fewer words to make the sign more
 8   distinctive, and Ms. Hitchcock proposed using a QR code for more information. Ms. Hitchcock
 9   will consult with Ms. Mackie and Mr. Henderson regarding the design.
10   XI. Staff Report
11   For the staff report, neither Mr. Behrendt nor Ms. Harris had anything to report to the
12   Commission.
13   X. 2027 Calendar for HDC meetings HC
14   Mr. Behrendt distributed the 2027 calendar for Commission meetings. Members agreed by
15   consensus to move the July 1 meeting to July 8 to avoid the holiday weekend.
16   XII. Report from HDC members who serve on other boards and committees
17   Mr. Welsh reported on recent Town Council meetings. He discussed the proposed rezoning of
18   Davis Court to a new residence multifamily district, the passage of an owner-occupancy
19   requirement for Accessory Dwelling Units (ADUs), the approval of five-story building heights
20   by right in a small downtown section, and the rezoning of lots along Dover Road. The
21   Commission also discussed the state legislature and the potential impact of state mandates on
22   local zoning.
23
24   Ms. LaRocca raised an inquiry regarding telecommunication equipment possibly being placed on
25   the New England Center. She praised Mr. Behrendt's response regarding the architectural
26   significance of the buildings and advocated for pushing for more state and National Register
27   listings in a non-adversarial manner. Chair Brickner-Wood suggested inviting UNH campus
28   planner Ken Weston to a future meeting to discuss the matter.
29   XIV. Adjournment - by 9:30 p.m.
30   The Commission concluded its business and prepared to adjourn.
