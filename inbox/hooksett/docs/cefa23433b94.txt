Persian Gulf War Memorial Project Proposal


Request: To install a monument for the Persian Gulf War in Veterans Park at Jacob Square.

Requestor: John H. Danforth, Post Chaplain, Hooksett Merrill-Follansbee American Legion Post
#37, on behalf of Post #37

Purpose: A dedication to the memory and honor all our residents who served in the Persian Gulf
War.

Background of the War:

The Persian Gulf War consisted of three major operations, Desert Shield, Storm and Calm. The
catalyst for the Gulf War was the invasion of Kuwait by Iraq on August 2, 1990. When UN
resolutions 660 and 661 failed to persuade the Iraqi government to withdraw, as a deterrent to
further invasions two US navy Battle Groups were deployed to the Persian Gulf and arrived on
August 8, 1990, followed by the deployment of US Air Force F-15s from the 1st Fighter Wing to Saudi
Arabia and began around the clock patrols of the borders with Iraq.

This began the buildup of military forces and other resources from the United States and the 42
Coalition nations, culminating with UN Resolution 678 of November 29, 1990. The resolution gave
Iraq a deadline of January 15, 1991, to withdraw from Kuwait and return the country to its legitimate
government. When Iraq failed to comply with Resolution 678 the air campaign and naval
bombardment was launched on January 16, 1991, thus the beginning of Operation Desert Storm.
The ground assault which would liberate Kuwait was officially launched on February 24, 1991,
while simultaneously US and Coalition forces invaded Iraq.

The subsequent advance was quicker than had been anticipated with reaching as far north in Iraq
as the Tigris and Euphrates Rivers when a cease fire was agreed upon taking effect on February 28th
and Iraq fully withdrew from Kuwait and the Kuwaiti government was returned to power.

The legacy of the war for those who served has changed from one of liberation of a country which
was overtaken and oppressed by a bully of a neighbor to one of physical and mental illnesses such
as posttraumatic stress disorder (PTSD), neurological diseases as well as various cancers related
to exposures from the oil well fires, (700 oil wells were set ablaze by the Iraqi Army upon their
retreat) as well as depleted uranium (used in ammunition greater than 20mm). Exposure to sarin
gas, which has also led to countless illnesses was delivered by various means, Scud Missiles,
released by Iraqi ground troops and unwittingly via detonation of Iraqi weapons and ammunition
dumps upon discovery by US and Coalition Forces.

Monument/Memorial Specifics:

Location: Veterans Park at Jacob Square; please see appendix A, location options. It has been
ensured the three location options which will not crowd or obscure any other memorials and will
not encroach on the property of the Hooksett Congregational Church.

Dimensions: The dimensions are the same as the Legion memorials which were installed in Head
and Martins-Ferry cemeteries. The measurements are three feet wide by four and one-half feet tall
(includes height of the base), the base measures four feet wide, one foot tall with an eight-inch top.

Proposal to Rename Lambert Park to​
Alfred J. Lambert Riverside Park at Annahooksett Falls​
Submitted by the Hooksett Heritage Commission

Summary​
 The Hooksett Heritage Commission respectfully proposes renaming “Lambert Park” to
Alfred J. Lambert Riverside Park at Annahooksett Falls. This name reflects the full
historical and cultural legacy of the site—honoring both longtime resident Alfred “Fred”
Lambert, who was instrumental in creating the park, and the Native American history of
the area, recorded in the early 1700s as Annahooksett Falls.

The Commission met with the Parks and Recreation Advisory Board on May 20,
2025. At that meeting:

   ●​ The Board approved (3-0) the installation of three historical information panels
      pending mutually agreed-upon locations.​

   ●​ The Board voted 2-1 against the proposed park name change. No specific
      reason was provided for this decision.​


We now bring this matter before the Town Council for consideration and support.


Historical Context​
This section of the Merrimack River was referred to in early 18th-century New
Hampshire Provincial Papers as Annahooksett Falls—a phonetic interpretation by
early European settlers of the original Native American name for the area. Tis name
means in Abenaki “Place of beautiful trees/near water” Extensive research by the
Heritage Commission, with consultation from representatives of the Abenaki and
Penacook tribes, confirmed the spelling “Annahooksett” as the most historically
accurate and respectful choice.

The addition of “Annahooksett Falls” to the park’s name is an opportunity for Hooksett to
recognize and honor its Indigenous heritage—the earliest known chapter in our town’s
story. Naming is a powerful way to educate, preserve memory, and show respect. This
land’s Indigenous connection should not be left in the footnotes of history, but
highlighted in the names we use and the places we protect.

At the same time, the name retains and elevates the legacy of Alfred J. “Fred”
Lambert, who led efforts in the 1970s–1980s to clean up the area and establish the

current riverside park. The name “Alfred J. Lambert Riverside Park” was historically
used at the time of the park’s original dedication and continues to be supported by the
Lambert family.


Why This Matters to Hooksett​
This proposed name reflects a complete and inclusive historical identity for the park
and enhances the town’s efforts to preserve and share local history. Benefits include:

   ●​ Educational value: Visitors will learn about the Native, industrial, and civic
      history of the land through the new signage and naming. There will be a curiosity
      that arises from visitors to the park wanting to know what this name means and
      that will lead them to learn more which connects well to our historical information
      signs that have been approved for the park by Parks And Rec. ​

   ●​ Community pride: Incorporating local heritage into public spaces increases a
      sense of belonging and connection. It is proven that when people feel connected
      to the place they choose to live it fosters a stronger sense of pride and a desire
      to take part in there community more actively as they now know more about it. ​

   ●​ Leadership: Hooksett would join a growing number of New England towns
      incorporating Indigenous history into parks and landmarks. Communities like
      Portsmouth, Exeter, Concord and more have done similar projects incorporating
      native place names in public places. ​

   ●​ No cost to taxpayers: All signage and installation expenses will be covered by
      the Heritage Commission using existing heritage funds, and a NEW beautiful
      park sign will be provided to enhance the Park area. ​

   ●​ Part of a broader vision: The renaming aligns with the Commission’s Heritage
      Trail project, which seeks to connect sites across Hooksett Village through
      storytelling and signage.​


Implementation & Next Steps

   ●​ Cost: Covered in full by the Heritage Commission.​

   ●​ Signage: Three historical markers are already in production. A new park entry
      sign would be fabricated upon approval of the name change, with designs offered
      to the Hooksett Town Council and parks And Rec before final design is decided
      upon. ​

   ●​ Collaboration: The Commission will work with the DPW and Parks and
      Recreation on installation logistics and timelines, in coordination with Rob
      Hebert and his team.​

   ●​ Installation timeline: To be determined based on DPW availability and in
      collaboration with Parks and Rec.​

   ●​ Community engagement: A re-dedication ceremony and educational rollout are
      being planned for Summer 2025.​


Request for Action​
We respectfully ask the Hooksett Town Council to:

   1.​ Approve the renaming of Lambert Park to Alfred J. Lambert Riverside Park
       at Annahooksett Falls.​

   2.​ Authorize coordination between the Heritage Commission, DPW, and Parks
       and Rec for signage installation.​


This proposal allows Hooksett to preserve and elevate its past while setting an example
of thoughtful, inclusive community storytelling. We believe Mr. Lambert, a lover of
Hooksett history, would be proud to see the park used in this way.

Thank you for your consideration.

Meeting:         Town Council - 28 May 2025
Department:      Administration
Staff Contact:   Leann McLaughlin, Project Coordinator

BACKGROUND INFORMATION:
Implementing a multi-factor authentication (MFA) policy is essential to enhance security and protect
sensitive information. As cyber threats continue to evolve, relying solely on traditional username and
password credentials is no longer sufficient. Passwords can be easily stolen, guessed, or
compromised through phishing attacks and data breaches. MFA adds an additional layer of protection
by requiring users to verify their identity through two or more factors—in this case, it would be
something they know (password) and something they have (a mobile device or security token).
The Town of Hooksett continues to enhance its cybersecurity practices. As part of the transition to a
.gov domain, all employees, elected officials, and volunteers with a town-issued email address will be
required to migrate their accounts to the new .gov platform and enable multi-factor authentication
(MFA) as part of the process.

This policy amendment has been reviewed by legal counsel, Primex, and the Management Team. It
has also been forwarded to the Unions for input; however, as of the date of this staff report, no
feedback or concerns have been received.

This update serves as an amendment to the existing Computer Policy outlined in the Personnel Plan.

5.1       Social Media Policy. This policy establishes guidelines for the Town of
          Hooksett’s social media usage that includes: Defining the town’s social media
          assets as limited public forums; creating basic expectations of conduct for the
          use of social media by town officials; establishing the process to designate
          “official” town social media; and codifying how official social media assets will

                                SOCIAL MEDIA POLICY

Adopted by the Hooksett Town Council
James Sullivan, Chair 04/28/2021
Timothy Tsantoulis, Vice Chair 04/28/2021
Andre L. Garron, Town Administrator 04/28/2021

Purpose: The Town of Hooksett has established social media sites primarily in order to
inform residents about Hooksett programs and events, to encourage dialogue between
residents about these programs and events, and to exchange information and knowledge
between residents and municipal authorities about these programs and events. The
town’s official social media accounts or sites are not intended to be traditional public
forums for the general exchange of ideas and viewpoints, but rather a limited public forum
for discussion of specific programs and events. Courts have recognized that
governmental entities, such as the Town of Hooksett, may create limited public forums
for specific purposes and limit discussion on the forum to those specific purposes. The
Town of Hooksett shall remove content and postings not consistent with the specific
purpose of the social media accounts and these Guidelines.

Summary: New and emerging online platforms are fundamentally changing the way the
world works, offering us new ways to collaborate and to engage with the public. The
Town of Hooksett encourages the use of social media to further the outcomes of the Town
and to assist us in our mission to better engage with our Community. The Town
Administrator has an overriding interest and expectation in deciding who may "speak,"
and what is "spoken," on behalf of the Town of Hooksett to our respective residents. To
ensure that communication through Town social media networks is consistent and in the
best interest of the Town, this policy provides uniform guidelines for the use of social
media.

Scope: Applies to all Town employees, volunteers, Town officers, and members of
municipal boards, committees or commissions, when acting in their capacity as a member
of such entities or as a representative of the Town, that create or contribute to blogs,
wikis, social networks, virtual worlds, or any other kind of social media on behalf of or as
a representative of the Town.

General Definitions

Social Media: Online presences that enable users to create and share content or to
participate in social networking. The Town of Hooksett considers any website, blog, or


                                                                                               Page 96 of 110
                                                                                     Agenda Item #17.7.


networked software application where publishing information to an audience is possible
to fall under its definitions and policy controls related to social media.

Social Media Network: Specific collectives of online communications channels,
websites, or applications dedicated to community-based input, interaction, content-
sharing and/or collaboration.

Social Media Account: A specific, identifiable interface on a social media network that
enables users to post information and interact with audiences using the same network via
posts or other communications, contemporaneous or not.

Public Forum: A place that has a long-standing tradition of being used for, is historically
associated with, or has been dedicated by government act to the free exercise of the right
to speech and public debate and assembly.

Limited Public Forum: A public forum created by the government voluntarily for
expressive activity that may be restricted as to subject matter or class of speaker.

1. General Details
   a. Wherever possible, the use of Social media by Town of Hooksett employees and
      elected or appointed officers will comply with all appropriate municipal policies,
      regulation, and forms of governance, including but not limited to:
              i. This policy
             ii. Town of Hooksett’s Media Relations Policy
            iii. Town Charter
            iv. Administrative Code
             v. Computer Use Policy
            vi. Personnel Plan

   b. The Town of Hooksett’s main websites (www.hooksett.org, www.hooksettfire.org,
      www.hooksettpolice.org) will be the Town’s primary and predominant internet
      presence. All other officially recognized internet presences such as those
      represented by other websites, detached blogs, software-based social media
      networks, and through designated accounts as applicable, are considered purely
      supplementary, and will strive to not exceed the purposes delimited within this
      policy.

   c. When using personal or other third-party social media accounts, town employees
      and elected or appointed officers will strive to never represent personal opinion as
      officially representing the interests of the town.

   d. Official social media accounts for individual departments, boards, commissions or
      committees may be created with approval of both the relevant administrating


                                                                                              Page 97 of 110
                                                                                     Agenda Item #17.7.


      department head (where applicable) and the Town Administrator according to the
      process described in the Administrative Procedures section of this policy.

   e. Maintenance of approved social media accounts shall be the responsibility of the
      relevant administrating department head or their designees. Websites and social
      media accounts for individual departments or boards, commissions or committees
      that are not appropriately monitored or maintained shall be discontinued by order
      of the Town Administrator. The Town Administrator and the administering
      Department Heads shall hold an active password and/or administrator access to
      all applicable officially approved social media accounts in use.

   f. The use of social media networks shall serve the following purposes:
              i. To provide information to the public;
             ii. To increase the Town government’s ability to broadcast its messages
                 to the widest, most diverse possible audience;
            iii. To disseminate time-sensitive information as quickly as possible and;
            iv. To funnel actionable municipal inquiries to appropriate municipal

   g. Quasi-judicial public bodies of the Town of Hooksett including, but not limited to,
      the Zoning Board of Adjustment and the Planning Board, shall not have official
      social media.

   a. No social media network or account will be deemed official without express written
