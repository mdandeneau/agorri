      PUBLIC INPUT
        1. Two 15-minute Public Input sessions will be allowed during each Council
           Meeting. Time will be divided equally among those wishing to speak, however,
           no person will be allowed to speak for more than 5 minutes.
        2. No person may address the council more than twice on any issue in any
           meeting. Comments must be addressed to the Chair and must not be personal
           or derogatory about any other person.
        3. Any questions must be directly related to the topic being discussed and must
           be addressed to the Chair only, who after consultation with Council and Town
           Administrator, will determine if the question can be answered at that time.
           Questions cannot be directed to an individual Councilor and must not be
           personal in nature. Issues raised during Public Input, which cannot be resolved
           or answered at that time, or which require additional discussion or research,
           will be noted by the Town Administrator who will be responsible for researching
           and responding to the comment directly during normal work hours or by
           bringing to the Council for discussion at a subsequent meeting. The Chair
           reserves the right to end questioning if the questions depart from clarification to
           deliberation.
        4. Council members may request a comment be added to New Business at a
           subsequent meeting.
        5. No one may speak during Public Input except the person acknowledged by the
           Chair. Direct questions or comments from the audience are not permitted
           during Public Input.

BACKGROUND INFORMATION:

Hooksett Town Hall – Council Chambers, 35 Main Street, Hooksett, NH. The purpose of the public hearing is
to discuss the proposed amendments to Roadway Excavation Ordinance # 00-13. This notice is per Chapter
231:132-a of the NH Revised Statutes annotated, and section 3.6 of the Hooksett Town Charter. The full texts
of the proposed ordinance amendment and the new ordinance are available on the Town Clerk web pages via
www.hooksett.org for your inspection. Questions should be directed to the Hooksett Public Works
Department at 603-668-8019.

BACKGROUND INFORMATION:
The objective of the NH Route 3A (NH 3A)/Hackett Hill Road and NH 3A/Main Street Intersections
Project is to improve safety and capacity for all users at these important intersections. The project
includes the design and construction of improvements that will make the intersections more efficient.
It will also include replacement of the existing culvert that carries NH 3A over Brickyard Brook near
the Hackett Hill intersection that has experienced some deterioration.

A project website has been set up that provides significant information regarding the project including
funding sources, and the design process. It can be found on the Town Website.

Municipally Managed project. The Town (me) is managing the project under the strict guidelines of
the NHDOT. We have to follow those guidelines in order to receive funded by the State and federal
government. Our contract with the State is on the Town website. The funding breakdown is:

        Design phase cost: 1/3 Town, 2/3 State
        ROW costs: 1/3 Town, 2/3 State
        Construction Costs 10% Town, 10% State, 80% Federal

The design process we are using requires an Engineering Study to be done, then we’ll move on to
Preliminary Design, then Final Design, then Construction.

The Town hired McFarland Johnson as our engineering consultant after a rigorous process. We used
a process called Qualification Based Selection in order to hire the most qualified engineer.
Gene McCarthy, Matt Moore and Scott Lister are here from McFarland Johnson.

We are currently in the Engineering Study phase. Under this phase we’ll do a survey of the area,
We’ll identify any natural, cultural, social historical and economic resources that may be affected.
We’ll take traffic counts and review existing traffic studies, take borings of the soils in the area, begin
to identify any ROW needs and get any other information needed to move forward with the project.
This is one public meeting of several to get input from the community to guide us on how to proceed.


                                                                                                        Page 7 of 87

Once the Engineering Study is completed and approved by the NHDOT, we’ll move onto the
Preliminary Design phase. This will consist of plans of the approved concept with drainage design,
erosion control plans, ROW acquisition plans, and other information. Once the Preliminary Plans are
approved, then the Final Design Plans and Right of Way acquisitions will be completed and
ultimately, the project will be advertised to bid.

Any ROW takings will follow the process outlined in the NHDOT ROW Manual found on their website.
The Engineering processed used and the Contract between the Town and the State can be found on
the Town’s website.

The Planning Board and the Economic Development Board have expressed their approval (both
unanimously) of the direction the project is going and the use of roundabouts in the design. In
addition, both Boards have agreed that taking the property at #464 West River Road would benefit
the project.

Gene McCarthy from McFarland Johnson will provide a presentation of the project.

Name of Department Submitting Request: Community Development

1. Please provide the wording of the proposed article.

To see if the town will vote to raise and appropriate the sum of $135,000.00 to be added
to the Town Wide Digital Mapping Systems Capital Reserve Fund previously
established. The estimated tax rate impact is $0.04.

2. Voters’ Guide Explanation.
The system currently in use by the Town of Hooksett is out of date, inaccurate, and in
need of updating. The current reserve for this project is $136,318.35 as of August 31,
2024. The total cost for the project is estimated at $537,000 and is tentatively scheduled
for Fiscal Year 2027-28. The project includes hiring a firm to conduct a flyover of the
Town utilizing LIDAR technology which will project accurate topography, aerial
photography, and the accurate location of buildings and rights of way to within 1 to 6
inches. The GIS maps are used daily by the Planner, the Town Engineer, and Code
Enforcement to address inquiries to their various offices. The maps are also used by
residents to provide information required on building permits, by developers who wish to
construct in town, and the maps aid in the creation of plans presented to the Planning
Board and Zoning Board of Adjustment.


As noted in the pictures above, the current system does not accurately reflect the
location of existing roadways or structures. The picture on the left is taken from the
current GIS system. It shows the home located in the roadway, with most of the
property encumbered by the right of way. As shown in the Google image to the right, the
home is not in the roadway, is set adequately back from the right of way, and the right of
way does not extend directly to the right of the home. The Town relies on accurate
mapping data to advise developers and guide townwide infrastructure projects.
Inaccuracies have potential to cause ownership disputes, unexpected and costly
obstacles to underground infrastructure planning, and difficulty in obtaining exact

with the existing outdated mapping data, and a comprehensive in-house update of the
Town Map will not be possible aside from the annual update of the Tax Maps provided
to the town by a third party. Those Tax Maps do not match the GIS map.

3. If this article is not passed at Town Meeting or approved by the Town Council,
what affect would this have on your department goals and programs?
Each division of the Community Development Department – Planning, Engineering, and
Building – relies heavily on the use of these particular maps in the basic performance of
their respective functions. The Department utilizes maps, aerial photography,
topographic plans, property surveys, and other planimetrics to develop infrastructure
and site development plans with a high level of precision. Current zoning, utility
infrastructure, wetlands, topography, and building footprint maps are very outdated and
need to be revised. The Department has goals to update all current planning maps
through 2027. The digital information provided by the proposed flyover would not only
correct errors in the Town’s existing maps but would also provide additional data
necessary to comprehensively update all official Town maps. Updated GIS and
mapping data will allow the Department to better aid developers, Town residents, utility
providers, NHDOT, and other State agencies in all aspects of planning, building, and
engineering.

STAFF REPORT

To:              Town Council
Title:           Halloween 2024 - October 31, 2024 from 6:00pm-8:00pm
Meeting:         Town Council - 09 Oct 2024
Department:      Administration
Staff Contact:   Geraldine Ciardelli, Executive Assistant

BACKGROUND INFORMATION:
The Town of Hooksett, like many municipalities, traditionally provides guidance for Halloween Trick-
or-Treating festivities to residents. Departments receive some of their highest volume of inquiries
every year on the holiday, pertaining to subjects such as hours, dates and standard practices. The
Town in the past has merely posted a set of Trick-or-Treating hours on the same day as Halloween.
Some departments also have periodically participated or directed community events. Deviation from
the date of Trick-or-Treating sponsored by the Town is something that's sparked controversy in the
past. Since the COVID-19 pandemic, and after consultation among the Town Administrator,
Department Heads, and other relevant employees, it was the consensus of staff to propose the
following for Halloween 2024:

       Post Halloween Trick-or Treating date/hours as normal (Thursday, October 31st from 6:00pm-
        8:00pm)
    It is recommended that residents not participating in Trick-or-Treating turn off their home's
        external lights and/or post a clear, street visible sign indicating that the residence is not
        participating.
The Town would post this information on official websites, and disseminate the message through
notification services and social media. Staff would also refer to this information for inquiries as the
standard policy for this year if approved by Town Council.

RECOMMENDATION:
Vote to adopt the proposed approach for Halloween.

SUGGESTED MOTION:
"I motion to adopt the proposed Halloween Holiday Policy for 2024."

TOWN ADMINISTRATOR'S RECOMMENDATION:
I concur with the suggested motion

153   8.1 Public Hearing for a New Other Ordinance 2024-01 - Outdoor Dining
154
155   Opened Public Hearing
156
157   G. McGregor- The Town of Hooksett endeavors to promote and encourage outdoor dining as well as
158   the prioritization of pedestrian access and safety on both public and private lots located within the
159   Town. The Town is also committed to enhancing economic development advancement for our local
160   restauranteurs. As part of that effort, the Community Development office is proposing the addition of
161   a new Other Ordinance which will put a process in place to permit outdoor dining. During the COVID-
162   19 pandemic, the State of New Hampshire granted municipalities the authority to temporarily permit,
163   with relaxed restrictions, Outdoor Dining permits. The intent at the time was to allow a means for
164   restaurants to keep their employees employed and continue to operate so that their businesses could
165   continue to be successful, while keeping within health protocols. In 2021, Governor Sununu signed an
166   amendment to RSA 178-24, which allowed restaurants serving alcohol to continue to offer service,
167   within a clearly defined area under the control of the premise, which included shared space such as a
168   sidewalk or street with the approval of local officials. Applications would need to conform to local
169   zoning requirements and written approval from town officials is required. There are many benefits
170   attached to outdoor dining. It enhances the experience for residents and visitors frequenting our local
171   restaurants. Previously, the Community Development office allowed some establishments to continue
172   to offer this amenity, subsequent to the law change in July of 2021, but no formal process has yet been
173   put in place to ensure that all applicants are supplying the same information with the same level of
174   review. Each establishment is unique and should be evaluated on its own merits, but there should be
175   consistency in the application process and requirements.
