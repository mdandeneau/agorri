STAFF REPORT

To:            Town Council
Title:         Public Hearing for a New Other Ordinance 2024-01 - Outdoor Dining (Continued
               from June 26, 2024)
Meeting:       Town Council - 28 Aug 2024
Department:    Community Development
Staff Contact: Grant McGregor, Town Planner

BACKGROUND INFORMATION:
The Town of Hooksett endeavors to promote and encourage outdoor dining as well as the
prioritization of pedestrian access and safety on both public and private lots located within the Town.
The Town is also committed to enhancing economic development advancement for our local
restauranteurs. As part of that effort, the Community Development office is proposing the addition of a
new Other Ordinance which will put a process in place to permit outdoor dining.

During the COVID-19 pandemic, the State of New Hampshire granted municipalities the authority to
temporarily permit, with relaxed restrictions, Outdoor Dining permits. The intent at the time was to
allow a means for restaurants to keep their employees employed and continue to operate so that their
businesses could continue to be successful, while keeping within health protocols.

In 2021, Governor Sununu signed an amendment to RSA 178-24, which allowed restaurants serving
alcohol to continue to offer service, within a clearly defined area under the control of the premise,
which included shared space such as a sidewalk or street with the approval of local officials.
Applications would need to conform to local zoning requirements and written approval from town
officials is required. There are many benefits attached to outdoor dining. It enhances the experience
for residents and visitors frequenting our local restaurants.

Previously, the Community Development office allowed some establishments to continue to offer this
amenity, subsequent to the law change in July of 2021, but no formal process has yet been put in
place to ensure that all applicants are supplying the same information with the same level of review.
Each establishment is unique and should be evaluated on its own merits, but there should be
consistency in the application process and requirements.

Attached to this staff report is a proposed new Other Ordinance outlining the requirements for outdoor
dining areas in Hooksett. Also included is a copy of the Outdoor Dining Guidelines that will
accompany the Application for Outdoor Dining, and a copy of the appliable RSA. The documents
have been amended to include suggested changes by the ZBA Chairman, Planning Board, and Legal
Counsel which clarified the language and removed redundant language.

It should be noted that the intent of this ordinance is to give existing food and beverage
establishments a means to seasonally expand their operations, with the appropriate life safety
inspections, and to save them expense and time. This is not intended to be a means for

establishments to avoid site plan review when one is required. All applications would be reviewed by
Town staff first to see if the proposal rose to the level where site plan review would be required. For
those establishments seeking to seasonally expand onto existing sidewalks or into available green
space without substantially increasing parking demand, this process would be a means to do that
without having to go to the expense of hiring a surveyor to amend an existing site plan. Any applicant
who wanted to create a substantial outdoor area would require site plan review by the Planning
Board. New restaurants are being encouraged to include outdoor dining as part of their original site
plan process.

A quick overview of the process is as follows. Applicants will need to submit the required
documentation to the Town Planner in Community Development. The Planner reviews the application
to ensure that site plan review is not required. The application is then forwarded to Code, Fire-
Rescue, and DPW for review. DPW will review to ensure that there are no proposed town projects or
events that would interfere with the proposed dining area. Code and Fire-Rescue review to ensure
applicable life safety measures are met. Once the departments are satisfied that the plan is feasible,
safe, and would not require Planning Board review, the application is forwarded to the Town
Administrator who will issue the seasonal license. Town Council would approve the application if the
outdoor dining were to occur on a municipally managed sidewalk or public space. The applicant is the
responsible party to notify the NH Liquor Commission of any changes in their assembly permit issued
by Hooksett Fire-Rescue. Outdoor dining would be permitted April 1 through October 15. The
ordinance clearly defines when Planning Board review is required.

The Town Council delayed review of the suggested Ordinance to receive comments from the
Planning Board on the proposed ordinance. Such comments have been received and incorporated
into the text.

FINANCIAL IMPACT:
Small revenue for the processing of the application ($50.00) and $250.00 per day for any punishable
violations of the ordinance.

POLICY IMPLICATIONS:
Enactment of this Ordinance furthers economic development goals, and provides guidance for town
departments for review of applications of this type.

SECTION 1
INTENT AND INTERPRETATION

The Town of Hooksett recognizes the importance of outdoor dining to the success and vibrancy of
its eating and drinking establishments. The purpose of this article shall be interpreted in accordance
with that intent.

SECTION 2
REQUIRED APPROVALS

The use of sidewalks or other public space for the purpose of outdoor dining service is prohibited
without prior written approval of the Town of Hooksett Town Council.
 A.   Application requirements.
      (1) Permits will be required on an annual basis and will be valid from April 1 to October
          15. Permits are not continuous and will expire on October 15 of each year. No permit will
          be granted prior to April 1.
      (2) Applications will only be accepted by businesses licensed to serve food to the public.
      (3) Applications for outdoor dining occurring on a private lot shall conform to this Ordinance,
          with the exception that the annual permit shall be issued by the Town Administrator or

           (c) Setback dimensions for sidewalk to building.
           (d) Location of the proposed outdoor dining area and its components (tables, chairs,
               barriers, etc.), including dimensions and total outdoor dining area in square feet.
               The plan should show the number and location of tables, chairs, equipment and
               accessories, along with pedestrian access points and sidewalk separation. Other
               features, such as the location of the building, ramps, hydrants, fire lanes, lighting,
               natural features, parking and relationship to the road (labeled) shall also be
               included. If areas of the parking lot are to be used for outdoor dining, barriers must
               be in place to protect the patrons from parking lot traffic. The seating area must be
               fully enclosed with the exception of the minimum forty-four-inch entrance to the
               outdoor seating area. Proposed outdoor bars shall be noted on the layout plan, as

Purpose
The Town of Hooksett recognizes the importance of outdoor dining to the success and vibrancy of
its eating and drinking establishments. The Design Guidelines provide acceptable dimensions for
dining area access, sidewalk and parking lot barriers, and the layout of outdoor service areas. The
use of durable and high-quality outdoor furnishings and materials is encouraged to increase the
safety and comfort of residents and visitors. The diagrams in this document illustrate examples of
outdoor dining design; photographs and diagrams show acceptable furnishings.

Outdoor dining enhances the public realm and motivates town and regional residents to patronize
Hooksett’s eating establishments. Restaurant and café owners who offer outdoor dining shall
comply with these guidelines, which are applicable in all zoning districts where restaurants are a
permitted use.


Background
These Outdoor Dining Design Guidelines are promulgated in accordance with Other Ordinance #
2024-1 of the Town of Hooksett’s Code of Ordinances. The Outdoor Dining Ordinance was
adopted by resolution of the Town Council on XXXX, 20XX, and may be amended from time to
time by Town Council resolution.

Elements of a Good Seating Area1

Elements of a typical outdoor dining area include barriers, planters, tables, chairs, umbrellas, menu
display, heat lamps, and an access point. If wait service is not provided in the outdoor dining area,
the eating establishment is required to supply a waste receptacle. If wait service is provided, the
restaurant operator is not permitted to place a waste receptacle in the outdoor dining area.


Barriers

   1. The barrier access point must be controlled by the restaurant establishment.
   2. If alcohol is served, outdoor dining shall be separated from public pedestrian space on the
      adjacent public sidewalk by an enclosure on all four sides with an access point that is a
      minimum of 44” wide.
   3. The perimeter of outdoor dining areas that extend more than 3 feet into the public right-of-
      way shall be enclosed by barriers that are durable, removable, and maintained in good
      condition.
   4. Minimum height of barriers shall be 30 inches and maximum height shall be 36 inches.
   5. Per 2018 Life Safety Code, Existing Places of Assembly, §13.1.1.7, existing portions of
      the structure shall not be required to be modified, provided that both the following criteria
      are met:
           a. The new construction has not diminished the fire safety features of the facility.

Barrier Design
   1. Sectional barrier (generally defined as rigid barrier segments that can be placed together
       to create a unified appearance) is a desirable solution for outdoor seating areas. The barrier
       is portable but cannot be easily shifted by patrons or pedestrians. Sectional barriers must
       be of metal (aluminum, steel, iron or similar) or of wood construction and must be of a
       dark color (either painted or stained).
   2. Rope or Chain Rails are permitted if they meet the following guidelines. The rope or
       chain must have a minimum diameter of 1 inch in order to maintain detectability by the
       visually impaired. Vertical support posts (stanchions, bollards, etc.) must be constructed
       of wood or metal (aluminum, steel, iron or similar). The stanchion base must not be a
       tripping hazard. The base must be flat and measure no more than one half (1/2) inch above
       the sidewalk or parking lot surface. No domed stanchion bases are permitted.
   3. Any Barrier must be freestanding, without any permanent or temporary attachments to the
       buildings, sidewalks, or other infrastructure.
   4. Fabric inserts are not permitted to be used as part of a barrier.
   5. Chain link and other fencing such as cyclone fencing, chicken wire or similar
       appurtenances is prohibited. Materials not specifically manufactured for fencing or
       pedestrian control (including but not limited to buckets, food containers, tires, tree stumps,
       vehicle parts, pallets, etc.) and not expressly permitted elsewhere in these Guidelines may
       not be used as components of a barrier.
   6. Height. The highest point of a barrier (such as stanchion) must measure at least 36 inches
       in height, with the exception of planters.
   7. All barriers must be detectable to visually impaired pedestrians who employ a cane for
       guidance. The bottom of the barriers must be no greater than 27 inches above the sidewalk
       surface.
   8. Access opening. Any access opening in the barrier must measure no less than 44 inches
       in width.
   9. Planters may be used in place of or in addition to other barrier designs. They can be used
       in places where barriers are not required, such as when the seating area extends less than 3
       feet into the sidewalk.
   10. Planters may not exceed a height of 36 inches above the sidewalk or parking lot (this
       pertains to the planters, not the plants within them). Plants, whether live or artificial, shall
       not exceed a height of 108 inches above the level of the sidewalk or parking lot.
   11. All Planters shall have plants contained within them. If the plants die, they must be
       replaced, or the planters removed.
   12. Any barrier, planter or planting shall not obstruct vehicular sight distance.

Access and Circulation
All outdoor dining areas must leave at least 5 feet of unobstructed pedestrian space. This 5 feet of

into the 5-feet-unobstructed zone. If a perimeter enclosure is used, adequate space must be
provided within the enclosed outdoor dining area to permit movement of patrons and waitstaff.

DEFINITIONS


Full Service Outdoor Dining: Table service by waitstaff is provided to patrons seated in
outdoor dining areas. This includes food and beverage service, including alcoholic
beverages.


Outdoor Seating: Seating provided by a food or beverage establishment, which provides
seating at tables located outside of the business premises but does not provide waitstaff.


Private Lot: For the purposes of this Ordinance, refers to the lot upon which a restaurant is
located, where seating will not be provided on a municipal sidewalk. The lot itself is owned
by a private entity.


Public Way/Space: For the purposes of this Ordinance, refers to seating that is located on
a municipal sidewalk or in a municipal parking lot or the lot itself is owned by the Town of
Hooksett.

Purpose
The Town of Hooksett recognizes the importance of outdoor dining to the success and vibrancy of
its eating and drinking establishments. The Design Guidelines provide acceptable dimensions for
dining area access, sidewalk and parking lot barriers, and the layout of outdoor service areas. The
use of durable and high-quality outdoor furnishings and materials is encouraged to increase the
safety and comfort of residents and visitors. The diagrams in this document illustrate examples of
outdoor dining design; photographs and diagrams show acceptable furnishings.

Barriers

   1. The barrier access point must be controlled by the restaurant establishment.
   2. If alcohol is served, outdoor dining shall be separated from public pedestrian space on the
      adjacent public sidewalk by an enclosure on all four sides with an access point that is a
      minimum of 44” wide.

      way shall be enclosed by barriers that are durable, removable, and maintained in good
      condition.

   5. Per 2018 Life Safety Code, Existing Places of Assembly, §13.1.1.7, existing portions of
      the structure shall not be required to be modified, provided that both the following criteria

           a. The new construction has not diminished the fire safety features of the facility.
           b. The new addition does not result in an increase in the required minimum number
              of separate means of egress in accordance with §7.4.1.2

       is portable but cannot be easily shifted by patrons or pedestrians. Sectional barriers must

   2. Rope or Chain Rails are permitted if they meet the following guidelines. The rope or

Access and Circulation
All outdoor dining areas must leave at least 5 feet of unobstructed pedestrian space. This 5 feet of
pedestrian space must be clear of obstructions caused by trees, tree wells, posts, hydrants, or any
other infrastructure. In addition, no part of an outdoor dining area (including plants) may extend
into the 5-feet-unobstructed zone. If a perimeter enclosure is used, adequate space must be
provided within the enclosed outdoor dining area to permit movement of patrons and waitstaff.
Waitstaff may not serve patrons from beyond the perimeter enclosure. All outdoor dining areas
must be ADA compliant.

   178:24 Authorization for Other Areas. – A business holding a license authorizing the consumption of
alcoholic beverages on its licensed premise may petition the commission for permission to extend service for
beverages and liquor to any clearly defined area under the control of the licensee. In addition, a licensee may
expand into a shared space, such as a sidewalk or street, with the approval of local officials. Petitions shall
include written plans and diagrams that shall provide detailed information on the proposed extension of alcohol
service. Petitions shall also conform to local zoning requirements and include the written approval of local
officials. Businesses served by a waste disposal system, as defined by RSA 485-A:2, XI, shall obtain approval
from the department of environmental services for any expansion under this section, unless the total number of
seats remains the same or is reduced. Businesses that are on municipal sewer may expand seating as permitted
by the municipality. The commission may approve plans submitted by licensees and may add such terms and
conditions as it deems appropriate to preserve public health and safety. The commission may deny, in its
discretion, any petition under this section which constitutes a risk to public health, safety, or welfare of any
community.

BACKGROUND INFORMATION:
All Public Works divisions fuel up their diesel vehicles at the Recycle and Transfer Station as needed.
The underground diesel storage tank holds 10,000 gallons and is typically filled up when there is
around 1,500 gallons remaining, which is about every 2-3 months. The Public Works Department
typically purchases 27,000-30,000 gallons of diesel each year between all divisions.

Public Works also purchase 3,000-3,500 tons of road salt for the winter season through a State
contract.

When purchasing this quantity of diesel and salt, the invoice amount is above the cutoff for
Administrator’s approval based on the town’s Purchasing Policy under the Administrative Code
Section 5.3.2. This request is for Town Council’s approval to allow Public Works to obtain the best
pricing available at the time of re-fueling and purchase road salt through a State contract. Public
Works would provide, at a minimum, three quotes at the time of purchase to support the decision to
purchase diesel from a certain vendor.

FINANCIAL IMPACT:
Varied and to be paid out of current fuel and salt budget.

POLICY IMPLICATIONS:
If approved by Town Council, the Public Works Department would not be required to bring each
purchase to council for approval.

Recycle and Transfer Station and road salt through a State contract for FY 2024-2025.

I.) Introduction

The Town of Hooksett is seeking proposals for Information Technology Services related to the
Town’s networked computer system, equipment, and infrastructure.

Hooksett, New Hampshire was incorporated in 1822 and operates under the Town Meeting/Town
Council form of government. It is located in Merrimack County, which is in the south-central
part of New Hampshire, approximately 26 miles north of the Massachusetts state line. The
Town’s area covers about 36.3 square miles and has approximately 6,000 parcels. The 2022
Census estimated 14,740 residents of Hooksett. Currently, the town employs approximately 200
full or part-time employees and 90 or so volunteer officials that fill municipal boards, committees,
and commissions.

II.) Background

Hooksett has utilized a variety of contractual services to meet its basic information technology
needs in the past and into the present. Increased demand for services and responsiveness from
staff, however, has led to an intensifying reliance on technological assets in the form of hardware,
software, and more elaborate relationships with service providers.

Town staff is cognizant of this shift and has further recognized the need to not only have these
individual elements work in unison, but also to be more deliberate in the planning and execution
of these systems on a town-wide scale.

With an ultimate goal of efficiently leveraging existing and future IT infrastructure, the town has
engaged in a more rigorous planning process for technology systems. Staff members continuously
evaluate the utility of current and future technology with an eye on refining and/or expanding
capability as it relates to the needs of Hooksett’s citizens. To be brief, staff wants to ensure:
“Hooksett has a system in which information technology allows for maximum efficiency and
transparency for both internal and external stakeholders.”

As the primary instrument for day-to-day technical management and consultation, the contract

III.) Scope of Services

The Town of Hooksett envisions the technology services contract serving three distinct areas:
   1. Operational Excellence:
          a. Ensuring seamless operation of Hooksett’s networked computer system, equipment,
              and related infrastructure across five separate buildings.
          b. Responsibilities encompass installation, maintenance, and administrative duties as
              defined by town staff, including, but not limited to:
                    i. Installation: Physically or virtually placing and setting up hardware,
                       peripherals, and network technology at designated town locations.
                   ii. Maintenance: Keeping endpoints functional via proactive updates, repair,
                       and network connectivity upkeep.
                 iii. Administration: Managing backend town systems, user management,
                       connectivity, software suites, threat tracking, and security infrastructure
                       maintenance.
   2. Service Offerings:
          a. Services include:
                    i. Installation, maintenance, and administration of endpoints infrastructure for
                       main service centers.
                   ii. Management of associated endpoint technology and peripherals.
                 iii. Maintenance and administration of LAN/WAN infrastructure, routers,
                       switches, modems, and firewalls.
                  iv. Installation and management of software, upgrades, and patches, including
                       antivirus, backup software, Microsoft Office products, and departmental
                       applications.
                   v. Provision of general or emergency IT support during business hours and
                       beyond.
                  vi. Collaboration with third-party providers for updates and network privileges.
                 vii. Work with the town’s phone service provider to maintain the phone system.
                viii. Assistance with user and network access management.
                  ix. Support for Right-to-Know requests and website management.
                   x. Procurement assistance for new hardware.
   3. Strategic Planning and Management:
          a. Facilitation of strategic planning and management of IT systems.
          b. Provide recommendations for budget planning.
          c. Activities include maintaining equipment inventory, documentation, making
              recommendations for system improvement, advising on projects and capacity
              building, providing updates and reports, engineering, project management,
              collaborating with IT planners, and maintain secure system passwords.

198   16.1. New Town Personnel Plan policy - No Employee Pets in the Workplace
199
200   A. Garron- this was initiated with concerns regarding pets being in the workplace. We did not have a
201   formal policy in place before. In the interest of promoting the health, safety and welfare of all Town
202   employees, no employee pets are allowed anywhere in or about town property. Service animals or
203   approved support animals for persons with disabilities are welcome. An exception to this policy is for
204   extenuating circumstances (i.e., pet emergency/urgent medical needs) whereby the Town
205   Administrator or his/her designee, as recommended by the department head or his/her designee, may
206   authorize a temporary employee pet in the workplace for a specified period of time. The employee
207   shall request the exception in writing to his/her department head who then may recommend in writing
208   to the Town Administrator. A temporary employee pet must be leashed/crated at all times and the
209   employee shall provide proof the pet vaccine(s) has not expired. The employer has the right to have
210   the temporary employee pet removed at any time from town property (i.e., excessive barking,
211   unleashed/not crated).
