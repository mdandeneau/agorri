      PUBLIC INPUT
        1. Two 15-minute Public Input sessions will be allowed during each Council
           Meeting. Time will be divided equally among those wishing to speak, however,
           no person will be allowed to speak for more than 5 minutes.
        2. No person may address the council more than twice on any issue in any
           meeting. Comments must be addressed to the Chair and must not be personal
           or derogatory about any other person.
        3. Any questions must be directly related to the topic being discussed and must
           be addressed to the Chair only, who after consultation with Council and Town
           Administrator, will determine if the question can be answered at that time.
           Questions cannot be directed to an individual Councilor and must not be
           personal in nature. Issues raised during Public Input, which cannot be resolved
           or answered at that time, or which require additional discussion or research,
           will be noted by the Town Administrator who will be responsible for researching
           and responding to the comment directly during normal work hours or by
           bringing to the Council for discussion at a subsequent meeting. The Chair
           reserves the right to end questioning if the questions depart from clarification to
           deliberation.
        4. Council members may request a comment be added to New Business at a
           subsequent meeting.
        5. No one may speak during Public Input except the person acknowledged by the
           Chair. Direct questions or comments from the audience are not permitted
           during Public Input.

Title:         Elm Grove Companies-Community Development Block Grant (CDBG) Application
               Public Hearing
Meeting:       Town Council - 08 Jul 2026
Department:    Community Development
Staff Contact: André Garron, Town Administrator

BACKGROUND INFORMATION:
Each year NH CDFA administers the Federal HUD Community Development Block Grant Program
(CDBG) for housing. This program partners eligible Communities with Developers who will provide
affordable housing. Hooksett is interested in partnering with the Elm Grove Company to invest in
renovations at the Residences at River Hill located at 512 W. River Road to maintain these 76 units
as high quality affordable units. Lakes Region Planning Commission (LRPC) may serve as Grants
Manager. LRPC, on behalf of the Town and the Developer, asks that you schedule the required
public hearing for review of the grant application, as well as the Housing and Community
Development Plan and the Residential Anti-displacement and Relocation Assistance Plan on
July 8th. At the July 8th Public Hearing we will vote to submit the grant as well as adopt both Plans.

The proposed project involves significant re-investment into exterior and interior renovations. Exterior
scope includes façade upgrades (hardi-board siding, window replacements, and brick painting), new
paver patio, solar installation, and dog park. Interior scope includes new common space flooring and
paint, lighting upgrades, and the conversion of existing basement storage into six new studio
residential units. The project will also convert all 76 units to be workforce housing. Together, these
improvements reflect Elm Grove’s approach to ownership: invest in the asset, respond to what
residents and the community want, and deliver a product that raises the bar rather than merely
meeting it. The result will be a meaningfully improved property—more energy-efficient, better-
appointed, and an addition of workforce housing capacity—without altering the building’s footprint.
This project has been structured in close coordination with CDFA and NHH, whose involvement is a
direct reflection of the trust Elm Grove has earned as a long-term affordable housing partner in New
Hampshire. These partnerships reflect the confidence those organizations place in Elm Grove’s ability
to deliver and sustain quality affordable housing at scale.

FINANCIAL IMPACT:
Although the applicant for federal funding is the Town of Hooksett, Lakes Region Planning
Commission (LRPC) will be responsible for all grant administration costs. $30,000 is provided to
cover the costs of grant administration. In the event that an audit is required for this grant funding,
Elm Grove Companies is committed to covering the cost of the audit that the company is responsible
for. No costs will be borne by the Town of Hooksett. Also, having been designated a Housing
Champion in 2024, the town may qualify for funding to support this project.

                    HOUSING AND COMMUNITY DEVELOPMENT PLAN (HCDP) - 2026

Town of Hooksett Housing and Community Development Plan (HCDP) identifies needs which currently
exist or are anticipated during the next three years. The Plan provides a basis for guiding the Town of
Hooksett housing and community development objectives and actions. The Housing and Community
Development Plan is consistent with the current master plan of the Town of Hooksett.

This document also outlines the Town of Hooksett efforts to include citizen participation in
implementing activities funded through the Community Development Block Grant. That information is
found in the section below titled “Citizen Participation Plan.”

Housing and Community Development Goals and Objectives

 The Plan’s goals and objectives are identified below and are consistent with the national Housing and
Community Development Act of 1974, as amended, and the state’s objectives listed in Chapter CDFA
300 Community Development Block Grant (CDBG) Program Rules (CDFA 305.01 & 310.01). These goals
and objectives are both short and long term. Priority will be given to the needs of low and moderate-
income persons, minorities and disadvantaged people.

 The Town of Hooksett states that as a matter of policy, involuntary displacement of households from
their neighborhoods, by actions of the Town of Hooksett shall be minimized.

Goals and objectives of this plan, both long and short-term, are consistent with following broad national
objectives:

National Objective 1: direct benefit to low and moderate income persons or households;
National Objective 2: the prevention or elimination of slums and blight; and
National Objective 3: Elimination of conditions which seriously and immediately threaten the public
health and welfare.

Goals and Objectives of this plan also addresses as many of the following state’s objectives as
appropriate for CDBG grant awards, and priority will be given to projects that have a public benefit, in
both the short and long term as follows:

State Objective 1: Implementing the Housing and Community Development Plan and conforming to the
municipality’s master plan and ordinances;

State Objective 2: Preserving and promoting existing neighborhoods and community centers;

State Objective 4: Solving community problems with long term benefits and innovative solutions;

Citizen Participation Plan

Town of Hooksett will seek to engage its citizens in implementing housing and community activities
funded by the Community Development Block Grant. Its citizen participation efforts will be consistent
with the State of New Hampshire’s citizen participation plan. Specifically, Town of Hooksett will adhere
to the following steps to engage its citizens:

        1)     Publishing a statement of proposed activities for any application proposed to be
        submitted by Town of Hooksett so that affected citizens have an opportunity to submit
        comments on the proposed activities.

        2)       Provide adequate notices, specifically to persons of low- and moderate-income as well
        as the general public, for all public hearings to discuss proposed or approved CDBG applications.
        Such notices will include the statement of proposed activities or how to obtain such statement.
        At least ten days prior to any hearing, the notices will be published in a daily newspaper of
        general circulation in the municipality; AND posted as a printed legal notice in at least three
        other public places.

        3)      Hold two or more public hearings on the proposed application at times and locations
        convenient to potential beneficiaries, accessible to persons with physical disabilities, and that
        meet the needs of non-English speaking residents, if appropriate, to obtain citizens’ views
        before adoption of resolution or similar action by the local governing body authorizing the
        submission of the application. At least one public hearing will be held prior to submitting any
        CDBG application and another during the course of all approved projects.

        4)     Provide at public hearings information concerning the amount of funds available for
        proposed community development activities and the range of activities within the project.

        5)     Provide at public hearings information concerning the amount of funds that will benefit
        persons of low- and moderate-income.

        6)     Provide at public hearings information about potential program income and the
        Program Income Reuse plan, if program income is anticipated.

        7)       Furnish citizens with the plans made to minimize the displacement of persons and to
        assist persons actually displaced as a result of grant activities, if displacement is anticipated.

        8)      Provide technical assistance to groups representing persons of low- and moderate-
        income requesting such assistance in developing proposals.

        9)      Provide citizens with reasonable notice of substantial changes proposed in the use of
        grant funds and providing opportunity for public comment;

        10)     Any modifications or amendments to the project will be made in accordance with the
        same procedures required in points (1) through (3) above for the preparation and submission of
        a statement of proposed activities.

      SAMPLE RESIDENTIAL ANTIDISPLACEMENT AND RELOCATION
                        ASSISTANCE PLAN

Every effort will be made to minimize temporary or permanent displacement of
persons due to a CDBG project undertaken by the municipality.

However, in the event of displacement as a result of a federally funded award,
The Town of Hooksett will comply with the Uniform Relocation Assistance and
Real Property Acquisition Policies Act of 1970, as amended, to any household,
regardless of income which is involuntarily and permanently displaced.

If the property is acquired, but will not be used for low/moderate income housing
under 104(d) of the Housing and Community Development Act of 1974, as
amended, the displacement and
relocation plan shall provide that before obligating and spending funds that will
directly result in such demolition or conversion the municipality will make public
and submit to CDFA the following
information:

   a. Comparable replacement housing in the community within three (3) years
      of the commencement date of the demolition or rehabilitation;

   b. A description of the proposed activity;

   c. The general location on a map and appropriate number of dwelling units
      by number of bedrooms that will be demolished or converted to a use
      other than as low and moderate income dwelling units as a direct result of
      the assisted activity;

   d. A time schedule for the commencement and completion date of the
      demolition or conversion;

   by number of bedrooms that will be provided as replacement dwelling
   units;

BACKGROUND INFORMATION:
466:14 Warrants; Proceedings. – The town or city clerk shall annually, between June 1 and June 20,
present to the local governing body a list of those owners of dogs that have failed to license or not
renewed their dog licenses pursuant to RSA 466:1. The local governing body shall, within 20 days
from June 20, issue a warrant to a local official authorized to issue a civil forfeiture for each
unlicensed dog. The warrant may also authorize a local law enforcement officer to seize any
unlicensed dog. The civil forfeiture may be sent by certified mail, or delivered in hand, or left at the
abode of the dog owner. The cost of service shall not exceed $7 and may be recovered by the city or
town in addition to the amount of the civil forfeiture. If the unlicensed dog is seized, it shall be held in
a town or city holding facility for a period of 7 days, after which time full title to the dog shall pass to
the facility, unless the owner of the dog has, before the expiration of the period, caused the dog to be
licensed. The owner shall pay the facility a necessary and reasonable sum per day, as agreed upon
by the governing body of the town or city and the facility, for each day the dog has been kept and
maintained by the facility, plus any necessary veterinary fees incurred by the facility for the benefit of
the dog. Before a local law enforcement officer seizes any unlicensed dog, a written warning shall be
given to the dog owner.

The list and warrant will be presented at the meeting.

77          Saunders, from Lakes Regional Planning Commission, regarding Elm Grove CDBG Grant
78          Project Narrative
79
80   Shanna Saunders, Lakes Regional Planning Commission on behalf of the Southern New Hampshire
81   planning commission: Asking that a public hearing be scheduled for July 8th as this is required for
82   them to apply for the CDBG Grant. Noted the Grant application is due at the end of July so timing is
83   important as they must complete the public hearing prior to applying for the grant
84
85   A. Garron: States Community block grants can only be issued through a Town/City. States Elm
86   Grove currently has a multifamily project in Hooksett and they are looking to convert some of those
87   into affordable workforce housing utilizing funding from the Community Development Block Grant.
88   States they have had a discussion with the Planning Board and received positive feedback.
89
90   Brian Webster, Elm Grove Properties: Provided project overview stating, The site is an established
91   and occupied residential community that currently serves workforce and market rate housing
92   needs. States there is currently two studios, 14 one bedroom, and 54 two-bedroom units. States
93   they are proposing to do interior and exterior reinvestment and improvements. Notes that the
94   biggest part of the proposed project on the interior is turning the existing storage spaces located in
95   the basement, into 6 new studio apartments. States that the building will be fully converted to
96   workforce housing.
97
98   Matthew Rose, Environmental Planner and grant writer for Lakes Region Planning Commission:
99   States each year the state of New Hampshire is eligible to receive housing and community

100   development block grant funding from HUD, which is the US Department of Housing and Urban
101   Development. States CDBG is a Community Development Block Grant which is designed to provide
102   assistance for improving economic opportunities and community revitalization needs for low to
103   moderate income persons. States that the town of Hooksett would be the grant applicant and Elm
104   Grove would be the sub recipient of that grant. Lakes Region Planning Commission would take on
105   the grant writing for the grant application and then grant management if the grant is awarded.
106   States the grant application has a maximum award amount of $750,000, which is what they are

130   averaging program (40% of the property has an affordability restriction at income and rent levels
131   that represent 60% of the area median income). States that the income averaging program allows
132   you to choose a number of set asides from 20% AMI or Area Median Income up to 80% AMI
133   averaging to that 60%. States this means it will allow Elm Grove to accept more residents of a
134   variety of incomes. States they have portable voucher/section 8 portable voucher holders that
135   reside there and they will be able to keep those folks there and actually accept more.

159   J. Sullivan motioned to accept the fee of $250 and approve the application for a Secondhand
160   Dealer License from the Bass Pro Shop; seconded by R. Lapierre
161   Motioned Passed (5-1)
162   D. Smith opposed
163
164   Bass Pro Shop has submitted an application for a Secondhand Dealer's License along with the
165   required $250 fee as per PAWNBROKERS AND SECONDHAND DEALERS Ordinance # 2014-1. Chief
166   Sargent has reviewed and approved the application. The Town Clerk requests the approval of the
167   Town Council to issue the license.
168
169   Karina Towne, Town Clerk: Introduced the topic and noted the Police Chief has reviewed the
170   application and has his own concerns
171
172   Chief Justin Sargent, Hooksett Police: States we have 3 gun stores in Hooksett; Bass pro shops,
173   Shooters, and Wicked Weaponry. States he has no problem approving this for Bass Pro but states
174   that the other two stores do not have this license and up until now, this has never been something
175   that's been enforced therefore he is looking for some guidance on how to then enforce this, such as
176   going to the other gun stores in town and ensuring that they also get this licensed. States he feels
177   this is seems like it may be an unnecessary license and the ATF already controls the sale of firearms
178   and they have the ability to check it to see if a gun is stolen. Stating, there would be no need for
179   them to get involved. Reiterated he does not understand the need for these second hand license,
180   especially when they're already governed by the federal government.
181
182   D. Smith: Asked what even brough this to the table?
183
184   Chief Sargent: States they are looking to buy back guns and then turn around and sell them and this
185   is new for them so they are applying for this now. Noted he is happy to enforce this but does not
186   feel it is necessary
187
188   J. Sullivan: States that this is the ordinance that exists and that we need to enforce the laws that are
189   in place, but if this is not a necessary ordinance then we need to address that separately
190
191   Chief Sargent: Notes that if the Town would like to enforce this, he can go to the other businesses
192   who do not have this but if they refuse, asked who will be suspending their license?
193
194   L. Laliberte: States that Bass Pro is just doing their due diligence but had questions about the
195   ordinance and was unsure if firearms were put in before federal regulations were changed. Also
196   noted going to the other store to enforce the ordinance may not be necessary as it is on the Town
197   that this has not been enforced.
