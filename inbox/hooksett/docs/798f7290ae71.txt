To:            Town Council
Title:         Karina Towne, Town Clerk, to proceed with the civil forfeiture process for the
               licensing of dogs for the 2025 year as presented
Meeting:       Town Council - 11 Jun 2025
Department:    Town Clerk's Office
Staff Contact: Karina Towne, Town Clerk

BACKGROUND INFORMATION:
466:14 Warrants; Proceedings. – The town or city clerk shall annually, between June 1 and June 20,
present to the local governing body a list of those owners of dogs that have failed to license or not
renewed their dog licenses pursuant to RSA 466:1. The local governing body shall, within 20 days
from June 20, issue a warrant to a local official authorized to issue a civil forfeiture for each
unlicensed dog. The warrant may also authorize a local law enforcement officer to seize any
unlicensed dog. The civil forfeiture may be sent by certified mail, or delivered in hand, or left at the
abode of the dog owner. The cost of service shall not exceed $7 and may be recovered by the city or
town in addition to the amount of the civil forfeiture. If the unlicensed dog is seized, it shall be held in
a town or city holding facility for a period of 7 days, after which time full title to the dog shall pass to
the facility, unless the owner of the dog has, before the expiration of the period, caused the dog to be
licensed. The owner shall pay the facility a necessary and reasonable sum per day, as agreed upon
by the governing body of the town or city and the facility, for each day the dog has been kept and
maintained by the facility, plus any necessary veterinary fees incurred by the facility for the benefit of
the dog. Before a local law enforcement officer seizes any unlicensed dog, a written warning shall be
given to the dog owner.

The list and warrant will be presented at the meeting.


RECOMMENDATION:
To sign the warrant authorizing the civil forfeiture process for the licensing of dogs for the 2025 year
as presented by the Town Clerk

SUGGESTED MOTION:
Motion to approve the civil forfeiture process for the licensing of dogs for the 2025 year as presented
by the Town Clerk

TOWN ADMINISTRATOR'S RECOMMENDATION:
I agree with the suggested motion

                         PLANNING BOARD
The Planning Board’s primary duties include reviewing land use applications for compliance with
the Town Development Regulations, the Town Zoning Ordinance, and the Master Plan.
Applications include residential and commercial site plans, amended site plans, major and minor
subdivisions, project phasing proposals, lot line adjustments, lot mergers, condominium
conversions, and site plan waivers.

From July 2024 through June 2025, the Planning Board held 19 meetings, 1 workshop, and
participated in 3 site walks. The Planning Board reviewed 23 formal applications, amended several
regulations, participated in many discussions, and viewed several conceptual plans. The land use
applications heard included:

Amended Subdivisions Approved

   1. 1 and 5 Dartmouth Street (Amended buffer screening plan)
   2. 5 Eastpoint Drive (Amended Condo Plan)
   3. 10 Prescott Heights Road (Unmerging of previous voluntary lot merger)

      NH-3A.

   3. 129 Merrimack Street (4 residential lots)

reviewed the document, which will be presented to the Town Council and the Budget Committee
in the coming months.

Future Goals of the Planning Board include:

   •   Continue to develop the TIF district.
   •   Increase understanding of the TIF District and infrastructure benefits made possible (with
       voter support) due to increased “captured” property values.
   •   Assist the Wastewater Department in planning for a gravity sewer line from Bert Street to
       Thompson Ave.
   •   Assist the various Water Precincts in planning for interconnections and well sites.

181   Tony Lacasse- on behalf of the Heritage Commission we request to modify the name of Lambert's Park
182   to Alfred J. Lambert Riverside Park at AnnaHooksett Falls. With this name change the Heritage
183   Commission would be responsible for the payment and installation of a new park sign with the new
184   name. This park is a historical site in NH and in Hooksett. By adopting this name, the town will adopt
185   the area’s name honoring the heritage of the park and include all the eras and name recognition that
186   has been used to reference the park. It is our mission to care for and restore Hooksett history. We
187   want to honor not only Mr. Lambert who created the park, but we want to honor the history that was
188   before Mr. Lambert and connect all the history to spark interest in the area. Historical Context This
189   section of the Merrimack River was referred to in early 18th-century New Hampshire Provincial Papers
190   as Annahooksett Falls—a phonetic interpretation by early European settlers of the original Native
191   American name for the area. Tis name means in Abenaki “Place of beautiful trees/near water”
192   Extensive research by the Heritage Commission, with consultation from representatives of the Abenaki
193   and Penacook tribes, confirmed the spelling “Annahooksett” as the most historically accurate and
194   respectful choice. The addition of “Annahooksett Falls” to the park’s name is an opportunity for
195   Hooksett to recognize and honor its Indigenous heritage—the earliest known chapter in our town’s
196   story. Naming is a powerful way to educate, preserve memory, and show respect. This land’s
197   Indigenous connection should not be left in the footnotes of history but highlighted in the names we
198   use and the places we protect. At the same time, the name retains and elevates the legacy of Alfred J.
199   “Fred” Lambert, who led efforts in the 1970s–1980s to clean up the area and establish the current
200   riverside park. The name “Alfred J. Lambert Riverside Park” was historically used at the time of the
201   park’s original dedication and continues to be supported by the Lambert family.
