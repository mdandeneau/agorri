BACKGROUND INFORMATION:
Remove Item from Tabled (tabled on April 10, 2024 pending confirmation that all other
bordering water precincts concurred with the service area boundaries).

The Hooksett Village Water Precinct (HVWP) wishes to confirm the limits of their service area
boundaries to more accurately depict those boundaries at the parcel level and facilitate Town
development, planning and engineering activities. No new infrastructure expansion is included. A
plan of the current service area is provided.

Per State requirements, the HVWP has petitioned and obtained the signatures of at least 10 Precinct
voters to approve of the service area boundary delineation. The Petition dated February 27, 2024 is
attached. Statutes require that the Petition be ratified by the Precinct’s voters prior to it taking effect.
To that end, it was approved at the HVWP’s Annual Meeting held on February 27, 2024. A copy of
the meeting minutes (Petition info is near the end) is attached.

At this time, the Petition must now be submitted to Town Council for consideration. The HVWP is
requesting that the Town Council approve the Petition after holding a duly-noticed public hearing, in
accordance with State requirements.

If TC approval is granted, documentation of that approval needs to occur in the records of the Town
and Precinct within 60 days of the approval.

A description of the boundaries of the Precinct’s current service area is attached. As shown on
“HVWP Service Area, Hooksett, NH” attached hereto.

Also attached are confirmation from the bordering water precincts that the boundaries are accurate.
These precincts include the Central Hookset Water Precinct, the Manchester Water Works, and the
Pennichuck Water Works.

place them more in line with similar communities. The last time these rates were increased was 3
years ago in 2021. See the attached study which shows various communities in NH and other states.
Also noted are Hooksett's current rates and the rates being proposed here currently. The proposed
rates are now split between resident and non-resident rates. This would allow the department to
collect more reimbursement from non-resident, non-taxpaying individuals. Adopting these new rates
would put our community more in line with the average of these communities listed on the study.
Additionally, these rate changes would only effect those with private insurance or who are self-pay;
not those with Medicare, Medicaid or the VA.

FINANCIAL IMPACT:
No cost to the town, however this would increase revenue in the ambulance revenue account.

POLICY IMPLICATIONS:
The town's ambulance billing policy would be updated to reflect the new rates.

reflect the new rates.

STAFF REPORT

To:              Town Council
Title:           Fire Department Fee Schedule Change
Meeting:         Town Council - 11 Sep 2024
Department:      Fire and Rescue
Staff Contact:   Joseph Stalker, Captain

BACKGROUND INFORMATION:
The Hooksett Fire Rescue Department is seeking an increase in the fire prevention fee schedule to
place them more in line with surrounding communities. This is a listing of fees for Life Safety Plan
review, Fire Alarm / Sprinkler Inspections, various residential and commercial permits and assembly
inspections such as churches and restaurants and movie theaters. The last time these rates were
increased was 10 years ago in 2014. See the attached study which shows various communities in
Merrimack County. Also noted are Hooksett's current rates and the rates being proposed here
currently. Adopting these new rates would put our community more in line with the average of these
communities listed on the study. Additionally, we are seeking to implement two (2) new fee
categories; the first being for adult foster care inspections. The town has roughly 90 residences where
these clients reside. Secondly, a new fee category titled 'Other / Replacement' covering minor fire
systems such as replacing sprinkler heads and/or smoke detectors.

FINANCIAL IMPACT:
The fees and these increases are revenue to the Town.

POLICY IMPLICATIONS:
None

RECOMMENDATION:
Recommendation to accept the updated fees as proposed.

SUGGESTED MOTION:
Motion to accept the proposed Fire Prevention Fee Schedule as presented.

TOWN ADMINISTRATOR'S RECOMMENDATION:
I concur with the suggested motion.

BACKGROUND INFORMATION:
On March 22, 2024, the Town Council approved up to $42,500 of ARPA funds to upgrade the
telephone system and integrate all departments into a unified system. Given the cost, a request for
proposals (RFP) was necessary. The Town received five bids. Block 5 and Town Staff worked with
the two lowest bidders to determine that all requirements were included with their bids.

Telephone and Network Technologies (TNT) is located in Hooksett and has 40 employees available
to support the town. After review of their bid, the cost did increase to $48,623.74 (from their original
bid), due to items that were not originally included. This cost would be for installation and a 3 year
phone licensing contract. With the TNT bid, every three years, the Town would have to pay $8,617.74
for the licensing renewals. These costs includes a cloud based phone system. The recurring monthly
cost is $571.61.

TSE Digitalvoice is Hooksett's current provider and has 6 employees available to support the town.
TSE provided an addendum to their bid to answer for items that did not seem to be included in their
original bid. When doing so, there was no additional cost for those items. TSE's installation cost
remains at $45,010.00 and a recurring monthly cost of $600.00/month. TSE hosts the phone system
in their Bedford data center. However, they are offering the option to build a voice platform on the
Grandstream UCM Cloud (Amazon's AWS platform). This will add an annual cost of $1,550 for the
licensing but will reduce the one time installation cost by $700 as that is the hardware cost of the two
voice servers that would be installed in their Bedford data center. Based on comments from
Department Heads, the cloud option is better suited for the town

Block 5 also has a project cost of $2,808.00 to work as a liaison between Hooksett and the telephone
vendor, reconfigure firewalls and failover internet connections at the town buildings.

The Town Administrator will collaborate with Department Heads to review the budget to cover the
cost difference.

FINANCIAL IMPACT:
TNT's (Hooksett) one time installation cost would be $48,623.74 (including an annual cost for 3 years
of $8,617.74), and a monthly recurring cost of $571.61.
TSE's (Bedford) one time installation cost would be $44,310, monthly recurring cost of $600 and
$1,550 annually.
Block 5's one time project cost of $2,808.00.

RECOMMENDATION:
Chief Colburn's recommendation is to proceed with Telephone and Network Technologies (TNT)
for many reasons. The Fire Department has prior experience with TNT, as they installed their current
phone system seven years ago. During the installation, TNT ensured that everyone was well-
prepared, completed the installation of the new system, and confirmed its full operation before
removing the old system. The installation was conducted with professionalism and neatness. It
involved installing all new CAT 6 cabling at both stations and removing the old cabling. Regarding
service, there has been no issues; they typically arrive on-site the same day they are called.
Whenever Brian or Tyler from TNT are contacted, they have been very responsive. Most service calls
are related to system changes that need to be made on-site. Regarding TSE, there has been some
challenges with phone outages at the Safety Center.

When TSE took over, there was assurance that a failover system would be in place to prevent the
issues that were experienced with the previous phone provider. Despite those assurances, there has
been several instances of phones going down. The response time from TSE has occasionally not met
the expectations of the department, and there have been explanations provided that attribute the
outages to external factors. Recently, there was another phone outage, which was due to the hosting
being conducted at TSE's Bedford office rather than in a cloud environment that offers greater
security and multiple backup locations. During a power outage in Bedford, TSE's generator failed,
resulting in a loss of phone service. This incident underscores the risks associated with hosting at a
single physical location and suggest that such a setup may not be ideal for the Safety Center.

Mark Glisson, Assistant Library Director, has not worked with either company other than TNT's
visits while creating a bid for the Town. During their visits to the library, TNT demonstrated a thorough
understanding of their needs and provided a detailed bid. Mark was impressed with Brian and Tyler
during their visits.

Chief Sargent's recommendation is to proceed with Telephone and Network Technologies because
they are based in Hooksett and having 40 employees is beneficial to a town of Hooksett's size.

Motion to award RFP #2024-12 to Telephone and Network Technologies located in Hooksett with a

to review the budget to cover the cost difference.

Addendum to clarify questions upon review of our bid


   1. Were switches included in the bid?
   2. Is there redundant internet in this bid offer?
   3. Is there firewall protection on this system?
   4. Is there an alternate location to host the Town wide system other than TSE
      datacenter?


Data switches for voice equipment

The bid provided 2 -48 port Grandstream Layer 2 Power over Ethernet data
switches at the Hooksett Safety Complex that will be set up for two secure
separate networks via 2 voice VLANs.
The Town offices will use the existing Data Switch(s) that power the phones now.
If the phones are currently powered by power supplies plugged into wall outlets,
then TSE will provide 1 48 port Gb PoE data switch at no cost.
TSE will provide the small Fire Station with an 8 port PoE data switch at no cost.
TSE will provide 1 - 24 port Gb PoE data switch at the library at no cost.
TSE will provide 1 - 16 port Gb PoE data switch at Public Works Offices at no cost.
The recreation garage/building will use a power brick for power as they do now.
The Recycling center will us power cords as they do now.


Redundant internet

If the town has redundant internet at the Safety Complex or Town Hall, then the
voice firewall will be set up to utilize the fiber internet as the primary internet and
the AT&T FirstNet cellular or Comcast Cable internet as the backup.

The TSE data center has Consolidated EDIA fiber internet and Comcast Metro E
fiber internet. Both will be incorporated in the redundancy plan discussed in the
executive summary submitted in the bid. To sum up what was presented, we are
planning to have the phones be able to communicate to the voice servers by way of
both internet providers should one internet provider have a outage.

Firewall protection on our voice servers

The voice server that sits in our Data center is 100% protected by a strong multi
layered firewall. Only devices that pass MD5 authentication during registration of
the device to the server are allowed. The firewall provides DoS protection and
Fail2Ban has programmable blacklisting, allowing only 4 attempts to pass the MD5
registration. Please see the Grandstream Security attachment provided.

The phones at each site can work through your existing firewalls for the small
locations (they do now). The fire and police phones will run through our existing
voice firewall. We can set up the switch(s) there to create two separate secure
voice networks behind that voice router and the Town Hall will run behind the
existing voice router.


Alternate location to host town wide voice server (not hosted at TSE’s datacenter)

Alternatively, we can build the voice platform on the Grandstream UCM Cloud
which is Amazon’s AWS platform. This adds an annual cost of $1,550 for the
licensing to run the phones on the same platform. The licensing will support up to
200 extensions. Same system but they host it instead of TSEdigitalvoice hosting
the voice servers at our data center. If you choose this option the one time cost of
the equipment proposed will be reduced by $700. This is the hardware cost of the
two voice servers that we would be installing in our data center.
