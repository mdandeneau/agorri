35   6. BUDGET OVERVIEW OF FISCAL YEAR 2027-2028
36
37   A. Garron: Provided a budget overview stating, the FY 2027–28 proposed budget totals
38   $22.6 million in expenditures, a 1.24% increase over the FY 2026–27 budget of $22.3
39   million. Noted that the RD MNT Resurfacing (road paving) line within the Department of
40   Public Works does not include funding for road paving in FY 2027–28. In March 2022,
41   voters approved a special warrant article providing an additional $600,000 annually for
42   road paving for five years, with FY 2026–27 being the final year of that additional funding.
43   Noted, the plan is to once again to seek voter approval through a special warrant article to
44   provide funding for road paving. Stated this approach would allow the Town to continue its
45   road resurfacing program while giving voters the opportunity to authorize the additional
     2 | Town Council Budget Review Meeting Minutes September 2, 2026


46   funding. If the RD MNT Resurfacing line had been level funded in FY 2027–28, total
47   proposed expenditures would have been approximately $23.2 million, representing a
48   3.92% increase over the FY 2026–27 budget.
49
50   A. Walczyk: Noted this seems more of a necessity and may need to be in the budget and not
51   presented as a warrant article
52
53   R. Lapierre: Asked if this is for 1-year
54
55   A. Garron: Stated this is annually for an additional 5-years

58   operating budget

60   A. Garron: Stated, on the revenue side, the FY 2027-28 budget projects $8.1 million, which
61   is a 3.87% increase compared to the $7.8 million budgeted in FY 2026-27. Wastewater
62   revenues are not included in the FY 2026-27 or the FY 2027–28 budgets. Noted that the
63   proposed Comprehensive Fund Summary includes revenues that offset the amount of
64   property taxes needed to fund the budget. Under the Revenue category, the line titled
65   “Taxes” represents Yield, Excavation, and Payments in Lieu of Property Taxes. The “Total
66   Revenues Less Expenditures” line represents the amount of property tax revenue needed
67   to balance the budget.

111   4. Employer share of NH Retirement has decreased by -8.2% or approximately $173,000.
112   5. This budget does support requests for direct financial contributions to Hooksett-ties, and
113   Old Home Day based on the conversations had by the Town Council for the current year.
114   6. Assessing has additional funds to hire temporary office help using an agency.
115   7. The Community Development's budget includes funds to update the traffic impact fee
116   rates in the Planning & Zoning Division and one new part-time Code Enforcement Officer in
117   the Building Inspection Division.
118   8. The town welfare line is recommended at $50,000, which is $8,000 less than the current
119   budget and includes new funds for the Home Health & Hospice Care.
120   9. The Finance budget includes funds for one part-time Assistant Finance Director. This
121   new position will support operations and ensure redundancies in the Finance Office.
122   10. The Fire budget includes funding for the "frozen firefighter" to bring them to full
123   staffing; "PFAS Free" personal protective equipment; and a training exercise for the
124   Emergency Operations Center.
125   11. The Police Department’s budget includes funds for the "frozen police officers" bringing
126   them to full staffing and additional funds for vehicle maintenance and fuel. The proposed
127   budget does not include the replacement of cruisers, which the TA recommends funding
128   with a warrant article.
129   12. In the Department of Public Works' highway division, which has funds for the "frozen
130   Truck Driver" position bringing them to full staffing, funds to stripe lines on all the town
131   roads, a reduction of $600,000 in road paving as of the March 2022 warrant article which
132   has ended, and the plan is to request road paving funds in a separate warrant article. In the

134   pave the road behind the Head Cemetery Chapel. Both items have been delayed for three
135   years due to the default budgets. In the recycling and transfer division, the tipping fee line
136   has increased by 4% due to the long-term contract for trash removal. There are no funds to
137   reimburse the condo associations, which ended in September 2025. The proposed budget
138   supports the Transfer Station being open every other Saturday.

140   A. Garron: presented the Library Trustees' recommended budget which supports:
141   1. Restoring some of the hours that were reduced in FY 2026-27. Currently, the library
142   opens at 10:00 and closes at 7:00 (Monday to Thursday). The proposed budget would
143   allow the library to meet the demands of the public and open at 9:30 (each day) and close
144   at 7:30 Monday to Thursday.
145   2. Additional funds for Books & Materials, the backbone of a Library.
146   3. Building Maintenance has been increased by $3,500, bringing the budgeted amount to
147   $22,500. This remains $11,802 below the average annual expenditure of $34,302 over the
148   past five budget years.
