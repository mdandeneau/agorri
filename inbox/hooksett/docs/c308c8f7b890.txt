BACKGROUND INFORMATION:
On September 9, 2026, the Town Council received a presentation from Carol Granfield of Municipal
Resources, Inc. (MRI) regarding the findings and recommendations of the Town's Salary Market
Analysis Update. The study reviewed 33 non-union position classifications, compared compensation
levels with nine comparable communities, and developed a proposed FY2027 Classification Pay
Plan. The report concluded that while the Town has remained competitive, adjustments are
recommended to six part-time employee’s salaries

FINANCIAL IMPACT:
The estimated FY2027 cost associated with implementing the proposed changes is $11,669.

RECOMMENDATION:
  1. Accept the findings and recommendations of the Salary Market Analysis Update.
  2. Adopt the proposed FY2027 Classification Pay Plan effective 07/05/2027.
  3. Incorporate $11,669.00 into the FY2027-2028 operating budget to bring six part-time
     employees to the minimum salary level of their assigned pay grades.
  4. Consider MRI's recommendations regarding the Human Resources Coordinator and Project
     Coordinator positions, including future classification, title, responsibility level, and
     compensation considerations identified in the report.

SUGGESTED MOTION:
Motion to accept the Salary Market Analysis as presented to include the recommendations listed
above.

TOWN ADMINISTRATOR'S RECOMMENDATION:
I agree with the suggested motion. There are positions recommended for change in salary, position
and title and should be addressed over the fiscal year.

                            SALARY MARKET ANALYSIS UPDATE

                                          AUGUST 2026


                                        INTRODUCTION


Scope of Services

The Town of Hooksett, NH engaged Municipal Resources, Inc. (MRI) to conduct a comprehensive
wage survey and market comparison and analysis encompassing 33 non-union position
classifications. The current pay structure will be reviewed, and a salary survey of positions will be
sent to comparable communities and organizations. This report addresses the findings and
recommendations and will assist with both recruitment and retention of talented, qualified
employees during this current competitive market.


                                   COMPENSATION REVIEW

Study Process

This project included the following steps:

   •   Review of the Current Pay and Classification Plan- the current Pay and Classification Plan
       was reviewed, which includes wages and classification grades for the positions included
       in the study.

    •   Thorough Market Analysis using comparable communities by means of size, location, and
        competitive recruitment area.
    •   Salary Market data analysis.
    •   Review of job descriptions and condensed Position Analysis Questionnaire (PAQ)
        completed by employees.
    •   Development of updated Pay and Classification Plan.
    •   Final preparation of Report to the Town of Hooksett, NH.


                                         PROJECT REPORT

Details of the Methodology

In the Town of Hooksett, NH, an evaluation of classification and compensation levels was
undertaken for the positions included in the study. At the same time, salary data from
comparable communities to the town were gathered and analyzed. Results were received and
analyzed from the following 9 communities:

                                           Amherst
                                            Derry
                                           Durham
                                            Exeter
                                          Goffstown
                                         Londonderry
                                          Merrimack
                                          Plymouth
                                          Windham


Organizations surveyed were selected and agreed to be utilized jointly by MRI and the town.
Some of the factors reviewed in determining appropriate comparable communities included
items such as population, form of government, size of budget, location, duties of positions, and
possible competitors for some positions.


Market Survey - Salary

Municipal Resources, Inc. conducted a thorough salary survey and market analysis to determine
market competitiveness for positions included in this study. To measure the pay rates among
competitors for these positions, MRI utilized current salary data from comparable organizations
identified above.

The Market Data Report compares minimum and maximum pay rates to the average and 75th
percentile. The following is a summary of the data report columns:

    ➢ For each position analyzed, data points are collected from the labor market. The Data
      Points indicate the amount of salary information for each position surveyed. In other
      words, the number of organizations that reported data for a particular position.

    ➢ In each cell, the top number indicates the minimum salary the organization pays, and the
      bottom number indicates the maximum salary the organization pays.

    ➢ The Average is the sum of the survey readings divided by the number of organizations
      reporting. The Average shows the average minimum salary among the comparables over
      the average maximum salary/wage among the comparables.

    ➢ The 75th Percentile shows the 75th percentile of minimum and 75th percentile of
      maximum. The percentile rank looks at the array of data points and gives each data point
      a percentile rank, where the lowest data point is 0%, and the highest data point is 100%.
      In an array of survey readings, sorted from low to high, this indicates that 75% of the
      market surveyed pays at or below this salary rate.

The Market Data Report displays how the town pay rates compare to the communities surveyed
at the average and 75th percentiles, i.e., if they are higher than the minimum/maximum or lower
(displayed by a minus) than the minimum/maximum pay rates. The town should fall within 10
percent plus or minus of the pay rate desired to be comparable. The salary rate analysis based
on the Market Data Report (Appendix A) indicates the pay plan structure needs to be updated
to continue to meet the Average pay rates for some positions. The updated proposed
Classification and Pay Plan reflects this update. (Appendix B).


Classification and Pay Plan

The town currently has a pay plan with an open range with a minimum to maximum range of 40%
that includes 20 grade levels. This was developed the last time a full compensation study was
conducted in 2022.

In addition to conducting a market salary data review of comparable communities to determine
external market equity, job descriptions were reviewed along with updated PAQs that were
completed by employees and reviewed by Department Heads. The PAQs updated any
information pertaining to position responsibilities and requirements. These were reviewed and

                              FINDINGS AND RECOMMENDATIONS


It was found that some positions may require modifications, as the salary analysis indicates the
current minimum and/or maximum rates for some positions are beyond the preferred 10%+-
range. Thus, adjustments should be explored and are recommended with the actual pay ranges.

Market Data Report (Appendix A)

The Market Data Report provides current data from nine comparable communities. The salary
market analysis indicates that while the town did an admirable job maintaining close to the
average salary for many positions, an updated Classification and Pay Plan needs to be
implemented to reflect salary comparability to meet the average pay rates of the communities
that were surveyed and are competitors for similar positions. This will also assist with potential
compression issues that may affect some positions in the future. A benefits review was not
conducted as a part of this study, and it is our understanding that the town will continue to review
the benefits to ensure employees have a positive total compensation package that includes both
salary and benefits.

Proposed FY27 Classification and Pay Plan

An updated Classification and Pay Plan has been developed that reflects the town’s current Salary
and Grade Schedule Model, which lists the proposed Grade, Position Classification, and hourly
and annual minimum and maximum pay rates. The proposed recommended plan continues to
have 20 Grade Levels and still has a 40% range from the minimum pay rate to the maximum pay
rate in each of the grade levels. See Appendix B. This updated pay plan assists the town in having
a more competitive pay structure. The new pay structure recommends reclassification of some
positions as a result of the external market, along with ensuring internal equity from reviewing
the duties and responsibility levels of positions included in the study.

Additionally, there are six employees who do not meet the minimum pay level in their proposed
grade assignment. It is strongly recommended that the town increase the pay level for these
individuals to meet the minimum. The total projected cost for this is $11,669. The town may want
to further determine if additional funds are available, it may want to increase compensation of
some employees based on performance, longevity, or other factors on a fair and equitable basis.
At minimum, those employees who do not meet the minimum pay rate in the proposed grade
should be increased to the minimum pay rate as recommended by this study.

The town should continue to update these plans annually with cost-of-living increases to address
inflation. A thorough analysis, such as was done in this study, should be conducted at least every
three years in order to maintain a competitive plan.


Positions Requiring Future Town Direction

During the course of this study two positions were reviewed that warrant future consideration
by the town. The positions require more development by town administration regarding
responsibility levels, requirements of the position and appropriate compensation. The two
positions are the Human Resources Coordinator and the Project Coordinator. Both of these

positions were developed by prior town administration, and it appears that as the town has
progressed with current administration, both may warrant more appropriate compensation
levels as well as title adjustments. The following information pertaining to the positions is
provided to assist the town with determining the best direction to proceed in the future with
both positions.


Human Resources Coordinator

This position continues to evolve into more of a Human Resources Director position whereby the
individual is managing all related human resource responsibilities to include recruitment,
selection, benefits, policies and involvement with labor relations and contracts. If town
administration continues to utilize this position at this level, it is recommended the title be
changed to Human Resources Director or Manager, and compensation be increased to match the
responsibility level. If not, some of these responsibilities should be addressed by the Town
Administrator. The current Human Resources Coordinator does possess the requirements and
experience to perform the position at the Human Resources Director level which was not the
case when the position was initially created with other personnel in the position.


Project Coordinator

This position was created by prior town administration and has been utilized to assist the Town
Administrator and all departments with a variety of special projects and associated work
generated in town administration. There currently is not an exact comparable position at the
level it is in other municipalities. Some of the duties being completed by this position may be
more at the level of either an Assistant to the Town Administrator or an Assistant Town
Administrator. It is recommended that town administration determine how this position will be
utilized by the Town Administrator, retitle the position, and increase the compensation to
address the title responsibilities and requirements.

                                        CONCLUSION

The town’s compensation program has fallen somewhat behind the labor market for some
positions especially at the minimum pay rate, and changes in the pay schedule should assist with
keeping all positions comparable to the market. MRI received sufficient and extensive data for
this analysis and is confident in the market results.

Municipal Resources, Inc. has provided the town with a thorough salary and market survey and
analysis that should assist with having an unbiased review of data to assist the town with
recruitment and retention of these positions during an extremely competitive market.

I.     PARTIES TO THE AGREEMENT

This Agreement, dated __________________, is to retain professional consulting services for the
Town of Hooksett, New Hampshire (the Client), to be provided by Municipal Resources, Inc. (MRI),
and is lawfully entered into between the Client, by its authorized representative, and MRI, by its
authorized representative, Alan S. Gould, President.


II.    SCOPE OF WORK

                             Comprehensive Recruitment Package

The following describes the activities we propose to undertake in a comprehensive executive
recruitment process for the position of Hooksett Town Administrator.

           1. Meet with the appointing authority to review the recruitment process, receive
              input toward developing an "Ideal Candidate Profile and Challenge Statement"
              against which all candidates will be screened, and discuss if and how you would
              like to involve community members and employees in the process. This helps us
              to:

                          a. Promote the “place” of your community and highlight those special
                             qualities and unique characteristics that will separate your
                             community from others that may be seeking to fill similar positions.

                          b. Identify critical organizational issues and challenges;

                          c. Clarify roles, responsibilities, and expectations for the position;

                          d. Understand the qualities, strengths, and characteristics of
                             candidates envisioned for the position;


                                                                                                     Page 25 of 67
                                                                                        Agenda Item #15.1.


                       e. Characterize the most desirable management strengths,
                          behavioral styles, personal attributes, and motivating values
                          needed in the ideal candidate to increase the probability of success
                          in the role; and

                       f. Identify the likely issues and opportunities that the next manager
                          must be prepared to address.

            This can be accomplished in a number of different ways, including interviews with
            elected/appointed officials, staff, and surveys. This information gathering process
            also allows us to develop pertinent essay questions that are specific to your
            community. We are also able to establish an email address specifically for your
            recruitment that allows an opportunity for all members of the community to
            provide input. Once approved, the Ideal Candidate Profile and Challenge
            Statement will be posted on MRI’s recruitment website for potential candidates
            to review.

         2. We will work with the appointing authority to develop a timeline for the
            recruitment process so that the Client and all candidates can plan accordingly.

         3. We will develop ad copy, recommend advertising venues, and coordinate
            placement of the ads (advertising costs are not included in the lump-sum fee, and
            are billed directly to the client unless otherwise agreed). Resumes are typically
            received for at least 30 days.

         4. We will research MRI’s database and contact potential candidates from other
            similar recruitments we have conducted in the past 12 months.

         5. We will canvass MRI’s professional network to identify and reach out to promising
            potential candidates to invite their application.

         6. We will receive and hold all resumes in confidence until the semi-finalists are
            chosen for interview. We have found that assured confidentiality will increase the
            number and quality of applicants rather significantly. We acknowledge receipt of
            all resumes and keep candidates apprised of their status at each selection point
            throughout the process.

         7. We will provide you with an overview of relevant information about the candidate
            pool, answer questions, and review selection criterion at each decision point
            throughout the process.

approval for their current site in 1997 and amended plan, which added a garage and showroom, in
1999. In 2000, Merchant Motors amended their site again. In 2008, a drainage swale plan was
submitted, no approval is shown on the plan (All site plan approvals are attached)

constructed in accordance with the approved plan and the snow storage area is provided in the area
approved by the Town of Hooksett Planning Board. Mr. Cote mentioned at Town Council’s August 26,
2026, meeting, Public Input, in a prepared statement, that he had contacted Bruce Thomas, former
Town Engineer, about his water issue with Merchants Auto in 2019. Bruce visited Mr. Cote’s land

277          14.2. Update to Ambulance Service Billing & Collection Policy
278
279   Joseph Stalker, Captain (Fire and Rescue): As a municipality-run ambulance service, the
280   Hooksett Fire Rescue Department has a policy outlining the standardization of billing and
281   collection procedures. In this policy is a process for individuals who have utilized the
282   ambulance service to apply for financial hardship. As it is currently written, requests for all
283   amounts must go before the Town Council for approval in non-public session. The Fire
284   Rescue Department is seeking to modify this policy to allow the Fire Rescue Department -
285   Administration to have the authority to grant these approvals for amounts up to $500.00.
286   Any amount surpassing $500.00 will continue to be forwarded to the town council for
287   approval. We feel this change would be the best use of time for the town administration,
288   town council and Fire Rescue Department.
289
290   R. Lapierre motioned to accept the policy revisions to the Ambulance Service Billing &
291   Collection Policy as amended plus revisions to the paramedic intercept billing
292   paragraph as discussed; seconded by A. Walczyk

299   at this amount.
