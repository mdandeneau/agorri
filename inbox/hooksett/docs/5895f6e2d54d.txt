Title:         Public Hearing per RSA 33:8-a to finance Martins Ferry and North River Road
               project in the amount of $2.8 million.
Meeting:       Town Council - 08 Jan 2025
Department:    Finance
Staff Contact: Christine Tewksbury, Finance Director

BACKGROUND INFORMATION:
RSA 33:8-a requires a public hearing before Council can recommend the bonding article.

Martins Ferry Road Intersection Project – Warrant Article

The Town needs to reconstruct the intersection of Martins Ferry Road and North River Road including
roadway realignment and construction of a new bridge. The purpose of the project will address
speed concerns and will eliminate the risk of flooding and erosion and redirect the stream to its
natural course. It will also eliminate significant maintenance concerns regarding the existing
guardrails and drainage culvert.

Currently, the guardrails on both sides of the bridge are in disrepair. The guardrail on the southeast
corner must be replaced with a bridge rail because replacing the guardrail will not meet code
requirements. Installing a bridge rail will include removing all of the pavement over the entire culvert,
dismantling and replacing a portion of the concrete slab on top of the culvert, re-forming the concrete
slab to accept the bridge rail, then installing the new slab, with a new membrane over the entire
culvert structure and replacing the pavement. In addition to the above, any other areas where the
culvert has deteriorated will have to be repaired or replaced.

If the warrant article is not passed and the work isn’t done, the Town will remain at risk of erosion
along the steep embankment of Messer Brook. The stream edge is extremely close to the near
vertical slope against Martins Ferry Road. Restoring the channel to its original flow pattern will
completely eliminate this potential issue and any future slope maintenance. Two years ago the Town
spent $133,970 on erosion repair along Martins Ferry Road. Because the slope between Martins
Ferry Road and Messer Brook is so steep, the likelihood of additional erosion remains.

The brook as it exists presents a flooding hazard. The 90 degree angles that the brook takes could
result in debris getting hung up and causing backups. In addition, there is an existing sewer main
that runs through the culvert at an elevation close to the water line creating additional risk of flow
restrictions.

The intersection as it currently exists is very flat over the culvert. The minimal cover over the culvert
makes it impossible to properly grade the road to direct runoff away from the road. The proposed
project will include the proper slopes and drainage infrastructure to handle runoff.

The existing culvert has been posted no trucks due to its low structural rating. A new bridge will be
safe for all vehicles to pass over it (although posting the roadway “No Thru Trucks” is still an option if
the Town Council chooses to do this). The design will be completed based on a design speed of 30
miles per hour with a fairly sharp curve to slow traffic down.

If constructed, the bridge will be inspected by the State of New Hampshire Department of
Transportation Bridge Inspection Division every two years (at no cost to the Town). In addition, the
bridge will qualify for State/Federal Funding for future maintenance.

Putting Messer Brook on its natural course has garnered the enthusiastic support of several natural
resource agencies, such as the New Hampshire Wetlands Bureau, the New Hampshire Natural
Heritage Bureau, the New Hampshire Fish and Game Division and the U.S. Army Corps of
Engineers.
The proposed project calls for a Timber Bridge that will beautify and enhance the area.

If the warrant article is not passed and the work isn’t done the Town will spend significant funds
replacing these guardrails.

Public Hearing notice was in the December 20, 2024, edition of the Union Leader.

The estimated cost of the project is $2.8 Million and will be fundedas follows:
Fund Balance $341,167.70 This include the 2022 SB401 Bridge Aid, 2023 SB207 Bridge Aid and the
2023 Block Grant received from the State.
Roadway Impact Fees $380,832.30
Note or Bond $2,075,000.00

STAFF REPORT

To:              Town Council
Title:           Housing Champions Designation
Meeting:         Town Council - 08 Jan 2025
Department:      Community Development
Staff Contact:   Grant McGregor, Town Planner

BACKGROUND INFORMATION:
The NH Housing Champions program was established by the legislature in 2024 with the goal of
creating more housing in NH. The criteria for a municipality to be designated as a Housing Champion
are set forth in NH Code of Administrative Rules BEA 500. The criteria include existing infrastructure
which makes housing production feasible, ordinances the allow for multi-family, 55+, workforce
housing, and other housing deemed important by the State of NH, in addition to several other
considerations.

Hooksett achieved the designation and is now eligible to receive matching grant funding and loans
(full funding to be repaid) from NH BEA for water, sewer, stormwater, and highway infrastructure that
will accommodate increased housing production. There is $3.5 million available for infrastructure
grants. Hooksett is also eligible to receive a $10,000 credit for each workforce housing unit that
receives a CO.

FINANCIAL IMPACT:
Matching funds may need to be in place to apply for an infrastructure grant. It is possible that
contributions set forth by a developer can be used as matching funds.

POLICY IMPLICATIONS:
Community Development Department is hopeful that this designation will lead to housing production
to supplement commercial growth and increase of employees working in Hooksett.

RECOMMENDATION:
Special Recognition

SUGGESTED MOTION:
None

TOWN ADMINISTRATOR'S RECOMMENDATION:
The Town of Hooksett is one of 18 communities to receive the designation of housing champions. I
appreciate the efforts of Grant McGregor, Dana Pendergast, and LeeAnn Moynihan for submitting for
this designation.

                                  WARRANT ARTICLE REQUEST FORM


Date of Request: September 24, 2024           Date of Town Meeting: March 2025

Name of Department Submitting Request: Community Development
Martins Ferry Road Project
1. Please provide the wording of the proposed article.
To see if the town will vote to raise and appropriate the sum of $2,800,000.00
(gross budget) for the reconstruction of the intersection of Martins Ferry Road and
North River Road including roadway realignment and construction of a new bridge
(the Project); of that amount, to appropriate $344,167.70 from fund balance; to
appropriate $380,832.30 of that amount from Roadway Impact Fees; and to
authorize the issuance of bonds or notes of not more than $2,075,000.00 of that
amount in accordance with the provisions of the Municipal Finance Act (RSA 33)
and to authorize the Town Council to fix the date, maturities, denominations,
interest rate and other details of said bonds or notes; to authorize the Town Council
to apply for, accept and expend any federal, state, or private funds that are
available in respect of the Project to reduce the amount that must be bonded or to
pay debt service on such bonds or notes; and furthermore to raise and appropriate
the sum of $61,875.00 from taxation for the bond issuance costs and first years
debt service payments on such bonds or notes? The estimated tax rate impact for
first year payment is $0.02. (3/5 ballot vote required.)

2. What is the intent and purpose of article?
To reconstruct the intersection of Martins Ferry Road and North River Road
including roadway realignment, realignment of Messer Brook and construction of a
new bridge. The purpose of the project will address speed concerns and will
eliminate the risk of flooding and erosion and redirect the stream to its natural
course. It will also eliminate significant maintenance concerns regarding the
existing guardrails and drainage culvert.

3. If this article is not passed at Town Meeting or approved by the Town
Council, what affect would this have on your department goals and
programs?
If the warrant article is not passed, the Town will spend significant funds replacing
guard rails and performing other repairs of the existing culvert including
dismantling and replacing a portion of the top concrete slab on top of the culvert
and replacing the pavement. Without this project, the Town will remain at risk of


FINANCE/Forms/Warrant Articles Request Form           Latest revision: October 2011

                                                                                      2
erosion along the steep roadway embankment adjacent to Messer Brook.             In
addition, safety will not be improved.

Note that three years ago the Town spent $133,970 on erosion repair along Martins
Ferry Road. Because the slope between Martins Ferry Road and Messer Brook is
so steep, the likelihood of the occurrence of additional, ongoing, costly erosion is
exceedingly high. This project eliminates the possibility of any significant erosion.

4. Estimated cost?
The estimated cost of the project is $2,800,000.00 and will be funded as follows: 1)
State’s portion comes from one-time funding of 2022 SB401 and 2023 SB270 for a
total of $344,167.70, which is currently in the Town’s fund balance. 2) The Town
Council has/will obligate Roadway Impact Fees in the amount of $380,832.30. 3) a
bond in the amount of $2,075,000.00. The estimated cost to finance $2,075,000.00
over 20 years with an interest rate of 3.75% would cost the Town an estimated
$583,000.00 in interest over the life of the note. Payments would range from
$129,000.00 or 4 cents at today’s assessed valuation for the second year to
$71,000.00 or 2 cents at today’s assessed valuation in the last year.

5. Is any further information necessary for the deliberation?
If constructed, the bridge will be inspected by the State of New Hampshire
Department of Transportation Bridge Inspection Division every two years (at no
cost to the Town). In addition, the bridge will qualify for State/Federal Funding
for future maintenance.

Putting Messer Brook on its natural course has garnered the support of several
natural resource agencies, such as the New Hampshire wetlands bureau, the New
Hampshire Natural Heritage Bureau, the New Hampshire Fish and Game Division
and the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers.

Title:           Proposal for Emergency Medical Services (EMS) education

Department:      Fire and Rescue

BACKGROUND INFORMATION:
Annually the department provides emergency medical education and training to it's members through
a contracted vendor. This continuing education is required of the members who hold state EMS
licenses and national registry certifications. In the past, this has been renewed annually, however the
department has opted to enter into a 3 year contract for the following: Doing so locks us into lower
rates, saving the department money out of the ambulance revenue account. Secondly, it alleviates
the time and effort needed to renew in years 2 and 3. An RFP process, as outlined in the town's
administrative code, was performed and we received one (1) proposal back from a vendor; the
TrueNorth Group.

For the past 6 years, our department has contracted with the TrueNorth Group for EMS education
services. Throughout these years, the education provided by his company has been engaging,
professional and up-to-date on modern day medicine. Having this caliber of education for our
department directly contributes to the high quality service we provide every day. In addition to the
continuing education provided, the TrueNorth Group also provides the department with their CPR
recertifications as well as specialty certifications for the Paramedics.

FINANCIAL IMPACT:
Total cost of $40,950 paid over the course of three (3) years from the Ambulance Revenue Account.
(Year 1: $13,150, Year 2: $13,650 and Year 3: $14,150)

POLICY IMPLICATIONS:
Purchasing policy from the Hooksett Administrative Code followed through the RFP process.

RECOMMENDATION:
Recommend to accept the proposal from the TrueNorth Group for a 3 year contract to provide EMS
Education Services to the Town of Hooksett, NH Fire Rescue Department for a cost of $40,950.

SUGGESTED MOTION:
Motion to accept the proposal as presented; a three (3) year contract by the TrueNorth Group to
provide EMS Education Services to the Town of Hooksett, NH Fire Rescue Department for the cost of
$40,950.
