1. Two 15-minute Public Input sessions will be allowed during each Council
   Meeting. Time will be divided equally among those wishing to speak, however,
   no person will be allowed to speak for more than 5 minutes.
2. No person may address the council more than twice on any issue in any
   meeting. Comments must be addressed to the Chair and must not be personal
   or derogatory about any other person.
3. Any questions must be directly related to the topic being discussed and must
   be addressed to the Chair only, who after consultation with Council and Town
   Administrator, will determine if the question can be answered at that time.
   Questions cannot be directed to an individual Councilor and must not be
   personal in nature. Issues raised during Public Input, which cannot be resolved
   or answered at that time, or which require additional discussion or research,
   will be noted by the Town Administrator who will be responsible for researching
   and responding to the comment directly during normal work hours or by
   bringing to the Council for discussion at a subsequent meeting. The Chair
   reserves the right to end questioning if the questions depart from clarification to
   deliberation.
4. Council members may request a comment be added to New Business at a
   subsequent meeting.
5. No one may speak during Public Input except the person acknowledged by the
   Chair. Direct questions or comments from the audience are not permitted
   during Public Input.

BACKGROUND INFORMATION:
RSA 33:8-a requires a public hearing before Council can recommend the boning article. This project
is part of the overall system project planned for the Exit 10 sewer system project. This current
project includes upgrades to the Martins Ferry force main, upgrades to the Martins Ferry Pump
Station, and a new double barrel sewer force main under Merrimack River. Future work (not
included with this bond request) includes the sewer pipe installation to the business in the Route
93, Exit 10 area (such as Home Depot, Market Basket, Walmart, etc…). Providing sewer will
provide significant economic and environmental benefits to the area. Several of the major
businesses in the area are having issues with their septic systems. This project will resolve
those issues and allow for more development.

A breakdown of how the funding will be allocated is as follows:

a. Upgrades to the Martins Ferry force main                            $5,250,000
b. Upgrades to the Martins Ferry pump station                          $2,500,000

d. New Kimball Drive Pump Station                                      $3,500,000

Total:                                                                $12,500,000
Note: Pipe installation from the new Kimball Drive Pump
Station to the Exit 10 businesses will be part of a future project.

TIF District Explanation: State law (RSA 164-K) allowed the Town Council to designate
a specific underdeveloped commercial and industrial area as a Tax Increment Finance
District. The current assessed value of the TIF when established and the tax revenue
that is generated from the district is determined to be the baseline value. Any new value
that resulted from development within the district is called a Tax Increment and would
be used to help finance any new spending for infrastructure improvements within the
specified district. Other than the Sewer Commission funding noted above, this revenue
will be the only funding used for this project.

Title:         Public Hearing per RSA 33:8-a to finance the new scale house and retaining wall
               at the Transfer Station in the amount of $1,325,840.00.
Meeting:       Town Council - 14 Jan 2026
Department:    Finance
Staff Contact: Ben Berthiaume, Public Works Director

BACKGROUND INFORMATION:
RSA 33:8-a requires a public hearing before Council can recommend a bonding article. In March of
2025, voters approve funding for the engineer and design of a new scale house. The town hired
Warrenstreet Architects Inc. to design a cost effective, utilitarian pre-engineered metal building to
accommodate the needs of the recycling and transfer facility. In November of 2025 the Recycling &
Transfer Advisory Board recommended the proposed project amount of $1,325,840.00.

The existing scale house is a repurposed garage that does not provide adequate services for
employees or the public and is at the end of its useful life. The existing retaining wall for demolition
and bulky waste is made of stacked concrete waste block that has shifted over time and needs to be
replaced. The new scale house and retaining wall will meet current building codes and labor
department requirements and create an improved and safer experience for the residents.

Public Hearing notice was in the December 31,2025 edition of the Union Leader.


FINANCIAL IMPACT:
The bond will be paid off using the fees collected at the Recycling and Transfer facility and held in the
Solid Waste Disposal Fund. Over the last five years, the average collected in fees has been
$161,033.00 per year. As of October 31, 2025, there was $140,467.00 available in the fund.

The estimated cost to finance the full amount of the work of $1,325,840.00 over 20 years with an
interest rate of 4.30% would cost the Town an estimated $594,851.00 in interest over the life of the
note. Payments would range from a high of $130,000 in the second year to $67,000 in year 20.

No general tax dollars will be used as this bond would be paid off using the Solid Waste Disposal
Special Revenue Fund.

2. Ben Berthiaume, DPW Director will give presentation
3. Listen to public input
4. Chair will close Public Hearing

STAFF REPORT

To:              Town Council
Title:           Removal of Lock on Gate on North Candia Road (Class VI)
Meeting:         Town Council - 14 Jan 2026
Department:      Community Development
Staff Contact:   Grant McGregor, Town Planner

BACKGROUND INFORMATION:
The Conservation Commission previously requested that a lock be placed on a gate on North Candia
Road. Shooting activity on property fronting North Candia Road prompted the Conservation
Commission to make the request relative to Conservation Easements that the Commission stewards.
It was unknown at the time that Class VI Roads are required by State Law (RSA 231:21-a) to be
accessible for all public travel. Per RSA 231:21-a, I, the Town Council has the authority to "regulate
such structures to assure such public use, and may cause to be removed any gates or bars which fall
into disrepair or otherwise interfere with public use of the highway."

Since Fall 2025, when a request was sent to the Fire Dept to remove the lock on the gate, one of the
gate posts has been removed and the gate opened (unauthorized). The Town Council's authority
under RSA 231:21-a, I to "regulate such structures to assure such public use, and may cause to be
removed any gates or bars which fall into disrepair or otherwise interfere with public use of the
highway." should be used to determine permanent solution to the gates and bars in question.


RECOMMENDATION:
The recommendation is to repair the gate in disrepair with the former lock mechanism
removed/replaced in accordance with the DPW Director and Fire Captain's suggestions or to remove
the gate.

SUGGESTED MOTION:
Motion to repair the gate in disrepair with the former lock mechanism removed/replaced in
accordance with the DPW Director and Fire Captain's suggestions.

OR

Motion to remove the gate.

TOWN ADMINISTRATOR'S RECOMMENDATION:
I agree with the suggested motion.

                                                 TRANSPORTATION
                              CHAPTER 231
              CITIES, TOWNS AND VILLAGE DISTRICT HIGHWAYS
                                                  Laying Out Highways
                                                         Section 231:21-a

     231:21-a Uniform Provisions Governing Class VI Highways. –
  All class VI highways, whether such class VI status resulted from a layout pursuant to RSA 231:21, a
  discontinuance subject to gates and bars pursuant to RSA 231:45, or by the failure of the town to maintain and
  repair such highway in suitable condition for travel thereon for 5 successive years or more as set forth in RSA
  229:5, VII, shall be subject to the following provisions:
  I. All such highways shall be deemed subject to gates and bars; provided, however, that any gates or bars
  maintained by private land owners shall be erected so as not to prevent or interfere with public use of the
  highway, and shall be capable of being opened and reclosed by highway users. The selectmen may regulate such
  structures to assure such public use, and may cause to be removed any gates or bars which fall into disrepair or
  otherwise interfere with public use of the highway.
  II. Even though, as set forth in RSA 231:93, class VI highways are not subject to any municipal duty of care or
  maintenance, the municipality shall have the same regulatory authority over such highways as is the case with
  class V highways, including but not limited to the authority to regulate their use pursuant to RSA 41:11 and RSA
  47:17, VII, VIII and XVIII, to regulate the excavation or disturbance of such highways pursuant to RSA 236:9
  through 236:11, to regulate driveways and other access pursuant to RSA 236:13, and to establish weight limits
  pursuant to RSA 231:191.

418   17.3 Adoption of SB245 Ambulance Billing Rates
419
420   Joseph Stalker, Captain: Earlier this year, the State of NH signed into law Senate Bill 245, which
421   eliminates balance billing and sets standard reimbursement rates for ambulance services by insurance
422   carriers. This bill applies to those with private insurance such as Anthem, Harvard Pilgrim, etc. and
423   does not affect Medicare, Medicaid or VA patients. In addition to eliminating balance billing, this bill sets
424   standard rates for ambulance services to bill insurance carriers at 325% of the current Medicare rates.
425   In order for this to be put in place, the HFRD will establish contracts with each of the major insurance
426   carriers, which they are required to take part in. To be in compliance with this law, the HFRD is seeking
427   to adopt this rate as outlined in Senate Bill 245
