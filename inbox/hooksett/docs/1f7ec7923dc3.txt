      PUBLIC INPUT
        1. Two 15-minute Public Input sessions will be allowed during each Council
           Meeting. Time will be divided equally among those wishing to speak, however,
           no person will be allowed to speak for more than 5 minutes.
                   Anyone requesting auxiliary aids or services is asked to contact
                the Administration Department five business days prior to the meeting.
                                                                                                  Page 2 of 97
2. No person may address the council more than twice on any issue in any
   meeting. Comments must be addressed to the Chair and must not be personal
   or derogatory about any other person.
3. Any questions must be directly related to the topic being discussed and must
   be addressed to the Chair only, who after consultation with Council and Town
   Administrator, will determine if the question can be answered at that time.
   Questions cannot be directed to an individual Councilor and must not be
   personal in nature. Issues raised during Public Input, which cannot be resolved
   or answered at that time, or which require additional discussion or research,
   will be noted by the Town Administrator who will be responsible for researching
   and responding to the comment directly during normal work hours or by
   bringing to the Council for discussion at a subsequent meeting. The Chair
   reserves the right to end questioning if the questions depart from clarification to
   deliberation.
4. Council members may request a comment be added to New Business at a
   subsequent meeting.
5. No one may speak during Public Input except the person acknowledged by the
   Chair. Direct questions or comments from the audience are not permitted
   during Public Input.

Date of Request: 7/27/2026                        Date of Town Meeting: March 2027

Name of Department Submitting Request: Assessing

1. Please provide the wording of the proposed article.

Capital Reserve Funding – Revaluation
To see if the Town will vote and appropriate the sum of $220,000.00 to be added to the
Revaluation Capitol Reserve Fund previously established. The sum to come from fund
balance and no amount to be raised from taxation. The estimated tax rate impact is
$0.00.

2. Voters’ Guide Explanation.
As of August 31, 2026, the account has $29,852.98. This money is being set aside to
help pay for the Town’s next property revaluation, which will begin in late spring 2028.

The Town must reappraise property values every five years to make sure property taxes
are fairly distributed. This requirement comes from the New Hampshire State
Constitution, Article 6, and RSA 75:8.

For the past four revaluations, the Town has completed a Statistical Update. The costs
were approximately:
   • 2009: $161,231
   • 2013: $137,300
   • 2018: $114,000
   • 2023: $124,000+
The estimated cost of the 2028 Statistical Update is $250,000.

Passing this article will allow the Town to continue setting aside money needed to pay
for the 2028 revaluation and meet the Town’s legal requirement to update property
values.

3. If this article is not passed at Town Meeting or approved by the Town Council,
what affect would this have on your department goals and programs?

If this article continues to fail, the Town will need to include the full $220,000.00 in both
the operating budget and default budget for FY 2028–29. This would cause a significant
increase, or “spike,” in the tax rate for that year.

4. Is any further information necessary for the deliberation?
No

Proposed Reorganization Plans by the Administrator
In accordance with Section 10.12 of the Town Charter, the Town Administrator hereby submits the following

Background
The Administration Department currently consists of the following positions:
        Executive Assistant
        Project Coordinator
        Human Resources Coordinator
        Part-Time Administrative Assistant
The Town Administrator currently serves in a dual capacity as both the chief administrative officer of the Town and the
Director of Economic and Community Development. While this structure has allowed the Town to address community and
economic development needs, the continued growth and complexity of these responsibilities have demonstrated a need
for dedicated leadership within the Community Development Department.
Additionally, the Town is undertaking a transition in leadership due to the impending retirement of the current Town
Administrator. Recruiting and retaining a future Town Administrator with responsibility for both general administration
and community and economic development functions may prove challenging and may not provide either area with the
level of attention necessary to achieve the Town's long-term goals.
Furthermore, the Town Council identified as a 2022 goal the need to evaluate the creation of a Community
Development Director/Department Head position. This proposal is intended to address that goal while establishing a
sustainable organizational structure for the future.

Proposed Reorganization
The Town Administrator proposes:
    1. Reclassifying and elevating the current Project Coordinator position to Assistant Town Administrator.
    2. Assigning the Assistant Town Administrator to serve as Department Head of the Community Development
        Department and as the Town's Economic and Community Development Director.
    3. Transferring responsibility for the day-to-day management and leadership of community and economic
        development functions from the Town Administrator to the new Assistant Town Administrator position.
The Assistant Town Administrator will continue to support the Town Administrator in organizational management while
providing dedicated oversight and leadership of community and economic development initiatives. A proposed job
description outlining the duties, responsibilities, and qualifications of the position accompanies this proposal.
Benefits of the Reorganization
This reorganization will:
      Provide dedicated leadership and management for the Community Development Department.

BACKGROUND INFORMATION:
The Tax Increment Finance (TIF) District Committee recommends moving forward with the Sewer Pipe River Crossing
Project.
The Hooksett Sewer Commission has initiated work on the sewer force main project, which is scheduled for completion in
2027. In addition, the Commission has committed funding toward the Martins Ferry Pump Station upgrade. The next
phase of the overall sewer infrastructure program, and a critical component in providing sewer service within the Exit 10
area, is the construction of a double-barrel sewer pipe crossing beneath the Merrimack River.

The attached project plans provide detailed information regarding the location and installation of the sewer crossing. The
project will begin at the Martins Ferry Pump Station, extend approximately 110 feet/per pipe beneath the Merrimack River,
and terminate at the future Kimball Drive Pump Station site. Upon installation, the pipes will be capped and remain
inactive until funding is secured for construction of the Kimball Drive Pump Station.

The TIF District Committee and the Hooksett Sewer Commission believe this project will serve as a catalyst for future
development within the Exit 10 area by providing critical wastewater infrastructure. The availability of this infrastructure is
expected to encourage businesses and property owners to invest in their portion of the sewer system, advancing
economic development and supporting long-term growth in the district.


FINANCIAL IMPACT:
TIF District Funded- Est. Cost $1M ($455/foot per pipe installed)

POLICY IMPLICATIONS:
Hooksett's Tax Increment Finance (TIF) District plan was created to provide sewer and water
infrastructure within the Exit 10 and 11 area. The infrastructure improvement plan specifically
identifies all improvements proposed within the TIF district, and the sewer line crossing the Merrimack
River is one of the projects identified. The TIF plan includes the funding sources that could be used
to fund projects identified in the plan, including Tax increment. Staff confirmed with legal counsel
state tax increment can be used to fund projects within the district.

RECOMMENDATION:
The Tax Increment Finance (TIF) Committee recommends that Town Council approve the
expenditure of TIF funds, in the amount of $1million, to install double barrel sewer lines from Martins
Ferry Pump station to the west side of the Merrimack River to the proposed location of the Kimball
Drive Pump Station.
