Persian Gulf War Memorial Project Proposal


Request: To install a monument for the Persian Gulf War in Veterans Park at Jacob Square.

Requestor: John H. Danforth, Post Chaplain, Hooksett Merrill-Follansbee American Legion Post
#37, on behalf of Post #37

Purpose: A dedication to the memory and honor all our residents who served in the Persian Gulf
War.

Background of the War:

The Persian Gulf War consisted of three major operations, Desert Shield, Storm and Calm. The
catalyst for the Gulf War was the invasion of Kuwait by Iraq on August 2, 1990. When UN
resolutions 660 and 661 failed to persuade the Iraqi government to withdraw, as a deterrent to
further invasions two US navy Battle Groups were deployed to the Persian Gulf and arrived on
August 8, 1990, followed by the deployment of US Air Force F-15s from the 1st Fighter Wing to Saudi
Arabia and began around the clock patrols of the borders with Iraq.

This began the buildup of military forces and other resources from the United States and the 42
Coalition nations, culminating with UN Resolution 678 of November 29, 1990. The resolution gave
Iraq a deadline of January 15, 1991, to withdraw from Kuwait and return the country to its legitimate
government. When Iraq failed to comply with Resolution 678 the air campaign and naval
bombardment was launched on January 16, 1991, thus the beginning of Operation Desert Storm.
The ground assault which would liberate Kuwait was officially launched on February 24, 1991,
while simultaneously US and Coalition forces invaded Iraq.

The subsequent advance was quicker than had been anticipated with reaching as far north in Iraq
as the Tigris and Euphrates Rivers when a cease fire was agreed upon taking effect on February 28th
and Iraq fully withdrew from Kuwait and the Kuwaiti government was returned to power.

The legacy of the war for those who served has changed from one of liberation of a country which
was overtaken and oppressed by a bully of a neighbor to one of physical and mental illnesses such
as posttraumatic stress disorder (PTSD), neurological diseases as well as various cancers related
to exposures from the oil well fires, (700 oil wells were set ablaze by the Iraqi Army upon their
retreat) as well as depleted uranium (used in ammunition greater than 20mm). Exposure to sarin
gas, which has also led to countless illnesses was delivered by various means, Scud Missiles,
released by Iraqi ground troops and unwittingly via detonation of Iraqi weapons and ammunition
dumps upon discovery by US and Coalition Forces.

Monument/Memorial Specifics:

Location: Veterans Park at Jacob Square; please see appendix A, location options. It has been
ensured the three location options which will not crowd or obscure any other memorials and will
not encroach on the property of the Hooksett Congregational Church.

Dimensions: The dimensions are the same as the Legion memorials which were installed in Head
and Martins-Ferry cemeteries. The measurements are three feet wide by four and one-half feet tall
(includes height of the base), the base measures four feet wide, one foot tall with an eight-inch top.

Title:           Multi-Factor Authentication Policy - Review, adopt and add to the Personnel Plan
Meeting:         Town Council - 14 May 2025
Department:      Administration
Staff Contact:   Leann McLaughlin, Project Coordinator

BACKGROUND INFORMATION:
Implementing a multi-factor authentication (MFA) policy is essential to enhance security and protect
sensitive information. As cyber threats continue to evolve, relying solely on traditional username and
password credentials is no longer sufficient. Passwords can be easily stolen, guessed, or
compromised through phishing attacks and data breaches. MFA adds an additional layer of protection
by requiring users to verify their identity through two or more factors—in this case, it would be
something they know (password) and something they have (a mobile device or security token).
The Town of Hooksett continues to enhance its cybersecurity practices. As part of the transition to a
.gov domain, all employees, elected officials, and volunteers with a town-issued email address will be
required to migrate their accounts to the new .gov platform and enable multi-factor authentication
(MFA) as part of the process.

This policy amendment has been reviewed by legal counsel, Primex, and the Management Team. It
has also been forwarded to the Unions for input; however, as of the date of this staff report, no
feedback or concerns have been received.

This update serves as an amendment to the existing Computer Policy outlined in the Personnel Plan.
The highlighted section reflects the proposed addition.
