        1. Two 15-minute Public Input sessions will be allowed during each Council
           Meeting. Time will be divided equally among those wishing to speak, however,
           no person will be allowed to speak for more than 5 minutes.
        2. No person may address the council more than twice on any issue in any
           meeting. Comments must be addressed to the Chair and must not be personal
           or derogatory about any other person.
        3. Any questions must be directly related to the topic being discussed and must
           be addressed to the Chair only, who after consultation with Council and Town
           Administrator, will determine if the question can be answered at that time.
           Questions cannot be directed to an individual Councilor and must not be
           personal in nature. Issues raised during Public Input, which cannot be resolved
           or answered at that time, or which require additional discussion or research,

STAFF REPORT

To:              Town Council
Title:           Town Council 2024-2025 Goals Update
Meeting:         Town Council - 23 Apr 2025
Department:      Administration
Staff Contact:   Geraldine Ciardelli, Executive Assistant

BACKGROUND INFORMATION:
Town Council had a workshop on October 2, 2024 to establish short and long-term goals that will
move Town's vision forward. The following were
the goals set forth with a plan to report updates at the second Town Council meeting of each month:
Priority 1- Goal #3: Townwide Services – Recycling, Condo Trash and Water Distribution to
Well Areas
    Get recycling reinstated
    Remove credits/reimbursements for condo trash pickup
    Coordinate a NHDES workshop for citizens that have had their water tested by NHDES
       (Farmer Road / Auburn Road area)
    Work with water precincts, citizens and businesses to distribute public water (Farmer Road /
       Auburn Road; Radioactive / contaminated water)
Priority 2 - Goal #1: Improve Communication with Council, Staff, Unions and Community.
    Better communication with Town Council & staff
    Better prepared with all information pursuant to the respective request
    Further stabilization of labor relations with long-term labor agreements with unions and
       retaining key staff within the budget
    Renew partnership with SNHU
    Identify business/public perception of Town departments (i.e. UNH Survey model)
    Council Retreat for consensus on “big picture” of the Town
Priority 3 - Goal #2: Town Administrator Budget
    Remove sewer fees / interest from Board of Assessors calculations. Stop the town from footing
       the bill for sewer fees / accounts that are in arrears.
    Limit and/or consolidate warrant articles based on need vs. want
Priority 4 - Goal #5: Community Development & Master Plan
    Determine purpose for a Community Development Director / department head.
    Coordinate the opening of Hooksett’s Merrimack River Airport (sea planes)
    Continue to support/fund T.I.F. District
Priority 5 - Goal #4: Hooksett NHDOT Roadway Improvements
    NHDOT Hooksett Road Improvement current and future projects reporting (i.e. quarterly) to the
       Town to ensure Hooksett residential and business interests are met

To:            Town Council
Title:         Police Department purchase of 6 new Mobile Data Terminals from Northeast MDT
               in the amount of $33,030.13
Meeting:       Town Council - 23 Apr 2025
Department:    Police Department
Staff Contact: Justin Sargent, Chief of Police

BACKGROUND INFORMATION:
The Hooksett Police Department currently operates with ten Mobile Data Terminals (MDTs), which
are the tablets installed in each frontline police vehicle. As of October 1, 2025, six of these ten units
will become obsolete, as they will no longer support Windows updates. This presents a significant
security risk and renders them unsuitable for law enforcement use.
These MDTs are essential tools used to create reports, upload body camera footage, conduct records
checks—including warrants and BOLOs—and perform other mission-critical tasks. Additionally, these
six outdated units have recently been flagged as a safety hazard, with reports of spontaneous ignition
causing damage to both personnel and town property. An email from Primex providing further detail
on this issue has been included for your review.
The cost to replace the six MDTs is quoted at $33,030.13. These would be purchased from an
approved single-source vendor. It is important to note that, according to the vendor, a delay in
purchasing may result in a cost increase of up to 34% due to anticipated tariffs.
A plan had previously been established to fund these replacements through alternative sources, but
recent budget constraints have made that approach unfeasible. I am proposing to use funds from the
current budget, specifically from the full-time wage line, to cover the cost of these six replacements.

FINANCIAL IMPACT:
$33,030.13

POLICY IMPLICATIONS:

Within the last month, Primex Police members have reported (2) fires involving Patrol PC
Rhino Tab MI, a Portable Tablet MDT, used in cruisers. Details on incidents are:

Both involve Rhino Tab M1portable tablets (MDT’s) One purchased in 2017; the second in
2019.
Installed in 2 different types of vehicles at different departments.
Purchased from and installed by 2 different vendors.
Both fires appeared to originate in their lithium batteries. No definitive cause and origin
investigation has been completed at this time.
Both involved removeable tablets, and both had been plugged into the docking stations.
Both smoked and ignited in cruisers. Substantial losses were incurred but neither resulted in
total vehicle losses. One resulted in an injury.
As a result of these incidents, we recommend:
    All departments utilizing these tablets (MDT’s) review and follow all manufacturer
       guidelines regarding the installation, operation and maintenance of their tablets.
    Special care should be focused on the recharging and power sequence for the tablets.
    We recommend that consideration be given to limiting the charging and plugging in of
       the tablets in vehicles that are unattended.
    Batteries should be inspected for damage and if they are being used beyond the
       manufacturer's recommended service life, consideration should be given to
       replacement if necessary.
    Any department that has had a similar event should contact the manufacturer. If
       possible, please let us know at Primex as well.
