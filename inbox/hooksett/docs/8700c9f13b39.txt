PUBLIC INPUT
  1. Two 15-minute Public Input sessions will be allowed during each Council
     Meeting. Time will be divided equally among those wishing to speak, however,
     no person will be allowed to speak for more than 5 minutes.
  2. No person may address the council more than twice on any issue in any
     meeting. Comments must be addressed to the Chair and must not be personal
     or derogatory about any other person.
  3. Any questions must be directly related to the topic being discussed and must
     be addressed to the Chair only, who after consultation with Council and Town
     Administrator, will determine if the question can be answered at that time.
     Questions cannot be directed to an individual Councilor and must not be
     personal in nature. Issues raised during Public Input, which cannot be resolved
     or answered at that time, or which require additional discussion or research,
     will be noted by the Town Administrator who will be responsible for researching
     and responding to the comment directly during normal work hours or by
     bringing to the Council for discussion at a subsequent meeting. The Chair
     reserves the right to end questioning if the questions depart from clarification to
     deliberation.
  4. Council members may request a comment be added to New Business at a
     subsequent meeting.
  5. No one may speak during Public Input except the person acknowledged by the
     Chair. Direct questions or comments from the audience are not permitted
     during Public Input.

Title:         Public Hearing to accept a donation in the form of labor and material from Kiwanis
               in the amount of $20,000 for the Heads Pond Trail "Access for All" project per RSA
               31:95-e
Meeting:       Town Council - 10 Jun 2026
Department:    Community Development
Staff Contact: Bonnie Pierce, Administrative Assistant

BACKGROUND INFORMATION:
Hooksett Kiwanis Donate $20K To the Head's Pond Trail "Access for All" Project Located within the 483-
acre Head’s Pond Conservation Area, the Head’s Pond Trail is a scenic 1.75-mile crushed gravel trail that is
enjoyed by people of all ages and abilities. With its relatively wide, flat trail surface and beautiful natural views,
it has become one of Hooksett’s most popular outdoor recreation destinations.

However, access from the parking area to the trail presents a significant challenge—particularly for wheelchair
users, parents with strollers, seniors, and others with limited mobility. The existing entrance consists of a steep
and rocky slope that can make access difficult, and in some cases impossible.

To address these concerns, the Hooksett Conservation Commission, in partnership with the Hooksett Kiwanis,
has initiated the Head’s Pond Trail “Access for All” project. The project will create a longer, gentler, and safer
approach from the parking area to the trail. The design meets Accessibility Guidelines for Outdoor
Recreation Areas and Trails and includes two paved accessible parking spaces and updated signage.

The Hooksett Kiwanis have a long history of involvement with the Head’s Pond Trail. In 2008, the Kiwanis
spearheaded the original trail development by organizing volunteers to construct the trail along a former
railroad bed. Since then, the organization has remained an active partner through support for trail signage, trail
maintenance, and construction of a bog bridge in 2022.

The Hooksett Kiwanis have now offered to support the “Access for All” project through a generous
$20,000 cash donation. The donation will be paid directly to Advanced Excavating and Paving, LLC, the
contractor selected for the project. Construction is anticipated to begin in mid-June 2026.

The Town will receive the donation through labor provided by Advanced Excavating and Paving, LLC.

BACKGROUND INFORMATION:
Dear Town Administrator Garron and Hooksett Town Council.

My wife Trish and I would like to thank the Council for the opportunity for us to bring the POW/MIA Mission to
Hooksett. The urgent, yet continuing mission behind these flags is both personal and sacred to us.

On November 7, 2019, with bipartisan support from the 116th Congress, the National POW/MIA Flag Act
became federal law, requiring that the POW/MIA flag be displayed at federal facilities on all days that the flag
of the United States is also displayed. It is only fitting that towns and cities who also honor and respect the
sacrifice of those for whom the flag flies take a role in helping to educate the public regarding this essential
mission.

On May 27th we approached the Town Council with a request to donate a POW/MIA Flag to the Town to be
flown at Veterans Park. After the meeting my wife and I discussed the matter further and realized that this one
flag is not enough and that we are called to do more. We hereby amend our donation request to include
POW/MIA flags for all the available municipal flagpoles in Hooksett. We respectfully request the Town Council
to consider and accept our donation of POW/MIA flags for each of these locations.

We took an inventory of the current town buildings with flagpoles, and we came up with the following 10
locations:

Town Hall, 35 Main Street
Veterans Park, 1 Veterans Drive
Hooksett Fire Station #1, 1 Riverside Street
Hooksett DPW, 210 West River Road
Arthur Donati Field / Larry Brown Memorial, Donati Drive
Donati Memorial Soccer Field, Grant Drive
Hooksett Public Library, 31 Mount St Mary’s Way
Hooksett Central Water Precinct, 10 Water Works Drive
Hooksett Safety Center, 15 Legends Drive
Hooksett Wastewater facility, 1 Egawes Drive

The Flags cost around $35 each, depending on how we can source them. These flags are 3’ x 5”, 3 ply,
double-sided, heavy denier nylon, and are guaranteed made in the USA. We also will donate any necessary
items (clips, rope, etc.) to ensure that the flags are mounted safely and properly.

We recognize the short life expectancy of outdoor flags flown 24/7 year-round, and we are prepared to replace
these flags as they wear out. We anticipate the total estimated annual cost of the project to not exceed
$500.00; however, we are flexible with regard to any reasonable additions to this list.

We are committed to this mission and to those for whom we have dedicated our voices. We believe that this
should not be simply a ‘flag-raising activity’. We ask for permission to schedule an event whereby we would
install these flags in a formal ceremony as a sign of respect for all our POWs, MIAs and their families. We
would coordinate all the pertinent details and keep the Council apprised of all developments, and we welcome
your presence and participation.

We suggest a tentative date of Friday, 26 June 2026 for this event. We would install the flags in the morning,
ending before noon at the Town Hall with a rededication of the Table of Honor currently stationed in the Town
Hall lobby. The invitation to this event shall be extended to any and all local veteran support organizations, as
well as anyone who wishes to support this mission.

Thank you for your support of the POW/MIA Mission.

Until they all come home,

Rich & Trish Caruso

Title:         Accept a donation in the form of labor and material from Kiwanis in the amount of
               $20,000 for the Heads Pond Trail "Access for All" project per RSA 31:95-e.
Meeting:       Town Council - 10 Jun 2026
Department:    Community Development
Staff Contact: Bonnie Pierce, Administrative Assistant

BACKGROUND INFORMATION:
Hooksett Kiwanis Donate $20K To the Head's Pond Trail "Access for All" Project Located within the 483-
acre Head’s Pond Conservation Area, the Head’s Pond Trail is a scenic 1.75-mile crushed gravel trail that is
enjoyed by people of all ages and abilities. With its relatively wide, flat trail surface and beautiful natural views,
it has become one of Hooksett’s most popular outdoor recreation destinations.


However, access from the parking area to the trail presents a significant challenge—particularly for wheelchair
users, parents with strollers, seniors, and others with limited mobility. The existing entrance consists of a steep
and rocky slope that can make access difficult, and in some cases impossible.


To address these concerns, the Hooksett Conservation Commission, in partnership with the Hooksett Kiwanis,
has initiated the Head’s Pond Trail “Access for All” project. The project will create a longer, gentler, and safer
approach from the parking area to the trail. The design meets Accessibility Guidelines for Outdoor
Recreation Areas and Trails and includes two paved accessible parking spaces and updated signage.


The Hooksett Kiwanis have a long history of involvement with the Head’s Pond Trail. In 2008, the Kiwanis
spearheaded the original trail development by organizing volunteers to construct the trail along a former
railroad bed. Since then, the organization has remained an active partner through support for trail signage, trail
maintenance, and construction of a bog bridge in 2022.


The Hooksett Kiwanis have now offered to support the “Access for All” project through a generous
$20,000 cash donation. The donation will be paid directly to Advanced Excavating and Paving, LLC, the
contractor selected for the project.

Construction is anticipated to begin in mid-June 2026.

The donation to the town is in the form of labor, as the cash payment is being made directly to
Advanced Excavation & Paving, LLC.

   BACKGROUND INFORMATION:
   Amendment #1 - Purpose Section
   The current Personnel Plan language in the Purpose section states that employees will be provided with
   replacement pages when policy provisions are updated. In practice, this process is no longer utilized. Instead,
   employees are asked to sign an acknowledgement form each time a change is made.

   Under the Town’s current practice, an acknowledgment form is distributed to employees each time a change to
   the Personnel Plan is made. While this approach ensures timely notification, it has proven to be administratively
   inefficient and can result in multiple acknowledgment requests throughout the year.

   The proposed revision simplifies the current process by allowing employees to continue receiving notifications
   of changes as they occur, while moving to a single annual acknowledgment. This approach keeps employees
   informed while reducing the need for multiple sign-offs throughout the year, making the process more efficient
   and easier to manage for administrative staff.

In accordance with RSA 32:7, all appropriations shall lapse at the end of the fiscal year unless it
meets one of six exceptions:
    1. Encumbered by a legally enforceable obligation created by contract
    2. Capital Reserve Funds and Trust Funds
    3. Issuance of Bonds or Notes
    4. Anticipated Grants from State, Federal or private sources
    5. Special warrant article at properly noticed meeting for which appropriations is available
    6. Special warrant articles that are written longer than one year, but not over five years

subject to change as items may be received or services rendered. Also, additional items may need to
be added to the lists.
