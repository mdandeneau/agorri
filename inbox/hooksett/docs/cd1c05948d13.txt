        1. Two 15-minute Public Input sessions will be allowed during each Council
           Meeting. Time will be divided equally among those wishing to speak, however,
           no person will be allowed to speak for more than 5 minutes.
        2. No person may address the council more than twice on any issue in any
           meeting. Comments must be addressed to the Chair and must not be personal
           or derogatory about any other person.
        3. Any questions must be directly related to the topic being discussed and must
           be addressed to the Chair only, who after consultation with Council and Town
           Administrator, will determine if the question can be answered at that time.
           Questions cannot be directed to an individual Councilor and must not be
           personal in nature. Issues raised during Public Input, which cannot be resolved
           or answered at that time, or which require additional discussion or research,
           will be noted by the Town Administrator who will be responsible for researching
           and responding to the comment directly during normal work hours or by
           bringing to the Council for discussion at a subsequent meeting. The Chair
           reserves the right to end questioning if the questions depart from clarification to
           deliberation.
        4. Council members may request a comment be added to New Business at a
           subsequent meeting.
        5. No one may speak during Public Input except the person acknowledged by the

Stride Together: Hooksett Track & Wellness Project

Submitted By:

Samera Dalal – MSPM Candidate

University of New Hampshire

Date:

August 19, 2025

1. Business Overview / Background

The Hooksett town running track is a highly valued public asset used by residents of all ages for

walking, running, and gathering. However, the current condition of the track and surrounding

infrastructure poses safety concerns, lacks accessibility for individuals with disabilities, and fails

to fully realize the potential of the space as a community wellness hub.

The Stride Together project proposes a comprehensive enhancement of the Hooksett track area

to address these gaps. The project focuses on three key goals:

        •   Safety: Installing protective fencing along the edge nearest to the active railway line.

        •   Accessibility: Constructing ADA-compliant pathways using gravel and

            steppingstones.

        •   Wellness: Incorporating new benches, a strength-training zone, and pull-up/dip

            stations to support diverse outdoor fitness activities.

By improving safety, inclusivity, and wellness-focused design, this initiative aims to modernize

the facility and foster long-term community engagement.

2. Statement of the Business Opportunity

There is a unique opportunity to:

        •   Increase public safety through physical barriers near hazardous zones.

3. Desired Outcomes

       •   ADA-compliant gravel and stone pathways for universal access.

       •   A wellness zone with durable benches and outdoor pull-up/dip stations.

       •   A new post-project initiative: the Hooksett Youth Running Club.

4. High-Level Requirements

       •   Install approximately 1070 square feet of chain-link fence along the rail-facing side.

       •   Lay 10 yards of gravel and 500 steppingstones to form ADA-compliant pathways.

       •   Apply traditional orange and white durable running track paint.

       •   Install 10 ADA-compliant benches in the wellness zone.

       •   Install 2 outdoor pull-up/dip stations with full ADA-compliant installation.

       •   Contract layout and signage installation.

       •   Coordinate procurement, vendor contracting, and volunteer involvement.

       •   Launch and manage the Hooksett Youth Running Club.

5. Strategic Alignment

This project supports the Hooksett Town and Recreation Department’s mission to:

       •   Promote safe, healthy, and accessible public spaces.

       •   Improve town infrastructure with sustainable and inclusive designs.

“In government accounting, fund balance is the difference between operating assets and liabilities
resulting in a surplus or a deficit. A common misconception is that fund balance is a cash account,
associated with or correlated to a government’s bank account balance. But unlike a personal bank
account, a general fund balance is not a “cash-account;” it is a measure of equity between revenues
and expenditures.” Katherine Heck, Government Finance Advisor for NHMA.

The Town's Unassigned Fund Balance Policy is to increase the minimum growth by .75% each year
before applying an up to level of $300,000 to the tax rate until the unassigned fund balance reaches
the 8% target balance. It is essential that the Town maintains an adequate level of unassigned fund
balance to mitigate financial risk that can occur from emergencies and economic downturns.

We are still waiting on NH Department of Revenue to review information with regards to setting the
tax rate.

1. Business Overview / Background

The Hooksett town running track is a highly valued public asset used by residents of all ages for

walking, running, and gathering. However, the current condition of the track and surrounding

infrastructure poses safety concerns, lacks accessibility for individuals with disabilities, and fails

to fully realize the potential of the space as a community wellness hub.

The Stride Together project proposes a comprehensive enhancement of the Hooksett track area

to address these gaps. The project focuses on three key goals:

        •   Safety: Installing protective fencing along the edge nearest to the active railway line.

        •   Accessibility: Constructing ADA-compliant pathways using gravel and

            steppingstones.

        •   Wellness: Incorporating new benches, a strength-training zone, and pull-up/dip

            stations to support diverse outdoor fitness activities.

By improving safety, inclusivity, and wellness-focused design, this initiative aims to modernize

the facility and foster long-term community engagement.

2. Statement of the Business Opportunity

There is a unique opportunity to:

        •   Increase public safety through physical barriers near hazardous zones.

I. Purpose
      .This policy establishes the guidelines for the deployment and management of a
      Department-sanctioned Station Service Dog (hereinafter "K9") to serve as a resource for
      Critical Incident Stress Management (CISM), general wellness, and department
      morale. The K9's primary purpose is to aid in the emotional decompression of all
      personnel following stressful events and to contribute to a positive and supportive station
      environment. The Station Dog is a working animal, and is authorized to operate within all
      Department facilities under the direct control of the assigned Handler.


II. Application
All members of the Hooksett Fire Rescue Department shall follow this guideline

III. Procedure

A. K9 Handler Assignment and Responsibility

 1. Exclusive Handler

The K9 shall be exclusively assigned to one (1) designated Handler. The Handler is responsible
for the K9’s daily care, welfare, and training to ensure it meets the temperament standards
required for its Certification and morale duties. The handler is responsible for the overall well-
being of the service dog. This includes, but is not limited to:
* Ensuring the dog is always under their control or the control of an approved designee.
* Carrying appropriate supplies for the dog, such as food, water, waste bags, and a first-aid kit.
 * Promptly cleaning up after the dog.
* Ensuring the dog is not a disruption to daily operations or emergency response activities.

 2. Training and Certification

 The K9 must have completed a 1-year in-house obedience training, Certified AKC Canine good
 citizen (CGC, AKS Urban CGC, and AKC Advanced CGC.


3. Work Environment and Control

The K9 shall accompany the Handler during all assigned duty shifts and shall remain under the
direct control of the Handler. When the K9 is engaging with personnel, the Handler must
maintain control via leash, voice commands, or other effective means. The K9 may be excluded
from specific operational areas (e.g., patient care compartments, hazmat zones) at the discretion

The service dog's involvement in public outreach and events is intended to enhance community

* The dog should be on a leash/harness or electronic collar and under control.
* The public should be educated on how to properly interact with the service dog.
* The handler or designee reserves the right to remove the dog from any situation that becomes
overly stressful for the animal or is not conducive to its role.

from the K9’s actions during its employment with the Department.

   •   Handler Termination: The Handler is involuntarily terminated from the Hooksett Fire

   •   The Department’s liability for the K9 ceases on the date of transfer, with the exception of
       the time the K9 was employed by the Department (as outlined in Section 3.2).

197
198    Debie Clayton, HealthTrust Benefits Coordinator- HealthTrust is a nonprofit public risk pool in New
199   Hampshire that has been providing benefits and services to public sector employers and employees for
200   more than 40 years. We strive to set rates that are accurate, meaning the rates set for the upcoming
201   plan year represent our actuary’s best estimate of the amount of contributions needed to cover
202   expected costs of claims and administration, and other expenses, as well as the amount necessary to
203   replenish or maintain the capital adequacy reserve at the target level. As the industry changes and
204   evolves, HealthTrust continues to outperform benchmarks based on a variety of performance
205   indicators, delivering robust benefits at a lower cost than its competitors and the private insurance
206   market.
207
208   Every October the HealthTrust Board of Directors meets to set rates for the following calendar year.
209   Prior to the full Board meeting, there is a Finance Committee meeting and two Public Hearings on
210   Rates. When the Board meets in October, they review the recommendations presented to them by the
211   Finance Committee as well as all of the comments and feedback received at the Public Hearings.
212   Trend is the largest component of the annual cost projection. Trend is an estimate of the amount that
213   medical and prescription drug costs and utilization will increase from when they were incurred during
214   the experience period to when they will be incurred during the rating period. For this time period, a
215   trend factor of 8.4% was utilized. When rates are set in October of 2025 for the following calendar
216   year, trend is compounded over 20-months.
217
218   For medical, the January renewal groups experienced an overall or average rate change of 11.3%,
219   which includes a 5.3% Capital Risk Charge. Rate changes for individual rating entities ranged from 3.7%
220   to 35.6%.
221

222   The Capital Risk Charge is the amount that HealthTrust uses to replenish reserves that were used to
223   pay for unexpected claims. HealthTrust has always offered fixed contribution, non-assessable coverage
224   to our member groups. If claims exceed contributions, HealthTrust utilizes reserves to pay for any
225   losses that may occur in the year and replenishes them through prospective rate adjustments in future
226   renewals. The member group is only responsible for their contributions (i.e., premium) and cannot be
227   held liable (except for prospective rate increases) if claims exceed contributions during the period of
228   coverage.
