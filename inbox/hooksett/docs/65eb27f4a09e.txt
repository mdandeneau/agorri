                       SIGNAGE REGULATION OF TOWN ROADS
                               ORDINANCE # 00-26

The Town of Hooksett ordains that, pursuant to RSA 41:11 of the New Hampshire State Statues
and Section 3.6 of the Hooksett Town Charter, the following ordinance is hereby enacted in the
Town of Hooksett:

SECTION 1

NO PARKING - Parking is prohibited at designated areas as indicated by posted signs. An
inventory list of all approved "No Parking" signs is maintained at the Town Clerk's office and at
the Highway Department for public inspection.

SECTION 1.1

PARKING PROHIBITIONS –
      A. A person shall not park a vehicle upon any street, highway, or town parking facility
         for the principal purpose of displaying it for sale, washing, maintenance or repairs;
         repairs of an emergency nature will be excluded.
      B. A person shall not park a commercial vehicle (as defined below) upon any
         residential street, or town parking facility at any time, unless the actual loading or
         unloading of materials is occurring thereof.
      C. A person in violation of this provision shall be subject to fines and penalties as set
         forth in Administrative Enforcement of Parking Violations Ordinance #00-28, under
         “NO PARKING ZONE”. Violators can be removed under the direction of the
         Hooksett Police Department. Charges for towing and storage shall be paid by the
         owner for release of the vehicle.

Commercial Vehicle: A motor vehicle or trailer, not including a resident’s personal vehicle that
has been modified to accommodate a disability, or a vehicle used exclusively for agriculture or
farming when located on and accessory to a permitted agricultural use, that exhibits one or
more of the following characteristics:
       1. Has more than two (2) axles.
       2. Has a height greater than seven (7) feet.
       3. Has a length greater than (19) feet.
       4. Has a Gross Vehicle Weight Rating (GVWR) of greater than ten thousand (10,000)
           pounds.
       5. Is registered as other than “passenger”, “antique” or “street rod” vehicle, and is
           designed, maintained, or used primarily for the transportation of passengers,
           property or freight for hire, compensation, or profit.
       6. Has special ramps, hoists, buckets, cranes, arms, platforms, or similar features
           designed primarily to lift, tow or carry motor vehicles, equipment, trailers, materials or
           workers.

SECTION 2

NO THROUGH TRUCKING - Trucks exceeding three ton GVW are prohibited on roads with
signage marked "No Through Trucking". Exclusions shall be trucks making a delivery to a
home or business on the prohibited road. An inventory list of all approved "No Through
Trucks" signs and designated "No Through Truck" streets are maintained at the Town Clerk's
office and at the Highway Department for public inspection.See No Through Trucking
Ordinance #26-01 for additional information. An inventory list of all approved “No Through
Trucks” signs and designated “No Though Trucking” streets are attached to the ordinance and
maintained at the Town Clerk’s office and at the Highway Department for public inspection.

                                 NO THROUGH TRUCKING
                                    ORDINANCE #26-01

SECTION 1: PURPOSE AND AUTHORITY
The Town of Hooksett ordains that this ordinance is adopted to protect public safety, preserve
roadway infrastructure, reduce noise and vibration impacts to residential neighborhoods, and
maintain the character of local streets by restricting through-truck traffic on designated
roadways.

This ordinance is enacted pursuant to the Town’s police powers and applicable state law
authorizing municipalities to regulate traffic on local ways.

SECTION 2: DEFINITIONS
No Through Trucking:
The use of a roadway by a truck for the purpose of traveling between two points without an
origin or destination on that roadway. Trucks are prohibited from using the roadway as a
shortcut. Trucks are permitted only when they originate, terminate, or must travel on the
roadway to access:
    • A residence
    • A business
    • A construction site
    • A delivery or service location


Truck:
Any vehicle registered or operated as a commercial motor vehicle, including trucks, truck-
tractors, tractor-trailers, combination vehicles, and vehicles bearing a U.S. Department of
Transportation (DOT) number. This definition excludes passenger vehicles.

Local Delivery or Service:
Travel by a truck for the sole purpose of making a delivery to, pickup from, or providing services
at a residence, business, construction site, or other lawful destination located on a roadway
designated as “No Through Trucking.”

SECTION 3: PROHIBITION
  A. No truck operator shall operate a truck on any roadway posted with official “No Through
      Trucking” signage for the purpose of through travel.
  B. Through travel shall mean travel where the truck’s origin and destination are not located
  on, and do not require access from, the posted roadway.

SECTION 4: EXEMPTIONS


                                                                                                     Page 7 of 62
                                                                                              Agenda Item #6.1.


The following vehicles and activities are exempt from this ordinance:
   1. Trucks making a local delivery, pickup, or service call to a residence, business, or
       construction site located on the posted roadway.
   2. Emergency and public safety vehicles while engaged in official duties.
   3. Town, State, or utility vehicles performing construction, maintenance, repair, or
       emergency response.
   4. School buses and municipal transit vehicles.
   5. Vehicles operating pursuant to a temporary written permit issued by the Highway
       Department or Town Administrator for extraordinary circumstances.

SECTION 5: SIGNAGE AND DESIGNATED ROADWAYS
A. Enforcement of this ordinance shall apply only to roadways that are clearly posted with
Town-approved “No Through Trucking” signage.
B. An inventory list of all approved “No Through Trucking” signs and all designated “No
Through Trucking” roadways shall be maintained at:
   • The Town Clerk’s Office;
   • The Highway Department; and
   • As attached to this Ordinance.
Such records shall be available for public inspection during normal business hours.

SECTION 6: ENFORCEMENT
A. This ordinance shall be enforced by the Hooksett Police Department.
B. Enforcement shall be based on the determination of whether a truck is engaged in prohibited
through travel or permitted local access, as defined herein.

SECTION 7: BURDEN OF PROOF FOR EXEMPTIONS
The operator of a truck claiming an exemption under this ordinance shall, upon request by a law
enforcement officer, provide reasonable documentation or explanation demonstrating eligibility
for the exemption, including proof of a local delivery or service call.

SECTION 8: PENALTIES
  A. Pursuant to RSA 265:9, failure to follow the posted traffic control device (No Through
     Trucking) may be cited and subject to enforcement action.
  B. Each occurrence and each day a violation continues shall constitute a separate offense.

SECTION 9: APPEALS
Any person cited under this ordinance may appeal the citation in accordance with applicable
state law governing traffic.

SECTION 10: SEVERABILITY

Title:         Public Hearing for Adoption of New Ordinance #2026-02 Sale of Tax Deeded
               Property As Justice May Require
Meeting:       Town Council - 22 Apr 2026
Department:    Administration
Staff Contact: André Garron, Town Administrator

BACKGROUND INFORMATION:
RSA 80:80, III governs how a municipality handles tax liens and tax deeded property. RSA 80:80
contains a provision that allows the governing body to authorize the disposal of tax liens or tax
deeded property in alternative ways beyond public auction or sealed bid -- "as justice may require."
The phrase "as justice may require" is a provision that gives the Town Council discretion to dispose of
tax liens or tax deeded property in ways other than public auction or sealed bid, when circumstances
justify it.

Specifically, the section of RSA 80:80, III, states, "III. The governing body may, by a specific article in
the town warrant, or by ordinance, be authorized to dispose of liens or tax deeded properties in a
manner other than as provided in this section, as justice may require. Before proceeding under this
provision, the governing body shall make an affirmative finding that disposal by a method other than
sealed bid or public auction is in the public interest." If the Town Council adopts this section of RSA
80:80, III, "as justice may require" allows a range of options designed to serve fairness and municipal
needs such as engaging a real estate agent to market and sell the property, sell undeveloped parcels
to adjoining landowners to support consolidation for affordable housing, open space or negotiate a
conveyance back to a former owner. It can also address redevelopment, preserve community
character, resolve title complexities or support affordable housing.

Adopting RSA 80:80, III would also provide clarity and reinforce the Town's authority with respect to
prior and future tax-deeded property sales.

           SALE OF TAX DEEDED PROPERTY AS JUSTICE MAY REQUIRE
                            ORDINANCE #26-02


SECTION 1 – PURPOSE AND INTENT
The purpose and intent of this ordinance is to authorize the Town Council to sell tax deeded
property by means other than through public auction or sealed bid, as justice may require.


SECTION 2 – AUTHORIZATION
This ordinance is adopted pursuant to the authority set forth in RSA 80:80, III.


SECTION 3 – REQUIREMENTS
The Town is entitled to sell a tax deeded property “as justice may require”, and thus in a manner
not involving public auction or sealed bid, so long as the Town Council finds that it is in the
public’s interest to do so.


SECTION 4 - DEFINITIONS
For purposes of this ordinance, and consistent with RSA 80:80, VI, the term “as justice may
requires” means that the Town Council, after making an affirmative finding that sale of the
property other than by public auction or sealed bid is in the public interest, may sell a property in
one of the following ways:

   A. Engage a real estate agent or broker to list and sell the property, including a sale
      conditional on the buyer's obtaining development approvals;

   B. Sell undeveloped parcels to abutters for consolidation into adjoining lots for the purpose
      of affordable housing development, preserving open space, or reducing development
      density; or

   C. Convey the property to a former owner, or to a third party for benefit of a former owner,
      upon such reasonable terms as may be agreed to in writing, including the authority of the
      municipality to retain a mortgage interest in the property, or to reimpose its tax lien,
      contingent upon an agreed payment schedule, which need not necessarily reflect any
      prior redemption amount. Any such agreement shall be recorded in the registry of deeds.
      This paragraph shall not be construed to obligate any municipality to make any such
      conveyance or agreement.

SECTION 5 – EXPIRATION OF AUTHORITY
The authority provided in this ordinance shall continue indefinitely, until rescinded.

BACKGROUND INFORMATION:

Samera Dalal is currently completing her Master of Science and Project Management capstone
project, which focuses on enhancing the track at Donati Park through the Stride Together – Hooksett
Track & Wellness Project.

To date, Samera has successfully secured full funding for four ADA-compliant benches. Donation
documentation has been submitted to the Department of Public Works, which will forward the
materials to the Town Council for formal acceptance in accordance with Town procedures.

The primary remaining funding need is for the installation of perimeter fencing. This component is
critical to the project, as it is the most important safety component, ensures controlled access and
aligns with the Town’s risk management standards. Samera is requesting $18,800 of funding support
from the Town for this essential safety feature.

In addition to fundraising efforts already completed, Samera has been engaging local businesses for
sponsorship opportunities and has obtained three estimates for each major component of the project.
She is also pursuing additional funding sources, including a grant through the New Hampshire
Charitable Foundation. As part of this process, Town Council approval is required for her to apply for
grant funding on behalf of the Town.

Samera has been working closely with Public Works staff, including Ben Berthiaume, Evelyn Horn,
and Rob Hebert, throughout the project. She also presented the project to the Parks and Recreation
Advisory Board on March 16, 2026, at which time the Board supported her moving forward to the
Town Council to request funding.

For transparency and proper financial handling, all donation checks have been collected by Samera
and submitted to the Department of Public Works, where they were received in accordance with
Town procedures. These donations will be brought forward to the Town Council for formal
acceptance.

Additional details regarding the project scope, timeline, budget, and funding progress are included in
the attached materials.

 The objective of the Stride Together – Hooksett Track & Wellness Project is to enhance a high-use

 public recreational asset by improving safety, accessibility, and wellness infrastructure. The project

 adds value to the Town of Hooksett by reducing safety risks, improving ADA compliance, and

 increasing inclusive recreational opportunities for residents of all ages and abilities.

 This project aligns with the Town’s strategic priorities for community wellness, safety, and

 inclusivity, as well as the Parks & Recreation Advisory Board’s goals for accessible recreational

 infrastructure. Expected results include the installation of protective fencing, an ADA-compliant

 walkway, inclusive fitness equipment, and seating amenities.

 Key deliverables include:

Stride Together – Hooksett Track & Wellness Project


                                        Executive Summary

The Stride Together – Hooksett Track & Wellness Project is a community-driven initiative

focused on improving safety, accessibility, and wellness at the Hooksett Town running track. The

project will enhance existing infrastructure to better serve residents of all ages and abilities

through the installation of protective fencing, an ADA-compliant asphalt walkway, inclusive

fitness equipment, benches, and rubber mulch surfacing.

This project received Hooksett Town Council approval on November 5, 2025, and is being

coordinated in collaboration with the Hooksett Departments of Public Works and Parks &

Recreation.

                                         Project Overview

Background:

   •   Public recreational projects improve health equity, social cohesion, and long-term

       engagement.

   •   The Hooksett running track is widely used but currently has safety hazards near an active

       railway, limited accessibility, and insufficient fitness amenities.

Project Goals:

   1. Safety: Install protective fencing along the railway edge.

   2. Accessibility: Build an ADA-compliant walkway with Asphalt.

   3. Wellness: Provide inclusive fitness equipment, benches, and Fiber wood surfaces to

       encourage active lifestyles.

Outcomes:

   •   Safer, fully accessible track environment.

   •   Increased community participation.

   •   Launch of the Hooksett Running Club with sustainable programming.
