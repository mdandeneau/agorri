      PUBLIC INPUT
        1. Two 15-minute Public Input sessions will be allowed during each Council
           Meeting. Time will be divided equally among those wishing to speak, however,
           no person will be allowed to speak for more than 5 minutes.
        2. No person may address the council more than twice on any issue in any
           meeting. Comments must be addressed to the Chair and must not be personal
           or derogatory about any other person.
        3. Any questions must be directly related to the topic being discussed and must
           be addressed to the Chair only, who after consultation with Council and Town
           Administrator, will determine if the question can be answered at that time.
           Questions cannot be directed to an individual Councilor and must not be
           personal in nature. Issues raised during Public Input, which cannot be resolved
           or answered at that time, or which require additional discussion or research,
           will be noted by the Town Administrator who will be responsible for researching
           and responding to the comment directly during normal work hours or by
           bringing to the Council for discussion at a subsequent meeting. The Chair
           reserves the right to end questioning if the questions depart from clarification to
           deliberation.
        4. Council members may request a comment be added to New Business at a
           subsequent meeting.
        5. No one may speak during Public Input except the person acknowledged by the
           Chair. Direct questions or comments from the audience are not permitted
           during Public Input.

To:              Town Council
Title:           FY 2025-26 Budget and Warrant Articles
Meeting:         Town Council - 22 Jan 2025
Department:      Finance
Staff Contact:   Christine Tewksbury, Finance Director

BACKGROUND INFORMATION:
1) Operating Budget: (This information is subject to change at the Budget Committee's Public
Hearing on January 16, 2025)
The Town Council recommended a budget of $22,683,776.00 and the Sewer Commission
recommended a budget of $2,717,419.00 for a combined total of $25,401,195.00.

The Budget Committee reviewed these budgets and made the following changes:
Added $2,000.00 to both the Finance and Wastewater budget for increased costs related to the
annual audit.
Added $16,000.00 to DPW's electric lines based on the increase in electric supply rates.

The Budget Committee's recommended operating budget for FY 2025-26 is $25,421,195.00.

2) Default Budget:
Approve and sign the FY 2025-26 Default Budget in the amount of $24,502,751.00. See Attached
State form 2025 MS-DTB and 2025-26 Default.

3) Warrants:
Confirm attendance at the Deliberative Session, Saturday February 1, 2025, and reassign articles if
necessary. See attached Warrant Article List.


FINANCIAL IMPACT:
The tax rates below are only estimates to provide an idea of what the cost would be if the voters
approved all proposed articles.

The operating budget of $25,421,195.00 less anticipated revenues in the amount of $10,353,525.00
has a net effect on the tax rate of $4.70. If all the proposed warrant articles were to pass, this would
add 73 cents for a total Town share of $5.43 an increase of 61 cents over the current town share of
the tax rate of $4.82.

STAFF REPORT

To:              Town Council
Title:           Town Council 2024-2025 Goals Update
Meeting:         Town Council - 22 Jan 2025
Department:      Administration
Staff Contact:   Geraldine Ciardelli, Executive Assistant

BACKGROUND INFORMATION:
Town Council had a workshop on October 2, 2024 to establish short and long-term goals that will
move Town's vision forward. The following were
the goals set forth with a plan to report updates at the second Town Council meeting of each month:


Goal #1: Improve Communication with Council, Staff, Unions and Community.
   Better communication with Town Council & staff
   Better prepared with all information pursuant to the respective request
   Further stabilization of labor relations with long-term labor agreements with unions and
      retaining key staff within the budget
   Renew partnership with SNHU
   Identify business/public perception of Town departments (i.e. UNH Survey model)
   Council Retreat for consensus on “big picture” of the Town

Goal #2: Town Administrator Budget
   Remove sewer fees / interest from Board of Assessors calculations. Stop the town from footing
      the bill for sewer fees / accounts that are in arrears.
   Limit and/or consolidate warrant articles based on need vs. want
Goal #3: Townwide Services – Recycling, Condo Trash and Water Distribution to Well Areas
   Get recycling reinstated
   Remove credits/reimbursements for condo trash pickup
   Coordinate a NHDES workshop for citizens that have had their water tested by NHDES
      (Farmer Road / Auburn Road area)
   Work with water precincts, citizens and businesses to distribute public water (Farmer Road /
      Auburn Road; Radioactive / contaminated water)
Goal #4: Hooksett NHDOT Roadway Improvements
   NHDOT Hooksett Road Improvement current and future projects reporting (i.e. quarterly) to the
      Town to ensure Hooksett residential and business interests are met

58   Public Hearing Opened
59
60   B. Thomas- The Town needs to reconstruct the intersection of Martins Ferry Road and North River
61   Road including roadway realignment and construction of a new bridge. The purpose of the project will
62   address speed concerns and will eliminate the risk of flooding and erosion and redirect the stream to
63   its natural course. It will also eliminate significant maintenance concerns regarding the existing
64   guardrails and drainage culvert. Currently, the guardrails on both sides of the bridge are in disrepair.
65   The guardrail on the southeast corner must be replaced with a bridge rail because replacing the
66   guardrail will not meet code requirements. Installing a bridge rail will include removing all of the
67   pavement over the entire culvert, dismantling and replacing a portion of the concrete slab on top of
68   the culvert, re-forming the concrete slab to accept the bridge rail, then installing the new slab, with a
69   new membrane over the entire culvert structure and replacing the pavement. In addition to the above,
70   any other areas where the culvert has deteriorated will have to be repaired or replaced. If the warrant
71   is not passed and the work isn’t done, the Town will remain at risk of erosion along the steep
72   embankment of Messer Brook. The stream edge is extremely close to the near vertical slope against
73   Martins Ferry Road. Restoring the channel to its original flow pattern will completely eliminate this
74   potential issue and any future slope maintenance. Two years ago, the Town spent $133,970 on erosion
75   repair along Martins Ferry Road. Because the slope between Martins Ferry Road and Messer Brook is
76   so steep, the likelihood of additional erosion remains. The brook as it exists presents a flooding hazard.
77   The 90-degree angles that the brook takes could result in debris getting hung up and causing backups.
78   In addition, there is an existing sewer main that runs through the culvert at an elevation close to the
79   water line creating additional risk of flow restrictions. The intersection as it currently exists is very flat
80   over the culvert. The minimal cover over the culvert makes it impossible to properly grade the road to
81   direct runoff away from the road. The proposed project will include the proper slopes and drainage
82   infrastructure to handle runoff. The existing culvert has been posted no trucks due to its low structural
83   rating. A new bridge will be safe for all vehicles to pass over it (although posting the roadway “No Thru
84   Trucks” is still an option if the Town Council chooses to do this). The design will be completed based on
85   a design speed of 30 miles per hour with a fairly sharp curve to slow traffic down. If constructed, the
86   bridge will be inspected by the State of New Hampshire Department of Transportation Bridge

158   G. McGregor- The NH Housing Champions program was established by the legislature in 2024 with the
159   goal of creating more housing in NH. The criteria for a municipality to be designated as a Housing
160   Champion are set forth in NH Code of Administrative Rules BEA 500. The criteria include existing
161   infrastructure which makes housing production feasible, ordinances the allow for multi-family, 55+,
162   workforce housing, and other housing deemed important by the State of NH, in addition to several
163   other considerations. Hooksett achieved the designation and is now eligible to receive matching grant
164   funding and loans (full funding to be repaid) from NH BEA for water, sewer, stormwater, and highway
165   infrastructure that will accommodate increased housing production. There is $3.5 million available for
166   infrastructure grants. Hooksett is also eligible to receive a $10,000 credit for each workforce housing
167   unit that receives a CO. Based on the master plan and the projected growth the need for housing units
168   will be needed. The Town of Hooksett is one of 18 communities to receive the designation of housing
169   champions.
