Meeting Minutes
                      North Hampton Planning Board
                      Tuesday, October 20, 2026 — 6:30 p.m.
                      Town Hall, 231 Atlantic Avenue


Call to Order and Attendance
Vice Chair Jennifer Kotzen called the work session to order at approximately 6:30 p.m. All Planning
Board members were present, and a quorum was established. Members identified in the recording
were Jennifer Kotzen, Shep Kroner, Nancy Monaghan, John Sillay, Phil Wilson, and Laura Sher.
Jonathan Pinette, ex-officio Planning Board member and Select Board representative, was also
present. Town Administrator Michael Tully attended. Carol Ogilvie, a consultant supporting the
Town’s planning and zoning functions, participated remotely; she is not a Planning Board member.

I. Continued Business — 2026–2027 Zoning Ordinance Amendments
Lot line adjustments on nonconforming lots. Ms. Kotzen explained that the Board had previously
voted to advance the proposed amendment to a public hearing, but the hearing had not been noticed
during the administrative transition. Members discussed taking up the matter at the next Planning
Board Work Session, November 17, 2026.
Ms. Monaghan moved to advance the lot line adjustment amendment proposal to the November
17, 2026 work session. Second by Mr. Pinette. The vote was unanimous in favor of the motion (7-
0).
R-1 and R-2 district descriptions. Members considered draft revisions to the names and definitions of
the R-1 and R-2 districts. Mr. Kroner questioned whether the descriptions should expressly recognize
accessory dwelling units (ADUs). Ms. Kotzen considered ADUs accessory uses rather than necessary
elements of the district descriptions. Mr. Wilson suggested addressing ADUs in the permitted-uses
list.
Ms. Monaghan moved to advance the R-1/R-2 district description amendment to a public
hearing on November 17, 2026. Second by Mr. Pinette. The vote was unanimous in favor of the
motion (7-0).
Mr. Tully asked whether the Board wanted both a marked-up version and a clean consolidated version
of the proposed changes; Ms. Kotzen indicated both would be useful.
ADU size requirements. Mr. Kroner summarized the Long Range Planning Committee’s consensus
proposal to permit ADUs from 600 to 950 square feet, compared with the existing 720-to-800-square-
foot range described during the discussion. The committee had considered a 400-square-foot
minimum but had not reached consensus. Members discussed affordability, the conversion of existing
garages, the relative impacts of smaller and larger units, potential short-term rentals, septic systems,
impervious surfaces, groundwater and wetland impacts, and the possibility of further state legislative
changes.
Mr. Sillay questioned the evidence and practical meaning of the stated impervious-surface thresholds.
Mr. Wilson discussed natural filtration and the environmental tradeoffs of sewer and septic systems.
Mr. Pinette raised questions about regional water use and enforcement of owner-occupancy
requirements. Ms. Sher questioned whether an ADU could exceed the size of the principal dwelling.
Ms. Kotzen favored an incremental approach while acknowledging the need to expand housing
options.
Ms. Monaghan described a separate, preliminary committee concept to amend workforce-housing
provisions to permit smaller, potentially 400-to-600-square-foot units subject to affordability
requirements. She emphasized that this concept was not part of the ADU amendment then under
discussion and had not been voted upon.
Members reviewed draft ADU text replacing “footprint” with square footage, setting a proposed 600-
square-foot minimum and 950-square-foot maximum, defining floor-area measurements, and
renumbering affected subsections. The Board discussed receiving a clean proposal at its next meeting
and considering whether to advance it to a December public hearing. Ms. Monaghan expressly
clarified that her suggestion was not a motion. No vote on the ADU proposal was taken. It will be
discussed further at the November 17, 2026 Planning Board Work Session.

II. New Business — Senate Bill 564
The Board discussed SB 564, described at the meeting as signed July 10, 2026 and effective April 1,
2027. Members considered potential effects on municipal regulation of utility placement within
setbacks, perimeter and residential buffers, wetlands buffers, conservation areas, and subdivision open
space. Ms. Ogilvie identified February 2 as the deadline for submitting final ballot language to the
Town Clerk and explained that some changes may instead belong in site-plan or subdivision
regulations, which have a different adoption process.
Questions included the status of raised septic systems as structures, wetlands and aquifer protections,
conditional-use and special-exception authority, conservation easements, and whether existing
ordinance provisions could remain enforceable. Members distinguished the bill’s treatment of
ordinary buffers and setbacks from wetlands buffers and conservation areas but did not reach a
definitive legal interpretation.
Ms. Ogilvie agreed to research the bill and provide a memorandum of findings. The Board expected to
continue its discussion at a subsequent meeting. No motions were made.

III. Other Business
Committee updates. The Long Range Planning Committee update was covered in the ADU
discussion, including the possible separate workforce-housing proposal. No update was reported for
the Rules and Procedures Committee. Ms. Monaghan indicated that the Capital Improvements
Committee had completed its work for the year. No Select Board update was reported. Mr. Kroner
indicated he had no update from the Rockingham Planning Commission (RPC). Ms. Ogilvie had no
further Planning and Zoning Administrator update.
Upcoming applications. Members discussed a workforce-housing application, a possible additional
application described in the transcript as involving a park or an undefined use, and the preliminary
consultation previously held for 14 Lafayette Road. Mr. Tully said no further submission had been
received for 14 Lafayette Road. The Board also briefly discussed the RKG Financial report and
zoning decision expected in connection with the workforce-housing matter. Mr. Tully offered to
circulate Mr. Sununu’s presentation materials and a link to the Channel 22 recording.

Approval of Minutes
Mr. Pinette moved to approve the October 6, 2026 Planning Board meeting minutes. Second by
Mr. Wilson. The vote was unanimous in favor of the motion (7-0).
Mr. Pinette moved to approve the October 13, 2026 Planning Board meeting minutes. Second by
Mr. Wilson. The vote was unanimous in favor of the motion (7-0).
