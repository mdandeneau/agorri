27   Discuss and understand Question 2 on November 3 ballot
28   Chairman Martino said this question appears on the ballot November 3 and there is nothing the Budget
29   Committee can say or recommend.
30
31   State Representative Jim Maggiore said HB-1300 was introduced in the House in 2025 as HB-675 and
32   defeated on the floor; ended up as non-general amendment introduced in election law, a bill for
33   redistricting of congressional districts. It then became HB-1300, passed House 183-170 and is now law,
34   and the State mandates that it go on every ballot. He said there are two parts to the bill and both require
35   3/5 majority to pass; if passed, it then becomes a tax levy for the North Hampton Elementary School.
36
37   Mr. Maggiore said the tax cannot exceed last year’s tax levy except for CPI plus any bonds and increases
38   to taxable revenues. There is an override procedure (RSA 32-5) you can consider. Each town would have
39   to have override on its ballot. He said it does not affect the municipal tax rate, only the school portion.
40   Selectman Sununu asked for clarification about new construction and whether the tax cap only applied to
41   the line item in the School Budget for SAU. Mr. Maggiore said your questions came up many times but we
42   did not have the specificity and clarity for us to have confidence to answer, and said he also has a 7-page
43   document from DRA asking questions that are still not fully flushed out and answered.

36   Economic Review and Discussion - James Sununu
37
38   Selectman Sununu said the data being presented is mostly national and state data. He started with
39   Quarterly GDP Growth in the country and said the pattern is lukewarm and cold, and the trillions being
40   spent on AI Data Centers is what is propping up the economy (Quarter 2 GDP growth 2026, 2.2%). He said
41   CPI was coming down in 2024 and 2025 to 2% federal target but has picked up and is recently 3.7% and
42   not going in the right direction; only lever is for feds to increase interest rates.
43
44   Unemployment Rate shows more than a decade of almost unprecedented slow to steady unemployment
45   and remains fairly tight but the employment economy is pretty steady. New Hampshire Median Income
46   is a bit erratic, ramps up in 2012 with big drop in 2022 because of inflation and lag of adjusting wages;
47   trend line is steadily rising but state may have impact with more people retiring. Wage and Salary Growth
48   private vs public sector; public sector wages lag private sector wages as public sector wages are contract

 3   personnel wages and benefits.

 5   10-Year Treasury Rates: Dramatic changes in last few years; 10-Year Treasuries are usually the bench mark
 6   to determine mortgage and bond rates; most significant were rates bottomed out in 2020 and started to
 7   rise again steeply and rapidly; still at historic low point for rate. Mortgage Rates 30-year Fixed: it is
 8   significant when mortgage rates go up that high, on the heels of an historic low. People are reluctant to
 9   leave a 2% mortgage and take on one at 7%, a major issue that affects housing and the natural transition
10   of a community. Mr. Heitman asked if portable rates like in Canada and Europe would alleviate the
11   situation; Selectman Sununu said it would but is not legal in the United States.
12
13   New Hampshire Housing Prices: Up about 55% in the last few years with median household income at
14   26%; increasing dramatically; New Hampshire has been through 2 other housing cycles which were not as
15   dramatic a runoff as it is now. He said this affects who can move into the community anywhere on the
16   seacoast and said New Hampshire Housing shows an affordability gap, needing a median income of
17   $103,503 to afford a $160,996 median home.
18
19   New Hampshire Housing Facts: June 2026 median home price is now $575,000, needed to afford is income
20   of $189,000, about $91/hr; median effective rent is $2,511/month with $80,000/yr to afford, New
21   Hampshire needs 76K more housing units by 2040, production must rise from 5K to 8K units/year. Rent
22   has slowed to 2% increase year-over-year. The single biggest issue in the state is housing affordability.
23   Labor Force Participation: New Hampshire 64.8%, national 61.81%, both trending down largely due to
24   retirement.
25
26   Labor Force by Age Group: 1992: 11% of work force over 55, 2024: 33% over age 55; these workers, more
27   likely to be on fixed income, will be retiring and affected by property tax increases and in need of more
28   services; we need to insure a strong stream of younger workers to drive economic growth. Social Security
29   trust fund will run out in 2032; money paid in today is going out to today’s retirees. If Congress does
30   nothing there will be a 22% benefit cut. In New Hampshire 1/3 of people are collecting social security and
31   it would be catastrophic.
32
33   North Hampton Tax Rate History: Shows combined tax rate, where North Hampton ranks in the state,
34   showing downward drops for revaluations; similar to other towns. Tax Rates by Function: Shows School,
35   municipal and county with a gap between town and school which has dropped over the years. Chairman
36   Martino asked if the school has done any demographic work on 15 years from now. Mr. Ferreira said it is
37   relatively superficial but every year they do a contract with NESDEC on enrollment projections based on
38   birth rate and said he would include the data in the budget materials.
