 7 In attendance: Robert S. (“Shep”) Kroner, Chair; Jennifer Kotzen, Phil Wilson, Nancy Monaghan, John Sillay,
 8 Laura Sher, and Jonathan Pinette; Michael Tully, Town Administrator; and Attorney Will Reddington, legal
 9 counsel representing the Planning Board.
10 Chair Kroner called the public meeting to order at 6:00pm on October 13, 2026, in the North Hampton Public
11 Library Program Room. He stated that the meeting had been called to address the requested amendments to
12 the Greystone Village restrictive covenants following the Board’s discussion the previous week.

13 I. Continued Business

14 1. Applicant: J & S Greystone Village, LLC, Joseph Roy, P.O. Box 1627, North Hampton, NH 03862.
15 The applicant requested an alteration of the property’s restrictive covenants. Property Owner: J & S Greystone
16 Village, LLC, P.O. Box 1627, North Hampton, NH 03862. Property Location: 223 Lafayette Road; M/L: 021-001-
17 000; Zoning Districts: I-B/R, Industrial – Business/Residential District, and R-1, High Density District.
18 In attendance for this application:
19 Scott Anders, attorney for the applicant/seller.
20 Mr. Kroner explained that, in his view, the prior discussion had become circular because the Board had not
21 received the proposed covenant changes in complete document form. A redlined declaration was now before
22 the Board. He described the covenants as part of the project’s 2005 approval and financial restructuring and
23 stated his opinion that the sale-approval provision restricted trade. He emphasized that the Board was being
24 asked to consider the proposed amendments.
25 Mr. Sillay identified language in Section 5.12 referring to approvals required under Section 5.11 that would no
26 longer be required if the proposed amendments were adopted. Counsel suggested removing the introductory
27 cross-reference as a condition of approval and providing for final review by Town Counsel. Ms. Sher identified
28 a similar cross-reference in Section 5.9.
29 Ms. Kotzen asked whether broadly deleting references to the Planning Board could also remove protections
30 unrelated to sale approval or the certified property manager requirement. Counsel clarified that the proposed
31 deletions concerned those two requirements and related references, not Section 12.1, which addressed Board
32 consent to covenant amendments. Ms. Kotzen and Mr. Sillay stressed the importance of retaining the
33 amendment-approval requirement and restrictions relating to senior housing, the number of home sites, and
34 stone walls.
35 Mr. Wilson identified two typographical errors for correction. Counsel recommended conditioning any
36 approval on final review of the document by Town Counsel. Ms. Kotzen noted that the additional cross-
37 reference corrections were not yet reflected in the document before the Board.
38 Mr. Wilson questioned whether removing the Board’s approval of a future sale would weaken enforcement of
39 the remaining covenants. He cited the requirement for a principal occupant over age 55 and restriction on
40 occupants under 18, the 68-home-site limit, protection of stone walls and rural character, and environmental
41 sensitivity of undeveloped portions of the property. He distinguished the ability to consent to future
42 amendments from the ability to refuse consent and expressed concern about preserving the protections on
43 which residents had relied when purchasing their homes.

44 Counsel responded that the proposal concerned two specific requirements and that the Board would retain its
45 right to consent to or withhold consent from other covenant amendments. He stated that removing sale
46 approval would not remove enforcement mechanisms for the remaining restrictions. In discussing potential
47 violations, he distinguished Board revocation of site plan approval from enforcement action by the Town. He
48 later identified Article VI’s provisions for enforcement through Rockingham County Superior Court and
49 arbitration.
50 Mr. Kroner compared the proposal with other approvals containing restrictions that continued after a sale
51 without requiring Board approval of the transfer. Mr. Wilson distinguished conservation easements held by
52 third parties and described concerns about enforcement costs and willingness to pursue violations. He stated
53 that residents had invested savings in homes placed on leased land in reliance on the existing covenants and
54 that he was concerned about the prospective purchaser’s reported reference to future projects.
55 Ms. Kotzen stated that she had opposed the sale approval at the prior meeting. She questioned whether
56 retaining approval of future sales could provide an additional incentive for an owner to comply with the
57 covenants. Mr. Sillay said he preferred to avoid reviewing every sale while ensuring that the other restrictions
58 could not be removed or modified without the required approval. He requested assurance from Town Counsel
59 that the remaining protections would be effective.
60 Mr. Kroner referred to the Board’s prior acceptance of an associate property manager and questioned the
61 value of retaining sale approval when consent could not be unreasonably withheld. Ms. Kotzen and Mr. Wilson
62 asked what would constitute reasonable grounds for withholding approval, particularly in light of concerns
63 about residents’ investments and continued ability to live in the community. Counsel stated that an objection
64 had to be supported by articulable grounds and cautioned against refusing a sale based on speculation about
65 future amendments or conduct.
66 Ms. Kotzen asked whether the purchaser could provide assurances to residents. Counsel distinguished asking
67 the purchaser to explain its plans from requiring rent restrictions. Mr. Wilson suggested a possible guarantee
68 to purchase a resident’s home at market value if continued occupancy became unaffordable; counsel stated
69 that such a condition was outside the Board’s jurisdiction. These suggestions were discussed but were not
70 adopted as conditions.
71 Mr. Pinette asked whether the Board could separately approve removal of the certified property manager
72 requirement. Mr. Wilson said he did not object to removing that requirement and recalled that the original
73 applicant had proposed the particular credential. Counsel said that the applicant had submitted the
74 amendments together and recommended action on the proposal as presented rather than splitting the
75 requests. Ms. Monaghan later also raised the possibility of removing the manager requirement while declining
76 the other change. No separate motion on the manager requirement was completed.
77 Ms. Kotzen questioned the practical value of retaining sale approval if the Board would not withhold approval
78 under the circumstances already presented. She distinguished approving the pending amendments from
79 reconsidering the prior sale approval. Mr. Wilson suggested revising the document to retain sale approval but
80 remove the certified manager requirement as a counterproposal. Mr. Sillay supported preserving protection
81 against later changes even if the pending sale approval remained in place.
82 The Chair and counsel stated that reconsideration of the prior sale approval should be addressed at a
83 separately noticed meeting. Counsel stated that such action would need to occur within 30 days of the
84 approval decision. No motion to reconsider or rescind the prior sale approval was made or voted upon at this
85 meeting.
86 Mr. Pinette asked what could occur if the Board declined or deferred action and the transaction failed. Counsel
87 described possible litigation and potential damages, while acknowledging that the outcome and amount could
88 not be predicted. Mr. Sillay emphasized that possible liability was different from established liability and
89 questioned whether failure to satisfy one prospective purchaser’s terms would establish a compensable loss.
90 Counsel repeatedly cautioned against assuming that the prospective purchaser would displace residents or
91 violate the covenants without evidence.

 92 Ms. Kotzen expressed concern that higher land rents could force residents to leave homes that could not
 93 readily be moved or sold for their invested value. She asked for engagement with the purchaser and more
 94 concrete assurances. Mr. Kroner questioned the economics of the reported purchase price and rental income.
 95 Mr. Sillay also questioned what changes might be needed to achieve the purchaser’s expected return. Counsel
 96 characterized conclusions about future rent increases or development based on the purchase price as
 97 speculative.
 98 Mr. Kroner asked why the redline would also remove the transferee’s written attestation that it would comply
 99 with the declaration and perform its duties and monitoring requirements. Counsel responded that a
100 subsequent owner would take the property subject to recorded restrictions. Mr. Sillay asked counsel to clarify
101 his role; counsel confirmed that he represented the Planning Board and that his firm represented the Town.
102 Mr. Tully noted that a representative of the seller was available to speak.
103 Mr. Pinette observed that the current owner could also raise rents and that enforcement of the remaining
104 covenants would continue under counsel’s explanation. He expressed concern about exposing the Town to
105 litigation and favored approval with the discussed corrections. Mr. Sillay distinguished the current owner’s
106 established community relationships from uncertainty about the purchaser. Ms. Kotzen stated that her
107 decision depended on whether the Board could reasonably withhold approval based on the concerns raised.
108 At Ms. Kotzen’s request, Attorney Anders addressed the Board for the applicant/seller. He stated that he had
109 been told that the purchaser would withdraw if the amendments were not approved and that the intent would
110 then be to sue the Town. He characterized the sale-approval restriction as an unlawful restraint on trade and
111 described possible damages in terms of the difference between the pending offer and a later sale. Mr. Wilson
112 and Mr. Sillay disputed that the Board had imposed a new restraint, noting that the covenants had been
113 proposed by the original applicant and were already recorded.
114 Ms. Kotzen asked why the purchaser considered the changes essential. Attorney Anders discussed possible
115 financing and future resale concerns and said the purchaser was acquiring properties to hold rather than
116 immediately resell. In response to Mr. Wilson’s appraisal question, he said the appraisal recommended seeking
117 removal of the sale-approval requirement. He stated that the purchaser was not seeking removal of the
118 environmental restrictions, but only the sale-approval and certified property manager requirements.
119 Mr. Sillay again asked for the Board’s counsel’s opinion about the asserted illegality of the sale-approval
120 provision and sought private consultation. Mr. Tully explained that the Board could vote to enter a nonpublic
121 session during the posted meeting and could use another room.
122 Mr. Sillay moved that the Planning Board meet with legal counsel in nonpublic session under RSA 91-
123 A:3II(e). The Motion was Seconded by Mr. Kroner.
124 Vote was 6 to 0 in favor of the motion with 1 abstention by Ms. Sher
125 The Board left the public session to meet with counsel at 7:40 pm
126 The Board returned to public session, and Mr. Kroner announced that it was back from recess at 8:03 pm
127 Mr. Pinette proposed approval of the amendments with Section 12.1 unchanged. Mr. Kroner suggested
128 introductory wording identifying the proposed amended covenants shared by Attorney Anders on October 8,
129 2026. Mr. Pinette incorporated the discussed cross-reference and typographical corrections, and Mr. Sillay
130 seconded the motion.
131 Mr. Pinette moved that the Planning Board consent to the proposed amended restrictive covenants as
132 shared by Greystone’s attorney, Scott Anders, on October 8, 2026, subject to the following conditions:
133      a. Any reference to Planning Board approval in Section 5.11 be removed.
134      b. The final draft be reviewed by Town Counsel.
135      c. No changes be made to Section 12.1.
136 Second by Mr. Sillay.

137 Discussion of the motion – Members clarified that Section 12.1 would remain unchanged and discussed the
138 scope of deletions in Section 5.11 and related references. Counsel confirmed that the final draft would be
139 reviewed by Town Counsel. At Mr. Wilson’s request, counsel restated the motion twice; Ms. Monaghan
140 confirmed that no changes to Section 12.1 were included. Mr. Pinette accepted the final restatement. The final
141 restatement retained the three conditions above.
142 The vote was 6-1 in favor of the motion with Mr. Wilson opposed.
143 Mr. Kroner stated that the agenda was concluded, Mr. Wilson called for adjournment, and Mr. Kroner declared
144 the meeting adjourned at 8:07 pm
