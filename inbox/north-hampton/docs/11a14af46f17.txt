Meeting Minutes
                       North Hampton Planning Board
                       Tuesday, October 6, 2026 at 6:30pm

These minutes were prepared as a reasonable summary of the essential content of this meeting, not as a tran-

In Attendance: Robert S. (“Shep”) Kroner, Chair; Jennifer Kotzen, Phil Wilson, Nancy Monaghan, John Sillay,
Laura Sher, and Jonathan Pinette; and Rick Milner. All seven Board members and Mr. Milner were seated at
the table in person at the start. There were no changes in Board attendance during the meeting apart from the
general recess.
Chair Kroner called the meeting to order at 6:35pm. He explained that additional public seating had been ar-
ranged at the Library with a Zoom connection so participants could see and hear the Board and be recognized
to speak. Mr. Milner asked whether there were objections to the arrangement; no objection was recorded in
the transcript.

I. Continued Business

1. Case #25:14 — Applicant: EMKG Holdings, LLC, Mark Gagne, 15 Lafayette Road, North Hampton, NH
03862.
The applicant requested an extension of the time limit for satisfying the conditions of approval for proposed
construction of an 11,622-square-foot commercial building to be used for automobile storage. Property
Owner: EMKG Holdings, LLC, 15 Lafayette Road, North Hampton, NH 03862. Property Location: 15 Lafayette
Road; M/L: 003-084-000 and 003-085-000; Zoning District: I-B/R, Industrial – Business/Residential District.
In attendance for this application:
Tim Black, applicant’s representative; Mark Gagne, applicant.
Mr. Black stated that the application was approved on October 7, 2025. He reported that the conditions were
ready for submission except for the fire suppression requirement. The applicant had retained a fire safety engi-
neer and was working with Town fire officials and the State Fire Marshal. He described the need to coordinate
the emergency plan with anticipated changes to fire standards and requested six additional months.
Mr. Milner stated that extensions were flexible and that one year was commonly granted. Mr. Kroner said he
had suggested one year to provide sufficient time. The Board discussed an extension measured from the exist-
ing 2026 expiration date.
Mr. Pinette moved that the Planning Board extend the time for satisfying the conditions of approval by one
year from the existing October 7, 2026 expiration date, through October 7, 2027. Second by Ms. Monaghan.
The vote was unanimous in favor of the motion (7-0).

000; Zoning Districts: I-B/R, Industrial – Business/Residential District and R-1, High Density District. The Board
also considered the proposed sale during the meeting, as recorded below.

Applicant’s attorney Scott Anders; Lisa Bianco, representative of the prospective owner, North Hampton MHC

Mr. Kroner described the matter as administrative business rather than a public hearing. He identified two is-
sues: whether the proposed purchaser met the requirements of the restrictive covenants for the sale, and
whether the Board would consent to changes removing provisions of Section 5.11 under Section 12.1. Mr.
Milner stated that the matter had been separately posted. Mr. Kroner proposed allowing public comment,
avoiding repeated remarks, and focusing on the sale and covenants.
Mr. Wilson moved that the Planning Board find that it had no authority to change the restrictive covenants.
Second by Ms. Monahan.
Discussion of the motion – Mr. Wilson distinguished deed covenants from conditions of site plan approval. He
stated that the covenants ran with the land and that persons granted enforcement rights could choose
whether to enforce them. Mr. Sillay asked who could amend the covenants and whether they continued indef-
initely. Mr. Pinette asked whether legal advice had been obtained. Mr. Milner stated that a Town Counsel com-
munication had been distributed to Board members.
The applicant’s attorney disputed the Board’s interpretation and requested authorization to undo the disputed
provisions, asserting that the Board could consent to their modification. Mr. Kroner recalled that the cove-
nants had arisen through an agreement associated with earlier financial difficulties at the property, rather than
as conditions imposed by the Board.
Mr. Milner restated the motion as a finding that the Planning Board had no authority to change the covenants.
The vote was 5-1-1 in favor of the motion.
The Board then discussed whether the purchaser’s submitted property-management credential satisfied Sec-
tion 5.11. Ms. Kotzen said she did not object to declining enforcement of the certified property manager re-
quirement in the absence of an identified problem. Mr. Wilson questioned whether the submitted credential
was equivalent to the certification identified in the covenants and said residents’ concerns should be heard.
Written correspondence: The applicant’s attorney read a letter from Cindy and John Vinciguerra, 30 Greystone
Way. They supported eliminating the certified property manager and buyer-approval provisions, described sat-
isfactory maintenance, and expressed concern that certification costs would be passed through in lot rent.
Mr. Milner read a letter signed by approximately 30 people concerning the proposed sale of Maple Leaf and
Greystone, limited information about the purchaser, the cost of a resident-owned alternative, and risks to resi-
dents on limited incomes. He also read a further letter asking the Board to disapprove the sale or postpone
action until the purchaser provided future rent information. That letter asserted that the restrictive covenants
permitted the Board to withhold approval reasonably and protected leaseholders.
Public comment: Mr. Kroner invited comments focused on the property-management requirement and sale
and stated that the time was 7:12pm.
Effie Velardo identified herself as the author of the two letters Mr. Milner had read. She described the effects
that increased lot rents and uncertainty about the purchaser could have on elderly residents, the difficulty of
selling or relocating manufactured homes, and her view that the Board could stop the sale under the cove-
nants.
Donna Lynch, 36 Greystone Way, supported removing the certified property manager requirement. She de-
scribed satisfactory existing management and concern about an additional rent increase to cover a certified
manager. She asked why the Board had concluded it could not change the provisions.
Jim Maggiore, 17A Hobbs Road, urged the owners to consider rent stabilization to give residents predictable
increases. He described a possible cap tied to the consumer price index plus 2 percent, no more than annually,
excluding special infrastructure assessments. He presented this as a legislative proposal rather than an existing
requirement.
Nancy Kilcrease, 10 Hickory in Maple Leaf, described the cost of a resident-owned cooperative alternative and
asked for clarification of the purchaser’s identity and the purchase and sale agreement. Mr. Kroner and Mr.

that additional cost to residents, and favored allowing the sale to proceed.
Ms. Kotzen moved that the Planning Board reconsider the earlier finding concerning the Board’s authority to
change the covenants. Second by Ms. Monaghan for discussion.
Discussion of the motion – Members reviewed Section 12.1 and distinguished the owner’s authority to pro-
pose an amendment from the Board’s consent to a specific amendment. Mr. Milner referred to the privileged
Town Counsel communication and gave an interpretation of counsel’s advice. Ms. Sher favored removing the
Board’s name from the document and retaining the first sentence of Section 5.11.
The vote was 6-0-1 in favor of the motion with Mr. Wilson abstaining.
Mr. Milner restated the original motion. Mr. Wilson then withdrew his motion, explaining that the Board could
consent or decline consent to an owner’s proposed change but could not independently change the covenants.
Mr. Pinette stated that he withdrew his second. Mr. Milner stated that the motion was no longer under con-
sideration.
Mr. Wilson moved that the Planning Board approve the purchase and sale of the property as presented by
the applicant. Second by Mr. Pinette.
Discussion of the motion – Mr. Wilson stated that “as presented” included the submitted documentation and
affidavit and requested that the motion retain that wording. Ms. Kotzen asked whether approving the sale
would amend the covenants and expressed concern about effects on residents and the scope of the Board’s
review. Mr. Wilson responded that the sale motion did not amend the covenants and stated his view that the
Board could not regulate future rent increases.
At Ms. Kotzen’s request, Jim Maggiore returned to discuss the possibility of the owners voluntarily following
prospective rent-stabilization proposals. He identified himself as a state representative and distinguished pos-
sible statewide requirements from enabling legislation that could permit local action. Ms. Kotzen clarified that
the suggestion involved a voluntary commitment before legislation became law.
Mr. Sillay questioned whether the sale should be approved before further consideration and suggested that
the Board might consent to the owners’ removal of the disputed provision. Mr. Milner cautioned that the sale
should be considered within the criteria specified in the covenant document and that approval should not be
unreasonably withheld. Mr. Kroner agreed with keeping the review within that scope.
The vote was 4-1-2 in favor of the motion with Ms. Kotzen opposed and Ms. Monaghan and Mr. Sillay ab-
staining.

3. Case #23:20 — Applicant: Vertex Towers, LLC c/o Francis Parisi, 225 Dyer Street, Providence, RI 02903.
The applicant requested an extension of the time limit for satisfying the conditions of approval for construction
of a wireless telecommunications facility/cell tower with associated driveway and utility improvements. Prop-
erty Owners: Richard E. Skowronski and Leila A. Hanna, 142 Mill Road, North Hampton, NH 03862. Property
Locations: Back lots off Mill Road in the vicinity of 142 Mill Road, accessed from the driveway between 140 and
144 Mill Road; M/L: 012-065-000, 012-067-000, and 012-072-000; Zoning Districts: R-1, High Density District
and R-2, Medium Density District.
In attendance for this application:
Francis Parisi, applicant’s representative.
Mr. Parisi reported that the applicant had supplied documentation responding to 14 of approximately 15 con-
ditions, including evidence of Verizon Wireless as an anchor tenant. He expected the remaining work to be
completed by year end and construction to begin after winter, but requested one year to allow time for re-
view. He also described other Seacoast tower projects in response to the Chair’s question.
Ms. Monaghan asked about the anticipated completion of conditions. Ms. Kotzen asked which conditions re-
mained outstanding and noted that Town Engineer and legal review were still needed. Mr. Parisi identified an
updated federal environmental-compliance report as the remaining submission and stated his view that fed-
eral regulatory changes would affect the requirements. He clarified that submitted information had not yet
received all Town approvals.
Mr. Milner explained that Town administration would coordinate review of the legal documents by Town
Counsel and stormwater materials by the Town Engineer. Mr. Parisi clarified that the requested extension cov-
ered the related site plan and conditional use approvals rather than a single permit.
Mr. Sillay moved that the Planning Board grant a one-year extension for the related approvals associated
with the Vertex Towers application. Second by Mr. Pinette. The vote was unanimous in favor of the motion
(7-0).

2. Case #26:19 — Applicant: Granite Post Green MHC, 168 Lafayette Road, North Hampton, NH 03862.
The applicant requested subdivision of a 2.61-acre lot from the 34.70-acre mobile home park property and site
plan review for demolition of the existing restaurant and construction of a vehicle self-storage facility. The ad-
vertised proposal included 16 individual, privately owned condominium storage units of approximately 1,600
square feet, with private social areas containing bathrooms, kitchenettes, lounge areas, loft space, and individ-
ual HVAC systems, within two 12,800-square-foot buildings and a separate car-washing building. Property
Owner: Granite Post Green MHC, P.O. Box 1627, North Hampton, NH 03862. Property Location: 160–164 Lafa-
yette Road; M/L: 017-082-000; Zoning District: I-B/R, Industrial – Business/Residential District.
In attendance for this application:
Drew Olowski, applicant’s representative, Haley Ward; an additional representative Deana Mae Jemery and
Joe Roy.
The representative reviewed the layout and stated that the full subdivision and site plan applications followed
an earlier preliminary consultation. The proposal separated an approximately 2.6-acre parcel from the larger
manufactured housing park.
The representative stated that the application had proceeded using a self-storage classification, requiring a
ZBA special exception, but that the applicant’s later legal memorandum questioned whether self-storage was
the appropriate classification. The applicant had not yet appeared before the ZBA. Requested waivers included
the 300-square-foot maximum storage-unit size and another requirement concerning unit openings. The rep-
resentative said Town Engineer comments could be addressed in a revised submission.
Mr. Kroner referred to staff opinions classifying the use as self-storage and suggested that the applicant could
seek a special exception or challenge the use determination through the ZBA. Mr. Sillay questioned whether
the use fit self-storage, warehouse, or an activity space and raised concerns about overnight or residential use.
Mr. Milner said cooking facilities could affect classification and emphasized that his comments identified issues
for review rather than imposed conditions.
Mr. Wilson questioned whether the plans contained features of a dwelling. Mr. Olowski clarified that the ap-
parent shower was a closet and that the units were proposed with toilets and sinks. Ms. Kotzen distinguished a
warehouse serving a business from self-storage of individuals’ belongings and said more than one use category
might apply. Mr. Sillay noted that the proposed frequent use of the units differed from conventional storage.

Mr. Milner stated that the building inspector and he regarded the use as self-storage and discussed ZBA review
of both the classification and special exception. He distinguished an earlier shared automobile-storage or club
proposal from individually owned units with separate amenities. Members asked whether owners could store
others’ property, rent units, conduct gatherings, or use the spaces without operating-hour limits. Mr. Olowski

independently and referred to a planner’s opinion that the subdivision submission was complete. Members
discussed lot dimensions, frontage remaining on the parent parcel, and existing structures.
Mr. Wilson moved that the Planning Board find the subdivision application complete and take jurisdiction.
Second by Mr. Pinette. The vote was unanimous in favor of the motion.
Mr. Kroner opened the public hearing specifically on the subdivision at 9:22pm. He invited comment on the
proposed new lot and closed the hearing approximately 30 seconds later, stating that no public comment had
been offered. Members clarified that subdivision approval would not constitute site plan approval.
Mr. Kroner asked whether demolition would wait until the use was approved. Mr. Olowski confirmed that it
would.
Mr. Wilson moved that the Planning Board approve the subdivision subject to the following conditions:
a. A certificate of monumentation and a recordable plan.
b. State subdivision approval, if applicable.
c. Driveway permitting and subsurface system approval, if applicable.
d. Recording of the subdivision with the Rockingham County Registry of Deeds.
e. Checks payable to the Rockingham County Registry for required recording fees.
f. Payment by the applicant of all Planning Board costs, including consulting, engineering, and legal fees.
g. No changes to the approved subdivision plan except to satisfy conditions of approval.
Second by Mr. Pinette.
Discussion of the motion – Mr. Sillay asked whether the approval applied only to the subdivision. Mr. Wilson
confirmed that it did and that subdivision approval did not approve the site plan.
The vote was unanimous in favor of the motion (7-0).
