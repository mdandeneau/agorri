 4 Call to Order and Attendance

 5 Chair Shep Kroner called the regular meeting of the Town of North Hampton Planning Board to
 6 order at approximately 6:35 p.m. on Tuesday, November 3, 2026. Regular member Nancy

 8   Board members present were Chair Kroner, Jennifer Kotzen, John Sillay, Phil Wilson, Laura
 9   Sher, Jonathan Pinette (Select Board representative), and seated alternate Timothy Harned. Town
10   Administrator Michael Tully was also present. Temporary Planning and Zoning Administrator
11   Carol Ogilvie, Jennifer Rowden of the Rockingham Planning Commission, and Kyle Talente of
12   RKG Associates participated remotely.

13 I. Continued Business

14 1. Case #26:09 — North Road Realty Investments, LLC
15   The applicant requested lot line adjustment, subdivision and site plan review for a proposed 27-
16   unit multifamily residential development and one commercial lot on M/L 017-098-000 and 017-
17   099-000 in the vicinity of 165 Lafayette Road, together with a conditional use permit for
18   development within the Aquifer Protection District. The properties are owned by the Mary D.
19   Hale Revocable Trust (Mark Hale, Trustee) and Seacoast Birth & Family Connection, LLC,
20   respectively, and are in the I-B/R (Industrial–Business/Residential) District.
21 In attendance for this application:
22 Michael Garrepy representing the applicant was in attendance. Jeff Donahoe was identified as the
23 applicant’s consultant and was present. Kyle Talente of RKG Associates attended remotely as the
24 Town’s financial review consultant.
25   The applicant said it had received RKG Associates’ financial analysis and responses to
26   subsequent questions but had additional concerns about the comparison data and methodology.
27   The applicant requested time for its consultant and RKG to discuss their approaches before
28   returning to the Board. The applicant stated that its proposal included four workforce-housing
29   units; its representative understood RKG’s analysis to indicate that six to eight units might be
30   financially viable but did not agree to a revised number at this meeting.
31   RKG’s representative summarized two principal differences between the analyses: accounting for
32   potential value from a parcel previously subdivided for a possible NHDOT acquisition, and
33   estimated sale values for duplex units compared with row-house units. He described a valuation
34   of approximately $430 per square foot for duplex units compared with approximately $370 per
35   square foot in the applicant’s model. The applicant’s representative indicated acceptance of
36   RKG’s adjustment for potential parcel value but continued to question other assumptions.
37 Ms. Kotzen questioned reliance on a 15% profit-margin benchmark and said the applicant’s
38 previously modeled 12.8% margin was a more relevant starting point for her review. She asked
                                                         Planning Board — November 3, 2026   |   Page 2 of 3

39   how a nine-unit workforce-housing scenario would affect that margin. The RKG representative
40   said he could run the model but did not have the figure available. Mr. Wilson emphasized that the
41   appropriate workforce-housing requirement was for the Board to determine and involved
42   considerations beyond financial feasibility. Mr. Sillay and Mr. Harned clarified that the
43   consultants’ meeting should address methodology, not make a recommendation or decision
44   reserved for the Board.
45   The Board discussed who should attend the consultants’ meeting. Ms. Kotzen agreed to
46   participate as a Planning Board representative. The Chair then summarized a proposal to continue
47   the case, authorize the two consultants to meet with Ms. Kotzen, and have them return to report
48   their findings to the Board.
49   Mr. Pinette moved to continue Case #26:09 and authorize the two consultants to meet with
50   Ms. Kotzen and return to report their findings to the Board, as proposed immediately
51   beforehand by the Chair. Second by Mr. Sillay. The vote was unanimous in favor of the
52   motion.
53   Following the Board’s vote, Chair Kroner invited public comment. An attendee asked whether
54   the case would return in approximately one month; no substantive comments on the application
55   were offered. The Chair stated that he opened the public hearing at approximately 7:05 p.m. and
56   closed it at approximately 7:06 p.m.
57 The Chair asked Mr. Tully and Ms. Ogilvie to coordinate a meeting between the consultants and
58 Ms. Kotzen. He identified December 1, 2026, as the date for continued consideration of Case
59 #26:09.
60 2. Case #26:19 — Granite Post Green MHC
61   The applicant sought subdivision of approximately 2.61 acres from its 34.70-acre mobile-home-
62   park property at 160–164 Lafayette Road (M/L 017-082-000) and site plan review for
63   replacement of the existing restaurant with a vehicle self-storage development consisting of 16
64   condominium storage units within two buildings, with a separate car-washing building. The
65   property is in the I-B/R District. The applicant requested a continuance to December 1, 2026,
66   while pursuing matters before the Zoning Board of Adjustment (ZBA). No applicant presentation
67   was recorded for this item.
68 In attendance for this application:
69 No applicant representatives were in attendance as a previous request to continue had been
70 submitted.
71 Mr. Wilson moved to continue Case #26:19, as requested by the applicant, to the Board’s
72 next meeting on December 1, 2026. Second by Mr. Pinette. The vote was unanimous in favor
73 of the motion.
