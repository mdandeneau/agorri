                           North Hampton Conservation Commission
                               Meeting Minutes - October 13, 2026


Administrative: The meeting was called to order at 7:05 p.m. and recorded via Channel 22 in the Town
Hall. The agenda was posted in compliance with RSA 91-A requirements.

Members Present: Lisa Wilson (Chair), Kathy Grant (Vice Chair), Andy Vorkink, Jay Chabot, Dave
Ciccalone, Jennifer Kotzen, and Phillip Thayer.

Approval of September 8, 2026 Draft Meeting Minutes: After review of the September 8, 2026
minutes, a motion was made by Kathy Grant, duly seconded by Jay Chabot, to approve the minutes.
The motion carried.

New Business:
Car-Condominium Storage Facility: Applicant Granite Post Green MHC,168 Lafayette Road, North
Hampton, NH 03862 (Map 17 Lot 82); North Hampton, New Hampshire within the Aquifer Protection
District (APD).
         The Commission reviewed the proposal for potential impacts within the Aquifer Protection
District for the proposal to build a Condominium Car Storage Facility and a Car Wash Facility. The
project proposed to include a two-building 16-unit, 25,600 square feet Car Condominium Storage
Facility with 16, 1,600 separate units, each to include entertainment loft areas, kitchenettes, and
bathrooms. The Car Wash Facility is proposed to be 576 square feet for a total of 26,176 square feet for
the three buildings. The plans depicted 51% impervious surface coverage within a 2.61-acre parcel.
When a proposal exceeds the required 20% impervious surface threshold within the APD, enforceable
and realistic conditions of approval must be met.
         The Commission expressed concerns that the septic system was not designed to take into account
potential present and future uses of the facilities due to the residential-like elements planned for the
units. The plans depicted a 300-gallons per day septic system for all 16 units/ 15 gallons per day for 16
persons. Yet plan C105 stated that the Owner is responsible to operate the system within its capacity and
that the daily flow to the leach field should be no more than half its approved design for successful
operation of the disposal system. Given the uncertainly of the number of persons occupying the
individually-owned units at any given time, the Commission recommended that the Applicant consider
installing a considerably larger or commercial septic system.
         The Commission stressed the need to ensure that storm basins are properly maintained and
adequately sized and recommended the installation of a water reclamation car wash system as apposed
to a holding tank which requires pumping and can cause illegal surface overflows. The Commission also
discussed the need to ensure that there will be no vehicular traffic over the septic field, adequate snow
storage areas, and a prohibition of overnight parking on the premises. The Commission recommended
that the plans be modified to prohibit the use of calcium chloride to reduce dust during construction and
that soils should be stabilized within a timeframe in accordance with NH best management practices for
Aquifer Protection Districts.
         A motion was made by Andy Vorkink, duly seconded by Jay Chabot to authorize the Chair to
draft a letter to the Planning Board to express the Commission’s concerns and recommendations for
the Car Condominium proposal. The motion carried.

Old Business:
Conservation Easement Subcommittee Update: Commissioners continue to collaborate with Kate
Dardinski to assess the condition of trails and to add and replace trail markers where needed. Kate
Dardinski has completed bog bridges at Dustin Booker Farm in addition to a bridge across the perennial
stream along the Nordstrom Eltzroth Woods trail. Andy Vorkink reported that the connecting trails from
Forest Hills Farm to Nordstrom Woods offer the opportunity for two and half mile or even longer hikes
through the trail network. There is also a gravel parking area for a few vehicles at the North Road
entrance at Nordstrom Eltzroth Woods for shorter, round-trip hikes along that trail. Once the North
Road trail extension is completed, the Commission will revise and create new maps that depict the
newly created trails.

Trail Maintenance Update: Phil Thayer reported that he noticed there are people on trails wearing only
neutral colored garments during this hunting season. Lisa Wilson will draft a message for the Friday
Folder to remind residents to take precautions during hunting season, which will include the New
Hampshire hunting season dates.

Philbrick Pond Saltmarsh Restoration Update: Kathy Grant, Jennifer Kotzen and Lisa Wilson
attended the September 14, 2026 site walk with Philbrick Pond project team members, landowners, and
other residents. Following the site walk, Kathy and Lisa participated in a zoom meeting with the project
team on 9.30.2026 to discuss its outcome and next steps. The group reached consensus that stream
channel restoration west of the trolley berm appeared to be a low-cost opportunity compared to the
replacement of trolley berm culvert, which in the near term should benefit the entire marsh system. At a
future date to be determined, the project team will reconvene with the full technical team to continue to
discuss restoration of the stream channel.

Other Business:
Community Garden Update: Rowan Kensington, Chair Agricultural Commission reported that the
Community Garden has been cleared of old debris and the next phase will be to till the garden plots to
make them more suitable for planting. The Agricultural Commission will continue to consult and work
with the Commission as plans progress.

NHACC Dues: The Commission typically receives an invoice for NHACC dues following the October
meeting. A motion was made by Phillip Thayer, duly seconded by Jay Chabot, to authorize the Chair
to submit payment up to $500 following receipt of an invoice for NHACC annual dues to ensure the
invoice is paid in a timely manner. The motion carried.
