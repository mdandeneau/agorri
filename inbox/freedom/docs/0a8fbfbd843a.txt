Department Head Updates:

Fire— Chief Cunio reported: 3 medical aids, 1 motor vehicle accident and 1 illegal burn.
Chief also reported he has not gotten pictures yet of the inside at 50 Eaton Rd.

Transfer Station- Alan L. reported he had an uncooperative resident this week not wanting
to comply with the rules he also stated that Mount Carberry changed its requirements for
construction debris and bulky waste, prohibiting metal in those loads. The transfer station
already has a separate metal container, and staff plan to publicize the rule so residents
place metal in the appropriate container rather than mixing it with construction debris.
There has been no feedback on Hazardous Waste Day.

Police — Chief Laferriere reported a soft, military-surplus protective vest was located for
use at the Transfer Station. Chief Laferriere discussed providing it to Alan.

The large radar/message-board unit was quoted at approximately $19,500. Three solar-
powered, pole-mounted speed displays would cost approximately $5,300 each, or $15,900
total, and could collect vehicle speed and time data at three locations, providing broader
coverage than moving one unit between locations. The Board asked Chief Laferriere to
verify whether the state now permits message-capable signs on local roads, since prior
restrictions reportedly applied to signs that flashed messages rather than merely
displaying speed. Potential initial locations included Eaton Road, Ossipee Lake Road, and
Village Road, with locations rotated periodically.

The FCC approved cameras for the Town Forest, intended to help prevent unauthorized
campfires. The Town is also repairing an older six-wheeler for use as a forest patrol vehicle
next year.
To date the police have had 30 arrests with one third of the arrests being felony level.

Municipal Land Gift: Jeff Nicoll asked the board for guidance regarding a parcel of land
that has been offered as a gift to the town that contains wetlands, and hydric soils. It was
discussed it could be useful as a work or staging area if the associated culvert or wing wall
must be replaced in the future. Road Agent Brooks recommended accepting the 4,975-
square-foot parcel associated with a proposed subdivision for the purpose of culvert
access and future staging.

Town Office- Stacy reported that a resident inquired about having a streetlight installed at
the corner of Davis Point Road off Loon Lake Rd. for students who wait for the bus in the
dark. Brad Perry of Eversource advised that the installation would not be straightforward
because there is no nearby transformer and the existing pole dates from 1968. The nearest
transformer is on Route 25, and the work may require roughly 220 feet of wire if the next
pole were to be used. The preliminary estimate was approximately $5,000 or more,
although Eversource had not completed a formal estimate. The board discussed moving
forward if the affected resident were willing to pay for having the light installed and the
Town would pay the monthly electric bill.

Truck Rate Adjustment: The Town reviewed temporary trucking-rate increases caused by
fuel costs and limited contractor availability, with the changes applying to larger trucks
through January 1, 2027. The Town implemented a temporary $10-per-hour increase for
larger trucks, including six-wheelers, ten-wheelers, and tri-axles. The light-truck rate was
not changed. The temporary rates expire or are scheduled for review on January 1, 2027.
Officials said the increase was needed because fuel costs had risen and contractors were
unwilling to move trucks at the former rates. The group noted that diesel costs remain
uncertain, so the adjustment was treated as a temporary measure. Stacy will put a copy of
the fuel rates in Scott’s box.

Kearsarge Energy Solar Project Tax Agreement: Andrew Bernstein, a representative of
Kearsarge Energy, presented a proposed 20-year tax agreement for a 4.99-megawatt AC
solar array. Before deciding whether to advance the proposal, the Select Board requested
validation from Town Assessor Rod Wood. The proposal included tax-agreement payments
totaling $375,844 over 20 years, an estimated current-use penalty of $19,579, and
approximately $33,000 in additional land-tax value, for a stated total of $428,727.
Bernstein said the proposed structure is based on agreements used in other New
Hampshire municipalities.

The proposed assessed value is approximately $4 million. Bernstein explained that the
value would depreciate by 10% annually before leveling off at 30%, with additional taxes
due if the facility is expanded or upgraded.

Eversource interconnection constraints limit the array to 4.99 megawatts AC. The final
panel count may change as equipment becomes more efficient, while the estimated
project footprint is approximately 20 to 24 acres. The project must undergo Planning Board
and Conservation Commission review, as well as applicable state review processes.
Town officials said the proposal would be reviewed by Town Assessor Rod Wood and the
New Hampshire Department of Revenue Administration. Bernstein agreed to provide a


                                             3
sample agreement template and supporting materials. The Town chose not to involve legal
counsel at this stage.

Bernstein requested an indication of the project’s viability by approximately November 15
because Eversource may require a costly interconnection study and system upgrades. He
also offered an alternative front-loaded payment schedule. Town officials planned to ask
Wood whether front-loading payments would benefit the Town during budget planning.

The Town identified the project’s proximity to two airports as an issue for Planning Board
review. Bernstein said similar projects have been subject to required glare analyses and
that airport proximity could be addressed through the applicable review process.
