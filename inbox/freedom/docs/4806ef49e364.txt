Town of Freedom
                                  Selectmen’s Meeting
                                Monday, October 26, 2026
Present: Selectmen Les Babb, Alan Fall, and Gary Williams; Stacy Bolduc, recording
secretary. The meeting was called to order at 6:30 p.m. at the Town Hall located at 16 Elm St.

Absent: Scott Brooks
Department Heads Present: Alan Libby, Chief Laferriere, Kyle Thomas
Public Present: Robert Rafferty, Jim Guido, Brian Taylor, Paul Elie, Jen Ulrich, Roberta
MacCarthy, Sue Hoople, Sara Butterfield, Anne Cunningham, Jeff Nicoll and Melissa Florio
and Karl Ogren.

Public Officials Meeting

Les reported that the Fire Department will burn the house at 50 Eaton Rd. due to mold and
disrepair. He noted that the Town previously used bonds for road projects but borrowing
costs have increased. The Ossipee Lake Rd. project is estimated at $3 million. The Select
Board awaits final school tuition details. Reported per-student tuition is $66,000 at Kennett
and $26,000 at Kingswood, with a total difference of $1.5 million; parents of students
attending prefer Kennett. Anne Cunningham asked how the school would be selected. Les
stated that the Freedom School Board is consulting legal counsel about placing the
question on the school warrant.

Transfer Station
Alan Libby reported this year’s budget is looking good. He shared the cost of the recycling
program is expensive. Last years cost to get rid of the recyclables was $23,000 this year it’s
up to $43,000. The bulk of it is cardboard so he is proposing buying a baler. On the market it
would sell for $100.00 a ton. He noted that the market does fluctuate.

Fire Department
Chief Cunio reported:
    • The department has responded to 363 calls to date.
    • Grant funding was received for 1¾-inch hose, nine nozzles, and enough spare hose
       to replace all truck hose.
    • The early-spring Ossipee Lake Response Plan covers open-water and ice rescue
       and includes Freedom, West Ossipee, Center Ossipee, Effingham, and the Center
       Ossipee dive team. The team deployed three times in Freedom this summer: one
       unfounded call, one drowning, and one incident on Loon Lake.

    •   The department continues pursuing grants to replace Engine 2 and handheld and
        full-size thermal imaging cameras.
    •   The school purchased a Knox box. The department also offers residential Knox
        boxes keyed for police and fire access.
    •   A warrant article will be submitted for a full-time position covering safety and
        station duties.

Police Department
Chief Laferriere reported:
   • The new k9 cruiser is outfitted and in use. The k9 is certified for finding drugs and is
        one of the top dogs in the county.
   • Currently the budget is under due to Chief Mullen retiring early last year.
   • Would like to do a warrant article for a cruiser for the new hire allowing each officer
        to have their own car. In 2028 with the increase in calls and arrests he will be
        requesting a forth officer.
   • There is now an office person available to assist the public with paperwork and
        other requests.
Facilities
    • Kyly Thomas reported all is well with the buildings and will continue on with the
         town next year.

Heritage Commission
Anne Cunningham reported limited Heritage Commission activity and submission of a
$10,000 LChip grant application. Once warrant articles are finalized, the Commission will
hold a public informational meeting on Town Office plans. No bond will be proposed. The
Commission will explore additional grants based on the warrant articles for Town Office
repairs.

Zoning Board of Adjustment
Karl reported that it has been a slower year than the past year.

Freedom Conservation Commission

Jeff Nicoll reported:
    • Two miles of forest boundaries were reblazed, focusing on the Duby and Ford lines
        and the Purity Springs border.
    • The rebuilt Trout Pond trail sustained no damage during spring storms.
    • The Scrub Oak Scramblers maintained trails and cleared trees to improve fire
        equipment access.

                                              2
   •   Trail name signs were installed and kiosks repainted.
   •   Freedom Old Home Week wagon rides were fully booked, with demand exceeding
       capacity. Jeff thanked Josh Battles for his assistance.
   •   Initial water quality testing established a baseline for Trout Pond for comparison
       with other lakes in town.
   •   The FCC is working with the forester on a proposed 125–135-acre timber harvest
       west of the airstrip, planned for next spring after conditions dry. The formal plan will
       be presented to the Select Board.
   •   The FCC reviewed 16 Zoning Board and DES applications this year and provided
       input on the Kearsarge Energy project on Babcock Rd.
   •   Seven to eight acres of the Danforth ponds were treated. An August DES survey
       found no further treatment necessary, reducing this year’s costs. The budget
       request will also be lower, even with the state’s 50% reimbursement grant.
   •   The Lake Host program, supported in part by Ossipee funding, successfully
       intercepted five milfoil fragments on outgoing boats, preventing spread to other
       water bodies.
   •   Members attended day-long education and training at the New Hampshire Lakes
       Congress on threats to New Hampshire lakes.


Planning Board
Melissa reported the board’s activity:
   • 1 major subdivision approved
   • 2 lot line adjustments
   • Held a Public hearing under RSA 414-A to recommendation action on a proposed
      land gift to the Town.
   • Conducted 18 short-term rental reviews
   • Sara Butterfield and Linda Mailhot serve on the Lakes Planning Commission and will
      share grant opportunities for the transfer station, fire department and planning
      board.
   • The Board draft warrant articles at it’s November meeting.
   • Lakes Region Planning indicated that should be some grant monies available for
      doing the master plan in 2028.
   • The third-party reviewer bid is still out.
   • The budget is under due to a slow year and a new checking account will be opened
      for land use board fees.
FEMA Update

Les updated the June storm was declared a disaster.
Les said everyone should have received their budget worksheets. If not let Stacy know.

Department Heads

Transfer Station
Alan Libby reviewed a $5,200 electrical invoice for installing heat beneath the office for the
hydraulics, replacing upstairs baseboard heat, adding an office thermostat, and replacing
the bathroom baseboard heat and thermostat. Two other electricians did not return his
calls; Alan will provide their names to Stacy for documentation.

Fire

Inspection and servicing revealed broken springs and valve issues on the tank truck. Three
quotes were sought under the purchasing policy. Lakes Region Fire Apparatus provided the
most accurate information and a $7,764 estimate, primarily for labor. Advanced Diesel had
scheduling constraints, and Donovan Spring and Equipment did not respond. Repairs were
expedited due to dry conditions and life-safety concerns. The truck is back in service.

Alan F. moved to accept the Simonetti Electric estimate. Gary seconded. Motion
passed.
