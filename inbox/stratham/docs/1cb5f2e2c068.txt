MINUTES OF THE OCTOBER 19, 2026 SELECT BOARD MEETING

MEMBERS PRESENT: Board Members Chair Allison Knab, Vice Chair Joe Anderson,
participating remotely Tedd Tramaloni

ALSO PRESENT: Town Administrator Tim Roache, Fire Chief Jeff Denton, Police Chief
Anthony King

At 7:00pm Ms. Knab opened the meeting and noted that member Ted Tramiloni would be
participating remotely because he is away from Stratham attending urgent family matters.

Mr. Anderson motioned to approve the minutes of October 5, 2026. Mr. Tramaloni seconded the
motion. Roll call: Knab-yes; Anderson-yes; Tramaloni-yes

Ms. Knab invited Chief Denton for his budget presentation. Chief Denton reported strong
progress across fire department initiatives. Paramedics have begun rapid sequence intubation
(RSI) training, supported by donations from the Chief Maloney Foundation and the Stratham
Volunteer Fire Department Association, with the goal of having all medics trained by mid- to late
spring and department certification targeted for mid-year next year. Call volume is
approximately 760 to date and is on track to reach 900 to 1,000 by year-end, representing about a
15 percent increase over last year, with mutual aid responses also rising.

The Fire Prevention Pancake Breakfast and Open House drew between 300 and 400 attendees,
showing strong engagement from families and children. Big water training was successfully
completed with participation from eight departments, achieving 750 gallons per minute and
earning positive feedback.

The ISO retrogression process will conclude on November 14, with training improvements
already submitted. The department has limited ability to improve its water supply score due to
uncertified cisterns and ponds.

The Chief also noted that the fire gear budget can be reduced because of an anonymous $180,000
donation from the Boston Foundation, which will fund thirty sets of PFAS-free gear for
firefighters, improving safety and reducing carcinogen exposure. The Chief and Board
expressed gratitude for the anonymous donor who, through the Boston Foundation, contributed
funding for new firefighting gear. Although the donor’s identity is unknown, it is believed they
may have a local connection, and the Chief intends to issue a public thank you.

The Chief reviewed key budget considerations. Vehicle maintenance costs are rising due to
unexpected repairs, including tire replacements caused by dry rot on older trucks. Building
maintenance expenses also exceeded projections after unplanned replacements to the sprinkler
system control panel, the heating system’s circulating pump, and several rooftop units. These
overruns will require increased budget allocations.

In response to public concerns about ambulance coverage, the Chief outlined the proposed
staffing model: an ambulance crew in-station 24 hours a day, preferably with a paramedic and an

EMT, three staff on the engine during the day and add a stipend position at night to cover the
engine and backup ambulance. He noted that while this will increase costs, the goal is to meet
community expectations and improve service. The proposed 24-hour shift model would allow
paramedics to work one day per week, which is expected to help with recruitment and retention.
If the department cannot fill the schedule through this model, they will explore hiring additional
staff and adding advanced EMTs. Plaistow ALS would continue to serve as backup, as it does
now. Chief reminded them that joining the Plaistow ALS program needs to be a warrant article.

The Board discussed the estimated cost to the town of approximately $15,000 and the required
timeline for continuing participation in the ALS program. If the town remains enrolled, it must
provide a one-year notice to withdraw, meaning it would participate for at least one more year
and could fully exit after the second year if the town’s own program becomes sufficiently robust.
Mr. Anderson asked whether other towns joining or opting out would affect Stratham’s costs.
Chief Denton explained that the 2027 program year is currently funded through donations, and
the financial model assumes participation by at least four towns. If more than four towns opt
out, the cost burden for remaining towns will increase. All participating towns are currently
evaluating their options. Chief Denton reaffirmed that by placing the warrant article before
voters they can decide whether to continue with the program. He feels continuing with the
program is a wise choice. Maintaining the service for the next few years provides valuable
backup support while the town works to strengthen its own paramedic program, which will take
time to develop. The Chief emphasized that the service already benefits the community—for
example, assisting Stratham’s EMTs during a current call—and serves as an important third layer
of paramedic coverage alongside mutual aid. The Board agreed that the additional protection is
worthwhile in the near term.

He also recommended that the town move toward hiring a full-time fire chief. He announced
that he will not continue in the role after his current term ends in March 2028, citing workload
demands. He noted the department has been informed, supports the transition, and that the goal
is to begin the hiring process upon approval, ideally bringing a new chief on by September to
participate in building the next budget. He expressed commitment to ensuring a smooth
transition over the coming year.

Mr. Anderson noted that hiring a full-time fire chief would require a warrant article because it
represents a change in the department’s organizational model. The Chief added that the items he
presented represent the major budget impacts for the coming year. He explained that preliminary
figures and supporting materials have been provided for the Board’s review and that further
discussion can take place as the budget process continues.

Ms. Knab then moved to the Curbside Collection agenda item. Mr. Roache provided a recap:
The town issued a request for proposals on August 28 seeking qualified firms to provide curbside
collection services. On October 5, three responses were received: one proposal from ELM
Services, one from Casella, and a no-bid letter from Waste Management. The proposals were
opened at the October 5 Select Board meeting, and the costs were read into the record. Staff
were then directed to evaluate the proposals, score them, and make a recommendation.

The proposals were reviewed by DPW staff, a Select Board member, and Mr. Roache. Casella’s
proposal scored higher across most categories except cost. ELM’s proposal was significantly
lower in cost but represented a very different firm with different levels of experience, which was
reflected in the scoring.

As permitted under the RFP, staff contacted both proposers. They were not focused solely on
lowest cost, but on determining the best overall fit for the town. ELM was asked several
questions regarding capacity for additional work, vehicle availability, and their ability to supply
two trucks four days per week—a departure from what they typically provide in other towns.
ELM responded promptly with solid answers that largely satisfied the committee.

Casella was contacted primarily due to the significant cost difference. Casella explained that
their higher price resulted from an internal corporate error that added new capital and carts to the
proposal. They were given the opportunity to correct the mistake, which brought their cost
closer to ELM’s. However, ELM remained the lower-cost option. Over five years, the
difference amounts to approximately $294,000. A key factor is the escalator: ELM proposed a 4
percent annual increase, while Casella proposed 6 percent, starting from a slightly higher base
cost.

After considering all factors, the review group concluded that, despite ELM’s lower cost,
uncertainties remained regarding ELM’s readiness to take on both this contract and the Town of
Kingston concurrently. Given the limited time available to implement the new service, the group
felt it was safer to recommend entering negotiations with Casella. This recommendation is now
before the Board.

Mr. Anderson asked why Casella’s proposal included a 6% escalator. Mr. Belanger of Casella
explained that it functions as a floor-ceiling provision. He noted that 5% has been typical in
recent years, but prior years saw increases up to 7%, largely due to rising labor costs. Casella’s
standard contract language uses the greater of 5% or the CPI “garbage index.” In this case,
however, they offered a floor-ceiling structure. In response to Mr. Anderson’s follow-up
question, Mr. Belanger stated that the garbage index is currently at 5% and has been fairly stable.
Mr. Ziolkowski of ELM expressed concern that after proposals were opened, Casella adjusted its
pricing, which he felt was inconsistent with the spirit of competitive bidding. Ms. Knab noted
that the RFP allows the town to negotiate with multiple proposers. Mr. Ziolkowski maintained
that he felt the adjustment still conflicted with competitive-bid principles. Mr. Roache stated that
the RFP had been reviewed by legal counsel. Ms. Knab acknowledged the comments and invited
additional questions.

Mr. Tramaloni stated that the Board should continue to discuss the value received in the
recycling portion of the contract and wanted that noted for the record. Ms. Knab said they have
discussed arranging presentations or gathering more factual information, as there is considerable

Mr. Anderson addressed Mr. Ziolkowski, noting that the primary concern with ELM’s proposal
was capacity. ELM currently serves fewer than 10,000 residents across its existing towns, while
Stratham alone has approximately 7,700 residents. Taking on Stratham would represent roughly
an 80% increase in workload. He said these concerns centered on staffing, carts, and trucks, and
thanked Mr. Ziolkowski for responding to those questions.

Mr. Ziolkowski replied that if the original pricing figures had remained unchanged, this would
likely be a different discussion. He suggested that ELM could have addressed the concerns if the
original cost difference had remained as bid.

Ms. Knab acknowledged that may be true, noting that no one wants to see major increases in
budget items. She added that the DPW Director is new and the town has had staffing transitions,
which limited the lead time for this process. Waste Management had already stated they did not
have enough time to prepare a bid. She thanked both firms for submitting proposals and said that
in the future it would be helpful to have more time to understand how a rollout with a new
company would work. Given the timeframe and the town’s experience with Casella over the past
few years, Ms. Knab stated she was comfortable with the recommendation to proceed with
negotiations with Casella.
