Chief King reported the Police Department is now fully staffed. A new Community Service
Officer, David Newman, will begin tomorrow, and the volunteer chaplain position has been
implemented. The Deputy Chief position has been posted internally and will follow a three‑step
process in coordination with the Town Administrator.

The department has received new Walthers pistols, with training and transition planned for
October. The awarded $30,000 live scan machine will be delivered on October 15. All officers
have completed the State‑required 24 hours of annual training, with most exceeding 40 hours.

The first phase of the budget is complete, and no issues are anticipated for the upcoming year. A
broken water heater will require a $6,000 replacement, slightly exceeding the building
maintenance budget line, but overall the department’s budget remains positive. An active threat
tabletop exercise was completed at CMS, with SMS scenarios planned for November.

Mr. Tramaloni observed accidents were up 78%. Chief King noted that most occur in plaza
parking lots. Distracted driving remains an issue. He submitted a grant application to assist with
that issue. Recently, he made 22 stops in four hours, with 18 for hands‑free violations.

Two new park ordinances will be publicized via the website, social media, and newsletter, with
signage being created by Mr. Hickey. Enforcement will follow the educational approach used at
Jack Rabbit parking. The new CSO will assist with outreach. Chief King is considering
pursuing grants for a side‑by‑side vehicle to improve park coverage, though it would have
limited trail access. Ms. Knab requested ongoing updates.

Ms. Knab recognized Matt O’Keefe and Paul Deschaine, Energy Commission members for their
update. Mr. O’Keefe reported the solar array at the police station was fully bought out and now
provides $8–9k per year in savings. The Commission has developed a solar assessment
calculator for use with future town building projects and continues to pursue grants to improve
feasibility. Mr. Deschaine praised Roy Byrnes, who is not yet a committee member, for his
work. They assisted the school district in securing funding for HVAC and thermal
improvements. The school will not install solar but completed renovations under budget,
allowing investment in windows and roof thermal upgrades.

Mr. O’Keefe provided an update on Community Power. Stratham has participated in the
Community Power Coalition of New Hampshire for three years; while the first year brought
substantial savings, CPCNH has not outperformed Unitil’s default rate in the past two years. The
Commission recommended halting automatic enrollment of new meters unless CPCNH’s rate is
lower than Unitil’s. New accounts currently get swept into CPCNH during quarterly refreshes.
Opt-out notices are sent, but people rarely take action. Opt-out rates remain roughly equal to
new enrollments, keeping participation steady. The Energy Commission is recommending the
Board authorize the Town Administrator to inform CPCNH to stop including new residents and
small businesses in the refresh, unless CPCNH’s rate is below the utility default rate.

Mr. O’Keefe outlined the Commission’s larger recommendation: if CPCNH’s upcoming winter
rates (released late December/early January) are not lower than Unitil’s default rate, Stratham
should begin the process of withdrawing from CPCNH. Three towns have already started this
process, though withdrawal steps are not clearly defined and may take several months to ensure
no outstanding supply or reserve costs remain.

Mr. Deschaine explained that reserve balances fluctuate seasonally, often going negative in
winter, which can complicate withdrawal timing and may require bonds or escrow, as seen in
Hudson’s case. Epping is pursuing a more collaborative withdrawal process. The Commission
noted alternative options exist, including contracting with a third-party supplier, which may still
offer better pricing. Overall, they recommended preparing to move forward if CPCNH continues
to underperform.

Mr. Anderson noted the new CPCNH rate should be available in January, allowing the Town to
decide whether to withdraw. If notice were given on February 1 with six months’ lead time, the
withdrawal period would cover winter, with reserves likely positive again by spring or summer.
Mr. Deschaine added that while reserve levels are one factor, additional costs such as hedging
also affect timing. CPCNH needs a firm withdrawal date so they know when to stop planning
for Stratham’s electricity load. Because they purchase power in the futures market to hedge for
that load, once Stratham’s load is removed, those hedging arrangements must be unwound, and
customers would need to transition either to a third-party supplier or back to Unitil. Mr. O’Keefe
emphasized the goal is simply to begin the withdrawal process, with the final date depending on
Stratham’s reserve balance and any hedging already purchased on the Town’s behalf.

Mr. Tramaloni asked about independent verification of CPCNH’s figures. Mr. Deschaine
explained that while CPCNH is auditable and relies on third-party data, there is often a reporting
delay of several months, complicating timely decision-making. Withdrawal requires a minimum
three-month period, often closer to six, and rejoining is not allowed for 12 months.

Ms. Knab expressed a loss of trust in CPCNH due to poor financial planning and decision-
making. Mr. O’Keefe emphasized that Stratham joined CPCNH solely for cost savings. Mr.
Deschaine noted CPCNH is aware of the Town’s concerns and has shown some improvement,
including returning to a positive financial position and increasing operating reserves. However,
delays in data reporting and long‑term reserve projections indicate ongoing challenges. He
added that CPCNH may need to add surcharges for several years to reach its reserve goals.

Future competitiveness will also depend on forthcoming PUC policy changes that could raise
default utility rates as utilities recover significant under-collected costs.

Mr. O’Keefe explained that CPCNH buys power for Stratham based on total meter load,
including residential and small commercial accounts. Because future supply is purchased in
advance, large changes in enrollment can create mismatches between purchased and actual load.
Discussion followed on how residents can opt out and whether awareness is widespread. While
outreach has been done, participation remains steady due to both new enrollments and limited
resident action. Mr. Anderson confirmed that opting out is simple, while Mr. O’Keefe noted
Unitil now places mid-period withdrawals on a fixed rate rather than a higher variable rate.
Despite outreach, meter numbers remain fairly stable.

Mr. Deschaine clarified that CPCNH already understands member concerns; declining
participation would not change their ability to respond due to financial constraints. The group
agreed to revisit the issue once new rates are released in late December or early January. Mr.
Anderson motioned to authorize the Town Administrator to immediately request CPCNH to stop
automatically enrolling new meters in the quarterly account refresh mechanism unless the
CPCNH rate is below the default rate of Unitil at the time of the refresh. Ms. Knab seconded the
motion. Roll call: Knab-yes; Anderson-yes; Tramaloni-yes. Mr. Deschaine noted this action
avoids inclusion in the October refresh. The Board will re-evaluate withdrawal in December or
January, acknowledging some legal costs may be involved. Ms. Knab thanked the presenters for
their work.

Ms. Knab recognized Mr. Crow from the Stratham Volunteer Fire Department Association,
which voted to donate $5,000 to support rapid sequence intubation (RSI) training, including
paramedic wages, classroom and clinical training hours, and instructor fees. The Association
plans an additional $3,000 donation next calendar year. Funds were raised through events and
community contributions. The Board thanked the Association, noting the high level of the
training. Mr. Anderson motioned to accept the Stratham Fire Department Association donation
of $5,000 for RSI training and associated wages. Mr. Tramaloni seconded the motion. Roll call:
Knab-yes; Anderson-yes; Tramaloni-yes.

Ms. Knab opened the public hearing for the Squamscott Fields project with Southeast Land Trust
(SELT). Ms. Sommers, SELT’s project manager, reviewed the multi-year effort to conserve a
91-acre parcel surrounding an existing Merrill family easement. The property includes forest,
wetlands, floodplain, high-priority water supply land, and river frontage. SELT began the project
in 2018, secured $500,000 in conservation funds in 2023, and signed a purchase-and-sale
agreement with the Merrills in January 2024. SELT was awarded a $2.1 million NOAA grant in

July 2025, requiring the Town and SELT to co-hold the conservation easement. SELT will
handle monitoring and enforcement under a cooperative agreement.

All due-diligence items have been completed and are under NOAA review. SELT anticipates
closing by late October and no later than December 30. Ms. Knab thanked SELT and the
Merrills for their long-term commitment and celebrated the significant federal funding.
Mr. Anderson expressed concern about public access being limited. Ms. Knab explained the
restrictions relate to the property’s history as a working farm, but passive public access is
allowed and has been carefully considered throughout the process.
