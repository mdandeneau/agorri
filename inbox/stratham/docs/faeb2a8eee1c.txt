MINUTES OF THE NOVEMBER 2, 2026 SELECT BOARD MEETING

MEMBERS PRESENT: Board Members Vice Chair Joe Anderson, Tedd Tramaloni

ABSENT: Chair Allison Knab

ALSO PRESENT: Town Administrator Tim Roache, Planning & Building Director Vanessa
Price

At 7:00pm Mr. Anderson opened the meeting and asked for a motion on the minutes. Mr.
Tramaloni motioned to approve the minutes from October 19, 2026 as written. Mr. Anderson
seconded the motion. Motion passed.

Mr. Anderson moved to the Planning and Building update and budget review. Ms. Price began
by reporting that the department is now fully staffed. To date, 834 permits have been issued,
which is consistent with last year's trends. She gave a brief update on current projects:
    • Porsche Dealership: Addition of service bays; work commenced following a pre-
       construction meeting last week.
    • 89-91 Portsmouth Avenue: Mixed-use development permits have been submitted and the
       developer agreement is executed.
    • 170 Portsmouth Avenue (Mighty Roots, a cabinetry business): Relocating from
       Greenland to Stratham for a new office building (~6,000 sq ft); foundation permits are
       expected this year.
    • 41 Portsmouth Avenue (Kia Dealership): Plans signed off by Planning Board chair;
       permits are being submitted with foundation work expected this winter and a target
       completion date of January 2028.
    • 57 Portsmouth Avenue (Tender Crop): Construction is delayed due to back-ordered
       equipment (e.g., freezer space). The opening date has shifted from late October to
       approximately six weeks from now.

Ms. Price reported on Planning Board and zoning amendments. The Zoning Board is currently
reviewing several zoning updates. Section 3.10 will be amended to delegate Heritage District
oversight back to the Heritage Commission to eliminate redundancy. Proposed changes to
Section 11 would remove conditional use permits for wetland and buffer disturbances, requiring
a ZBA variance instead; this is pending a quorum from the Conservation Commission. Section
5.4 is being updated to match state laws by allowing detached accessory dwelling units in
conservation subdivisions. Other updates include clarifying the use of freestanding supports for
temporary banners in Section 7 and restricting certain non-traditional uses in the Town Center to
maintain a village atmosphere. The board is also adding restrictions on recreational vehicles to
prohibit them in front yards or as permanent septic-connected residences. Additionally, two
parcels on Frying Pan Lane may be moved into the Gateway commercial district to
accommodate potential commercial growth while preventing sprawl. The department is also
reviewing building ordinance changes to align with state RSAs.

Ms. Price continued, stating the department is seeking new permitting software to reduce
workload and costs, with a potential rollout from current provider Avatar in January.

Ms. Price moved to budget and financials, stating her general budget remains flat for the current
year, though online permitting may add roughly $1,000 annually starting in 2027. A request for
$2,500 to $3,000 in special projects funding is being made to bring five neglected conservation
easements into compliance. Legal advertising is currently over budget due to a high volume of
hearings, though these costs are reimbursed to the General Fund. There is a modest 3.4%
increase for regional planning membership, while equipment costs will decrease following recent
furniture and plotter service purchases. A new plotter has been added to the CIP since the current
one is no longer being supported. The Conservation Commission budget is proposed at $3,500
to cover six conservation easements and annual training.

Mr. Anderson recognized Mr. Roache for the Assessing report. The Assessing Department
budget is expected to remain largely level-funded, with the only significant adjustments tied to
contractual increases and potential appeal-related expenses. Assessing payroll funds one part-
time staff member who supports assessing functions through the Town Clerk/Tax Collector's
Office and remains flat pending any future cost-of-living adjustments.

The tax map maintenance account is budgeted at $4,100 for the annual contract with
Cartographic Associates Technologies to update and maintain the Town's tax maps. The increase
from prior years reflects contractual pricing. The equipment and software maintenance account
funds the Avatar software used for online tax cards and also reflects a modest contractual
increase.

Contracted assessing services remain budgeted at $42,000 through the Town's agreement with
Whitney Consulting Group. The contract provides an assessor one day per week throughout the
year and runs through December 28. This contract does not include cyclical inspections,
measure-and-list work, permit inspections, or other field data collection activities.

Discussion focused on funding for the property inspections and data collection needed to support
the next town-wide revaluation, currently anticipated in 2029. Approximately $70,000 has been
set aside in the Capital Improvement Program (CIP) for this work. While revaluation costs
themselves are appropriate for capital reserve funding, Mr. Roache noted that the ongoing data
collection work may be more accurately categorized as an operating expense and could be
moved from CIP in the future.

Mr. Anderson asked about the status of inspections after the Town accelerated the inspection
schedule through a contract amendment. Mr. Roache reported that progress is currently behind
schedule. The revised plan shifted work originally intended for later years into 2025 and 2026,
with targets of approximately 1,200 parcels in 2025 and 1,900 parcels in 2026. Whitney
completed roughly half of the 2025 target and approximately 413 of the estimated 1,900 parcels
scheduled for 2026. Mr. Roache emphasized that the Town pays based on completed work and
has not paid for inspections that have not been performed. The importance of maintaining
accurate property records, particularly as preparation for future revaluations, is crucial. Mr.

Roache stated that Whitney has provided an updated timeline and that he believes the remaining
work can be kept on track.

The largest uncertainty in the budget involves legal and appraisal costs related to assessment
appeals filed by several larger businesses. No funding was included for these expenses in the
current year, and legal costs have already reached approximately $26,000 to $27,000. To better
prepare for similar situations in the future, Mr. Roache suggested including a placeholder of
$40,000 in contracted services for legal support associated with appeals. He noted that actual
abatement costs are paid from the overlay account rather than the operating budget. Additional
expenses have also been incurred for appraisals required during the appeal process.

Before establishing a final budget amount for appeal-related costs, Mr. Roache plans to review
any remaining cases with Superior Court to determine whether additional legal activity is
anticipated. If all current matters have been resolved, the amount budgeted for future legal and
appraisal expenses could be reduced. In response to questions about how current appeal-related
costs are being funded, Mr. Roache explained that no funds were specifically budgeted for legal
fees or appraisals, and the Town is currently relying on underspending in other areas of the
budget to cover those expenses. Further refinements to the budget will be made as more
information becomes available.

Mr. Anderson moved to correspondence item from Mr. Rosenbaum, which he believed he had
seen previously. He then moved to public comment. Mr. Rosenbaum, a Stratham resident for
approximately 2.5 years, spoke in support of his pending accommodation request to use a Class 1
pedal-assist e-mountain bike in the park. He explained that due to a past injury and ongoing
medical issues, the e-bike allows him to continue mountain biking and maintain an important
source of exercise. He emphasized that Class 1 e-bikes require pedaling, differ significantly
from scooters and other motorized devices, and are permitted on New Hampshire State Park
mountain bike trails. Mr. Rosenbaum asked the board to recognize this distinction and consider
his request favorably. He also expressed his appreciation for the park, highlighted his experience
with the National Ski Patrol and willingness to assist with safety efforts, and offered to volunteer
in support of the park and the community.

Mr. Anderson asked whether Mr. Rosenbaum would be interested in serving on the Trail
Management Advisory Committee (TMAC). Mr. Rosenbaum responded that he had attended
several TMAC meetings and would be willing to participate both on the committee and in trail
maintenance efforts. Mr. Anderson also mentioned an opening on the Conservation
Commission. Mr. Rosenbaum stated that he is nearing retirement, has professional experience
consulting with municipalities and utility districts, and is interested in conservation. He
indicated that he would submit a volunteer application and assist wherever needed.

Mr. Anderson explained that one Board member was absent and that the Board would take the
comments under advisement for further discussion when the full Board was present. Mr.
Tramaloni added that the issue had been a difficult decision because the Board had received
similar comments from other individuals who use Class 1 e-bikes for comparable reasons. The
Board thanked Mr. Rosenbaum for attending and indicated that the matter would receive further
consideration.

As part of his Administrative Report, Mr. Roache provided an update on the Lane property. He
reported that the Town's primary goal of making the Lane House weather-tight and prepared for
winter has been achieved. Heat and water service are operational, exterior repairs have largely
been completed, and the property is in good condition heading into the winter season.

Work continues on the large south-side porch, where the entire deck and underlying substructure
were removed and are being rebuilt. Masonry repairs on the exterior are also underway and
expected to be completed soon. Gutter installation was scheduled as the next phase of work. Mr.
Roache noted that approximately $30,000 to $40,000 in remaining repairs were approved for
completion this fall and that the project is moving forward as planned.

Mr. Roache also provided an update on the Tannery building. He reported that the interior
HVAC work has been completed and that the space is now ready to be rented. After reviewing
local market conditions, he estimated that the unit could reasonably be rented for approximately
$2,400 per month, including utilities. Because a separate utility meter has not yet been installed,
he suggested that including utilities in the rental rate would be the simplest approach and would
allow the Town to move forward with securing a tenant more quickly.

Continuing his Administrative Report, Mr. Roache provided additional updates on the Lane
property. He reported that the small historic outbuilding located behind the Lane House, which
originally functioned as a privy, has been fully restored on the exterior and is now being used for
storage. During the restoration process, substantial deterioration was discovered in the west-side
sill of the structure. While repairs will eventually be needed, the issue does not affect the current
usability of the building.

Mr. Roache also reported that the Cobble House remains occupied and that the property's heating
system has been upgraded from oil to propane, including replacement of the oil tank and related
equipment. Work on the new septic system is scheduled for mid-November. The project will
consist of a single leach field serving all three dwellings, supported by two tanks, with the
existing tanks to be removed. Excluding future bat mitigation and cleanup work in the barn, Mr.
Roache estimated that approximately $26,000 remains as we head into the winter.

Mr. Tramaloni asked whether the Town would be in a position to rent the main Lane House in the
spring. Mr. Roache responded that it would be possible if decisions are made regarding the
interior layout, intended use of the space, and renovation priorities. He noted that interior work

Mr. Tramaloni also inquired about the status of the bat mitigation issue in the barn and whether

Mr. Anderson asked who would be taking over Mr. Pond’s responsibilities following his

the Town is exploring the possibility of dividing the duties between the two positions. No final
decision has been made at this time.

Mr. Tramaloni asked for an update on the keyless access system and inquired about obtaining a
key fob. Mr. Roache said he would provide him with one. He also noted that the building’s
locks are scheduled to be rekeyed within the next few weeks. Mr. Tramaloni confirmed that
exterior doors would continue to have physical keys in addition to access cards. Mr. Roache
confirmed this and added that the Town intends to maintain stricter control over the distribution
and management of building keys.

There will be a special Select Board meeting on November 13th to review the Employee Manual.

Mr. Tramaloni asked about the recent Route 33 planning charrette conducted by the Town of
Greenland. Mr. Roache explained that Greenland received grant funding to undertake the project
in partnership with Plan New Hampshire. One of the concepts discussed included constructing a
ramp connection at Winnicutt Road and Bayside Road near the bridge crossing. While he
appreciated the effort to identify creative transportation solutions, Mr. Roache cautioned against
overbuilding the Route 33 corridor. He stated that the roadway should continue to function
effectively for both Greenland and Stratham but does not necessarily need to be expanded into a
larger regional thoroughfare.

Mr. Tramaloni described the proposal as a creative solution, though Mr. Roache expressed
uncertainty about whether it would be successful. Discussion also centered on a proposed
single-lane roundabout. Several Board members expressed surprise that a one-lane design was
being considered given current traffic volumes, while acknowledging that they were not traffic
engineering experts and that the project falls outside Stratham's jurisdiction.

Mr. Roache noted that the discussion raises broader questions for Stratham regarding the future
character of Route 33, particularly near Stratham Hill Park. He stated that he does not support
continually adding travel lanes and increasing roadway speeds, instead favoring the corridor's
continued function as a local or regional road rather than a primary route connecting Portsmouth
and towns west of Stratham. In his view, regional traffic is better accommodated by Routes 95
and 101.

Mr. Tramaloni added that the closure of Squamscott Road appears to have had a noticeable
impact on local traffic patterns. Based on his observations, traffic volumes at the Route 33 and
Route 108 intersection have increased, and he has experienced longer wait times, including waits
of several minutes when attempting to enter Route 33 from Winnicutt Road. He noted that traffic
that previously used Squamscott Road is now being redirected through other intersections,
illustrating how even relatively small roadway changes can significantly affect traffic flow. He
stated that it will be important to observe how those impacts develop over time.

Mr. Anderson turned the discussion to the Stevens Park project and asked Mr. Roache to
coordinate with Mr. Batchelder on parking and drainage improvements and with Mr. Hickey on
the pavilion component in order to move the project forward. The group agreed that the
individuals could work together as a team and report their progress back to Mr. Roache.
Mr. Roache noted that the Transfer Station redesign remains another outstanding project. He
stated that he had discussed the matter with Ms. Fowle and recalled that approximately $50,000
had been included in prior budget planning for engineering and design services this year.
However, he was not aware of any formal Board motion authorizing the expenditure from the
Capital Reserve Fund and indicated that such action should be approved by motion. He
explained that he would bring a proposal back to the Board outlining the scope of work and
associated costs. Updated proposals are expected to allow the project to move forward soon.

The Board discussed an anticipated design cost of approximately $50,000, with an estimated
$150,000 to be expended over two years for construction. It was noted that this particular
Capital Reserve fund currently contains more than $400,000, with few other anticipated demands
on the fund and additional contributions expected this year.

Mr. Anderson commented that both the Stevens Park project and the Transfer Station redesign
appear less complicated than previously thought. He noted that the revised pavilion concept has
simplified the Stevens Park project and expressed the view that the drainage and parking lot
improvements could likely be completed by staff. Mr. Roache added that Mr. Batchelder has
been seeking additional quotes for the parking lot work.
The Board discussed the vacant Community Service Officer position and whether the Town
should reconsider the structure of the role rather than simply reposting it as it has been in the
past. Mr. Anderson suggested exploring alternative approaches, including narrowing the
position's focus to animal control duties, sharing the position with a neighboring community to
create a full-time role, or considering other staffing models. Mr. Roache stated that he had not
yet had the opportunity to discuss the position with the Police Chief, while Mr. Anderson noted
that the Chief favors a full-time position.

Mr. Tramaloni emphasized the need for enforcement presence in the park, particularly as the
Board considers issues such as regulating Class 1 e-bikes and enforcing existing rules. He noted
that effective enforcement requires "boots on the ground," although he questioned whether that
necessarily means a full-time position. Mr. Anderson remarked that the need for coverage likely
exceeds the current 16-hour-per-week schedule, particularly during the busy season from April
through October.

The Board also discussed challenges associated with creating a full-time position, including the
difficulty of providing sufficient work year-round and the potential personnel issues associated
with a position that is full-time during the warmer months and part-time during the off-season.
While no specific solution was identified, members agreed that the position should be
reevaluated and that alternative staffing options should be explored.
