Ms. Knab invited Ms. Bakie for her budget presentation. Ms. Bakie presented her budget request.
A pay increase for the three Supervisors of the Checklist was requested again, as last year’s
request was not approved. The proposed increase is $600 per month total ($200 per supervisor).
She emphasized that supervisors’ responsibilities have grown significantly, including annual
voter list reviews, processing registrations, issuing and tracking required letters, updating records
for residents who move (including frequent updates for military voters), and ensuring
compliance with statutory procedures. The workload has become increasingly complex.

Additionally, Ms. Bakie requested an increase in the conference and training budget. Only she
will attend this year’s conference in Nashua. Next year’s conference in Lincoln will involve
higher costs for mileage, meals, lodging, and travel time. She expressed the need for expanded
funding to allow both the clerk and deputy to attend essential training.

Ms. Bakie shared that she is expected to be voted in as Second Vice President of the Town
Clerk’s Association this year. The role involves monthly meetings in Concord, and she noted
that she is steadily working her way up within the organization. She acknowledged some initial
hesitation due to the significant time commitment. She emphasized that the training she receives
regarding elections and motor vehicles brings strong value back to the town.

Ms. Knab asked for an update on document transfers. Ms. Bakie explained that the remaining
items are older state books and some old receipt books, which may not be necessary to preserve,
though they are historically interesting. She noted that she postponed the transfer this year for
that reason. The previous Town Clerk completed transfers annually, but Ms. Bakie shifted to
doing them in odd-numbered years due to the cost. Last year’s expense was approximately
$2,300. The town continues to use Co-file, as it remains the only available vendor in the state for
this service.

Ms. Bakie explained that budgeting for election costs can be challenging, especially in years with
only one election. It is difficult to predict expenses for ballots and programming until the actual

Ms. Knab welcomed Jen Rowden of the Rockingham Planning Commission for her presentation
on the Stratham Hill Park visioning. Ms. Rowden provided a high-level overview of the results
from the Stratham Hill Park visioning activities conducted earlier this summer. She explained
that the purpose of the process was to understand how the park’s use has shifted in recent
years—post-COVID and after the end of the Fair—and to evaluate growing demands on the
space. Public input highlighted top concerns, priorities, and both short- and long-term needs.
The visioning focused on identifying overlapping uses, potential conflicts among user groups,
and the need for a clear direction as the town approaches important management decisions. Ms.
Rowden noted she is happy to discuss any part of the full report previously shared in August.

Ms. Rowden provided an overview of the community engagement process. In May, a facilitated
forum with staff and key stakeholders drew about 25 participants to discuss constraints,
opportunities, and long-term visioning for Stratham Hill Park. In June, a publicly advertised
input session brought in roughly 30 attendees representing a wide range of park users. To
broaden participation, a public survey was released the following day, receiving 227 responses—
about 80% from Stratham residents and 19% from neighboring communities.

Feedback from all three activities highlighted several consistent themes. Residents value the
park’s natural environment and its role as a community gathering space, with popular activities
including walking, running, biking, dog-walking, cross-country skiing, snowshoeing and
playground use. Many respondents did not distinguish between the park proper and adjacent trail
areas, though comments reflected use across both. A key concern that emerged was the need for
a comprehensive maintenance plan, as park infrastructure is aging and community needs have
evolved.

Ms. Rowden noted that several recurring concerns emerged from the public input. Many
comments focused on existing rules—particularly those related to dogs, e-bikes, and parking—as
these are issues users encounter daily. Signage was another frequent topic, with some
participants wanting clearer rules, better directional and trail signage, and even historical
markers, while others felt there were already too many signs. A common point of confusion was
the distinction between Stratham Hill Park proper and adjacent properties, as the transition
between them feels seamless.

In terms of priorities, short-term desires included improved playground and bathroom facilities.
A need for clarification of parking rules—especially between the main entrance and Jack Rabbit
Lane—and better use of existing facilities such as trails, the fire tower, the agricultural buildings,

and the stage emerged. Long-term priorities included investment in preserving the fire tower,
noting that work on that effort has already begun.

Ms. Rowden added that improved communication about park offerings is also needed. For
example, several participants requested more music events, unaware that summer music already
takes place—indicating a gap between current programming and public awareness. Long-term,
many respondents supported creating a central location for park information, such as a kiosk
with seating, to provide a clear meeting point and better access to updates.

Residents also expressed interest in expanded recreation and entertainment opportunities—such
as yoga classes, additional biking activities, more music events, and occasional festivals.
Improving connectivity within the park, including clearer routes between parking areas,
ballfields, and playgrounds, was another common theme. Protecting natural resources remained
a broad long-term priority.

Participants also emphasized the need to identify potential funding sources for park
improvements, though few specific ideas were offered beyond suggestions like charging
nonresidents, noting that legal constraints may apply.

Ms. Rowden summarized the collective community vision: to maintain Stratham Hill Park as a
natural, community-centered destination that protects its rural character while enhancing
amenities, improving accessibility, and supporting community events—without changing the
park’s fundamental identity. She emphasized that the vision provides high-level guidance on
which the Board can base its decisions. She also offered recommendations, including reviewing
legal restrictions tied to the park’s original land parcels to understand possible options for
funding and fee structures.

Ms. Rowden outlined a second key recommendation: creating a current conditions report for all
facilities and infrastructure within the park proper, and potentially for adjacent areas as well.
This assessment would document what structures and amenities exist, their condition, whether
repairs are needed, and rough cost estimates for addressing them. Having this information will
help guide decisions about what should be repaired, repurposed, or potentially decommissioned.
She noted that some facilities are in poor condition, and understanding their value and cost
implications is essential for long-term planning. Much of this knowledge already exists among
staff, volunteers, and members of the Stratham Hill Park Association, and other committees, but
it needs to be gathered into a shared framework. Once the town has this baseline, the next step
could be another public input session supported by more detailed information. Following that,
hiring a landscape architect to draft conceptual options—with visual renderings—would help the
community understand possibilities for improvements, such as the future configuration of
playgrounds or other amenities.

Ms. Knab thanked Ms. Rowden and said the vision summary accurately reflected both the public
feedback and her own conversations with residents. She noted that most people want the park to
remain largely the same, with practical improvements rather than major new features—few
suggested anything dramatic. Ms. Rowden acknowledged there were some outliers, but overall
the community favored maintaining the park’s character while addressing needed upgrades.

Mr. Hickey noted that some preliminary work has already been done to catalog park buildings
and their current uses. Ms. Knab added that completing a full current conditions report would
not be a heavy lift and could help determine whether certain buildings should be repurposed or
removed. Mr. Tramaloni shared that the Heritage Commission has voted to hire a preservation
firm to document historically and culturally significant assets in the park, so that work is already
underway on a limited scale. Mr. Roache supported the idea of engaging a landscape architect
later in the process, emphasizing the value of visuals for community decision-making.

Ms. Rowden recommended including extensive photos in any conditions analysis, as many
residents were unclear about the purpose and condition of various agricultural and other
buildings. Mr. Hickey observed that some community members still picture the park as it was
decades ago, which differs significantly from its current state. Ms. Rowden added that thorough
documentation will help clarify what is in good condition, what could be repurposed, and what
may no longer be needed.

Ms. Knab noted that several people who could not attend the meeting were interested in the
report’s findings and asked whether Ms. Rowden could share her presentation or report. Ms.
Rowden said she intends to send the materials to everyone who subscribed to the survey—about
half of participants—and can share that email list with the town. She added that she is happy to
distribute the materials herself or through the town and recommended posting them on the town
website.

Mr. Tramaloni commented that while the visioning process was helpful, he had hoped for more
“blue sky” strategic thinking about the park’s long-term future. He suggested exploring broader
ideas such as a public-private partnership to create a dog park on the old Voc property with a safe
pedestrian crossing, or implementing an RFID-based gated parking system at Jack Rabbit Lane.
He noted that while improving bathrooms and playgrounds is important, he would like the
long-term vision to consider larger, town-wide recreational opportunities extending beyond the
park proper. Ms. Knab noted that Mr. Tramaloni’s broader ideas were outside the scope of
RPC’s work but still valuable. She added that Mr. Roache has also explored concepts related to
crossing Portsmouth Avenue and the Voc property. Ms. Rowden agreed that some public
feedback extends beyond Stratham Hill Park and should feed into larger community planning
discussions. Ms. Knab observed that these broader considerations align more closely with open
space planning. Ms. Rowden concluded that several themes in the report relate to community-
wide recreation and should be incorporated into future, broader planning efforts.

Ms. Knab moved to the Town Administrator report. Mr. Roache reviewed the budget, noting that
approximately 70% of funds have been spent with 75% of the year elapsed, which is on target.

Under Planning and Building, Mr. Roache reported that work has begun on updating the Local
Emergency Operations Plan, last completed in 2008. Mr. Anderson noted that a former
employee initiated the update, the Town has a reimbursable contract with Homeland Security,
and consultant Jane Hubbard of Hubbard Consulting is assisting. Planning and Building is also
reviewing the emergency road access between Lindt and Rollins Farm and may need to discuss
future maintenance responsibilities with the homeowners association. Further details will be
brought to the Board once the review is complete.

Mr. Roache continued by noting that a pre-construction meeting for the fire tower rehabilitation
project will take place within the next two weeks. Once contractor requirements for site
shutdown and management are understood, work will proceed. He added that this will be an
opportunity for the Communications Committee to help inform the public about the construction
and restricted access to the tower.

Mr. Roache continued his report, noting that a meeting with the Fire Department was held on the
28th and that budget work is still underway. He stated that a productive discussion with a labor
lawyer helped clarify per diem limits and requirements, and he has provided the Fire Chief with
the information needed to finalize the department’s budget.

Mr. Roache also reported that keyless access has been installed at the Municipal Center. Staff
are currently assigning permissions, with full implementation expected by the end of the week.

Mr. Roache provided an update on Stevens Park, noting the need for a discussion on the project’s
overall direction. He explained that the septic design permit expires in October and, given prior
conversations about not pursuing a septic system, the Board should formally confirm whether it
intends to shift away from the original plan. Ms. Knab recalled that the Board intended to obtain
additional estimates for the pavilion, as the initial quote was very high. She requested one or two
more for comparison. Mr. Roache noted that a private company in Epping had provided an
alternative estimate of $125,000. Mr. Anderson added that other vendors are available as well.
Mr. Roache described the concept for the pavilion, which would be similar to the Scamman
Pavilion. He suggested that using seasonal porta potties—potentially with stabilized
enclosures—could provide the same basic function at a fraction of the cost of building a full
septic system. Ms. Knab confirmed septic costs were the primary concern, and wondered if the
project could be designed to allow bathroom facilities to be added later. Board members agreed
that the pavilion is intended simply as a shelter. Mr. Anderson noted that drainage work and a
redesigned parking lot would also be required. Mr. Roache reported that he is obtaining updated
quotes for the parking lot. Mr. Tramaloni suggested that composting toilets, similar to those used
in National Parks, could serve as a reasonable alternative to porta potties and full septic
infrastructure. He recommended gathering more information and exploring additional options.
