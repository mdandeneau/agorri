GOFFSTOWN CONSERVATION COMMISSION
                           MINUTES TO MEETING OF SEPTEMBER 23, 2026

In attendance were Jean Walker—Chairman, Susan Tucker, Evelyn Miller, Amy Pollock, David
Nieman, Daniel King, and JoAnn Duffy - Planning & Economic Development Director.

Jean Walker called the meeting to order at 7:00 pm. She asked the Commission to introduce
themselves. Daniel King was appointed as the voting alternate member.

Plan Review:

Map 26, Lots 19, Site Plan Review for a proposal to build a residential duplex home (2-units)
on a lot that currently has a single-family home with an attached accessory dwelling unit
located on it. This will create a four-unit residential multifamily development. The proposed
duplex will not be attached to the existing single-family home that has the existing accessory
dwelling unit. The property is located at 40 Henry Bridge Road, owned by Andrew Cassidy,
Zoned: Agricultural.

Presentation was given by Matt Peterson of Keach Nordstrom Associates and Andrew Cassidy,
the property owner.

Matt Peterson reviewed the location of the property that abuts mixed uses nearby such as
commercial/industrial, residential and agricultural. Matt stated this is like a transitional area with
zoning of residential, agricultural and commercial industrial flex. This property is zoned
agricultural and is 3.82 acres in size and has 600 feet of frontage. Matt Peterson reviewed the
existing single-family with the attached accessory dwelling unit that is located on the property.
They are proposing to build a duplex on the property that will making this a multifamily
development. Matt showed the location of the Piscataquog River, the wetlands as well as the
100-year flood plain. Matt explained as part of stormwater management they are proposing a
small detention pond in the back. They will be adding 8,150 sq. ft. of pavement to the property
for the parking area and they have kept the impervious service to a minimum. In reviewing the
pre and post drainage, a 25-year storm will go from 1.53 to 1.41. A 50-year storm went from
2.05 to 1.88 and the 100-year went from 2.75 to 2.5. Matt Peterson reviewed the erosion control
measures with the Commission to protect the wetlands during construction. Matt Peterson
reviewed that there is a duplex being built on the abutting property that is also owned by the
applicant but is on a separate lot and is just a duplex.

The Commission briefly reviewed and discussed. Commission reviewed a portion of the lot is
within the flood plain but that is not where the development is occurring.

JoAnn Duffy noted that the lot has a 50 ft. setback from wetlands and not 100 feet because it is
a lot created prior to when the 100-foot wetland setback requirement was created.

Jean walker suggested they put the wetland placards at the 50 ft. wetlands buffer line.

Andrew Cassidy agreed to do and Matt Peterson stated that there is an existing tree line that
runs along that buffer where they could install the placards.

Amy Pollock asked how they would stabilize the steep slopes so no erosion went into the
wetlands.

Map 4, Lot 100A, Subdivision Review for a proposed two-lot subdivision off of 34 Worthley
Hill Road, owned by Robert & Catherine Rousseau 34 Worthley Hill Road, Zoned: R-1.

Presentation was given by Matt Peterson of Keach Nordstrom Associates.

Matt Peterson explained the location of the property on Worthley Hill Road. Matt explained that
the owners are selling their current house and building on the new lot. There is currently 2.02
acres with over 375 ft. of frontage on the lot. There is a 100 ft. utility easement that runs through
the parcel. Once subdivided the new lot will be 1.43 acres. Matt explained the lot configuration
to the Commission that will wrap around the existing lot. Matt explained the buildable area in
the back of the lot. Matt Peterson explained whatever house gets constructed will have to have
drip edgers to deal with the pre and post drainage. There will be a bio retention area will be
constructed to deal with the drainage along the front. They are looking at adding rain gardens
as well.

The Conservation Commission briefly discussed. The Commission discussed there are no
wetlands on the property. The pool, the patio and the pool house will all be removed. The
Commission stated they do not see any issues as the drainage is being addressed.

David Nieman motioned that the Commission is satisfied with all issues that have been
addressed and the applicant will addressed the drainage with the Department of Public
Works. The Commission has no issues of environmental concerns. Motion was seconded
by Amy Pollock, vote d all in favor (6-0-0), motion carries.

Other Business :
JoAnn Duffy reviewed updates with the Commission regarding RSA 674-21-b where the law
now allowed septics, wells, electric distribution, drainage structures, etc., within or along any
designated buffer areas including but not limited to perimeter buffers, residential buffers,
setbacks, open space areas of a subdivision or lot, provided that such areas are not wetlands as
defined by RSA 482-A:2,X and that such areas are not protected shoreland as defined in RSA
483-B:4,XV. JoAnn Duffy further reviewed that the municipalities shall permit within or along
wetland buffers or conservation areas of a subdivision or lot by a special exception or
conditional use permit, provided that such area are not wetlands as defined in the same quoted
RSA.

The Commission briefly discussed the changes. JoAnn Duffy stated that this will need to be
added to the zoning changes for the 2027 Zoning Ordinance. The Commission discussed
private septics being installed in open space developments could create an issue to the other
owners who own a share of the open space.

David Nieman stated if the land is privately owned by a land trust he doesn’t think a private
owner would be allowed to install a septic on land that is not owned by the association. David
Nieman stated he would think the developer at the time would address during the subdivision
process.

Amy Pollock stated the RSA also states that the Conservation Commission and the Planning
Board can still review and provide comments.

JoAnn Duffy stated she would look into further.
