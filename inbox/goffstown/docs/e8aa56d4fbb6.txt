6.1 Interest In Vacant Alternate Position

Chair Bowen welcomed Brian McGonigle, who had submitted a letter of interest in joining the
Council, and invited him to describe his background and motivation.

Mr. McGonigle stated that he had lived in Goffstown for nearly one year with his wife, son, and

6.2 Ideas For New Projects For The Council

Internship proposal. Director Duffy introduced Alan Diaz, a sophomore at Southern New
Hampshire University, who had contacted the Town about serving as an unpaid intern for the
experience. She reported that the Town Administrator, Derek, had asked that a plan be prepared
defining duties, reporting structure, and time period before an internship began.

Mr. Diaz proposed a project to draw St. Anselm College students to Goffstown businesses,
including Pinardville, rather than to downtown Manchester for evening activity. He drew on his
time as a St. Anselm student and as an employee of a Pinardville fitness center. He suggested a
social media presence (Facebook, Instagram, Snapchat), on-campus promotion of events,
participation in career fairs, and additional internships across town departments. He also
suggested seasonal events such as fall and holiday activities and car shows.

Director Duffy reported that the Town had met with St. Anselm's Chief Financial Officer
approximately six months earlier. He had described the same pattern of students using
Manchester for nightlife and shopping, and the college was receptive to working together. She
noted that the Pumpkin Fest was run by the Main Street Program and that Pinardville had no
comparable program. She also cited a Hooksett business study in which businesses ranked
additional community events as their top request.
Vice Chair Bruno said the proposal addressed the long-discussed question of what attracts
younger people to Goffstown. Member Halen asked whether on-campus students dine in campus
facilities or shop locally. Director Duffy and Member Hamlin noted that students in campus
apartments are likely the ones seen shopping at Hannaford, and Chair Bowen noted students also
frequent local coffee shops.

Select Board Representative Douglas asked how businesses would be engaged. Mr. Diaz cited
student foot traffic, part-time employment, and local food establishments as incentives. Mr.
Douglas urged the Council to choose an actionable project with measurable outcomes, to define
whether the goal would benefit students or businesses, and to consider how success would be
measured. Mr. Diaz proposed surveying St. Anselm students on what they wanted from
Goffstown, tracking sign-ups at career fairs, and possibly using student ID discounts. Mr.
Douglas agreed that discovery through a survey should come first, followed by a plan. Mr. Diaz
stated that he could commit approximately 20 hours per week.

It was agreed that an intern would need to report to a full-time Town employee, and Director
Duffy agreed to serve in that role. Mr. Douglas recommended preparing a job description that
notes the position is unpaid and obtaining approval from Town Hall. Chair Bowen outlined the
process: Mr. Diaz would work with Director Duffy on the HR meeting, reporting process, and
written job description, then bring a draft survey to the Council for feedback. St. Anselm's
guidelines for distributing materials to students would also need to be researched.

Council focus and direction. Director Duffy reviewed a list of roughly 22 projects the Council
had started, completed, or discussed over the past three to four years. She stated that the Council
needed substantive work to pursue and asked members to set goals for the next six to eight
months. Some items on the list, such as the Main Street Program and the Hadley Falls Dam, were
noted by members as not being projects in themselves.

Members described the Council's difficulty maintaining momentum between meetings and its
overlap with the Main Street Program. They also recalled that earlier outreach to Pinardville
businesses had met with limited interest, with owners saying they were doing well. The Council
reviewed prior work, including a commercial property inventory, a review of town-owned land, a
population and median age study, a Facebook page, and a web page describing how to open a
business. Members also noted that ribbon-cutting ceremonies are held jointly with the Greater
Manchester chamber, which has revived its regional economic development group.

Director Duffy reported that:

      Commercially zoned land is limited, and recent state law allowing multifamily housing in
       commercial zones has a potential to reduce it further.
      Public utilities are lacking in many business areas.
      A residential project near the Manchester line may be able to connect to utilities through
       the Tower Lane project.
      The recently sold dome facility plans four additional sports fields and a new parking lot,
       including a land purchase from the adjacent cemetery.
Utilities and infrastructure were discussed at length. Liberty Utilities had told the Council
roughly eight months earlier that extending natural gas along the Mast Road corridor would be
cost prohibitive, even accounting for the Villa property and other planned projects. Director
Duffy described the TIF district approach and said she was aware of only small grants such as
AARP grants of about $500. Members discussed reviving all-boards meetings to improve
coordination among departments. They also discussed fiscal pressures on the Town, including
concerns about tax levels and departmental needs.

Members reviewed the business-opening web page. Chair Bowen noted that about ten business
owners he had spoken with reported no problems. The Council agreed to evaluate periodically
whether the process could be improved.

Members also discussed the Master Plan and its relationship to zoning, and variances granted for
multifamily conversions. Chair Bowen attributed these in part to state workforce housing goals
and noted that the Council could advocate for business opportunities and site-ready parcels
during the next Master Plan update. Members discussed a proposed mixed-use development near
Starbucks, whose developer did not want first-floor commercial space. Director Duffy noted that
the Planning Board may waive that requirement according to the regulations. She also noted that
a new use for the former Rite Aid building was expected shortly. Members suggested using
social media to identify the types of businesses residents and students want for vacant
storefronts.

RSA 79-E, grants, and outreach. Members raised RSA 79-E, a state program the Select Board
administers that freezes the assessed value increase from qualifying renovations for generally
five years (sometimes three). The area covers Main Street and Mast Road in Pinardville, and the
investment must exceed a threshold of about $75,000 or a percentage of building value. Director
Duffy explained that applicants must apply before beginning work. She cited a recent renovation
next to the municipal offices that would have qualified but did not apply. Members agreed that
more outreach and education to property owners and builders is needed. They also noted two
restaurant properties currently for sale.

Select Board Representative Douglas identified additional funding sources, including CDBG
economic development grants of up to $500,000 for businesses with physical expansion and
capital infrastructure needs, and regional micro-grants of $2,000 to $5,000 for start-up
businesses. The Council agreed to consider how to promote these programs.

Council direction. Chair Bowen proposed that, between meetings, the Council: (1) define Mr.
Diaz’s role and begin developing a survey, including a rough draft and research into St. Anselm's
distribution rules; (2) explore ways to learn which businesses residents and students want, and
how to create a mechanism to find out; and (3) research grants and develop site-ready site plans
for prospective businesses. He stated that the Council tends to lose focus when pursuing more
than two or three initiatives. He also said he would arrange for the chief executive of the dome
facility to present at a future meeting. No formal action was taken on this item.
