Police Chief – Highway Safety Committee Recommendations

Police Chief Eric Sereno presented the recommendations of the Highway Safety Committee,
which met on September 16, 2026. The Committee addressed six items. Chief Sereno noted
this was a particularly busy cycle for the committee.

Sign

Chief Sereno reported that the intersection of Lynchville Park Road and Oak Lane has been
the subject of ongoing concern related to line-of-sight issues caused by vegetation on private
property, which the town has been unable to require the landowner to cut back. A resident
living at the intersection brought renewed concern, citing the additional hazard posed by

Safety Committee voted 5-0 to recommend the installation of a three-way stop sign at the
intersection, requiring vehicles approaching from Mass Road, from the dead end of Oak

Sage Court & Cinnamon Drive – Request for Stop Sign

Chief Sereno reported that two residents on Sage Court requested a stop sign at the
intersection of Cinnamon Drive and Sage Court. The Highway Safety Committee noted that
virtually every other intersection in the surrounding neighborhood already has a stop sign,
and that the anticipated extension of Acorn Drive into Sage Court is expected to increase
traffic volume through the area, making Sage Court a more prominent through route to
Blackbrook Road. The committee supported the installation of the stop sign with the
understanding that it was a logical and consistent addition to the existing traffic control
framework in the neighborhood.

Motion: Vice Chairman Douglas motioned to approve the amended traffic regulations
establishing a stop sign at the intersection of Sage Court and Cinnamon Drive. Selectman
Lemay seconded. VOTE: 5-0-0 Motion passed unanimously.

Snook Road – Request for Speed Limit Signs

Chief Sereno reported that a resident on Snook Road raised concerns about excessive vehicle
speed. The Police Department deployed a traffic survey radar unit for approximately one
week. Data collected showed the overwhelming majority of vehicles were traveling at 30 to
35 miles per hour, consistent with the posted speed limit. Only one anomalous reading,
estimated to be between 50 and 70 miles per hour, was recorded among several hundred cars
surveyed. The Highway Safety Committee voted 5-0 to recommend no action on the request
for additional speed limit signage.

Motion: Selectman Lemay motioned that no action be taken on the request for speed limit
signs on Snook Road. Selectwoman DeCesere seconded. VOTE: 5-0-0 Motion passed
unanimously.

Snook Road – Request for Blind Driveway Sign

Chief Sereno reported that a second resident on Snook Road, located further along the road at
a curve, requested a blind driveway sign. The committee reviewed the request and
determined that there is sufficient distance between the curve and the driveway's egress point
to not warrant such signage under established criteria. The committee noted that overgrowth
on the resident's own property may be contributing to reduced sightlines and suggested that
the Department of Public Works speak with the homeowner about trimming that vegetation

East Union Street – No Parking on One Side Of Road

Chief Sereno reported that this matter originated within the Police Department after officers
encountered difficulty navigating East Union Street during a call due to vehicles parked on
both sides of the road. The concern is heightened by the possibility that larger emergency
apparatus, such as fire trucks, could be effectively blocked from accessing the street during
high-attendance events at Saint Lawrence Church. Chief Sereno acknowledged that this is a
longstanding community issue, and that the new parish priest has been receptive to dialogue
about the situation, with church leadership exploring options to address parking demand
internally. Town Administrator Horne had previously facilitated outreach to the church.

The Highway Safety Committee voted 3-1-1 on a recommendation that the odd-numbered
side of East Union Street be designated as a no-parking zone for the full length of the road,
with an additional no-parking zone between signs on the curve toward the bus station. Chief
Sereno noted that this was the first time in his six years as Police Chief that a Highway Safety
Committee vote was not unanimous. The dissenting view, shared by Selectman Lemay, was
that restricting parking on East Union Street would likely push the problem to adjacent
streets, including West Union Street and Hatch Street, which already experience
overcrowding during school sporting events.

The Board agreed that the significance of this matter warranted a public hearing before
any action is taken.

Wallace Road & Shirley Hill Road – 4-Way Stop at Intersection

Chief Sereno reported that the Highway Safety Committee considered the intersection of
Wallace Road and Shirley Hill Road, near the Bedford town line, following a pattern of
accidents in the area. The committee noted that several measures have been in place at the
intersection over the years, including rumble strips, solar-powered flashing lights, and
vegetation clearing by the Department of Public Works, which has improved line-of-sight
conditions. The primary remaining hazard identified by the committee was the speed of
vehicles descending Shirley Hill Road toward the intersection, with limited visibility for
traffic approaching from the Bedford direction due to the wooded hillside.

The committee debated the merits of a four-way stop as opposed to a three-way stop, noting
that three-way configurations have historically caused confusion at similar intersections in
the region and resulted in additional accidents. The committee voted 5-0 to recommend the
installation of a four-way stop at the intersection, with additional advance warning signage on
both approaches of Shirley Hill Road, and the addition of LED solar-powered flashing lights
on both stop signs to improve nighttime visibility. A concern was raised regarding the
potential difficulty of vehicles stopping and restarting on the hill during winter conditions;
Chief Sereno acknowledged this but noted that the majority of modern vehicles are equipped
with all-wheel or front-wheel drive.

2027 Dept Head Budget

Town Administrator Horne presented an overview of the proposed 2027 General Fund
operating budget. Department heads were directed to prepare their budgets using a 5% cut
line as a must-have threshold, with requests up to 7% available for further discussion. Capital
and operational requests were separated, with Capital Improvement Plan (CIP) items drawn
from the board-approved CIP matrix. The only modification to the CIP matrix reflected in the
proposed budget was the Department of Public Works' transition to a vehicle lease-financing
model.

On the personnel side, the budget includes all funded positions from the 2026 operating
budget, wage adjustments for voter-approved collective bargaining agreements covering
DPW Teamsters and firefighters, and a one-dollar placeholder for the two CBAs currently
under negotiation with patrol officers, dispatchers, and clerks. Step advancements were
included for eligible non-union employees, budgeted effective January 1, 2027, consistent
with recent legislative requirements regarding default budget calculations. No non-union
wage matrix adjustment was included in the budget as presented, though Town Administrator
Horne noted that each one-percent matrix adjustment would cost approximately $71,000.

Town Administrator Horne reported that the operational portion of the budget reflects a 3.7%
increase overall, inclusive of all departments and debt service. The CIP portion of the budget
has effectively doubled, from approximately $1,300,000 in the 2026 default budget to
approximately $2,700,000 in the proposed 2027 budget. The primary drivers of the CIP
increase include the restoration of road reclamation funding that was cut by the budget

vehicle leasing program for DPW equipment.

Vice Chairman Douglas asked about the aggregate impact if a non-union matrix adjustment
of approximately 3% were to be included, and Town Administrator Horne indicated that this
would bring the operational budget increase to approximately 5%. The Board discussed the

Administration

Town Administrator Horne presented the Administration budget, noting that a significant
number of retirements over the past two years had moderated wage-line increases, as
replacement employees were hired at lower points on the compensation matrix. The
Administration budget reflects a 5.2% increase, or approximately $174,000, driven primarily
by three categories.

The first significant increase is in IT hardware, up $53,325. Town Administrator Horne
explained that the AI-driven surge in demand for computing components has substantially
raised replacement costs for desktops, laptops, printers, and police vehicle mobile data
terminals, with no changes to the lifecycle replacement schedule. A new item in the hardware
line is a planned replacement program for the town's 75 CCTV cameras, which were installed
beginning in 2020 and are now beyond their five-year warranty period. The proposed
program would replace 20% of cameras annually at an additional cost of $22,500 per year.
Town Administrator Horne noted that if the Board wished to find savings, this item could
potentially be deferred, with any remaining 2026 funds used to purchase an initial stock of
replacement cameras.

The second area is computer software, up approximately $11,000, reflecting the IT
department's work to consolidate and eliminate underutilized licenses across the organization.
Three new software products were proposed: Civic Plus, a web accessibility compliance tool
required by an April 2028 ADA deadline, at $8,300 annually; Crunch Atlas Threat
Intelligence, an AI-based network security detection tool, at $25,000 annually; and a FOIA
right-to-know process management platform that would automate the tracking and redaction
workflow for public records requests, which is currently handled entirely through manual,
email-based processes. Vice Chairman Douglas inquired whether any of these software

Parks & Recreation

Parks and Recreation Director Rick Wilhelmi presented the Parks and Recreation budget,
noting that all figures are based on the 2026 default budget. The proposed 2027 budget
reflects a 1.8% increase, or approximately $11,500, over the default.

Director Wilhelmi reported that the full-time wage line reflects step advancements for
eligible employees. The part-time wage line was reduced from the default of $106,000 to
$102,659, based on a detailed analysis of hours worked by approximately 22 seasonal and
part-time employees conducted in collaboration with Finance Director Basora. Director
Wilhelmi acknowledged that this figure is difficult to project with precision. Notably, the
part-time wage line also incorporates scorekeepers and youth basketball officials who were
previously budgeted under contract services; those individuals have been reclassified as part-
time employees, resulting in a corresponding decrease in the contract services line of
approximately $5,600. Vice Chairman Douglas noted the apparent gap between the current
year's estimated part-time wage expenditure of approximately $90,000 and the proposed
$102,659, and Director Wilhelmi confirmed that the increase accounts for the step
advancements and the reclassification of contracted scorekeepers and officials.

The park operations line increased by approximately $10,000, reflecting planned replacement
of deteriorated bollards on the rail trail, increased fertilizer and seed costs, and the addition of
ADA-compliant handicapped portable toilet units at all park locations. The program cost line
increased by approximately $8,700, reflecting rising product costs with no new programs
being added.

Goffstown Public Library

Library Board of Trustees Chair Theresa Curtis and Library Director Patty Penick presented
the Goffstown Public Library's proposed 2027 budget, reflecting a 2% increase over the
default budget of approximately $20,000.

The primary areas of increase included a new furniture line for the replacement of aging
public-use furniture with cleanable, wipeable upholstery appropriate for public settings; a
computer and communications technology allocation reflecting the library's proportional
share of the town-wide five-year hardware replacement schedule, including laptops, routers,
switches, and hubs; a programs line increase consistent with a reported 20% increase in
program attendance; and an increase in the electronic subscriptions line, partially offset by a
decrease in the books and publications line, reflecting a deliberate shift of digital materials
such as CloudLibrary out of the physical publications line and into the electronic
subscriptions line.

Vice Chairman Douglas noted that the part-time wages line appeared to reflect a 14%
increase and requested clarification, as no new positions or hour changes had been described.
Director Penick indicated that the figures came from staff-provided data and that no changes
to staffing had been made. Town Administrator Horne indicated that staff would verify and
report back on that line at the next meeting. Vice Chairman Douglas also noted apparent
discrepancies between the former telecommunications line and the new split lines for
internet, mobile communications, and computer software, suggesting the total across those
lines was higher than the prior consolidated figure. Town Administrator Horne committed to
reconciling those figures and reporting back at the October 19th meeting.

In the CIP, the library is requesting $30,000 for replacement of the HVAC system serving the
third floor. Chair Curtis explained that parts of the existing system are no longer
commercially available and must be manufactured on a custom basis. The library is also
working with utility companies to complete a New Hampshire Saves energy survey prior to
undertaking the replacement. Chair Curtis reported that an elevator heater was installed
during a recent repair, which is expected to prevent recurring issues caused by hydraulic oil
thickening in cold temperatures.

New Business

Montelona Road Culvert Project Update

Department of Public Works Director Adam Jacobs reported that the Montelona Road culvert
replacement project, conducted by contractor John Neville, was completed ahead of schedule.
The road was paved and reopened to traffic as of the prior week. Minor punch-list items
related to landscaping and grass establishment remain and will be addressed before the
project is formally closed out. Director Jacobs expressed satisfaction with the quality of the
work and noted that the structure is designed to handle significant water flow events, having
already weathered several high-discharge occurrences during construction. The town is still
awaiting a determination from FEMA regarding a requested increase in the grant amount, as
bids came in approximately $95,000 higher than the original 2022 project cost estimate.
Director Jacobs indicated that HSAM staff expressed confidence that the increase would be
approved, given the limited number of projects statewide that proceeded under that grant
availability but noted that the timeline for FEMA's decision remains unknown. Staff were
directed to coordinate with the Finance Director and Town Administrator regarding
encumbrance of local funds at fiscal year-end if the determination remains pending.
