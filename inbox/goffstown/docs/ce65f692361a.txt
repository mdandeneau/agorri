Public Hearings – Adoption of Town Ordinances (NH RSA 41:14-b)

Chairman Craig read the public hearing notice into the record. The Board was conducting this
second of two required public hearings to accept public input on the adoption of three
proposed ordinances:

Proposed Camping on Public Property Ordinance

A proposed ordinance regulating and restricting any person from using or causing to be used
any streets, sidewalks, squares, parks, rail trail, public trails, or any other town-owned public
place as a camping place, absent prior written permission from the Goffstown Select Board or
its designee.

Proposed Fires and Cooking on Town Property Ordinance

A proposed ordinance regulating and restricting any person from starting, igniting, kindling,
or cooking with any fire, cooking fire, or deep pit barbecue on any property owned or
operated by the town, absent prior written permission from the Goffstown Select Board or its
designee.

Proposed Campers & Recreational Vehicles on Public Property Ordinance

A proposed ordinance regulating and restricting any person from occupying, storing, or
parking and remaining in any camper or recreational vehicle on any town-owned public
property, public street, or public right-of-way, except on public streets where and when
parking is otherwise permitted.

Chairman Craig noted that the Select Board is scheduled to vote on these proposed
ordinances at its October 19, 2026, meeting.

NHDES Drawdown of Hadley Falls Dam and Hadley Falls Dam Removal Project

Corey Clarke, Chief Engineer at the Dam Bureau of the New Hampshire Department of
Environmental Services (NHDES), appeared before the Board to provide a public
informational presentation as required under RSA 482:13 prior to any manipulation of water
levels outside of normal operations.

Town Administrator Horne confirmed that the NHDES had been successful in securing an
ARM Fund grant of $4,500,000 for the removal of Hadley Falls Dam, which had been
approved through the Governor and Executive Council process, and that consulting firm
contracts had been updated accordingly.

Mr. Clarke explained that a concern had emerged in the spring of 2026 from the Goffstown
Village Water Precinct regarding two legacy drinking water wells located approximately one
mile upstream of Hadley Falls in a gravel aquifer adjacent to the Piscataquog River. While
initial desktop analysis suggested dam removal impacts would extend only approximately 0.5
miles upstream, the precinct had raised concerns given their concurrent investment in
developing an additional well in the same vicinity. To address these concerns, monitoring
wells near the drinking water supply wells have been instrumented to collect baseline data
prior to any drawdown.

Mr. Clarke outlined the planned drawdown, which is scheduled to begin after the Pumpkin
Regatta on October 19, 2026. The drawdown is expected to take one to two weeks to
complete, during which the low-level gate, unused for approximately 20 years since the
cessation of hydropower operations, would be operated. Mr. Clarke noted the gate is in
deteriorated condition and that heavy equipment may be required. The intent is to replace the
existing gate mechanism with a stop log-based structure, providing better operational control
over future water discharge levels.

Following the drawdown, it is planned that the impoundment would be restored but held at a
water surface elevation approximately one to one and one-half feet below the current level.
Mr. Clarke explained the rationale for this partial drawdown: it allows for ongoing
monitoring of impacts to the drinking water wells over the winter months, provides a modest
additional storm buffer for this high hazard dam, and allows riverbank stabilization and
natural revegetation to begin.

Mr. Clarke also addressed a series of questions submitted by the Piscataquog River Local
Advisory Committee (PRLAC), covering the following topics:

      Rare and Endangered Species: NHDES has coordinated and will continue to
       coordinate with NH Fish and Game pursuant to RSA 211:11 regarding potential
       impacts to wood turtles, mussels, and other aquatic species. The consulting firm will
       assist in identifying species of concern during the drawdown.
      Archaeological and Historical Resources: Extensive photographic documentation
       and survey data will be collected throughout the drawdown, including documentation
       of a former wooden dam structure located upstream of the existing concrete dam.

      Sediment Contamination: Testing completed in 2025 identified detections of metals,
       semi-volatile organic compounds, and low-level PFAS within the impoundment. The
       only exceedance of soil health standards was arsenic, consistent with background
       levels present throughout New Hampshire. Additional downstream sediment testing
       was conducted in 2026, with results pending. Sedimentation booms will be placed
       upstream and downstream of the gate during the drawdown to capture any mobilized
       sediment. Mr. Clarke noted that flow is expected to be channeled primarily along the
       left (Mill Street) side of the river, where bedrock and gravel predominate, and away
       from sediment deposits concentrated on the river-right side.
      Contingency for High Water Events: Daily monitoring will occur throughout the
       drawdown period. The stop log structure will allow for controlled slowing or
       cessation of the drawdown if needed. Flood forecasting tools provide approximately
       10-day advance warning for this stretch of the Piscataquog River.
      Waterfowl and Public River Access: Impacts are anticipated primarily in the
       downtown area. Public signage will be placed along Mill Street and at Lyons Park to
       advise of changing river conditions and lower water levels.

Regarding the broader project timeline, Mr. Clarke noted that while construction was
originally anticipated for 2027, the design, permitting, and FERC surrender process may push
construction into 2028. He committed to returning to the Board with an update by mid-winter
2027 with greater clarity on the timeline. On the question of the 2027 Pumpkin Regatta, Mr.
Clarke stated that if needed, the impoundment could be temporarily raised to accommodate
the event.

Vice Chairman Douglas asked whether the water would be raised again following the
drawdown or left permanently lower. Mr. Clarke confirmed the water level would be restored
to approximately one to one and one-half feet below the current level and maintained at that
elevation until construction begins, rather than being left fully drawn down.

Appointment: Steven Elliott, 49 Ginger Drive – Re: Blasting Ordinance and Acorn
Drive Blasting

Steven Elliott, of 49 Ginger Drive, appeared before the Board to address municipal ordinance
enforcement and public safety accountability related to blasting operations conducted by GM
Drilling and Blasting at an Acorn Drive development. Mr. Elliott presented copies of the Fire
Chief's official investigation report, the town's blasting ordinance, and a summary of his
concerns, along with proposed ordinance amendments for the Board's future consideration.

Mr. Elliott stated that the Board had before it clear documentation of violations and requested
three specific actions: (1) a formal referral of the Fire Chief's investigative report to town
counsel for enforcement review; (2) direction to town counsel to establish a legal path for
issuing municipal citations for the seven documented over blasts and the associated false
statements; and (3) a formal administrative ban on future municipal permits for GM Drilling
and Blasting within the Town of Goffstown.

Mr. Elliott summarized the findings of the report, noting that GM Drilling and Blasting
exceeded permitted safety vibration limits seven times, failed to report any of those over
blasts to state authorities as required under NH Administrative Code SAF-C 1625.04, and
that the on-site blaster provided inaccurate information to the Fire Chief during inspections.
Mr. Elliott drew a distinction between the conduct of the individual blaster and the company's
broader responsibility, and argued that the combination of repeated violations, non-reporting,
and misleading of a town official constituted intentional fraudulent bypass of the town's
oversight.

Fire Chief Benjamin Selleck addressed the Board regarding his investigation. Chief Selleck
acknowledged that blasting is a highly technical discipline for which no specific investigative
training or certification exists, and that this was his first investigation under the applicable
NFPA standard. He confirmed that he had found seven over blast violations, with the highest
recorded vibration at 2.522 inches per second against a state-established upper safe limit of
2.0 inches per second, and a structural damage threshold of 4.0 inches per second for minor
drywall cracking. Chief Selleck noted that when he asked the on-site blaster during
inspections whether vibrations were within normal limits, the blaster indicated they were,
information the Chief subsequently determined to be inaccurate, though whether the
inaccuracy was intentional was unknown to him.

Chief Selleck further reported that following receipt of Mr. Elliott's complaint, he requested
records directly from GM Drilling and Blasting at the company level. The company was
cooperative and forthcoming. The company conducted its own internal HR review, the details
of which the Chief indicated he was content with. Chief Selleck forwarded all of his
investigative findings to NH State Police Explosive Ordnance Disposal (EOD), which holds
licensing authority over blasting companies. He understood a warning was subsequently
issued by state authorities, though he did not have a copy of that action in hand. He also noted
that the developer, upon learning of the violations, chose to proceed with rock hammering
rather than additional blasting at that location, and that no further blasting permit requests had
been received from GM Drilling and Blasting.

The Board engaged in a substantive discussion on the question of enforcement authority.
Town Administrator Horne clarified that enforcement of local ordinances occurs through
district court, and that a formal finding of violation would require the Fire Chief to seek
prosecution before the district court. Selectwoman DeCesere noted that the state itself had
elected to issue only a warning rather than pursuing prosecution, and that if the violations
were attributable primarily to the individual on-site blaster rather than the company, the
company's own HR actions and resulting conduct change were relevant mitigating factors.
Chairman Craig stated that he was not inclined to direct the town to spend resources pursuing
prosecution, given the civil remedy available to Mr. Elliott and the differing interpretations of
the facts.

Vice Chairman Douglas reviewed the penalty provision of the blasting ordinance (Section
14), which provides that a person found to have violated the ordinance is guilty of a violation
punishable by a fine up to the state maximum of $1,000 per offense, with each day of
violation constituting a separate offense. He noted that the seven over blasts occurred across
five separate days spanning April and May. Vice Chairman Douglas also noted that the
primary enforcement tool available under the ordinance, permit revocation, was not
accessible in this instance because all blasting had been completed before the complaint was
received.

Chairman Craig and Vice Chairman Douglas both expressed support for the Fire Chief's
judgment and process throughout the investigation, including his referral to NH State Police.
The Board took no action on Mr. Elliott's three requested items.

Vice Chairman Douglas indicated a desire to improve the ordinance going forward and made
a motion to direct that the blasting ordinance be updated to allow the town to deny future
permits to blasters or companies found in violation.

Motion: Vice Chairman Douglas motioned to update the blasting ordinance to include
provisions allowing for the denial of future permits in cases of documented violation. No
second was received. The motion failed for lack of a second.

Mr. Elliott, before departing, requested that the Board make a formal motion to decline
enforcement and record a roll call vote. The Board declined to do so. Chairman Craig noted
that the Fire Chief followed the ordinance and that the record of the evening's discussion is
public. No further action was taken on this agenda item.

Fire Chief / Emergency Management Director

Goffstown, NH Hazard Mitigation Plan Update 2026 Adoption

Fire Chief Benjamin Selleck and Town Administrator Horne presented the Goffstown 2026
Hazard Mitigation Plan Update. Chief Selleck, serving in his dual role as Emergency
Management Director, explained that FEMA requires all municipalities to update and submit
their Hazard Mitigation Plan every five years in order to maintain eligibility for federal
mitigation grants. The 2026 update has been completed and submitted to the State of New
Hampshire. The plan evaluates a broad range of potential hazards, environmental, human-
caused, and infrastructure-related, and develops strategies for prevention and response. Chief
Selleck noted that the plan is directly tied to current grant opportunities, including a culvert
project and the Goffstown High School generator project, both of which depend on an active,
