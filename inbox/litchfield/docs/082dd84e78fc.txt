6.​ Town Budget Review:

    4241.20 – Code Enforcement
    The Committee reviewed the Code Enforcement budget. The primary change identified was an increase in salary expenses,
    with an offsetting adjustment also noted. No further questions were recorded.

    Mr. Cutter asked whether Code Enforcement anticipated additional needs or changes as Mr. Daigle continued in the role. Mr.
    Daigle indicated that the $7,000 convention and training allocation might need to increase in the future to accommodate
    attendance at the International Code Council (ICC) annual conference. The conference would provide opportunities to
    exchange information with professionals nationally and internationally and to obtain ideas for improving departmental
    operations. Mr. Perry encouraged him to request additional funding if needed.

    Mr. Daigle was thanked for attending.

    4321.10 – Sanitation
    The Committee reviewed the Sanitation budget. Mr. Mellen explained that the contract was subject to competitive bidding
    this year. Mr. Mellen received a proposal from the existing vendor, Waste Management.

    The initial proposal presented a significant increase. Following discussions with the vendor, Mr. Mellen negotiated a revised
    agreement that would defer some first-year costs and distribute them across the following four years. The revised proposal
    was intended to reduce the immediate financial impact of the new contract. Ms. Kleiner offered to provide the Committee
    with a copy of the contract and a memorandum previously sent to the Select Board explaining the changes.

    Ms. Douglas asked whether spreading the costs over five years would result in annual increases of approximately 9%. Mr.
    Perry confirmed that the anticipated annual increase was approximately 9%. Members expressed concern about committing
    to a multiyear contract based on current fuel prices, particularly if diesel prices declined during the contract period. Mr.
    Mellen agreed to contact the vendor to clarify how fuel-price fluctuations might affect the agreement and whether the
    contract could be reopened for adjustments.

    Mr. Mellen identified increases in wages, diesel fuel, electricity, and the contract as the primary changes in the budget. The
    contract increase was approximately $43,000. Most other budget lines remained at levels similar to the previous year.

    Mr. Cutter asked about the expected lifespan of the skid steer used at the transfer station. Mr. Mellen stated that the
    equipment had recently been replaced and was expected to remain serviceable for at least four years. Members discussed
    whether future capital planning should include setting aside funds for the next replacement. He explained that the previous
    skid steer had been traded in, with the remaining balance incorporated into the purchase arrangement. Members also
    referenced the updated information available through the revolving fund materials.

    Mr. Lessard asked why Mr. Mellen had received no competing bids and whether other companies had been contacted. Mr.
    Mellen explained that other vendors had not responded to follow-up inquiries. In his assessment, competing companies were
    reluctant to prepare bids because Waste Management had provided the service for many years and competitors might not
    consider the effort and cost of preparing a proposal worthwhile. He identified Casella as another major company that might
    be able to provide the service but noted that it was experiencing operational challenges.

    Mr. Mellen emphasized the importance of reliable service and explained that smaller vendors generally lacked the capacity to
    meet the town’s needs. Waste Management’s proximity and local infrastructure were also considered advantages. He noted
    that

    4411.10 – Health Department

    4220.10 – Fire Department
    The Committee reviewed the Fire Department budget. Mr. Son noted the significant increase in the proposed budget and
    clarified that the largest portion reflected the additional hours approved by voters in March of the previous year. He provided
    this information to Committee members and members of the public following the meeting.

    Deputy Chief Glancy presented the proposed 2027 budget, explaining the overall increase, personnel costs, operational
    expenses, and cost-saving measures. The Department’s total proposed budget increase was $258,957, with approximately
    $218,400 attributable to the warrant article for overnight coverage. The remaining increase reflected contractual wage
    adjustments, insurance costs, and operational expenses, offset by reductions negotiated with the Board of Selectmen.

    Personnel Costs and Overnight Coverage
    Deputy Chief Glancy explained that the overnight coverage warrant article accounted for $218,400 of the proposed increase.
    The original staffing plan included $36,000 in stipends for two firefighters to provide overnight coverage, including an
    on-call officer and an on-call firefighter. The Department eliminated the on-call firefighter stipend, saving $18,200, while
    retaining the on-call officer stipend to maintain the planned coverage arrangement. Weekend coverage costs also increased
    based on applicable rate adjustments.

    Deputy Chief Glancy proposed increasing the Fire Inspector’s hours in 2027 to address the Town’s growth and the need to
    complete required inspections of local businesses. Previously, the Inspector had provided approximately four hours per
    week and primarily conducted school inspections and plan reviews because his other position was full-time in Hudson.
    Following his retirement from that position, he became available to conduct additional inspections during business hours.
    The increased hours would allow the Department to update business records and compile information for use by other town
    departments.

    Deputy Chief Glancy explained that the additional inspector hours would help prepare records for the transition to the new
    system planned for January. The increased workload was expected to be temporary rather than an ongoing annual expense.
    The Inspector had agreed to provide additional hours for the coming year but did not intend to sustain that schedule
    indefinitely.

    Wage Increases and Cost Offsets
    Deputy Chief Glancy reviewed the wage-related expenses and the adjustments made following a meeting with the Board of
    Selectmen. The proposed budget included contractual wage increases and other personnel-related adjustments. He also
    noted that the figures presented at the meeting incorporated updated health insurance costs. These updated costs differed
    from those reviewed in previous weeks and affected the overall budget calculation.

    Deputy Chief Glancy separated the budget presentation into personnel costs and operational expenses. The operational
    budget included proposed increases of about $54,000, offset by savings of just over $13,000, for a net operational increase of
    about $41,154. The cost offsets included reductions in employee screenings, Instructor Services, and Fire Department
    equipment. He credited the Finance Department with helping identify these savings.

    Health Insurance and Dispatch Services

    Electricity and Station Operations
    Ms. Douglas asked whether the electricity budget accounted for increased overnight use of the fire station. Chief Nicoll

    Chief Nicoll reported that it had begun adjusting lighting sensors so lights would activate when needed rather than remain

    Water expenses were based on current conditions. Station Maintenance increased by $250, Subscriptions increased by $15,

    Fuel Expenses
    Deputy Chief Glancy reported an increase in fuel costs and discussed concerns about potential price fluctuations. Mr. Son
    questioned the difference between a diesel price of $4.59 and $5.07. Chief Nicoll explained that the prices reflected
    differences between off-road diesel and on-road diesel, which is subject to applicable taxes.

    The Committee discussed whether off-road diesel could be used in vehicles operating on public roads. Members raised
    concerns about compliance with state and federal laws, potential liability, and the Town’s responsibility in the event of an
    accident. The discussion emphasized the importance of using the appropriate fuel for vehicles operating on public roads.

    Uniforms and Protective Equipment
    Deputy Chief Glancy reported modest increases in custodial supplies, uniforms, accessories, and protective gear. The
    Department’s membership has grown, increasing the need to outfit additional personnel. He also noted that the cost of a set
    of firefighting gear had increased by approximately $1,800 or more compared with previous prices.

    The Department planned to replace approximately five or six sets of gear in 2027 while maintaining an appropriate
    replacement schedule. Chief Nicoll reported receiving approximately 30 donated sets of gear from Londonderry that had
    replaced its equipment in connection with PFAS-related concerns. The donated gear helped outfit new members and reduced
    the Department’s immediate need to purchase equipment for them.

    Ms. Douglas asked whether the donated equipment would reduce the number of sets that the Department needed to
    purchase to maintain its replacement schedule. Deputy Chief Glancy explained that the donation primarily helped
    accommodate new personnel and that it did not anticipate changing its planned purchases for 2027. He indicated that the
    additional equipment might reduce purchasing needs somewhat in 2028.

    Medical Supplies and Incident-Related Expenses
    Deputy Chief Glancy reported no significant increase in medical supply costs. He explained that supplies used through the
    ambulance service could often be replenished through the existing contract, reducing the need for separate purchases.

    Other-related expenses increased slightly to cover extended incidents, including construction fires, water-related
    emergencies, and special events. The Department maintained a small budget for refreshments, such as pizza, for personnel
    working extended incidents. Seminar and convention expenses also increased because of higher registration and travel costs.

    Seminars and Conventions
    The Budget Committee asked why seminar and convention spending had been unusually low in 2025, at approximately

    and the Department considered it inappropriate to undertake its usual travel. Personnel attended local events but did not

    For 2027, four Department members planned to attend the Fire Department Instructors Conference (FDIC) in Indiana.
    Deputy Chief Glancy reported that the Department had recently purchased tickets and completed arrangements after

    Budget Increase Clarification
    During the discussion, Committee members sought clarification regarding the total proposed increase and the figures shown
    in the budget presentation. The Department reiterated that the overall increase was $258,957, with approximately
    $218,400 associated with the overnight coverage warrant article. Members also discussed the impact of the Board of
    Selectmen’s cost offsets and updated health insurance figures on the totals. The Department acknowledged that some figures
    shown on the presentation might not align precisely with the revised calculations.

    Questions Regarding Fire Inspector Hours
    The Budget Committee asked about the proposed increase of approximately 100% in the Fire Inspector’s budget compared
    with the prior year. The Department explained that the increase reflected the need to inspect local businesses, update
    records, and prepare information for the Town’s new system. Because the Inspector had previously worked full-time in
    Hudson, his availability for local inspections had been limited primarily to evenings and school-related reviews.

    The Department stated that the additional hours would address a backlog of inspections and information gathering during
    2027. It did not expect the increase to continue at the same level in subsequent years. The Inspector has agreed to provide
    additional hours temporarily to help the Department complete the necessary work.

    Fire Department
    The Budget Committee discussed changes in clothing and equipment costs for the Fire Department. Members noted that
    clothing expenses had increased by approximately $1,000, while another expense had decreased to $1,500. The Committee
    discussed the Department’s usage and associated costs.

    The Fire Department reported that more than 50% of calls occur after 6:00 p.m. The Department was responding to about
    90 more calls than during the same period last year, averaging about three calls per day. Nearly 50% of responses involve
    simultaneous or back-to-back calls, requiring personnel to divide resources between incidents.

    Mr. Hayes requested a memorandum detailing call volume by time of day, including a 12-hour breakdown separately and
    longer intervals, such as 4:00 p.m. to 8:00 p.m. The information will help evaluate demand during evening hours and
    determine whether the Department is effectively utilizing its per diem personnel. The Department agreed to provide the
    requested breakdown and include the required cover-page signature.

    4290.10 – Emergency Management
    The Emergency Management budget remained level, with no proposed increases. Chief Nicoll reported plans to equip the
    Community Emergency Response Team (CERT) to assist with nighttime and weekend road closures, reducing the need for
    firefighters to remain at roadblocks for extended periods.

    The Department was purchasing safety equipment, including vehicle warning lights, magnetic identification signs, barriers,
    and a trailer to support road-closure operations. No funds had been spent on the new equipment as of the week of the
    meeting. The purchases followed discussions with the CERT team regarding its future role and responsibilities.

    The Department reported that CERT membership had not increased significantly. The Department planned recruitment
    efforts for the upcoming Department Open House and a Town Fair scheduled for January. The CERT Team currently trains
    monthly in medical and firefighting skills but has primarily assisted with parades. Expanding its responsibilities is intended
    to increase participation and give members more meaningful opportunities to support the town.

    4215.10 – Ambulance and Dispatch Services
    Chief Nicoll reviewed the Ambulance and Dispatch contracts with Hudson, which were scheduled to expire on December 31,
    2026. In April, the Department notified Hudson that it would no longer accept per-call billing and requested flat annual fees
    for both ambulance and dispatch services. Under the existing arrangement, the town paid approximately $215 per
    ambulance
    response and $17 per dispatched call. These charges could increase substantially during major storms or other events that

    generate unusually high call volumes.

    Chief Nicoll explained that Hudson sometimes billed the Town for Dispatch Services even when local personnel had already
    collected the information and completed much of the initial work before contacting Hudson. A flat-fee arrangement would
    provide predictable annual costs regardless of call volume.

    Chief Nicoll also discussed Londonderry’s proposal to provide ambulance and dispatch services. Londonderry had expressed
    interest in serving Litchfield and had previously provided dispatch services to the town before New Hampshire established
    its 911 system. He described Londonderry as a highly capable ambulance provider, noting its advanced paramedic services,
    including Rapid Sequence Intubation (RSI). Londonderry had responded to three incidents in Litchfield requiring this
    capability during the preceding month to six weeks.

    Chief Nicoll reported that Londonderry also intended to train Litchfield’s 10 paramedics to provide the advanced service
    locally. This partnership would expand the Department’s medical capabilities and improve service delivery.

    Londonderry submitted its proposal in May, while Hudson’s proposal arrived in late June. Chief Nicoll reported that Hudson’s
    proposed costs were more than twice Londonderry’s. After reviewing the proposals, the Department selected Londonderry
    and negotiated a reduction in the first-year cost from $200,000 to $175,000 to help the Town remain within its tax cap.
    Londonderry agreed to defer the additional amount to the second or third year of the contract.

    As part of the arrangement, Londonderry planned to hire four additional firefighter-paramedics, dedicate a second
    ambulance to service, and hire another Dispatcher. Beginning in January, two Dispatchers would be available from 8:00 a.m.
    to 8:00 p.m. daily to support the increased daytime workload and provide dispatching services for other communities.

    Chief Nicoll explained that Hudson currently cross-staffs its ambulance and fire engine, meaning personnel availability can
    limit responses when multiple calls occur simultaneously. Londonderry would provide two dedicated ambulances at its
    central station, with additional ambulances available from two other stations, for a total of four ambulances if needed.

    Chief Nicoll expressed confidence that the agreement would provide reliable service at a lower overall cost. Under the
    proposed arrangement, Litchfield would receive one annual bill covering both Ambulance and Dispatch Services rather than
    individual per-call charges. Members noted that Hudson’s final billing could take approximately three months or longer after
    the contract ends, so the Town would need to retain its existing financial arrangements until all outstanding charges were
    reconciled.

    Mr. Son observed that the projected three-year cost savings compared with Hudson were substantial. They also
    acknowledged Londonderry’s agreement to reduce the first-year cost by $25,000 to support the current tax cap. However,
    he said the second-year increase could consume about 20% to 25% of the following year’s tax cap. He emphasized the
    importance of accounting for this increase during future budget planning.

    Chief Nicolls reported that Londonderry’s leadership described the agreement as a cooperative arrangement intended to
    cover operating costs rather than generate a profit. Members discussed the importance of maintaining a sustainable
    partnership while ensuring the town receives appropriate service.

    Ms. Douglas asked what would happen to the ambulance revolving fund after the Town ended its relationship with Hudson.
    Ms. Kleiner indicated that the fund would likely be closed once all outstanding bills had been received, reconciled, and paid.
    No warrant article was planned at that time; the Town would consider closing the fund after the transition was complete.

    4220.90 – Fire Hydrants
    The Committee reviewed the increase associated with Fire Hydrants. Ms. Kleiner stated that the majority of the increase
    relates to the QPAC charge, which is recalculated annually based on revenues, operating costs, and debt obligations. Debt
    restructuring affects the charges assessed to participating municipalities.


    Ms. Kleiner discussed concerns about the regional cost-sharing arrangement, under which Litchfield and other
    municipalities share infrastructure costs associated with larger communities, including Nashua. Additional costs also relate
    to safety improvements at the Pennichuck water treatment plant following reservoir contamination, including new filtration
    systems. One filter was reported to cost approximately $2 million and require replacement every three months.

    Ms. Kleiner reviewed the addition of 58 fire hydrants between 2025 and 2027, associated with new developments, including
    Rose Meadows and Tallarico. At an annual cost of $19.75 per hydrant, the additional hydrants represent a total budget
    impact of $114,550.

    Mr. Cutter questioned whether impact fees could offset municipal costs associated with new development. Ms. Kleiner noted
    that fire hydrants and fire protection are not eligible expenses under the applicable impact fee statute. Ms. Douglas
    challenged claims that new developments are revenue-neutral, citing additional costs for hydrants, school transportation,
    snow removal, road paving, and specialized student services. The Committee discussed the need to reassess how
    development-related municipal costs are calculated.

    Pennichuck Rate Increases and Debt Restructuring
    Ms. Kleiner discussed a projected 21.34% rate increase effective July 2027, attributed primarily to debt restructuring and
    water treatment plant improvements. The increase also reflects adjustments dating back to June 2026, requiring the
    recovery of approximately 12 months of additional costs. Ms. Douglas questioned why Manchester could provide water
    services at substantially lower rates and suggested contacting Manchester to better understand its cost structure. It was
    noted that annual rates require approval by the Public Utilities Commission (PUC).

    Ms. Douglas expressed concern that Litchfield residents are helping finance infrastructure in other communities without
    receiving a corresponding local benefit. She emphasized the importance of documenting these costs for future discussions
    regarding a potential buyout of the existing arrangement.

    The discussion also addressed the existing agreement with Saint-Gobain concerning properties affected by PFAS
    contamination. Under the agreement, Saint-Gobain covers the cost of connecting affected properties to public water when
    testing exceeds applicable limits, and PFAS contamination is the cause. Ms. Kleiner noted that this agreement does not
    automatically transfer to a new arrangement involving Litchfield. She cautioned against making significant changes that
    could adversely affect residents whose properties may require public water connections in the near future.

    Regulatory Oversight
    Ms. Kleiner acknowledged that the rate restructuring and associated changes remain under review and that the matter is still
    pending. The Committee discussed the importance of continuing to challenge costs associated with infrastructure that does
    not directly benefit Litchfield.

    Ms. Kleiner noted that the Mitchell Group files protests on the Town's behalf whenever a new rate case is submitted to the
    PUC, although the outcome of these protests is uncertain. She also discussed potential changes to the Consumer Advocate's
    role in representing ratepayers and residents.

    The Committee acknowledged that its options remain limited while the rate case is unresolved but emphasized the
    importance of ensuring that Litchfield is adequately represented and that the financial impact of the proposed July 2027 rate
    increase is fully documented.

    4520.10 – Parks and Rec
    The Budget Committee reviewed the Parks and Rec budget, which reflected an increase of $1,126. The increase was
    attributed to anticipated utility cost increases, while field maintenance and program expenses were expected to decrease.

    Mr. Son noted that the spreadsheet appeared to show an increase of $1,562 and questioned whether the fourth selection
    had affected the calculation. He requested clarification regarding the discrepancy and the Field Maintenance, line 650.

    Mr. Burns explained that the projected decrease in field maintenance was based on the current vendor contract of $99,995.
    The Commission planned to extend the contract with the current vendor. Committee members discussed the condition of the
    athletic fields. Mr. Burns confirmed that fencing repairs had begun and would continue incrementally. Mr. Son stressed that
    he had no plans to remove the portable toilets during the upcoming year.

    Utilities and Talent Hall Maintenance
    Mr. Bourque questioned the utility budget adjustments, noting that the Fire Department’s propane budgets had decreased by
    $2,000, while the Parks and Rec budget had increased by $2,000. Mr. Burns explained that utility costs varied by facility and
    that the adjustments reflected actual bills received. Propane cost at Talent Hall increased, while Fire Department utility costs
    had decreased.

    The Committee discussed insulation at Talent Hall and whether additional maintenance was planned. Mr. Burns reported
    that the Commission had issued a request for proposals (RFP) for floor repairs and was awaiting pricing. The Commission
    was considering repairing damaged areas of the floor using funds from the appropriate trust fund. Depending on pricing and
    scheduling, the work might not begin until spring.

    Ms. Douglas asked whether the trust fund contained sufficient funds for the project and whether other projects were planned
    for those funds.

    Recreation Facility and Playground Projects
    Mr. Burns reported that the Commission had issued a separate RFP for tennis court repairs due to significant cracking in the
    court surface.

    The Commission was also working to complete improvements to the retaining walls, patio, walkway, and parking lot at
    Sawmill Brook Park. The Commission proposed a noise barrier wall for the pickleball courts and adjacent areas. Playground
    repairs at Roy Memorial Park were progressing, including repairs to a slide. The Commission approved repairs to a glider
    and expected to address a rocking component that broke the previous week.

    Talent Hall Floor Protection
    Mr. Lessard asked how the Commission would protect Talent Hall floor after repairs, particularly during events involving
    snow-covered footwear, chairs, and heavy use. Mr. Burns explained that the Commission planned to require protective floor
    coverings for events, including concerts and comedy nights. Possible materials included Tyvek or similar coverings designed
    to protect the floor and prevent chairs from sliding. The Commission was also considering requiring a security deposit to
    address potential damage or cleaning costs.

    Mr. Burns confirmed that the Town has already charged outside organizations for facility use. Mr. Lessard emphasized the
    importance of establishing consistent requirements for all renters to ensure adequate floor protection.

    The Committee thanked Mr. Burns for attending the meeting.

    were higher than anticipated. Some proposals would also require the Town to relinquish substantial control over its systems
    by moving to a fully managed environment. He explored a potential collaboration with the School District, but that effort had
    not produced a workable solution because of the District’s existing workload. Additional discussions with other
    organizations were planned to identify a more suitable arrangement.

    The initial estimate for backup services included a $6,000 annual stipend and $4,000 in hourly support, for a total of
    $10,000. Mr. Brunelle explained that the amount was intended as an allowance for the proposed arrangement rather than a
    finalized contract. Committee members discussed the need to establish an appropriate amount based on actual requirements
    and noted that a previous backup services contract had cost approximately $13,000 annually.

    Ms. Douglas also asked about the term of the existing contract, which was identified as covering 2021–2025.

    Software Licensing Costs
    Ms. Douglas questioned the significant increase in VMware licensing costs, noting that the expense had been less than
    $1,200 in 2025 but was projected to exceed $12,000.

    Mr. Brunelle explained that Broadcom acquired VMware and subsequently changed its pricing structure. The Town’s annual
    cost

    had increased to approximately $8,000, then decreased to approximately $5,000 to $5,600, before rising again to
    approximately $12,000 during the latest contract negotiations. He noted that VMware was the only provider of the software
    currently used by the Town and that the company’s licensing practices had created challenges, including warnings when the
    Town did not respond to contract communications.

    The Department plans to evaluate alternatives to VMware as part of a project anticipated for 2027–2028. In the interim, Mr.
    Brunelle indicated that the Town intended to negotiate a three-year contract to provide continuity while developing a
    longer-term transition plan.

    Mr. Son emphasized the importance of incorporating these potential changes into the town’s long-term IT plan. He noted that
    transitioning away from VMware could involve significant capital costs that might not be reflected in the
    current operating budget. Members also discussed the importance of ensuring continuity if the Town’s existing IT support
    arrangements change.

    Mr. Brunelle stated that there were no immediate plans to leave the position and expressed a commitment to continuing to
    support the Town. He explained that the proposed one-year arrangement had been intended to give the Town time to
    establish backup coverage before making longer-term decisions.

    Mr. Perry clarified that Mr. Brunelle had initially sought a longer contract but had proposed a one-year arrangement because
    of concerns about the lack of backup support. The shorter term would allow time to develop an appropriate backup solution
    before determining the long-term arrangement.

    Committee members discussed the value of the Town’s current IT support and the potential cost of obtaining comparable
    services externally. They agreed that backup coverage was an important consideration and that long-term planning should
    account for staffing continuity, software transitions, and the potential costs of replacing existing systems.

    Generator Maintenance
    A Committee member asked about an increase in generator maintenance costs under line 630. Mr. Brunelle explained that
    the generators had been covered by warranties during their initial years of operation. After the warranties expired, the Town
    needed to obtain vendor support for maintenance and repairs, resulting in higher costs.

    Mr. Brunelle clarified that the generators serve the Town’s three communications sites, which support the public safety
    network. The generators are relatively small units, and the maintenance allocation accounts for the equipment at all three
    locations. Scheduled generator maintenance was expected to take place the following week.
