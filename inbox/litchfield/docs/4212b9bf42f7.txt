        b.​ Request to increase LPD Detail Cruiser Fee
            BOS Letter Detail Cruiser Fee

    Captain Tessier requested an increase to the police detail cruiser fee from $15 to $25 per hour. The fee has remained
    unchanged since February 2012. The Police Department explained that the current rate no longer reflects the costs

    associated with operating and maintaining fully equipped police cruisers used for private details, including fuel, oil
    changes, tires, routine maintenance, and general wear and tear. At the current rate, the Town subsidizes a portion of
    the cost of providing cruisers for private details.

    Captain Tessier noted that the Fire Department currently charges $25 per hour for the use of detailed vehicles.
    Increasing the police cruiser fee to $25 per hour would establish consistency between Town Departments. The
    Department also reported that the proposed rate is generally consistent with rates charged by other municipalities
    throughout New Hampshire. Examples cited included New Hampshire State Police at $28.05 per hour, and several
    municipalities at $25 per hour, including Hanover, Bristol, Franklin, Guilford, Raymond, Ridge, and Dunbarton. Derry
    charges the FEMA-established rate of $22.91 per hour.

    Captain Tessier explained that the proposed increase is intended to offset rising operating expenses rather than
    generate additional profit for the Town. The FEMA rate for a Ford Explorer/Interceptor was $22.91 per hour in 2023,
    which was the most recent rate available to the Department. The Board discussed whether the FEMA rate would
    accurately reflect the Town's costs because the Police Department operates different types of vehicles, including
    pickup trucks, which have varying operating costs.

   F.​ 2027 Proposed Budget Review
   Ms. Kleiner provided the Board with an update on the estimated 2026 revenues and the upcoming tax-setting process.
   She noted that the revenue figures must be filed with the New Hampshire Department of Revenue Administration
   (DRA). Once the official MS-1 is received, the Town will complete the tax-setting process using the final figures for
   Meals and Rooms

   and the Highway Block Grant.

   Ms. Kleiner stated that the Town had initially estimated approximately $15 million in assessed-value growth for
   2026. The actual growth was approximately $47,201,107, substantially exceeding the estimate. This increase in
   assessed valuation is expected to reduce the tax rate below the previously anticipated amount.

   Based on the 2026 budget and anticipated revenues, including the $168,000 appropriation for increased fire
   department coverage, the Town had previously estimated a tax rate of approximately $2.77 to $2.78. The revised
   estimate is approximately $2.72, assuming the remaining revenues, including Meals and Rooms, remain consistent
   with expectations. The revised estimate would result in a tax rate approximately $0.05 to $0.06 lower than previously
   projected.

   The Board discussed the effect of the revenue adjustment on the tax cap. Ms. Kleiner stated that the adjustment
   applies to the 2026 budget that was previously filed and increases the revenue recognized for the current year. While
   the adjustment lowers the current-year tax requirement and reduces the increase anticipated on the December tax
   bill, it also affects the calculation of the following year's tax cap. Ms. Kleiner advised the Board that the apparent
   increase in the amount over the tax cap is attributable primarily to the adjustment to 2026 revenues, rather than an
   increase in the proposed 2027 budget.

   The Board also reviewed adjustments to the proposed 2027 revenues. Following consultation with Town Clerk/Tax
   Collector Terri Briand, the Town agreed to use $150,000 for motor vehicle revenue. The Town also adjusted interest
   and permit revenues based on continuing positive trends. Following those adjustments, the proposed budget was
   approximately $118,000 over the tax cap.

   The Board discussed the appropriate assessed-value growth assumption to use when developing the 2027 tax rate.
   The Town had previously used $15 million as a conservative estimate, but actual growth reached approximately
   $47.2 million. Given the amount of ongoing construction, the Board noted that growth could remain strong and
   agreed that the appropriate estimate should be discussed further.

   Ms. Kleiner also reviewed the Veteran Tax Credit and abatements. She noted that additional funds should not be added
   to the overlay at this time because the revaluation has not yet been completed. The Board was advised that
   abatements resulting from the revaluation would be addressed separately.

   Ms. Kleiner reviewed the revenue documents requiring signatures. The MS-535 required signatures and submission to
   the DRA. The revised revenue figure from the MS-434, which required the appropriate signature.

   Captain Tessier informed the Board that the requested overtime was necessary because staffing does not operate on a
   one-for-one basis. Officers accrue earned time and holidays, and those absences must be covered to maintain
   operational standards.

    Police Department Budget – Vehicle Repair and Maintenance
    Mr. Perry next reviewed the Police Department Vehicle Repair and Maintenance line 4210-10-660. The Department
    had already reduced the proposed budget by $8,920. The Board discussed an additional reduction of $6,080, which
    would reduce the line to approximately $30,000.

    Captain Tessier acknowledged that the newly purchased vehicles should reduce maintenance costs and explained that
    the initial $8,920 reduction reflected that expectation. However, the Department expressed concern that an
    additional $6,080 reduction could leave insufficient funding for repairs to the aging fleet. Cruiser 8 was currently
    undergoing repairs for multiple issues, and additional vehicles were expecting repairs later in the year.

    Captain Tessier reported that routine maintenance, including oil changes, brakes, tires, lights, and wipers, had cost
    $12,236 in 2025. Reducing the account to approximately $30,000 would leave less than $18,000 for major repairs.
    The Board noted that a major engine repair could cost approximately $7,500 to $8,500.

    Ms. Kleiner discussed establishing a separate fund for police vehicle repairs. The proposed fund could be supported
    through a warrant article and potentially funded with $10,000 initially, providing a mechanism for accumulating
    funds for future repairs without relying entirely on the annual operating budget. The Board discussed a potential fund
    balance of approximately $20,000 to $25,000.

    Captain Tessier also discussed using police detail revenues. He explained that another New Hampshire town charges
    $55 per hour for detail work and uses the associated revenue for police vehicle purchases and maintenance.

    The Board discussed whether a similar mechanism could provide additional support for the Town's police fleet.

Motion:​         ( Selectman S. Perry / Selectman J. Brunelle ) Moved to reduce line 660, PD Vehicle Repairs and
                 Maintenance, down to $30,000. So, the total reduction from 2026 would be $15,000.
Vote: ​          (4-0-0) The motion carried.

    The Board also directed that the proposed warrant article for a Vehicle Repair Fund be communicated to the Police
    Chief.

    Approximately $17,000 remained in the current year's budget. He asked what training was planned and whether
    some of the proposed expenditures could be reduced.

    Captain Tessier explained that the Department had focused its recent resources on rebuilding staffing, addressing the
    aging vehicle fleet, and evaluating its facility. With several new officers, the Department is developing an internal

career-pathing program and intends to align future training with officers' assignments and professional development
needs. Planned training includes instructor development, specialized training, and programs designed to advance
officers' knowledge and skills.

Mr. Brunelle asked whether the planned training could be scheduled and paid for from the current year's remaining
budget. Captain Tessier stated that several training programs were already scheduled and that the Department
expected to spend a portion of the remaining $17,000. However, he did not expect to use the entire amount.

Mr. Perry reviewed the Police Department’s proposed training budget of $18,000. He reported that the Department’s
highest prior training expenditure was $13,400 in 2024, when the Department was also operating with seven fewer
officers. The Department spent approximately $8,000 in 2025 while prioritizing recruitment and rebuilding staffing

Captain Tessier explained that New Hampshire Police Standards and Training Council requirements have increased
from a minimum of six training hours per officer in 2022 to 24 hours, applicable to part-time and full-time certified
officers, as well as command staff. He emphasized that maintaining required training is essential to certification and
that training also supports officer recruitment and retention. Captain Tessier stated that he actively seeks low-cost and
federally funded opportunities, including an upcoming FBI-sponsored training costing $25.

The Board agreed that specialized training for Police, Fire, the Building Inspector, and Tax Collector should not be
reduced. Members discussed whether the Department could provide a list of anticipated training expenses supporting
the $18,000 request. Captain Tessier stated that he would provide that information to the Board. The Board noted
that the line had been significantly underspent and would continue to monitor it through the end of the fiscal year. The
Board also clarified that overtime associated with Police Training is budgeted separately under line 149.

The Board took no action to reduce the Police Department's Training budget at this time. Members expressed support
for maintaining adequate training, particularly given the Department’s relatively young workforce and the importance
of training for recruitment and retention.

Police Department – Open Position / SRO
The Board next reviewed 4210-10, line 111, concerning the Police Department's open position. The Board discussed a
proposal to reduce the position to a $1 placeholder, representing a reduction of approximately $74,630 in salary
funding. Captain Tessier opposed the reduction, explaining that eliminating the funding would limit its ability to
provide a more consistent School Resource Officer (SRO) presence and increase SRO hours. He emphasized that he
was not seeking to increase the authorized staffing level, but rather to restore a position previously removed during
budget constraints.

Captain Tessier reported that he had applied for a COPS grant and it had advanced through the first electronic
submission stage. He asked whether reducing the position to $1 would disqualify the Town from receiving the grant.
Mr. Brunelle stressed that voters had previously approved the position before the Budget Committee removed it. He
noted that the
Select Board did not defend the Police Department’s need for an additional officer.

Mr. Brunelle discussed funding the position for one-half of the fiscal year rather than fully funding it. Captain Tessier
explained that the grant would operate on a sliding scale, with federal funding subsidizing approximately:
    a.​ $51,000 in the first year.
    b.​ $41,000 in the second year.
    c.​ $31,000 in the third year.
    d.​ Fully funded by the Town in the fourth year.

Ms. Kleiner noted that a half-year funding approach was estimated to reduce expenditures by $62,466 while
preserving the position.

    Litchfield Select Board​      ​        ​        ​        ​        ​        ​       ​                September 3, 2026
    Mr. Perry estimated that, without the grant, the position and associated costs would total approximately $124,932. At
    the same time, the salary itself was listed at approximately $74,630 because retirement, insurance, workers’
    compensation, and other personnel costs are budgeted separately.

    Captain Tessier clarified that the position would provide additional part-time SRO hours. The existing arrangement
    provides approximately one day per week, and the additional position would allow the Department to increase those
    hours.

    The Board discussed the potential impact of partial funding on the 2028 tax cap, as well as how Town funding
    interacts with the COPS grant. Captain Tessier explained that the grant permits funding to offset or replace an officer
    previously removed because of budget constraints, which is uncommon among grant programs. Mr. Perry requested
    clarification regarding whether a half-year appropriation would affect the grant’s funding schedule and
    reimbursement. Captain Tessier indicated that the grant funding was scheduled to begin in 2027 but acknowledged
    that the precise treatment of a July start date required confirmation.

    Ms. Kleiner explained that the Department of Justice would base reimbursement on the actual amount paid for the
    position and that the Town would be required to document salary, retirement, workers’ compensation, and other
    associated costs. The Board continued to discuss whether partial-year funding would allow the Town to maximize the
    grant while limiting its initial budget commitment. The discussion ended with consideration of funding the position
    for one-half of the year while pursuing the grant opportunity.

    The Board discussed funding for a new police officer position in connection with a potential grant from the
    Department of Justice (DOJ). The proposed budget scale was intended to demonstrate increasing municipal costs and
    decreasing grant costs and was not required to remain at the illustrative amounts of $51,000, $41,000, and $31,000.

    The Board noted that the position would be funded for half the year and could begin immediately if the grant were
    awarded. If the grant were not awarded, the budgeted funds would remain available to begin the position on July 1.
    Captain Tessier supported the approach, noting that the Department had not been fully staffed since 2019 and had
    subsequently lost seven officers. The Board expressed support for restoring the Department to full staffing.

    The Board discussed the need to demonstrate a financial commitment to the grant application and reviewed the
    appropriate budget accounts and reductions.
