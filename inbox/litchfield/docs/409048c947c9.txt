I.​ Call to Order:

III.​ Business:
      A.​ Budget Update
      Town Administrator Kim Kleiner provided an update on the FY 2027 budget. She mentioned that the Town is currently
      $46,832 over the cap. She reported that an escalation factor had been identified in a website-related expense that
      had not previously been captured. After correcting the calculation, the amount increased to $3,834.

    Ms. Kleiner noted that the Town had received utility assessments and expected approximately $4.7 million in
    additional utility assessment revenue. At the current tax rate, this would represent approximately $12,471. The
    Department Heads reviewed their budgets and did not identify expenditures that appeared over-budgeted or readily
    reducible. Fuel remained uncertain because the budget was based on projected rates provided by the fuel company.

    B.​ 4220.10 Fire Department
    4220.10 – 127 Training Wages.
    Mr. Perry reviewed the Fire Department budget. A proposed reduction of $8,412 would reduce the Training budget to
    $40,000. The Department was currently tracking below $20,000, compared with approximately $36,000 spent the
    previous year.

    Deputy Chief Glancy explained that historical training expenditures were affected by staffing levels and the use of per
    diem personnel. Lower staffing levels had reduced training participation, while the Department continued to budget
    for training based on anticipated operational needs.

    Chief Nicoll reported that staffing had increased substantially and that the Department now has approximately 50
    firefighters, including per diem personnel. Four individuals have recently joined, including members with little or no
    prior experience, and two new members have enrolled in EMT training. Additional training was anticipated as the

    C.​ 4290.10 Emergency Management
    4290.10 – 740 Equipment Purchases
    Mr. Perry reviewed line 740, Emergency Management Equipment Purchases, with a proposed $1,500 reduction from
    the $5,000 budget. He noted that no expenditure had been made to date. Chief Nicoll explained that the Budget
    Committee had consistently reduced the line item over the past three years. He added that the Select Board had
    previously requested a CERT team, but it was not funded. A local company donated enough to make the CERT team
    operational.

    Chief Nicoll explained that it had intentionally waited to determine the CERT team’s specific role before spending the
    $5,000 appropriated for the current year. The Department has since ordered equipment, including backpacks,
    flashlights, magnetic vehicle decals, and amber warning lights. The equipment is necessary to allow team members to
    safely assist during storms and other incidents without assigning a fire apparatus. The Department expects to use the
    full $5,000 this year to outfit the team properly.

    Chief Nicoll stated that additional equipment and training will be needed next year. The CERT team has participated in
    joint training with neighboring emergency response organizations and plans to conduct additional wilderness
    search-and-rescue training. He requested that the full $5,000 allocation remain available this year so the team can
    obtain the equipment necessary to perform their duties safely and effectively.

    The Board discussed the value of the CERT team and its role in the community. Members noted that the volunteers are
    not compensated and primarily seek the equipment and training necessary to perform the duties requested of them.

    Chief Nicoll also discussed plans to provide the team with a designated table and signage for future community open
    houses to increase public awareness and encourage additional volunteers.

   The Board acknowledged the importance of maintaining the search team and its relationship with other emergency
   response agencies. Members emphasized that the proposed reduction was not personal and was intended as part of
   the overall budget review. Chief Nicoll reiterated that the additional funding would establish and properly equip the
   team.

   Fire Department Staffing
   The Board and Fire Department discussed current staffing levels and the need to maintain sufficient personnel to
   cover scheduled shifts. Chief Nicoll said they currently have about 50 members, including per-diem personnel, and
   would like to maintain a higher per-diem number to provide greater flexibility when full-time personnel are
   unavailable.

   Chief Nicoll explained that many per-diem firefighters work full-time jobs at other departments, and several
   employees work the same shifts. As a result, when an employee is absent, the Department may have limited personnel
   available to fill the vacancy. Maintaining a larger membership provides additional options for covering shifts. Chief
   Nicoll noted that it has maintained coverage, particularly on weekends and overnight shifts, but doing so requires
   flexibility.

   The Board discussed how staffing levels affect workers' compensation costs and overtime. Deputy Chief Glancy
   reported receiving about eight applications per week and explained that the Department attempts to maintain an
   appropriate balance between full-time, per-diem, and call personnel.

   Chief Nicoll discussed the increasing number of multiple and simultaneous calls. The Department recently
   experienced three calls within approximately 20 minutes, requiring additional personnel beyond the duty crew and
   per-diem staff. He stated that maintaining an adequate call force has reduced the burden on individual volunteers,
   who previously faced significant pressure responding to approximately 900 calls per year.

   Staffing Gaps and Overtime
   Mr. Brunelle asked about staffing gaps during periods when full-time personnel transition between shifts and call
   personnel are unavailable because of their regular employment. Chief Nicoll explained that coverage is particularly
   challenging during certain afternoon and early-evening hours and that personnel sometimes report early or remain
   beyond their scheduled hours to ensure the Town remains covered.

   Chief Nicoll explained that expanding full-time personnel to 10-hour shifts could address some of the coverage gaps,
   although additional per-diem staffing would still be necessary. The Board noted that relying on overtime to cover
   these periods increases labor costs and questioned whether expanding service coverage would ultimately reduce
   overtime sufficiently to offset the additional expense.

   The Board thanked the Fire Department for the information and continued discussion regarding staffing, coverage,
   and budget needs.
