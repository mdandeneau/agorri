    D.​ Amendment of Bylaws
        Litchfield_CIP_Bylaws

    The Committee discussed whether the Budget Committee representative should retain voting privileges. Mr. Bourque
    explained that the Budget Committee had previously opposed allowing its representatives to vote on Committees involving
    financial matters. The Budget Committee intended its representatives to serve in an informational capacity and
    communicate Committee discussions and recommendations back to the Budget Committee.

    This position also applied to representatives serving on the Facilities Improvement Committee (FIC).

    Mr. Minkarah reviewed its bylaws, which identified six voting members:
        a.​ One representative from the Planning Board
        b.​ One representative from the Budget Committee
        c.​ One representative from the Board of Selectmen
        d.​ One representative from the School Board
        e.​ Two citizen members.

    He discussed amending Article II, Sections 2.1 and 2.3, to designate the Budget Committee representative as a non-voting
    member. This change would reduce the number of voting members from six to five and adjust the quorum requirements.

Motion:​​       (P. Plansky / P. Stone ) Moved to amend the bylaws, changing the Budget Committee representative from a
                voting member to a non-voting member.
Discussion:​    None
Vote: ​ ​       (5-0-1) The motion carried. (B. Bourque abstained)

    Mr. Minkarah agreed to email the revised bylaws to Town Administrator Kim Kleiner.

   G.​ CIP Project Ranking
       a.​ Police Department
   Police Station
   The Committee reviewed the proposed new police facility, which had received a Necessary (N) ranking in the previous
   year’s CIP. The overall scope remained substantially the same.

   Members agreed that the facility was needed to address space limitations, storage deficiencies, and operational and
   procedural concerns. However, they debated whether the project warranted a higher priority classification. Mr. Perry
   argued that the facility was necessary because existing conditions were placing increasing strain on department
   operations. Mr. Parker distinguished between a necessary project and an urgent one, noting that the current facility
   remained operational despite its limitations.

   Members also discussed the project's history in the CIP and the voters' previous rejection of funding. The Town presented
   the proposed facility to voters, but they did not approve it. Members noted that the Police Chief was not seeking funding
   for the project in the current budget cycle while the Town addressed the school project. They expressed concern that
   presenting both major projects for funding simultaneously could jeopardize both proposals. Members anticipated that the
   Police Chief would return with updated plans after the school project's direction became clearer.

Motion:​​      (S. Perry / T. Tucker) Moved to rank the police station project as ‘Necessary.’
Discussion:​   None
Vote: ​ ​      (4-1-0) The motion carried. (Mr. Parker voted ‘No’)

   Police Cruiser Replacement
   Mr. Minkarah reviewed the police cruiser replacement schedule. The Department had purchased multiple cruisers during
   the previous year to address a backlog in vehicle replacements. The current schedule provides for replacing one cruiser
   annually through 2031, then returning to the previous replacement cycle. Members noted that the recent purchases had
   reduced the Department's immediate needs, although two older vehicles continued to incur repair costs.

Motion:​​      (S. Perry / M. Parker) Moved to rank the police cruiser replacements as ‘Necessary.’
Discussion:​   None
Vote: ​ ​      (5-0-0) The motion carried.

Vote: ​ ​      (5-0-0) The motion carried.

   Breathing Apparatus
   Mr. Minkarah discussed replacing the Fire Department's breathing apparatus cylinders. Members confirmed that the
   replacement would include all cylinders because equipment changes and compatibility requirements made a phased
   replacement impractical. Members also noted that the cylinders had reached or were approaching the end of their useful
   lives and required periodic pressure testing. Replacing the equipment together would maintain consistency across the

Motion:​​      (S. Perry / M. Parker) Moved to rank the cylinder replacement as ‘Necessary.’

       c.​ Highway Department
   Paving Program
   Mr. Minkarah reviewed the proposal to add $200,000 annually to the paving program operating budget. The item had
   been ranked as Urgent during the previous year. Mr. Perry agreed that the priority remained urgent because several roads
   were deteriorating and available funding was insufficient to maintain the road network.

   Mr. Perry questioned whether the proposed $200,000 annual investment over five years would adequately address the
   Town's needs. He noted that the amount would help but might not prevent the Town from falling further behind. Ms.
   Kleiner discussed reconsidering funding options after receiving the road analysis, including proposing another $200,000
   appropriation or considering a municipal motor vehicle registration fee. She acknowledged that the proposed funding had
   failed at the previous year's vote and that a registration fee might not generate sufficient revenue during the first year.

   Mr. Perry estimated that the Town could be approximately $1,500,000 behind in necessary road maintenance. Ms. Kleiner
   discussed the potential effect on residents' tax bills and noted that road conditions accounted for a significant portion of
   highway-related complaints.

Motion:​​      ( S. Perry / M. Parker ) Moved to mark the paving program as ‘Urgent.’
Discussion:​   None
Vote: ​ ​      (5-0-0) The motion carried.

   Highway Storage Garage
   The Committee revisited the Highway storage garage, previously classified as urgent. Mr. Perry emphasized that an
   enclosed garage would protect vehicles from rust and weather exposure, potentially extending their service lives by five to
   ten years.

   Mr. Perry discussed construction costs, which had exceeded initial expectations, with some estimates reportedly reaching
   twice the anticipated amount. One estimate was about $350,000, and another was $600,000. Ms. Kleiner attributed the
   increased costs to rising construction and material prices and discussed whether alternative building designs or a smaller
   structure could reduce expenses. One option was to build the building and slab first and defer heating installation.

   Ms. Kleiner also noted that the existing garage lacked sufficient space for vehicle repairs and preventive maintenance.
   Employees sometimes had to move multiple vehicles or relocate the backhoe to access the available workspace. The
   Committee acknowledged that performing maintenance in-house helped reduce costs and that adequate garage space was
   essential to supporting those efforts. Members discussed using impact fees from anticipated development to advance the
   project.

Motion:​        (S. Perry / M. Parker) Moved to classify the Highway storage garage as ‘Urgent.’
Discussion:​    None
Vote: ​ ​       (5-0-0) The motion carried.

       e.​ Library
   Library Septic System Project
   The Committee reviewed the library’s septic system project and discussed whether to make an exception to the $50,000


   threshold for inclusion in the Capital Improvement Plan (CIP). Mr. Bourque noted that the project had previously been
   included
   in the CIP to accommodate the library, whose individual projects generally fell below the minimum threshold.

   The Committee discussed whether the septic system project should remain on the CIP and whether to include all related
   expenses when determining the total project cost.

   Ms. Kleiner reviewed what the library had initially expected to use $34,921 in impact fees, with the anticipated cost
   changing after donations were received. She noted that additional expenses included test kits, removal of the old system,
   and approximately $10,000 for septic system pumping to prevent further deterioration. She also discussed using trust
   funds and donations.

   Ms. Kleiner noted that the Board of Selectmen had returned an electrical invoice estimated at $6,000 to $7,000 after
   determining that donation funds remained available. The Committee did not know how it would ultimately be paid.

   Ms. Kleiner reported that $24,912 had been spent from impact fees and that an additional $6,150 had been requested for
   payment from impact fees. Mr. Perry mentioned the previously approved amount of $34,921 and the anticipated $8,000
   cost difference associated with replacing the system with a different system.

   Members agreed that the Committee needed a complete accounting of all project expenses, including costs paid from
   donations, impact fees, trust funds, and the library’s operating budget. The current documented total was $41,673, but
   members expected additional expenses could bring the total to above $50,000. The Committee discussed how delaying its
   decision would affect the CIP schedule and the proposed public hearing, which was considered for November 17.

   Mr. Minkarah noted that delaying the review could require moving the public hearing to December 1, depending on the
   Committee’s meeting schedule.

Motion:​       ( B. Bourque / P. Plansky) Moved to return the library septic system project to the CIP, classify it as ‘Urgent’,
Discussion:​   None
Vote: ​ ​      (5-0-0) The motion carried.

       f.​ Conservation Commission
   Conservation Commission Land Purchase Fund
   Mr. Minkarah reviewed the Conservation Commission’s land purchase fund, which had been classified as Desirable the
   previous year. The proposal allocated approximately $2,000,000 over six years, with annual amounts intended to be
   distributed evenly. Members discussed the total allocation and questioned whether the project should remain a priority
   given other municipal needs, including road repairs.

   Ms. Kleiner mentioned the fund’s existing revenue from the Land Use Change Tax, of which the Conservation Commission
   receives 80%. She noted that recent development activity had generated additional revenue and discussed whether those
   funds would be sufficient to support future land purchases without the proposed annual allocation. The Committee also
   noted that the Conservation Commission had not identified a specific property for purchase but wished to maintain
   sufficient funds to act when suitable land became available.

   Mr. Perry discussed anticipated revenue from the Tallarico development, which included 26 lots, and estimated that the
   Commission could receive approximately $447,000 from the applicable land use change tax revenue. Ms. Kleiner noted
   that the Commission would collect these funds before issuing certificates for the affected properties. The Committee
   discussed reviewing projected revenues and existing fund balances before determining the appropriate priority for the
   land purchase fund.
