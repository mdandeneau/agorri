6.​ Budget Committee Vacancy Interviews:
    The Committee interviewed Ron O’Blenis, Joe Lessard, and Mike Parker for the vacancy created by Mr. Taylor's departure.
    Each candidate discussed their background, relevant experience, potential contributions, and interest in serving.

    Ron O’Blenis discussed his engineering background, including approximately 20 years in railroad engineering involving
    budgeting, structures, facilities, field operations, contracts, bridges, buildings, design review, and project evaluation. He
    also discussed work on the Central Artery/Tunnel Project and emphasized practical solutions, affordability, compromise,
    and distinguishing necessary work from desired work. He reported spending approximately 500 to 600 hours researching
    the history of the School District and Town budgets. He said he would consider running to continue serving after an
    interim appointment.

    Mr. O’Blenis stated that the Committee should review Town and School District budgets independently while considering
    their combined taxpayer impact. He referenced his participation in a 1994 committee evaluating Town Hall options and
    emphasized evaluating projects based on necessity, affordability, and available resources.

    Joe Lessard reported approximately 14 years of residency and employment with National Grid as a program manager

7.​ Town Budget Review:
    The Committee began its Town Budget review. Members had received the budget electronically and a memorandum from
    Ms. Kleiner concerning the Board of Selectmen's work.

    Mr. Son clarified that the Town was approximately $5,600 under the tax cap, rather than $4,700 over the tax cap as
    previously stated. The Board of Selectmen planned to consider an additional $50,000 reduction in taxes, which would
    place the Town approximately $55,000 under the tax cap before the Committee's budget voting process.

    The budget review was expected to take two to three weeks. Department Heads and Town Administrator Kim Kleiner
    would present budgets and answer questions. The Committee would not vote during the review sessions; information
    gathered would inform subsequent voting.

    Budget Development Process
    Ms. Kleiner stated that budget discussions began as early as March because several significant contracts were scheduled to
    expire, including Ambulance, Dispatch, and Solid Waste. The Town also anticipated changes involving Pennichuck and the
    Public Utilities Commission (PUC). Department Heads initially submitted a proposed budget reflecting a 9.28% increase.
    The Select Board reduced the proposal by $125,000, bringing the current budget to about $5,600 under the tax cap.

    The Committee discussed potentially using Fire Department impact fees to pay down the Fire Department bond. The Town
    added approximately $55 million to its valuation in 2026. Ms. Kleiner stated that using impact fees would depend on
    sufficient fee availability and would not necessarily be possible each year. Major contract increases expected this year are
    not anticipated to recur at the same level next year. A warrant article expected in March was also identified as a potential
    funding source.

    Members discussed a potential additional $50,000 Ambulance increase, recurring warrant article impacts, and
    monitoring revenues. Significant increases were identified in Fire, Police Administration, Hydrants, Road Maintenance,
    Ambulance, and Sanitation Administration. Infrastructure projects included McElwain and Kemo Circle. Ambulance costs
    also involved discussions with Londonderry, while Sanitation Administration reduced its proposed increase through
    negotiations with its service provider. IT remained an identified need.

    The Committee discussed a prior warrant article involving $200,000 for the Paving (Highway Department) budget. The
    Town clarified that the amount was not related to the highway block grant and that the current budget would not exceed
    the $200,000 amount discussed.

    4130.10 – Board of Selectmen
    The Board of Selectmen budget reflects a total increase of approximately $9,400, primarily involving wages for the Town
    Administrator and Administrative Assistant. The Administrative Assistant's salary had been increased earlier in the year
    because of additional responsibilities.

    Website Support increased because of the CivicPlus contract, which allows the implementation cost to be distributed over
    several years. Dues and Subscriptions increased slightly. A $750 expense associated with the Town Administrator's
    contract will be classified as dues or subscriptions rather than training. Clerical Support reflects contractual administrative
    assistance, while training was reduced because of the reclassification. The Selectmen’s Expense line was also reduced.

    Training and Professional Development: The Town will move unused training funds from Planning, Zoning, and similar
    committees to the Selectmen’s budget while continuing to support training and professional development. Required Fire
    and Police training remains in those departments' budgets.

    4130.30 – Town Meeting
    Town Clerk/Tax Collector Terri Briand stated that the Town Meeting budget will decrease by $7,058, primarily because
    elections will decrease from three to one. Wages, programming, and ballot postage will decrease, while provisions and
    supplies will increase due to maintenance contracts.

    4140.10 – Town Clerk
    The Town Clerk budget will decrease by $18,728 due to personnel changes affecting wages and benefits. Overtime will
    increase to $692 because the Deputy Town Clerk is now hourly rather than salaried. Software, dog tags, licensing, and
    postage costs will increase slightly. The Town is encouraging electronic billing to reduce postage and printing.

    The Town sent approximately 500 letters for the annual verification process, with another 150 remaining under the
    30-day National Change of Address (NCOA) process, for approximately 650 letters. The Town had already exceeded the
    current budget for this expense. The wage reduction primarily reflects personnel changes, while health insurance costs
    decreased because coverage changed from a family plan to individual coverage.

    4150.40 – Tax Collection
    The Tax Collection budget will decrease because of personnel changes and corresponding wage and benefit reductions.
    Overtime is divided between Tax Collection and Town Clerk budgets. Postage, software, subscriptions, and dues will
    increase, including costs for required seminars and training.

    4150.10 – Accounting
    Ms. White reviewed the Accounting budget and identified approximately $2,900 in wages, with minor increases and
    decreases totaling $400. No further questions were raised.

    4316.30 – Street Lighting
    The Street Lighting budget will remain level. The Town experienced four streetlight outages and worked with the utility
    provider to address them. Recent installations remain under warranty, and prior energy savings have stabilized costs.

    4411.20 – Mosquito Control
    The Mosquito Control budget will remain level. The Town's municipal pest management company monitors mosquito
    populations, conducts testing, and reports results to the State. The Town also provides community updates. Mosquito
    activity was relatively low this year.

    4152.10 – Property Revaluation
    The Town entered into a new contract with Avatar following an RFP process and received no other responses from
    Certified Assessors. The increased contract cost reflects additional State reporting, property evaluations, documentation,
    and compliance with new laws and restrictions.

    The Committee discussed funding requirements for 2027, 2028, 2029, and 2030. Ms. Kleiner also discussed Sansoucy
    Associates' specialized utility assessment services. The Town expects approximately $12,000 in additional tax revenue
    this year compared with $83,799 last year, while the service costs approximately $30,000 annually. Members questioned
    whether the current-year cost provides sufficient value and discussed whether another Certified Utility Assessor could
    provide the service at a lower cost. The Town remains under an annual contract.

    4153.10 – Legal
    The Legal budget remains flat. The Department initially proposed a small increase, but determined it was unnecessary
    after discussions with the Selectmen.

    Mr. Cutter confirmed that the Town and School District calendars had been added to the master calendar.

    4445.20 – Vendor Payments
    The Vendor Payments budget includes funding authorized through a warrant article and is currently approximately
    $42,000. General assistance expenditures totaled approximately $16,528 to date.

    Ms. Kleiner discussed legislative changes enacted in 2025 and 2026 requiring municipalities to review General Assistance
    Guidelines and Applications. Ms. White is reviewing the existing process and will work on the Welfare General Assistance
    Program and revised guidelines. The Committee discussed reimbursement requirements when an applicant resides in
    another municipality, residency requirements, domestic violence exceptions, and requirements for guidelines based on
    average local expenditures and costs.

    4583.10 – Patriotic
    The Patriotic budget includes a proposed $1,500 increase for flag maintenance. The Town maintains approximately 127
    flags. The proposed amount would establish a replacement cycle for flags that cannot be repaired.

    Ms. Douglas stated that the new maintenance line should instead be presented as a warrant article because volunteers
    have historically provided the service and it would constitute a new Town expense. Members discussed the number of
    flags requiring replacement, anticipated annual costs, and available donations. Mr. Perry noted that the Veterans group had
    donated $3,500. The Committee agreed to revisit the matter at the next meeting after additional information was
    distributed electronically.

    4194.10 – General Government Buildings
    Ms. Kleiner reviewed the General Government Buildings budget, noting a slight overall decrease despite increases in
    several categories. The Town continues proactive maintenance of municipal buildings. The Building Inspector, Ken Daigle,
    has evaluated building conditions, while Mr. Kidwell reviewed HVAC systems. The Town contracted for required HVAC
    filter replacement and cleaning.

    iRoof, LLC completed shingle replacement and other winter-damage repairs. Water intrusion continues to be evaluated.
    Budget increases include water, gasoline, and electricity.

    The Selectmen signed an agreement with Priority Power for Community Power. A public hearing will be scheduled as

    Upcoming Budget Review
    The October 1 meeting will include the Library; Road Agent and Road Maintenance; Planning Board; Zoning Board of
    Adjustment; Cemeteries; Police Department; Parks and Recreation; and IT. IT may be moved to the October 8 meeting.

    Health Insurance
    Ms. Kleiner discussed HealthTrust and efforts to strengthen reserves and reduce future cost fluctuations. The average
    municipal increase is expected to be 4.8%, with municipalities ranging from -11% to 12.9%. The industry average is
    approximately 9%. The Committee discussed a potential increase between 7% and 9%. A 10% increase was estimated to
    add approximately $40,000, while the expected increase would remain below the previously discussed tax impact. Mr. Son
    noted that the projected amount would remain approximately $15,000 below the tax impact. Final rates are expected by
    October 8, with the Committee's voting process beginning October 15. The Committee also expects a small Primex
    increase.

    Mr. Son discussed a potential 20% increase for an auditing company, estimated at approximately $7,000, and the
    possibility of using a smaller or larger firm.
