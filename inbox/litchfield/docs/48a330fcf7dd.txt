    GMS Renovation
    Superintendent Brian O’Connell reviewed the proposed renovation project at Griffin Memorial School (GMS). The
    project would address current facility challenges by renovating the main hallway area, including HVAC and other
    building systems, demolishing the 1960s wing, and removing the existing portables. The proposal would replace the
    portable classrooms with a new wing containing approximately 18 to 20 learning spaces, compared with
    approximately 19 existing learning spaces in the areas being removed.

    The project differs from the new-building proposal that has been under consideration for approximately eight years.
    The current proposal involves renovation and replacement on the existing site. The current project cost is
    approximately $38.9 million, although the District is working to reduce it. The estimate includes design contingency
    and currently assumes approximately 25% on soft costs, which the District also hopes to reduce. A figure of $35
    million was also referenced as the target project amount.

    Mr. O’Connell discussed the possibility of receiving state Building Aid. The District currently has an application with
    the state for Building Aid associated with the previously proposed new-building project. It is currently second on the
    state's priority list for that project. Because the District cannot switch projects and retain its position on the list,
    proceeding with the renovation project would eventually cause the District to lose its current position. The District
    understands that the state may include funding for Building Aid in the 2029 fiscal budget and intends to submit a new
    application for the renovation project. The District will also work to ensure that, if the renovation project is approved
    in March and building-aid funding becomes available in the future, the District can still access that funding.

    The District expects the application for the new project to be due in April 2027. Mr. O’Connell said he will make
    additional project information available to the public.

    CHS Exterior Work
    Mr. O’Connell reviewed proposed exterior work at CHS, including brickwork and other repairs. The building is in the
    25- to 30-year range where repairs are needed to maintain the facility. The project was included in the previous year's
    CIP.

    The proposed project cost is approximately $145,000, based on estimates from On-Site Insight and preliminary
    quotes received approximately a year and a half ago. The District is targeting impact fees to fund the work and has
    already contacted the appropriate parties regarding approval to use those funds.

    CHS Roof, HVAC, and Fire Alarm Improvements
    Mr. O’Connell reviewed a larger CHS capital project targeted for approximately 2031. The primary component is
    replacement of the CHS roof, which is expected to reach the end of its useful life at that time. The project estimate
    exceeds $2 million and also includes HVAC units and an upgrade to the fire alarm system. The project includes the
    LMS fire alarm system.

    The District has been setting aside funds in its capital reserve for the project and intends to complete the work
    together in 2031. However, a more immediate need has developed involving the CHS HVAC system. The District
    intends to address the HVAC issue now and use funds from the capital reserve established for the larger project. The
    reserve has sufficient funding because the District has been contributing to it in anticipation of the project.

    The District did not know the HVAC issue was this significant when it prepared the CIP materials in August. The

    Capital Improvement Plan Estimates
    Mr. O’Connell discussed the estimated costs included in the District's 25-year Capital Improvement Plan. The plan was
    developed using On-Site Insight reports and other facility assessments. He noted that actual project costs have varied
    considerably from the original estimates. Some estimates have been close to actual costs, while others have been
    substantially higher or lower.

    For example, the Music Room project ultimately cost about $8,000 less than the estimate. Mr. O’Connell noted that
    actual costs will need to be evaluated as each project moves forward. The HVAC work currently being pursued is
    estimated at approximately $190,000, which will reduce the larger project cost when that work is completed
    separately.

    District-Wide Capital Improvements
    Mr. O’Connell reviewed general district-wide improvements included in the budget and Capital Improvement Plan. The
    District has approximately $125,000 per year allocated for these types of capital improvements. Projects may include
    painting, field improvements, stair work, and other smaller facility projects.

    The $125,000 annual amount currently applies to CHS and LMS. GMS has not been included in that figure because
    the proposed GMS renovation project could make those improvements unnecessary.

    The District has an On-Site Insight facility report for GMS indicating that significant expenditures would be required if
    the proposed renovation project does not proceed. If the renovation project does not move forward, the District will
    revisit the facility needs and develop an alternative plan.

    Solar Project
    Mr. O’Connell discussed the District’s inherited solar project. Under the agreement, the solar provider must provide
    battery storage for a portion of the generated power, but the District has not yet received the equipment. The six-year
    period for making a buyout decision does not begin until the required equipment is received.

    The Board discussed establishing funds to preserve the District’s option to purchase the solar panels when the buyout
    period arrives. The District previously introduced a warrant article for this purpose but did not include it in the most
    recent March warrant. The estimated buyout value was previously approximately $600,000, based on the assumption
    that the buyout would occur when the panels were six years old.

    Because the panels will continue to age, their future value and potential buyout cost may be lower. The Committee
    suggested requesting a third-party reassessment of the panels and their value, as permitted under the contract.
    Members also noted that older panels may require additional maintenance, making development of a maintenance
    plan important.

    Mr. O’Connell discussed other options available under the agreement, including continuing to lease the panels for
    their useful life and replacing them at the end of that period. He also discussed the potential benefits of participating

    Gymnasium Improvements
    Mr. O’Connell discussed grouping gymnasium-related repairs at LMS and CHS within the CIP. Several repairs at LMS
    were completed this summer and are therefore excluded from the current pricing. The proposed CIP work includes
    LMS bleacher repairs, additional lighting and flooring work, and improvements at the CHS gym.

    The CHS gym is a new CIP proposal, projected through 2032. Mr. O’Connell noted that some CHS projects could
    potentially be completed earlier if funding opportunities were available.

    Mr. O’Connell discussed CHS gym lighting, which is currently targeted for 2032. The existing lighting is expected to
    have a useful life of about 30 years, and the District anticipates extending its use for several additional years. The
    proposed work involves specialized structural and facility lighting rather than basic wiring or occupancy sensors,
    which could significantly drive up costs.

    Mr. O’Connell also discussed the CHS gym floor. While the District would prefer to address the floor sooner if funding
    becomes available, 2032 remains the current target for purposes of fiscal planning. Members noted that the gym has
    areas requiring attention, similar to the LMS gym.

    The LMS gym project was prioritized because of termite-related issues. The work was completed over the summer,
    and Mr. O’Connell indicated the project has been successful so far and was completed at a relatively reasonable cost.
    The District may explore working with the same company on additional gym-related issues.

    CHS Auditorium
    Mr. O’Connell discussed adding the CHS auditorium to the CIP for similar reasons as the gymnasium projects. The
    auditorium was not previously included and is proposed for 2032, although the District may consider opportunities to
    address individual needs sooner.

    Potential auditorium improvements include upgraded sound absorption, stage repairs, and carpeting. The stage is
    approximately 25 to 30 years old and will eventually require repairs. The District previously obtained pricing for
    carpeting but prioritized improvements to the music room because of its more immediate needs.

    The District completed professional cleaning of the auditorium seats, which significantly improved their appearance
    and condition. Mr. O’Connell noted the importance of maintaining the auditorium because it remains a frequently used
    school facility.

    The CIP includes a $50,000 placeholder for auditorium improvements. This amount estimates the cost of the
    identified improvements over the next several years and serves as a preliminary placeholder.

    The proposed auditorium work includes sound absorption improvements, additional lighting upgrades, carpeting, and
    future stage work. The District previously improved the projection and sound systems. The District also addressed a
    curtain-related request for less than $1,000.

    The Committee thanked Mr. O’Connell for presenting the District's CIP projects.

   E.​ Adoption of Bylaws
       Updated CIP Bylaws - redlined

       Litchfield CIP Bylaws - Approved April 7, 2025
       August 6, 2026 - Draft of Updated CIP Bylaws

       Draft Definitions of CIP Priority & Status

       The Committee reviewed proposed revisions to its bylaws, comparing the existing language with the applicable statute
       and prior analysis. The revisions primarily clarified language, removed references to reports not required under the
       Committee's bylaws, and aligned the document with statutory requirements.

       Under the Committee’s core purpose, language was added clarifying that the Committee prepares an annually
       recommended program of expenditures based on submissions from departments and commissions.

       Additional language clarified the key components of the Capital Improvement Program (CIP), including how projects
       are classified based on urgency and other factors. A reference concerning impact fees was also added.

       The Committee discussed membership and voting rights, particularly whether the Budget Committee representative
       should remain a non-voting member. Members noted that the Budget Committee determines whether its
       representative may vote. At the same time, Mr. Bourque maintained that the representative should be permitted to
       vote on CIP matters because the representative participates in the Committee’s work. The Committee also identified
       the need to add a School Board representative to the membership provisions.

Motion:​​      (P. Plansky / M. Parker) Motion to authorize the Budget Committee member to vote on the CIP.
Discussion:​   None
Vote: ​ ​      (4-0-0) The motion carried

       The bylaws will be revised to remove the Budget Committee representative from the non-voting membership category
       and to reflect the voting status approved by the committee. The change will also be incorporated into Section 2.3. The
       Committee noted that the revised membership structure would result in six voting members.

       The Committee reviewed appointment provisions for members and alternates. The Select Board will appoint the
       representative, while boards, commissions, or committees will appoint their representatives. The Select Board will
       appoint citizen representatives-at-large under the applicable provisions. The Committee will revise language
       concerning alternate and non-voting members to reflect its decisions.

       The Committee also reviewed definitions and clarifying language concerning capital projects and building projects.
       The Committee revised Section 3.3 to include cost estimates based on comparable acquisitions or purchases. The
       Committee discussed whether library projects could be excluded from the CIP if they do not meet the $50,000 project
       threshold. Members clarified that projects below the threshold would not necessarily be removed from consideration
       but would need to proceed through the appropriate budget or warrant process. Projects meeting the threshold would
       remain eligible for inclusion in the CIP.

       The Committee reviewed a new section addressing project priority classification. Members clarified that the first four
       categories represent priorities, while the remaining three represent project status. The Committee agreed to change
       the heading to “Priority Classification/Status" to describe the categories more accurately.

       The Committee reviewed additional revisions concerning meetings, public transparency, required records, special
       meetings, and statutory references. Members determined that most of these changes were clarifying or editorial and
       did not substantively change the bylaws. The Committee discussed the process for the required public hearing and
       noted that the hearing has historically been conducted following a presentation and planning process involving
       the Board of Selectmen.
