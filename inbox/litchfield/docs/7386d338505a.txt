    E.​ Road Maintenance (Kemo Circle and McElwain) – Road Agent Kevin Brown
    2026 Paving Quotes
    Purchase Order: Paving of Kemo Circle
    Purchase Order: Paving of McElwain Drive

    Mr. Brown presented an update on the Kemo Circle and McElwain Drive drainage project. The original estimate was
    $46,000. After a pre-construction inspection with Continental, they identified additional drainage deficiencies,
    including deteriorating 24-inch galvanized pipe, barrel-block catch basins, and smaller 15-inch pipe at two crossovers.

    The project will use reinforced concrete pipe rather than plastic because of the installation depth. The cost difference
    between High‑Density Polyethylene pipe (HDPE) and Reinforced Concrete pipe (RCE) is approximately $9,000. The
    catch basins will also be replaced with precast concrete sumps. These changes increase the project cost by $38,210,
    bringing the revised project price to $86,610.

    Mr. Brown explained that the work is necessary to address existing sinkholes and complete the drainage project
    properly. He requested authorization to proceed with the revised project. The Board discussed funding the additional

    M.​ WEX Fleet Cards / Town of Litchfield
    ExxonMobil Info Sheet
    Exxon Mobil Info Pack-Business Pro

    Ms. Kleiner provided the Board with an update regarding the Town's approved credit application for WEX fuel cards.
    She is gathering vehicle and driver information for submission. The Board was also informed that the School District
    has its own EIN and therefore must submit a separate credit application.

    The Board discussed whether fuel card use should be limited to New England or expanded nationwide. The members
    agreed to limit use to New England. Department Heads traveling outside the region would use a credit card and seek
    reimbursement rather than use the fuel cards.

    Ms. Kleiner explained that each fuel card would be assigned to a specific vehicle and remain in that vehicle. Each
    authorized driver would have an individual PIN, allowing the Town to identify the driver and vehicle associated with
    each transaction. Drivers would also provide the vehicle's odometer reading.

    The cards will primarily serve as backup fuel sources when the Town's 500-gallon fuel supply is depleted, particularly
    during consecutive winter storms. The cards are not intended for daily use. Ms. Kleiner will provide the Board with the
    completed vehicle and driver information once the process is finalized.
