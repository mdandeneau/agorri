6.​ Town Budget Review:
    The Committee proceeded to vote on the Town budget.

    Town Budget Review and Voting Process
    Mr. Son introduced the Town budget voting process and explained that the Committee would begin with 4130.10, Board of
    Selectmen, and proceed through the remaining departmental budgets in sequence. Members would have an opportunity to
    make motions on individual budgets before the Committee moved to the next account.

    Mr. Son reminded members and the public that the October 15 meeting was not the only opportunity to vote on the Town
    budget. The Committee could continue its review and voting throughout the fall and winter as additional financial
    information became available.

    Outstanding items included the Primex insurance figure and the first-year costs of the Police Union Contract, both of which
    could affect the tax cap calculation.

    Outstanding Contracts and Service Procurement
    Town Administrator Kim Kleiner provided the Committee with updates on several outstanding matters that could affect the
    proposed budget. She reported that the Board of Selectmen had approved issuing a request for proposals (RFP) for auditing
    services. The Town’s existing auditing firm had been acquired again, resulting in a significant increase in costs. The RFP
    deadline was November 20, 2026, to provide the Selectmen with proposals for review and obtain a cost figure for the Budget
    Committee by early December.

    Ms. Kleiner also discussed cleaning services for Town Hall and Talent Hall. Town Hall cleaning costs fall under the
    Government Buildings budget, while Talent Hall cleaning costs fall under the Recreation budget. Because additional cleaning
    services had been requested, the Town would seek proposals to determine the associated costs. The RFP deadline was also
    set for November 20, 2026, to allow sufficient time for a decision and budget update.

    Mr. Perry informed the Committee that negotiations on the Police Chief and Police Captain contracts remained outstanding.
    Ms. Douglas asked whether the first-year cost of the police union contract could be accommodated within the tax cap. Ms.
    Kleiner explained that negotiations had concluded and that the contract was being prepared for signatures by the union and
    the Board of Selectmen. A warrant article would follow.

    Ms. Douglas discussed the potential application of the three-fifths supermajority vote requirement if a budget item exceeded
    the tax cap. She referenced previous instances in which such a vote had been required, including the fire truck purchase and
    other items from the prior year. Ms. Kleiner indicated that she did not believe union contracts were treated differently, but
    Mr. Son acknowledged that the final determination would depend on the completed contract and the resulting tax cap
    calculation.

    8. Revenue Adjustments and Tax Cap Update
    Ms. Kleiner presented updates to projected revenues and expenditures following the Town's review of its 2026 revenues
    with the Department of Revenue. The Committee was advised that these figures would inform the calculation of the 2026 tax
    rate and the available tax cap capacity for the proposed 2027 budget.

    Before the latest adjustments, the Town stood $32,074 below the tax cap. Updated revenue figures included an increase of
    slightly more than $102,000 in Meals and Rooms revenue and an increase of $13,000 in Highway Block grant revenue.


    Ms. Kleiner attributed the higher Meals and Rooms revenue to increased local business activity and additional town
    establishments serving prepared food.

    Although these increases improved projected 2026 revenues and reduced the 2026 tax levy, they also affected the available
    tax cap capacity for 2027. Following the initial adjustments, the Town’s position was approximately $24,000 below the tax
    cap, prompting further review of revenue estimates and departmental expenses.

    Additional Revenue Projections
    Ms. Kleiner explained that the Board of Selectmen reviewed three revenue categories for potential increases: motor vehicle
    registrations, interest income, and building permits. These adjustments reflected actual collections and anticipated
    development activity within the community.

    For motor vehicle registrations, she recommended increasing the budgeted amount from $150,000 to $175,000, a net
    increase of $25,000. The recommendation considered activity during the first nine months of 2026 and the final quarter of
    2025. The Selectmen used a conservative estimate to avoid overstating anticipated revenue.

    For interest income, officials recommended a $50,000 increase. Collections during the first nine months of 2026 had
    already exceeded $77,000, supporting the proposed adjustment.

    For building permits, officials recommended increasing the estimate from $20,000 to $30,000, a net increase of $10,000.
    Collections during the first nine months of 2026 had exceeded $48,000. Ms. Kleiner stated continued development activity,
    including projects at Colby Farm, Tallarico, and Rose Meadows, as support for maintaining a conservative but realistic
    projection.

    Ms. Kleiner clarified that the combined net revenue adjustments totaled $35,000, rather than the sum of the three gross
    recommendations, because some revenue had already been included in the proposed budget.

    Highway Department Position Adjustments
    Ms. Kleiner informed the Committee of two classification adjustments in the Highway Department budget. One employee
    held a commercial driver’s license (CDL) and performed duties consistent with a heavy equipment operator but had been
    classified as a light equipment operator. A second employee, previously classified as a laborer, required reclassification as a
    light equipment operator.

    Both changes resulted in salary increases, which had been incorporated into the Highway Department budget. Ms. Kleiner
    had provided the amounts of the adjustments in an email to Committee members. The additional personnel costs offset
    approximately $11,000 of the $35,000 in increases, affecting the Town’s revised position relative to the tax cap.

    11. Committee Discussion: Motor Vehicle Registration Revenue
    Mr. Shoemaker asked whether the Town could readily determine how much of the increase in motor vehicle registration
    revenue resulted from new registrations by residents moving into Litchfield, as opposed to other registration activity.

    Ms. Kleiner explained that the Town could not distinguish between registrations associated with new residents and those
    resulting from existing residents purchasing additional vehicles. However, the Assessing Department receives property
    deeds when properties are bought and sold and maintains a quarterly list of individuals who have moved to Litchfield. The
    Town Clerk uses this information to send welcome letters containing information about the Town and reminders regarding
    motor vehicle registration requirements.

    Ms. Kleiner noted that some new residents delay registering their vehicles until their existing registrations expire, although
    State law requires registration within a specified period. The Committee discussed whether new residential developments
    could provide a basis for estimating the impact of population growth on motor vehicle registration revenue. Members

    suggested reviewing occupancy data for Rose Meadows and the Corning and Tallarico developments and estimating the
    number of vehicles associated with new households. This information could help the Committee assess the impact of
    development on registration revenue and inform future Planning Board discussions regarding parking and garage
    requirements.

    Members also discussed the effect of vehicle values and replacement cycles on registration revenue. Newer, higher-value
    vehicles generally generate more revenue than older vehicles, but residents who purchase new vehicles may retain them for
    many years. Conversely, leased vehicles may be replaced every three to four years, potentially affecting annual revenue. Mr.
    Perry reported that the Town had nearly collected the amount forecast for the following year's motor vehicle registration
    revenue, with the final quarter expected to provide a cushion. Members emphasized the importance of developing realistic
    revenue projections to avoid overestimating revenue and creating budgetary pressures. The Committee agreed that further
    analysis would be useful but acknowledged that the issue could not be resolved during the meeting.

    Budget Review and Voting Procedures
    Mr. Son transitioned the Committee to the voting portion of the meeting and explained that members would review the
    proposed budget line by line and consider motions for reductions. Mr. Son noted that members should identify the specific
    line item and rationale when proposing a reduction, rather than making a general reduction to the overall budget. This
    approach would help the Board of Selectmen understand the Committee's recommendations and allocate reductions
    appropriately.

    Mr. Son clarified that the Committee would review the proposed budget during this session and address the default budget
    separately.

    Mr. Brunelle joined the meeting to answer questions about the Information Technology budget.

    Software Licenses – PDF and Photo Tools
    The Committee reviewed the software budget associated with Line 342, PDF and Photo Tools. Mr. Brunelle explained that the
    original $525 allocation covered Photoshop licenses for the Police Department and other users. An additional cost of
    approximately $2,600 reflected the transition to an enterprise-grade licensing arrangement.

    He explained that the enterprise license allows 10 licenses to be shared among users throughout the year. Although the

    Public Safety Communications Maintenance
    The Committee reviewed the public safety communications maintenance budget, which supports communications systems
    used by the Fire Department, Police Department, Highway Department, and schools across five sites.

    Mr. Brunelle explained that the original budget was $10,000, with $7,600 representing the fixed annual preventive
    maintenance contract. The remaining amount covers repairs, parts, labor, and other expenses incurred on a
    time-and-materials basis. During the current year, the Department had incurred approximately $10,000 in additional repairs
    beyond the fixed contract cost.

    Mr. Brunelle stated that he had initially requested $18,000 but subsequently determined that $15,000 would be a more
    appropriate budget allocation. The account also covers repairs to vehicle radios and other communications equipment in the
    municipal fleet.

    Members discussed the costs associated with specialized repairs, including antenna and aerial issues that can require tower
    climbers at an estimated cost of $2,000 per service call. Mr. Brunelle agreed to provide a more detailed breakdown of the
    fixed contract and variable repair expenses in future budget submissions. He also noted that annual contract increases
    generally range from 5% to 10% and that he had received an estimate for the 2027 contract.
