-Site Plan Review:

-Tax Map 11092, Lot 001; 63 English Range Road; 13 Lot Subdivision; Owner: RGNH Construction-Kevin Landry,
20 Rockingham Rd, Windham NH. Surveyor: James Verra & Associates, Inc, (JVA) 100 Griffin Road Unit C,
Portsmouth, NH. Presenters: Nicholas DiBartholomeo, Morgan Moody, Kevin Landry, and Ryan Fowler.
Nicholas DiBartholomeo and Morgan Moody state the land is a 43-lot parcel. Of the 43 lots, they will only be
disturbing 1300 square feet of wetlands. The current plan includes a 13-lot subdivision with 3,300 linear feet of
road whereit will have a vertical, granite curbing culvert system that goes to 4 different ponds. Mr.
DiBartholomeo highlighted on the drawing wherethe road will come in from Scenic Drive and showed whereit
will disturb wetlands. The first incompact from the road is at 1,265 feet and another point of impact at 40
squarefeet. In the northeastern area ofthe site is where the higher value wetland is located within the 100-
year flood area. Much of the proposed subdivision does not reach toward this wetland area. The wetland area
and vernal pools wereflagged in March and April 2026 and the wetland functions and values assessmentfor
New Hampshire Department of Environmental Services was completed. The plan is currently in the
environmental review process in regard to the vernal pools and the possible habitat fragmentation within the
proposed subdivision. The majority of the wetlands are forested wetlands with a few emergent wetlands along
the powerline corridor. The smaller wetland area is disturbed by an accompanying trail alongside it that exists.
Chairman Buzzanga inquired about a pipe noted on the plans. It was noted to be 134 feet long and 12 inches
wide. The culvert is proposed. Chairman Buzzanga confirmed that the wetland the road is going through was a
continuous wetland and the pipe will keep it continuous. It will be a pipe with outlet protection, and a rip wrap
apron to slow downanyvelocities. The pipe will be located 6 feet under the road. Commissioner Chabot asked
to see the set of plans that includes the grading lines as grading is important to wetland’s impact.
Commissioner Metts asked when they return, he would like to see the plans’ septics as well as the setbacks.
Commissioner Chabotalso stated that the updated plans should be stamped bya certified Wetland Scientist
along with a description of the wetlands and labeled on the plan. Chairman Buzzanga stated that they would
schedule a future site walk and a set of plans would be made available prior to the walk. Chairman Buzzanga
also asked them to review the size of the pipe and speak with Town of Derry Deputy Public Works Director
Mark L’Heureux concerning its current planned size.

Site Plan Review:

-Tax Map 14, Lot 15: 22.5 Overledge Drive; Phase V Subdivision; Owner: JAL, LLC 5 Madden Road,Derry, NH;
Presenters: Michael P. Butler, PE and Brent J. Cole, PE.

-Brent Cole of Granite Engineering introduced himself as the engineer of the project and was accompanied by
Tom Lannan of JAL, LLC. They are presenting Denali Estates Phase V. Prior phases brought before this Board
had smaller wetland impacts. The overview plan is coming in off of Daniel and Forest Roads which bring Phases
| and IV together. The future developmentof Phase V takes Forest Road and extends it into the vacant land
that is there. They are proposing 20 lots on a town-maintained road which is essentially Forest Street
Extension. Phase VI is not being proposed currently. Their objective now is to provide a preliminary overview
and answer high level questions regarding Phase V. They would like to schedule a site walk as they are
scheduled to go before the Technical Review Committee and return to the Commission with a fully developed
plan set. There are two wetland crossings. They have met with townofficials, including the Zoning Board, on
this project and have been vetted by the Town of Derry. The town has been involved in the design process
itself. Mr. Cole has had extensive conversations with the Department of Environmental Services regarding the
two wetland crossings. They have also spoken with N.H. Fish and Game about endangered species and how
they can help to mitigate with their plans. The wetlands are not stream crossings but forested wetlands that
drain from north to south downto the larger wetland complex. They are carrying the 25 foot no cut buffer that
has been applied to the entire project or at least the former Phases.

The current plans show a 4,600 square foot wetland impact that will include a cross culvert. Currently it is a 48-
inch concrete pipe. In the past, the State of New Hampshire has requested to have a 48-inch RCP pipe ona
non-stream wetland crossing. Mr. Cole explained “stream simulation.” The plan includes head walls. Mr.
Lannan recalled using organics in Phase |. The second wetland impact is about a 3,000 square foot impact. The
48-inch width pipes are about 150 feet long. A native wildlife mix will be used for the steep slopes near the
wetlands. Conversation ensued regarding how surface sand filters will be used and how they work. Regulations
in Derry as well as alteration terrain require all the impervious areas to not only be treated but mitigated and a
portion of it to be recharged back into the aquifer. A second sand filter is on the plans as well. Commission
Spoerl asked who will maintain the filters over time and Mr. Cole replied the town maintains them. The goal in
designing these systems is that they don’t fail, they are designed for the 100-year storm. Theyare built with
broadcasted weirs so if the outlet structure is overwhelmed thereis path for it to go that doesn’t necessarily
drain into anyone’s backyards and drains back to the wetlands. Maintenance is minimal and they are checked
annually. They are to be mowed twice a year. A future site walk is planned on this 80-acre parcel. The wetlands
have been recently flagged.


-Site Plan Review:

-Tax Map 7 Lot 77; 38 Jackman Road/Jackman Road Right-of-Way; Owner: Joe Mastriano, 3 Ball Avenue, Salem,
N.H. 03079; Engineer: Engineer Alliance, Inc., 194 Central Street, Saugus, MA 01906,Presenters: Eric
Bradanese,P.E.

Owner Joe Mastriano and Eric Bradanese, PE (who attended virtually.) Mr. Bradanese reminded the
Commission he had first presented the plan in August 2026 with preliminary plans for this project. There have
been some minor revisions since the plan was first presented. They were tasked with getting more wetlands
information that needs to be put on the plans such as the date of the wetland flagging, and the types of
wetlands which they have nowreceived from Bruce Gilday, Wetland Scientist. That information was added to
the plan sheets. They also added the 75-foot no cut buffer to the prime wetland to show what impact they
mayhavein that area. One item from the Town Engineering Department was to adjust some of the roadway
grading for the proposed culvert that will run under Jackman Road right-of-way. The scope ofthis project is to
build a single-family dwelling and a garage with an Accessory Dwelling Unit (ADU) aboveit. Jackman Road is a
Class 6 Road that is not maintained by the Townof Derry. There is a portion of the road maintained by an
abutter to this site. The last 625 feet that leads to the frontage of the property would be maintained by Mr.
Mastriano. This will be constructed as a 20-foot-wide gravel road. This was coordinated with Town engineering
and the Fire Departmentto assure that they have maintained enough access both horizontally and vertically.
This ensures the roadway grades and stormwater workefficiently. This road will provide a significant storage
volume for stormwater impacts. Chairman Buzzanga confirmed the 60 feet he saw on the plans is between the
added boulders and the edge of the wetland. Mr. Bradanese stated there is no direct impact on the wetland.
-Commissioner Chabot expressed some concerns she noted on September 14, 2026. Work had already begun
as she saw a large machine working and noted trees had already been cut. She was pleased to see silt fence
had been erected but was notsureit was putin the right place in terms of the 75-foot setback to the prime
wetland. She also stated she was notpleased to know the plan showed only 60-feet distance from the
wetland. She reminded the Commission that the standard is 75-feet setback from a prime wetland. Mr.
Mastriano agreed that some site prep work had been done with some tree clearing into the proposed house
area. He stated he avoided the wetland area. Mr. Bradanese shared that he had discussions with a Compliance
Specialist named Sephera M. Michailides at the N.H. Department of Environmental Services. He said he sent
the plans to her and the Department had no specific concerns. Mr. Bradanese explained the property frontage
for the site is basically entirely within the 150-foot buffer to the prime wetland. This area of concern was a
request to have the hammer head turn around for fire access. The total impact from the edge of roadwayall
the way through the site wherethe building is going to be is 77,000 square feet. Discussion ensued regarding
seeking relief from the setback from the Department of Environmental Services. Beth Carver, Land Manager,
added she spoke with the Derry Code Enforcement Officer who confirmed work had ceased at the location. Mr.
Bradaneseoffered his apologies, stating it was a misunderstanding regarding the preparation work that was
done on the site. Chairman Buzzanga asked that the corner of interest they had spoken about would be
flagged prior to a scheduled site walk and reminded everyone the prime wetland area must remain in its
existing conditions. Chairman Buzzangashared that the Commission will schedule a site walk soon.
