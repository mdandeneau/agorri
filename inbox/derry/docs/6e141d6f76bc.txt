General Information

• Tonight’s presentation is designed to be informative as a prompt
  for future discussion and policy decisions.
• The Town has occasionally reviewed the levels of exemptions
  and credits granted.
• The Town operates on a fiscal year budget – ending June 30.
• The State of New Hampshire Department of Revenue
  Administration has oversight on municipal taxing authority.
General Information

• Annually, the Town’s budget is calculated by the maximum
  allowable increase to the tax rate per Town Charter.
• By design, Veterans’ Credits and exemptions are a redistribution
  of the tax levy.
• CAUTION – 2026 is revaluation year and the FY 27 CPI – U tax
  rate increase was 3.3%.
• CPI – U Data will be finalized in January 2027, but the maximum
  allowable increase is trending towards 3.5% for FY 28.

  past two fiscal years.

  than the reported CPI.

  contracts expire June 30, 2029.
• If HB 1331 consolidates the Town and School budgets under a
  tax cap – impacts from School would need to be considered.

Veterans’ Tax Credit

• Program eligibility aligns with RSA 72:28 with service during
  times of a qualifying war or armed conflict.
• Derry’s Credit is $550 – State law allows up to $750
• Derry does not offer the All-Veterans Credit as other
  communities do.
• This program is a credit from the tax bill as opposed to a
  reduction in assessment.

Veterans’ Tax Credit Program

• Recent legislation prohibits Veterans from stacking multiple credits.
• Formerly, the Service-Connected Total Disabled Tax Credit was $2,400
  along with the standard $550 Veterans’ credit.
• Derry adopted a single $2,950 Service Connected Total Disabled Veteran
  Tax Credit, because Veterans could no longer claim both.
• The number of recipients at $2,950 increased to 105 in 2025.
• Standard Credit at $550 decreased to 583 recipients.
• The total number of recipients declined to 688, but credit amount
  increased to $625k.

• Lower Median Income and Assessed Value Per Capita should
  be evaluated in tandem as Town Council considers options.
• Most communities for both the neighbor group and to lesser
  degree in the peer group provide the maximum credit of $750.
• Using the 2025 figure of 688 Veterans, an increase of $50 would
  result in approximately $35k shifted to other taxpayers.
• Town Council should be aware of the trend of an increasing
  number of Veterans qualifying for disabled credits.
Blind Exemption

• The Town of Derry has supported a blind exemption under RSA
  72:37.
• This exemption has been the same at $40,000 since 2006.
• There are 12 qualifying recipients currently for a total of
  $480,000 in exempted value.
• Town Council could consider an increase to mirror inflation.

• However, changes to the exemption programs will depend on
  decisions about income/asset levels along with exemption amounts.
• Most programs have April 1, 2027 application dates, so decisions to
  increase would need to be finalized in early 2027.
• Town Council will have several other information points in October
  including Health Insurance, 2026 tax rate and the CPI will be

Introduction – The Town Council established as an FY27 goal the review of property tax
exemption and credits available to certain groups, predominantly the elderly exemptions and
veterans’ credits.


       This report takes us through the following:


   1) Programs now offered by the Town.
   2) Understanding program requirements which are set by State Statute and cannot be
       changed by the Town Council;
   3) Minimum program requirements as established by the State but can be adjusted by the
       Council;
   4) How Derry compares in its efforts to bring relief to elderly property owners and veterans
       when measured against surrounding communities and the State’s top ten communities in
       terms of population.
   5) Fiscal impact of any changes and the ability to fund those changes; it is important to
       recognize that any additional exemptions or credits granted may provide fiscal relief to
       program participants at the expense of the remainder of taxpayers who shoulder the
       transfer of that tax burden.

                               Elderly Exemption Program
   The 2024 DRA total value of elderly exemptions granted in this program is $24 million or
0.43% of the taxable assessment base. Although peer data is not available Statewide for 2025,
Derry’s 2025 figure is $22.1M on 183 accounts which is approximately 0.38% of the Town’s
taxable assessment base.


State-Mandated Program Requirements Elderly Exemptions –
   1) Applications due by April 15;
   2) Eligibility decision by July 1;
   3) Taxpayer appeal of denial to Board of Tax and Land Appeals or Superior Court by
       September 1;
   4) Applicant must have resided in State for at least 3 consecutive years prior to April 1;
   5) Owned by a resident, or the resident's spouse, either of whom meets the age requirement
       for the exemption claimed, and when they have been married to each other for at least 5
       consecutive years;
   6) Defines applicable and excluded income sources;
   7) Defines applicable and excluded asset levels (in addition to others, excludes value of
       primary residence);
   8) Should municipalities grant different exemption amounts based upon age, age categories
       shall be 65 years up to 75 years; 75 years up to 80 years, and 80 years or older;


State-Mandated Minimum Program Requirements Which Can be Adjusted Locally –
   1) Net maximum income of $13,400/single and $20,400 married; Derry allows
       $45,000/single and $55,000 married.
   2) Net asset level of $35,000; Derry has increased that level to $150,000.
   3) Minimum property tax exemption of $5,000 across all age brackets; Derry has opted to
       provide different levels at the three age brackets of $85,000; $125,000; and $165,000,
       respectively.

Baseline Comparison Data
       A community’s fiscal ability to assist its elderly homeowners can be measured in several
ways. The two baseline data points which are used in this report are the community’s Assessed
Value/Capital and Per Capita Income. The first data point is used to determine the relative
burden to the overall community when assuming additional tax obligations (as stated earlier,
exemption and credit programs do not reduce the community’s overall tax burden but reallocate
the amount being exempted or credited to other property taxpayers). The second data point is
useful when measuring an individual taxpayer’s ability to absorb the additional tax burden and
measure the impact of participating in the exemption programs once state-established
requirements are met.
       Like past reviews, Derry’s effort is compared locally to those eight communities which
share a common border with Derry, and to the 10 largest communities in the State. Many of the
larger communities share similar characteristics of Derry’s, such as a densely settled Downtown
area; extensive municipal services required to service its diverse development patterns; and the
daily influx of area citizens seeking services in Derry.

                                     VETERANS’ CREDITS

       The Town has participated in a program to recognize the service of its veterans who own
a home in Derry. For 2025, the programs authorized by the Town Council will result in a total
credit of $625,425 for eligible veterans.
State-Mandated Program Requirements Veterans Credit –
   1) Applications due by April 15;
   2) Eligibility decision by July 1;
   3) Taxpayer appeal of denial to Board of Tax and Land Appeals or Superior Court by
       September 1;
   4) Applicant must have resided in State for at least one year prior to April 1;
   5) Surviving spouses retain eligibility unless remarried.
   6) Husband and wife shall each receive the credit if so eligible.


State-Mandated Minimum Program Requirements Which Can be Adjusted Locally –
   1) Minimum credit of $50 may be increased up to $750 (Derry at $550).
   2) Town can limit eligibility to those who served during times of a qualifying war or armed
       conflict, defined as follows (RSA 72:28):
           a. "World War I" between April 6, 1917, and November 11, 1918, extended to April
               1, 1920 for service in Russia; provided that military or naval service on or after
               November 12, 1918 and before July 2, 1921, where there was prior service
               between April 6, 1917 and November 11, 1918 shall be considered as World War
               I service;
           b. "World War II" between December 7, 1941, and December 31, 1946;
           c. "Korean Conflict" between June 25, 1950, and January 31, 1955;
           d. "Vietnam Conflict" between December 22, 1961, and May 7, 1975;
           e. "Vietnam Conflict" between July 1, 1958, and December 22, 1961, if the resident
               earned the Vietnam service medal or the armed forces expeditionary medal;
           f. "Persian Gulf War" between August 2, 1990, and the date thereafter prescribed by
               Presidential proclamation or by law; and
           g. Any other war or armed conflict that has occurred since May 8, 1975, and in
               which the resident earned an armed forces expeditionary medal or theater of
               operations service medal.
                                                                                      12 | P a g e
3) Town can expand eligibility to all veterans who meet the standard definition of a veteran
   pursuant to RSA 21:50, served not less than ninety days and was honorably discharged.
   (All Veterans’ Tax Credit – RSA 72:28-b; Derry does not offer this credit).
       a. Towns are required to match the All-Veterans’ Tax Credit with the Veterans' Tax
           Credit no longer than over a three-year period.
4) Towns may grant a Tax Credit for Service-Connected Total Disability for veterans who
   are rated totally and permanently disabled from service connection by the United States
   Department of Veterans’ Affairs.
       a. This credit shall be a minimum of $700 and can be increased to $4,000 (Derry at

       b. It should be noted that recent legislation changed restricting Veterans from

           combined.

Conclusions –


   1. Derry has low Assessed Value per Capita and Per Capita Income, as compared to
       bordering communities and the 10 largest communities, which makes it more difficult to
       fund programs which shift the tax burden from one group of taxpayers to another.
   2. Derry’s Veteran population is aging, and they are experiencing declining health, which
       means that more Veterans may qualify for the higher disabled tax credit as their health
       declines.
   3. Derry’s $550 veterans’ credit is below most of the nearby communities and peer
       communities. Many have adopted the maximum $750 credit level.


Overall Summary


       Derry’s exemptions and credit amounts are reflective of the Town’s relatively small
property tax base. The amounts awarded are smaller compared to most municipalities, however,
the eligibility criteria, particularly for the elderly exemption program, is at median or greater.
Additionally, the amount of assets an applicant can retain is at or above median with respect to
the comparison groups.
       It does appear, however, that the Town is allocating less to exemption programs than in
the past. As cited previously, the total value of elderly exemptions granted in the elderly
exemption program is $24.0 million in Tax Year 2024, which is approximately 0.43% of the
Town’s taxable assessment base. This total amount of tax burden redistributed to other property
taxpayers is $1.09 million when Veterans’ Credits are included. This amount is approximately
1.05% of the overall property levy.
       It should be noted that the Town is in the process of completing a revaluation for the
2026 Tax Year, with an estimated average increase of 9% in valuation. For exemptions, the value
of taxes averted for each individual would diminish by the same amount as theoretically the tax
rate would drop. Veterans’ credits would remain unaffected. Concord recently announced large
valuation spikes which prompted them to adjust the maximum exemptions in category to adjust
for the new valuation. Merrimack is also in the process of increasing exemption ranges to
correlate with increases in assessments.

SUBJECT:            Adoption of Chapter 40 of the General Code - Electioneering at Polling Places


INTRODUCTION
 During the 2026 State Primary and in the days after, Election officials have received multiple
 complaints about electioneering that occurred in the parking area of the polling location.


BACKGROUND
 Many voters complained about the pamphlets being left in the booth, many complained that
 they were feeling accosted, some said they felt threated and intimidated by the person.
 There were complaints made to the Attorney General’s office, both by the Moderator and by
 voters. We have requested a report of the complaints they received. However, one complaint
 caused the Attorney General’s office to call the Moderator and request to speak to the officer
 in the building at that time, Captain Michaud.

DISCUSSION
 The Town Moderator has the power by RSA 659:43 to control Electioneering at the polling
 location, however that power does not extend to the parking lot itself. Upon review of
 various statutes, RSA 31:41-c gives the Town Council the power to set bylaws regulating the
 “distribution of campaign materials or electioneering or any activity which affects the safety,
 welfare and rights of voters at any election held for any such purpose in such town”.

 RSA 31:39 gives the Town Council the power to appoint who shall enforce these and can
 issue a penalty not exceeding $1000.00.

 With a large parking lot and the volume we have for large elections (18,148 voters in
 November 2024), the parking lot with the road closed has a lot of moving parts and having
 people handing things out and stopping the flow of traffic will be problematic to the police
 department.

FISCAL IMPACT
None

RECOMMENDED ACTION BY COUNCIL
Please review the attached ordinance under a new Chapter 40 in the general code. Two public
hearings are required, the vote would be scheduled for October 20, 2026.

SUBJECT:            Commercial Industrial Tax Relief for 12 A Street


INTRODUCTION
 The Council is requested to act affirmatively on an application for Commercial-Industrial Tax Relief for
 the Engineered Pressure Systems Inc (EPSI) new construction project at 12 A Street.

TOWN ADMINISTRATOR COMMENTS
 Please see comments within this report.


BACKGROUND
 The Town Council adopted Ordinance #2019-010 on October 3, 2019, re-authorized Ordinance 2026-
 002 on September 1, 2026, which expanded General Code, Chapter 138 to include a tax relief
 program for Commercial – Industrial property in Derry. To be eligible, the project must demonstrate
 that it will enhance the economic vitality of the Town and increase the commercial/industrial activity,
 including job creation. The program authorizes the Council to extend tax relief equal to 50% of the
 town and local school tax burden on the value of the improvements generated by the project, up to a
 period of ten years.


DISCUSSION
 The Council was alerted to the impending sale of 12 A Street last spring and the prospective buyer’s
 interest in some form of early tax relief in order to construct a building on the vacant parcel. The
 property has been purchased by Engineered Pressure Systems, Inc of 165 Ferry St, Haverhill, MA. EPSI
 proposes a ground-up 26,000 sf manufacturing facility, which will function as a permanent US
 Headquarters. This $10 million Private Capital Investment will contribute 17-25 full-time jobs, including
 high-skill engineering, technician and administrative positions.
 Staff investigated options for tax incentives. The Town Council has adopted the provisions of 79-E (only
 available in the Downtown) and the Commercial-Industrial Tax Exemption (project eligible). We
 consulted with Town legal counsel and the New Hampshire Municipal Association to identify any other
 potential programs; communities do enter into multi-year tax agreements with some taxpayers,
 however, those per statute need to be affiliated with energy production.
 It is the Derry Commercial-Industrial Tax Exemption Program which staff recommends Council
 consideration.

FISCAL IMPACT
 Staff recommends a 50% exemption on new value for a period of ten years, which is the maximum
 available under both state statute and town ordinance. Presuming the improvement budget
 ultimately values at $10M, the exemption if approved by the Town Council would be calculated as
 follows:
 Using the 2025 combined Town and School tax rate of $17.23.
 $8M in new assessed value x combination of town and local school rate ($17.23) x 50% = $68,920 in
 year one. The total value of 10 years of projected tax relief could total over $700,000. This property is
 located with the Derry Commerce Corridor TIF District.
