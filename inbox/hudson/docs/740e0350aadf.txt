Steve Whalen: So, this is just a quick overview of the re-evaluation. Thank you for being here, Steve Whalen. So,
our start-up meeting, we have the data collection. What we're doing is we're going out and we're collecting the
information on all the valid sales within the town. So valid sale would be arms-length sale that the buyer and
seller don't know each other. They wouldn't be family members or they wouldn't be between businesses. So, we
try to use those as our basis for the rest of the values. That would be the data collection phase. Collection of the
sales is talking about. We get the quality controls of all the way through. Also, up there for the commercial part,
we'll be sending out income and expense statements. So, what that's doing, that's asking the commercial
properties basically what their rent and expenses are. We're not asking what their gross is. So, if you own a
warehouse, for example, and you're renting it for $6 a foot, we'd like to know that it's $6 a foot and what your
expenses are. So, we're not asking for any type of gross besides the rental. The sales and income analysis, we're
analyzing all the current sales within the last few years. Again, those are the valid sales. Three approaches to
value would be the market. The market would be the sales. The cost approach would be another approach. And
the income approach, which typically is only used for commercial properties. The informal hearings after the field
review. So, after we come up with values, we'll do a field review, and that's basically a drive-by of all the
properties, comparing the property record card to the actual property that we're seeing. For example, looking if
it's a two-story property, garages, pools, anything you can see from the street. We will not be entering the
properties at that time. We'll just be driving by all those. Informal hearings would be once you receive your impact
notice, which would be the new value. You have the option on the letter that you receive would be you can make
an appointment to talk about your value if you feel it's not correct, and you'd have a chance to have a phone
appointment if you wish or an in-person appointment. And that will be available 24 hours a day online. You can
make your own appointment for either one of those, or there will be an 800 number that you can call and talk to
somebody and make an appointment also. So that will be going on for a while. And then the project delivery
would be the end of the project. After the hearings, we'll make any changes that we found that were necessary
to make, and then we'd deliver the final values to the assessor.

Recommended Motion: To authorize the Town Administrator to execute HVAC repair
and emergency service agreements, effective upon execution through June 30, 2027,
with Control Technologies, Inc. as the Town's primary HVAC contractor and Pinnacle
Rock Mechanical as the back up HVAC contractor, at the fixed rates, charges, and
markups submitted in their respective proposals, subject to final clarification and
acceptance of contract terms by the Town, with no guarantee of work and subject to
available appropriations and written authorization for each assignment, as
recommended by the Development Services Director.

Background: The Town issued a Request for Proposals for On-Call HVAC Repair and
Emergency Services with the intent of establishing a pool of up to three qualified contractors
to provide diagnostic, repair, replacement, controls, and emergency HVAC services for
Town-owned buildings and facilities. The solicitation specifically allowed the Town to award
fewer than three contracts if fewer qualified proposals were received.

Two proposals were received: Control Technologies, Inc. and Pinnacle Rock Mechanical.
Staff reviewed both proposals based on company qualifications and experience, personnel
and technical capabilities, availability and emergency response, similar experience and refer-
ences, pricing, and overall understanding of the Town's needs.

Both firms were determined to be qualified to provide HVAC services to the Town. Staff rec-
ommends retaining both respondents in order to provide service availability, specialized tech-
nical capabilities, emergency coverage, and additional capacity when the primary contractor
is unavailable or when the nature of a particular assignment is better suited to the secondary
contractor.

Control Technologies received the highest overall evaluation and is recommended as the
Town's primary HVAC contractor. Control has 40 years of experience, a staff of 17 HVAC
service technicians, extensive institutional and commercial experience, significant building-
controls capabilities, and committed to substantially shorter normal, urgent, and emergency
response times. Control also submitted lower regular-hour, overtime, controls technician, and
service-call rates.

Pinnacle Rock Mechanical is recommended as the Town's secondary HVAC contractor. Pin-
nacle has 25 years of experience, nine HVAC service technicians, broad experience servicing
commercial HVAC systems and equipment, and provided relevant institutional and public-
sector references. Pinnacle also offers 24/7/365 emergency coverage, a lower emergency
hourly rate, and a two-hour emergency call-out minimum.

The agreements would be effective upon execution through June 30, 2027. Neither agreement
would guarantee a minimum quantity, frequency, percentage, or dollar value of work. The
Town would retain the ability to assign individual work orders based on availability, response
time, technical qualifications, familiarity with the particular facility or equipment, past per-
formance, cost, and the best interests of the Town. The RFP expressly provides that assign-
ments do not have to be rotated equally among selected contractors.

RE:           Lowell / Birch / Belknap Road – Dredge and Fill Permit Fees

Recommended Motion To authorize staff to make payment to the New Hampshire
Aquatic Resource Mitigation Fund in the amount of $128,466.50 to purchase the
mitigation credits required by the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers for the Belknap
Road Extension project. Funding shall come from Corridor Funds, account 2070-
000-701, Zone 1 Traffic Improvements, as recommended by the Planning Board and
Development Services Director.

Background: The Town has received both the revised NHDES Standard Dredge and Fill
Permit and the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers verification for the Belknap Road
Extension project.

As a condition of its federal authorization, USACE requires the Town to purchase
mitigation credits from the New Hampshire Aquatic Resource Mitigation Fund before
work can begin within federally regulated waters. The ARM Fund payment required to
satisfy the USACE mitigation condition is $128,466.50. Staff recommends making this
payment so the federal mitigation requirement is satisfied and the project can continue
advancing.

NHDES will recognize this payment as offsetting a portion of the Town’s overall State
compensatory mitigation obligation. The revised NHDES permit provides the Town two
years to submit a compensatory mitigation plan addressing the remaining flood-storage
jurisdictional impacts. During that period, the Town will continue evaluating mitigation
alternatives and determine the most practical and cost-effective approach for addressing
the remaining State obligation. This allows the Town to satisfy the immediate USACE
requirement while preserving the opportunity to pursue local mitigation improvements.

Funding: Funding will come from Zone 1 Traffic Improvements (2070-000-701)
with current balance of $938,859.73.

                                                EXHIBIT B

                                           SCOPE OF SERVICES
The Town of Hudson (Hudson) will use this grant funding for designing and bidding a water transmission
main crossing the Merrimack River and associated booster pump station sized for regional drinking water
supply in South-Central New Hampshire. The work funded by this grant supplements a river crossing,
sized to address only Hudson’s water supply needs, currently being designed by Hudson under a separate
agreement between Hudson and Saint-Gobain Performance Plastics. The design elements funded by this
grant will include upsizing the proposed water main from 16-inch to 24-inch and upsizing the proposed
booster pump station. Work will consist of subsurface testing and site survey, regional booster pump
station design, regional water main design, and other associated eligible project costs.
This Agreement consists of the following documents: Exhibits A, B, C, and attachments, which are all
incorporated herein by reference as if fully set forth herein.

                                                EXHIBIT C

                                          METHOD OF PAYMENT
NHDES shall pay to the Grantee the total reimbursable program costs for eligible services and related
expenses in accordance with the following requirements:
Reimbursement requests for project costs shall be made no more than once per calendar month by the
Grantee using the Request for Disbursement form as supplied by the NHDES, which shall be completed
and signed by the Grantee. The disbursement form shall be accompanied by proper supporting
documentation based upon direct costs. The Grantee will maintain adequate documentation to
substantiate all project-related costs. All work shall be performed to the satisfaction of the NHDES before
payment is made.
This grant is not combined with any other funding source from NHDES for completion of the scope
outlined in Exhibit B. The total reimbursement shall not exceed the grant award of $339,000. Each
disbursement request will be paid using 100% grant funds up to $339,000.
Changes to the Scope of Services require NHDES approval in advance. All work must be completed prior
to the completion date in Section 1.7 of this Grant Agreement to be eligible for reimbursement.
