Recommended Motion: To accept the Police Department’s recommendation to purchase

Services (PSS) Revolving Fund.
Background:
The Police Department respectfully requests the Board of Selectmen to allow us to purchase
three (3) CargoRAXX systems for the three police cruisers currently being built by MHQ.
Purchasing CargoRAXX storage systems for our three new police cruisers would be a
worthwhile investment in officer safety, efficiency, and equipment organization. These
systems maximize the available cargo space by providing an additional level of storage while
allowing officers to organize and quickly access critical equipment, including medical bags,
emergency response gear, and other essential patrol supplies. By ensuring equipment is
properly secured and stored in consistent locations across vehicles, officers can locate what
they need quickly, particularly during emergencies when seconds matter. Additionally, the
durable construction and ability to transfer the systems between compatible vehicles make
them a cost-effective, long-term investment that can be reused as cruisers are replaced.
Ultimately, incorporating CargoRAXX into our new cruisers will improve the functionality
of our patrol vehicles, protect valuable equipment, and help ensure our officers have
immediate access to the tools they need to safely and effectively serve the community.

The CargoRAXXs will be purchased using the Police Safety Services (PSS) Revolving Fund,
which was established by Warrant Article in 2023. This funding comes from revenue
generated through Police Detail Cruiser fees with the intent of offsetting the cost of police
cruiser expenses.

the start of FY27 is $75,166.

Recommended Motion:

To award the On-Call Trenchless Pipeline Rehabilitation Services contracts to Vortex
and Insituform Technologies, based on their submitted unit pricing, through June 30,
2027, as recommended by the Public Works Director, with work authorized on an as-
needed basis, subject to available funding and compliance with the RFP requirements.

Background:

The Town of Hudson issued a Request for Proposals (RFP) for On-Call Trenchless
Pipeline Rehabilitation Services to provide maintenance and rehabilitation of existing
sanitary sewer and storm drainage infrastructure without conventional excavation.

The RFP was publicly advertised and distributed directly to four qualified contractors.
Bids were due on October 22, 2026, at 10:00 AM. The Town received three proposals
from Insituform Technologies, Vortex, and NWMCC.

The proposals were reviewed and compared based on submitted unit pricing for various
trenchless rehabilitation services, including UV-cured and thermal-cured pipe lining,
sectional repairs, sewer lateral lining, and manhole rehabilitation.

Based on the bid tabulation, Vortex provided the most competitive pricing for UV-cured
pipe lining and several sectional repair items, while Insituform Technologies offered
competitive pricing across a broader range of services, including thermal-cured pipe
lining, sewer lateral lining, and sectional repairs.
Based on the overall pricing submitted, the Public Works Department recommends
selecting Vortex and Insituform Technologies as the two preferred contractors. Having
two contractors available will provide the Town with flexibility, competitive pricing, and
improved availability when rehabilitation work is required.

Services will be authorized on an as-needed basis through purchase orders, with no
guaranteed minimum amount of work. The contracts will remain in effect through June
30, 2027 with the option to extend.

Recommended First Motion: To approve the project agreement between the New
Hampshire Department of Transportation (NHDOT) - State Project 45696, the Town of
Hudson, and the City of Nashua for the rehabilitation of the Veterans Memorial and
Taylor Falls Bridges, Bridge Nos. 109/068 and 110/068, in the amount of $2,400,000.
The project will be funded through an 80% federal share ($1,920,000), a 10% City of
Nashua share
($240,000), and a 10% Town of Hudson share ($240,000). The agreement shall be
contingent upon voter approval of the associated warrant article at the March 2027 Town
Meeting.

Recommended Second Motion: To designate the Development Services Director,
NHDOT Certified Local Public Agency (LPA) No. 1573, as the Town's principal
representative and project manager for the bridge rehabilitation project, and to authorize
him to execute all necessary project-related documents on behalf of the Town, subject to
applicable approvals and funding authorizations.

Background: The Town of Hudson and the City of Nashua, with assistance from the
New Hampshire Department of Transportation (NHDOT), previously completed the deck
rehabilitation of the Veterans Memorial and Taylor Falls Bridges.

More recently, a significant crack was discovered in one of the structural girders of the
Veterans Memorial Bridge, requiring emergency repairs and resulting in the bridge being
placed on NHDOT's Red List. These structural deficiencies were not part of the original
deck rehabilitation project and have highlighted the need for additional improvements to
both bridges.

Through the assistance of the Governor's Office and NHDOT, the bridge rehabilitation
project has been restored to the State's Ten-Year Transportation Improvement Plan, with
an initial funding commitment of $2.4 million.
This funding represents the first phase of a larger rehabilitation effort currently estimated
at approximately $6 million. While the overall project is anticipated to require additional
funding, the current commitment is limited to $2.4 million.

The Town of Hudson will serve as the project sponsor and lead agency, responsible for
overall project management, including engineering, design, permitting, coordination with
NHDOT and the City of Nashua, and construction administration.

Funding: The Town intends to present a warrant article at the March 2027 Town
Meeting seeking authorization for the overall rehabilitation project, estimated at up to $6
million.

The anticipated funding breakdown for the overall project is as follows:

                  Funding Source               Share             Amount
                Federal/State Funding         Up to 80%         $4,800,000
                   City of Nashua               10%              $600,000
                  Town of Hudson                10%              $600,000
               Total Estimated Project         100%             $6,000,000

At this time, only $2.4 million has been committed toward the project, including
$1,920,000 in federal funding and the anticipated municipal shares of $240,000 each
from Hudson and Nashua.

The remaining funding necessary to complete the full rehabilitation will be pursued
through future state and federal transportation funding opportunities.

The Town's financial participation and execution of the agreement will be contingent
upon voter approval of the March 2027 warrant article and any other required municipal
authorizations.

Re:     Natural Gas Supply – Competitive Supplier Agreement

Recommended Motion: “Motion to authorize the Town Administrator to finalize and
execute a natural gas supply agreement with Sprague Energy for the Town's municipal
natural gas accounts, with the objective of reducing utility costs and providing greater
budget predictability; Further such execution will be based on the price fixed within
the margin of error of $0.8605 per therm which generates savings.”

Background:

The Town currently purchases natural gas through Liberty Utilities for its municipal
facilities. Unlike the Town's electricity supply, which is procured through a competitive
supplier arrangement, natural gas is presently purchased at Liberty's variable cost-of-gas
rate, which fluctuates seasonally and is particularly susceptible to increases during the
winter heating months.

As part of the Town's ongoing efforts to manage utility expenditures, the Administration
has been working with The SpyGlass Group through its ePro+ energy management
program to evaluate competitive natural gas supply options. The initial review
encompassed 15 municipal natural gas accounts, representing approximately 50,469
therms in annual consumption.

SpyGlass solicited competitive pricing from natural gas suppliers and identified Sprague
Energy as the preferred provider. The proposed fixed-rate arrangement would establish a
predictable natural gas supply cost and reduce the Town's exposure to seasonal price
volatility.

A subsequent analysis compared Liberty's actual monthly cost-of-gas rates during the
2025–2026 heating season against a fixed supply rate of $0.8605 per therm. Based on the
consumption quantities evaluated, the fixed rate would have resulted in approximately
$11,859 in savings during the six-month heating season, representing a 26.7%
reduction in natural gas supply costs. When accounting for the lower variable rates
experienced during the summer months, the estimated savings over the full twelve-month
period would have been approximately $6,219.
While these figures are illustrative and do not guarantee future savings, they demonstrate
the potential financial benefits of securing competitive natural gas pricing. More
importantly, a fixed-rate agreement would provide greater budget predictability during
periods of significant market volatility. Liberty Utilities would continue to provide
natural gas distribution services to the Town's facilities.

Recommended Motion: “To support the continued evaluation of Hills Memorial
Library as the Town's primary municipal meeting facility and potential centralized
records storage location, and to direct the Town Administrator to develop a phased
implementation plan, including necessary building improvements, estimated costs,
funding options, and proposed space reallocations at Town Hall and the Oakwood
facility, for future consideration by the Board of Selectmen.”

Background:

In 2025, the Board of Selectmen established the Town Hall Advisory Committee (THAC)
to evaluate the Town's current and future municipal space needs, including the potential
renovation, repurposing, or replacement of Town Hall. A central consideration throughout
this process has been identifying fiscally responsible alternatives to address existing space
limitations and accessibility concerns. Through this review, staff has identified an
opportunity to improve space utilization across three municipal facilities: Town Hall, Hills
Memorial Library, and Oakwood.

Currently, Town Hall dedicates approximately 1,845 square feet, or 15% of its available
building space, to two meeting and assembly rooms used by the Board of Selectmen and
various municipal boards and committees. While these spaces serve important public
functions, they occupy a significant portion of the building at a time when administrative
departments are experiencing increasing workspace constraints.

The Administration is proposing that Hills Memorial Library become the Town's primary
municipal meeting facility, accommodating meetings of the Board of Selectmen, Planning
Board, Zoning Board of Adjustment, and other boards and committees. The building's
basement would also be evaluated for centralized municipal records storage, consolidating
records currently maintained at Town Hall and the Oakwood facility.

This approach would allow the Town to repurpose the meeting rooms at Town Hall for
administrative and departmental functions, while potentially relocating certain operations
from Oakwood back to Town Hall. This would free up space at Oakwood for other
municipal needs.
Hills Memorial Library has historically accommodated public meetings and municipal
activities, making its continued use as a public facility consistent with its longstanding role
in the community. Certain building improvements, including heating and cooling upgrades,
basement floor refinishing, and meeting technology enhancements, would be necessary to
support the proposed uses.

The attached presentation outlines the proposed space allocation, anticipated benefits, and
initial improvements associated with this approach.

SUBJECT: Recommendation to Select OpenGov as Preferred Vendor

Recommended Motion: “Move that the Board of Selectmen accept the recommendation
of the RFQ 2026-02 Working Group to designate OpenGov as the preferred vendor for
the municipal financial software replacement project and authorize the Town
Administrator to proceed with final scope development and contract negotiations;
Further to place the funding request within the FY2028 General Fund Budget subject
to approval by Town Meeting March 9, 2027.”

Purpose and procurement process
The Town issued RFQ 2026-02 to evaluate replacement of its existing MuniSmart financial
software and related municipal applications with a modern, integrated enterprise resource
planning (ERP) platform. Proposals were due August 24, 2026. Six firms responded:
Aclarian, BS&A, Concourse, OpenGov, Springbrook, and Tyler Technologies.
The eight-member Review Group evaluated vendor qualifications and references; required
and desired features; implementation approach and timeline; and ongoing support and
training. Members scored vendors individually, and the Group reviewed proposals, vendor
demonstrations, functional fit, third-party dependencies, and cost proposals. The technical
evaluation had a maximum of 85 points; cost was reviewed separately and is not included
in the attached scoring workbook.

Technical evaluation results

  Tyler Technologies            69.875                        2
  BS&A                          63.875                        3
  Springbrook                   55.250                        4
  Aclarian                      46.500                        5
  Concourse                     43.500                        6
Basis for selecting OpenGov

Overall functional fit: OpenGov received the highest average technical score. Its
proposal brings together financial management, budgeting and budget publication, tax
and revenue, utility billing, permitting and licensing, grants, and other municipal
functions within a more consolidated environment.
Budgeting and reporting: Integrated budget development, reporting, dashboards, and
budget-book publication are meaningful advantages for the Town’s annual budget
process and transparency objectives.
Reduced third-party dependency: BS&A offered a lower base price but did not
propose a complete replacement for all requested modules; additional Avitar and other
third-party solutions would be necessary and would add costs, interfaces, and
coordination. Tyler presented a mature solution but also had functional gaps and a higher
quoted investment.
Implementation and service: The Group considered implementation approach,
conversion, training, support, and operational continuity, recognizing that specialized
New Hampshire functions and integration details still require contract-level confirmation.
Best overall value: The recommendation reflects the combination of the highest
technical score, breadth of proposed functionality, and a competitive quoted cost among
the more comprehensive solutions—not the lowest sticker price alone.

Background

As we continue to finalize the proposed FY2028 Operating Budget, several expenditure
estimates have been updated since the Board's initial budget review. These adjustments
reflect newly available information, revised contractual costs, and other developments
affecting the proposed budget.

One of the more significant changes involves health insurance. The initial budget
submission included an estimated 8% increase in health insurance premiums, pending
confirmation of the renewal rates. The actual renewal reflects a 0.3% decrease,
representing a favorable adjustment from the original budget assumptions.

Additionally, the Town's workers' compensation and property and liability insurance
costs are lower than the prior year, resulting in further reductions to the proposed budget.

During the upcoming budget review, we will present these adjustments and discuss
additional changes made since the Board's first budget meeting, including:

   •    Updated health insurance, workers' compensation, and property and liability
        insurance costs.
   •    Funding considerations associated with the proposed municipal financial software
        replacement.
   •    Revised costs associated with the solid waste collection and disposal contract.
   •    Proposed warrant articles and their anticipated financial implications.
   •    Other adjustments and refinements incorporated into the FY2028 budget.

These updates will provide the Board with a more complete picture of the Town's
anticipated expenditures as we work toward finalizing the proposed operating budget and
associated warrant articles.
I look forward to reviewing the changes with the Board, identify their overall budgetary
impact, and seek direction on any outstanding items before advancing the budget through
the next phase of the review process.
