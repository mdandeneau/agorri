         1. Kratom Presentation
            Submitted by Police Chief Matthew Tarleton and Prevention, Enforcement & Treatment (PET)
            Officer Justin Agraz
            The Town Council was presented with health issues associated with Kratom use. Town Manager
            Paul Micali informed the Town Council that he accompanied Chief Tarleton and Officer Agraz on
            a visit to various smoke shops to see how the product is packaged and sold. He noted that Kratom
            displays are almost equal to the vape displays in several stores.

Officer Justin Agraz explained that Kratom and 7-Hydroxymitragynine (also known as 7-OH) are two
substances that are extremely popular at the moment. Officer Agraz explained that Kratom in its natural form
comes from tropical evergreen tree leaves in south-east Asia. He explained the difference between Kratom in
its natural form and the synthetic concentrated 7-OH. He stated that 7-OH is a product that is engineered to
contain significantly more 7-OH creating high potency products that differ substantially from natural kratom.
Officer Agraz explained that natural kratom has been used for 200 years in its native countries to improve
energy and pain relief. He noted that kratom began showing up in the United States in the early 2000’s,
becoming more wide spread in 2015. He advised the Town Council that in 2016 the DEA attempted to place
kratom as a schedule 1 drug but withdrew the notice after public back lash. Since 2016 the FDA has put out
numerous warnings about the use of kratom relating to the risk of addition, abuse and dependence. Officer
Agraz added that since 2016 nine states have banned kratom and other states are working towards a ban or
restricting kratom. He stated that in the spring of 2026 NH SB557 was proposed to regulate both kratom and
7-OH, the bill passed the Senate but failed in the House. He stated that there is no FDA regulations because
it is viewed as a dietary supplement.

Officer Agraz stated that kratom behaves more like a drug than a supplement due to the mix of alkaloids
target the MU opioid receptors. He stressed that when this is ingested the human body does not know the
difference between this and an opioid. Studies have shown on long term usage can quickly become a
dependency. Officer Agraz spoke about the rapid rise in calls for kratom usage. Officer Agraz stated that the
synthetic version 7-OH is particularly alarming. He explained that the natural version of kratom contains
approximately 2% or less of the alkaloid 7-Hydroxymitragynine but the synthetic 7-OH will contains up to
150 milligrams per pill. He explained to the Town Council to consume that amount in the natural form a
person would have to consume a half a pound of natural kratom. Officer Agraz spoke about the many ways

Officer Agraz explained that in 2026 the DEA filed an emergency ban on three compounds in 7-OH,

         7. Introduction of Kratom Ordinance [First Reading]
            Submitted by Town Councilor Nancy Murphy and Prevention, Enforcement & Treatment (PET)
            Officer Justin Agraz
            The Town Council reviewed and considered the proposed new Kratom Ordinance to be added as a
            chapter in the Merrimack Town Code, pursuant to Charter Article V. Councilor Murphy explained
            she co-sponsored a bill that unfortunately did not pass but there are two other bills that will
            hopefully pass at the State level. She stated that she does not want to rely on a bill at the State level
            and hopes that Merrimack will adopt the proposed Kratom ordinance. Councilor Murphy
            explained that her nursing experience has given her insight on how substance abuse impacts
            communities. She explained that there is the opportunity to prevent addiction by not allowing the
            products to be sold in Merrimack.

Councilor Murphy explained that she worked in conjunction with Officer Agraz and Chief Levesque to draft
an ordinance. Councilor Murphy read the proposed ordinance;
         Within the Town of Merrimack it shall be unlawful for any person or establishment to sell, offer to sell, give
away, deliver or publically display for sale any item prohibited all kratom, Mitragyna speciose extracts, enhancement
products and semi synthetic derivatives’ to include but not limited to 7-hydroxymitragynine, 7-hydroxy-mitragynine,
chemically modified kratom compounds, concentrated kratom in any form, any kratom product labeled extract,
enhanced, ultra, extra strength or maximum strength shot 50X, 100X etc. Natural kratom leaf in prohibitive form such
as capsules, tablets, liquids and drinks. Natural kratom leaf is not permitted to be processed into or marketed as a
food, drug or dietary supplement. The exception to this prohibition is natural unadulterated botanical form of kratom
leaf and ground natural kratom leaf shall be legal to purchase by any persons over the age of 21. The enforcement and
penalty shall say; the sale offer for sale donation, delivery or public display of any kratom or kratom derived product
whose packaging, labeling or marketing indicates the presence of prohibitive content processing adulteration or
modification shall constitute a violation of this ordinance. Any person or establishment who violates this ordinance
shall be subject to a fine of up to $50 per violation per item. Funds derived from this enforcement of this ordinance
shall be utilized by the Town of Merrimack for community substance use disorder related education and enforcement.
In addition all items in violation of this ordinance shall be confiscated and forfeited to the town and may be destroyed
by law enforcement officials.

Officer Agraz advised the Town Council that he is in full support of this ordinance because his goal is to take
Merrimack citizens safe from a very dangerous substance. He agrees that this ordinance will be enforceable.
Chair Harrington stated that she received an email from a Misty Brown who urges the town to permit the sale
of natural kratom to aid in the treatment of those who are in withdrawal from 7-OH.

Vice Chair Jobin clarified that the violation is the sale of these products not possessing it. Officer Agraz
confirmed. Vice Chair Jobin asked if the language is clear to the retailers, Officer Agraz stated that it is clear
to retailers. Councilor Albert questioned how high the fine can be, he stated that he does not believe the fine
is high enough. Officer Agraz reiterated that the fine is $50 per item. Councilor Albert would support a
higher fine to be an incentive not to sell it in Merrimack. He suspects it will serve as a motivator for other
communities. Councilor Rothhaus explained that he is not in favor of the age of 21 limitation he would
support the age of 18 years old. Councilor Rothhaus believes that this should be a State setting the tone on
this but he is not opposed to a town ordinance. Town Manager Paul Micali asked about the other
communities as it relates to the fine and age. Officer Agraz stated that most communities use the age of 21
and he does not recall the amount of the fines. He explained that these products are often sold where alcohol
and tobacco are sold and it would match those age requirements. Officer Agraz stated that the bills that the
State is considering also have the age restriction at 21 years. Councilor Thornton asked how a state law
