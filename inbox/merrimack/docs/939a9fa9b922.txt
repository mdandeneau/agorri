9/11 REMEMBRANCE CEREMONY - The Town of Merrimack, in partnership with American Legion
Post 98, Merrimack VFW Post 8641, and the Merrimack Police and Fire Departments, invites the
community to join us for a 9/11 Remembrance Ceremony commemorating the 25th Anniversary of
September 11, 2001. The ceremony will be held tomorrow, Friday, September 11, 2026 at
6:30 PM at the American Legion Post 98 located at 43 Baboosic Lake Road, Merrimack, NH
Please note: This is an outdoor event. The ceremony will take place in the parking lot at American Legion
Post 98.
       PARKING: Parking at American Legion Post 98 will be limited to American Legion members and
       vehicles displaying accessible parking placards. All other attendees should plan to park at the
       O’Gara Drive Skateboard Park. A Merrimack Police detail will be on site to assist attendees with
       safely crossing Baboosic Lake Road.

The Children of the American Revolution (C.A.R.) would like to recognize Reuben Cummings, who was a
15-year old drummer boy who took part in the Battle of Bunker Hill. Reuben was Merrimack’s only known
casualty of the Revolutionary War. There is an existing marker dedicated to him on the grounds of the Town
of Merrimack’s original Meetinghouse which is adjacent to the cemetery at the corner of Meetinghouse Road
and Turkey Hill Road.
       The C.A.R. would like to recognize Reuben for his service as a Revolutionary War youth and will be
       placing a granite marker during an event on Sunday, September 13, 2026 at 11:00 PM.

Legislative Updates from State Representatives
Councilor Murphy shared that last week through tomorrow they have been able to file legislative service
requests for the next legislative session, and she personally filed 3 LSR’s. Two of those LSR’s are repeats of
bills that she filed previously that have not yet been successful: HB1389, which was filed in 2024 and again
in 2025, provides for PFAS facility liability for contamination of ground water quality standards according to
federal regulations; and HB561, also filed previously, is relative to the issue with Continental Blvd being
unloaded from the state onto the town, making Merrimack responsible for upkeep, plowing, lane striping,
etc. at expense to the town. This bill prohibits the DOT commissioner from discontinuing or reclassifying
class 6 highways as well as prohibiting them from reverting it to municipalities unless they have town
government approval. The third LSR she filed was relative to a DUI bill that she wrote in 2025, which
mandates an online victim impact panel program by persons convicted of DUI or DUI pled down to
reckless/negligent driving. She also shared that she was recently appointed to the committee to study school
bullying, which came out of law from a senator from Keene and became effective in July. September 15th at
1:30pm is the next meeting for the school bullying committee.


Rep Rosemarie Rung shared that she filed 2 LSRs last week: one at the request of a constituent last year to
put specific expectations around mobile home sales, and the other was at the request of a conservation
commission chair in another part of the state regarding mandating shoreline permit applications be shared
with the municipality, local conservation commission if there is one, and river advisory committee (if there is
expected river impacts). She also shared that she plans on filing 3 bills: one bill would be to create a task
force to look at developing a common application for housing assistance, another bill is for a pilot program
for “adult court” that requires young adults coming from foster homes or troubled backgrounds to help them
prepare for adulthood and get on the right path and away from criminal activity, and the last bill is a
comprehensive salt mitigation bill that she is working on with NHDES to reduce the amount of road salt
contamination in ground water and invest in brine sprayers and other equipment to reduce road salt usage.

    3. Solid Waste Facility – Proposed Changes & Updates Discussion
       Submitted by Solid Waste Foreman Patrick Davis and Public Works Director Dawn Tuomala
       The Town Council to be presented with and to discuss the proposed Disposal Fee changes at the
       Transfer Station.

Solid Waste Foreman Patrick Davis and Public Works Director Dawn Tuomala were present to review their
proposed tipping fee changes at the Transfer Station. Mr. Davis shared that they have noticed some spots of
the facility that they’ve fallen behind on, such as not charging for bulky items or mattresses which take up a
lot of space in their trailers but don’t necessarily weigh a lot and increase their hauling trips. Because of this,
they’re looking to implement fees for mattresses and box springs at $25 each or $40 for the set. Additionally,
they are looking to add a fee for fire extinguishers at $7/each, and a $15 fee for small engines (lawn mowers,
weed whackers, etc.) at $15/each. He also shared that they have to increase their fees for commercial
recycling as their hauling fees are going up. They also would like to lower their construction and debris
pound limit from 1,000lbs to 750lbs as their rates are increasing.

MOTION made by Councilor Thornton and seconded by Councilor Landrigan to accept the proposed
Disposal Fee changes at the Transfer Station starting January 1, 2027.
MOTION CARRIES 7-0-0

    8. 250th Anniversary of the Founding of the United States of America and Commemoration of
       Revolutionary Patriot Reuben Cummings Proclamation
       Submitted by Town Council Chair Nancy Harrington and Vice Chair Jennifer Jobin
       The Town Council to issue a proclamation for the 250th Anniversary of the Founding of the United
       States of America & Commemoration of Revolutionary Patriot Reuben Cummings.

                                        The Town of Merrimack, New Hampshire
                                                by The Town Council

                                                    A Proclamation

                                In the year of our Lord Two Thousand and Twenty-Six

                    The 250th Anniversary of the founding of the United States of America
                                                     &
                       Commemoration of Revolutionary Patriot Reuben Cummings

WHEREAS, the year 2026 marks the 250th Anniversary of the founding of the United States of America,
commemorating the courage, vision, and determination of those who secured our nation’s independence; and
WHEREAS, the American Revolution was sustained through the sacrifices and service of ordinary citizens
who answered the call to defend the principles of liberty, self-government, and individual rights; and
WHEREAS, many of these citizens were young patriots, including youth as young as fourteen years of age
who served their communities and country through military service, vital support roles, and acts of
patriotism; and
WHEREAS, the National Society of the Children of the American Revolution, through its national project,
Liberty's Legacy, seeks to recognize and preserve the stories of the children and young people whose
contributions helped secure American independence; and
WHEREAS, September 13, 2026, marks the 250th Anniversary of the death of Reuben Cummings, a
Merrimack Patriot who gave his life at the age of 16 in service to the cause of American independence; and
WHEREAS, the Town of Merrimack proudly recognizes the men, women, and youth from our community
who served the Patriot cause during the American Revolution, enduring hardship, danger, and personal
sacrifice in support of liberty; and

WHEREAS, the legacy of these Patriots continues to inspire present and future generations to cherish the
freedoms secured through their devotion and perseverance; and

NOW, THEREFORE, BE IT PROCLAIMED, that the Town Council of Merrimack, New Hampshire
hereby recognizes and commemorates the 250th Anniversary of the founding of our nation and the 250th
Anniversary of the death of Patriot Reuben Cummings, honoring his sacrifice and the service of all who
contributed to the establishment and preservation of American liberty.

BE IT FURTHER PROCLAIMED, that all citizens are encouraged to reflect upon the courage, dedication,
and sacrifices of these Patriots, including the young people whose service helped shape our nation's history,
and to participate in activities and observances that preserve and promote the history and ideals of the
American Revolution.
