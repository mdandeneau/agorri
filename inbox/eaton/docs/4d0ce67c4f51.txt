The regular meeting of the Board of Selectmen took place on Tuesday, February 18, 2025

The Board held a Zoom session with Bob Craycraft of UNH regarding Thurston Pond.
Also present at this session was Paul Nuccio and Dagmar von Schwerin of the
Conservation Commission. Dick Fortin questioned the historical water testing data
compared to recent test results. Bob Craycraft explained that test results change annually
based on weather and noted that this past summer was a dryer summer. Bob Craycraft
explained that the transparency of the pond was down and noted that it could have been
impacted by the bad pollen season. Bob Craycraft noted that the median nutrients were
a little higher but that is not unusual. Bob Craycraft stated that the phosphorus level spiked
in June, but that could be due to the pollen. Bob Craycraft noted that the chloride level
has increased approximately 20 percent over the past 25 years, which could be due to
road salt. Bob Craycraft stated that in order to drive the levels up, a septic system would
have to be in total failure. Paul Nuccio questioned how to test septic systems without
singling out residents. Bob Craycraft stated that the E-Coli levels would be high and that
an odor would be evident. Bob Craycraft suggested that the Conservation Commission
educate residents that septic systems should be pumped every 2 to 5 years and
inspected. Dick Fortin asked for suggestions on how to proceed. Bob Craycraft
recommended that the Conservation Commission continue to gather water samples, add
testing locations, perform conductivity testing and educate residents. Dick Fortin noted
that Thurston Pond has always been unique for test results and noted that the alkaline
level is higher than the other ponds. Bob Craycraft stated that it could be due to geological
formations. Bob Craycraft stated that testing should begin early in the season for run-off
and then monitor during the different seasons and that conductivity should be monitored.
Paul Nuccio noted that one of Thurston Pond’s tributaries flow through a culvert and that
there is a large body of slow-moving water at that location. Bob Craycraft stated tributary
monitoring would give a better picture. Dick Fortin noted that there is beaver activity on
that side of the road.
