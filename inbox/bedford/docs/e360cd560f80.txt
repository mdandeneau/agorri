1. ROLL CALL: A meeting of the Bedford Facilities Committee was held on Thursday, October 8,
2026, 10 Meetinghouse Road. Participating were Town Councilors Gail Levesque (Chair), John Bator
(Vice Chair), and Phil Greazzo and resident members Dave Gilbert and Tom Lianza. Also present

3. PUBLIC COMMENT – None.

4. POLICE DEPARTMENT CURRENT STATUS AND NEEDS PRESENTATION

Chief Douidi outlined his 37 years of frustrations. Every time there’s a new Town Council or
Facilities Committee things are bounced around. He emphasized the need for a plan or a project
manager. He appreciated them trying to sell it to the public to get them to vote for it, especially
given the current high costs of fuel and goods. He appreciates them trying to get it as an article,
because they don’t want to lose interest and he will support that. The Department is out of space
and had been out of space for years. If it’s a new building, they would like to be on Wallace Road
or where they are now. They don’t want to be on South River Road or Pulpit Road. He hears
rumors about a new PD where they are or kick the Fire Department out or let’s get Highway out.
He doesn’t know what’s going on or where they are. It seems like it gets washed around and it’s
confusing. He knows everyone acknowledges that they are out of space, but nobody wants to pay
for it. How do they sell that? Throw $40 million or $60 million against the wall and say we can
have a plan? What’s the plan. He wished he could know what the plan is. Are there changes or
are they sticking with one plan. That’s the frustrating part. He probably won’t see it in his career.
That’s why the deputy chief is going to be close with his stats and know what’s going on with
everything. As a long-time Bedford resident and long-time employee, all the buildings need
something. There is no security at the PD. The parking is terrible. They’ve taken every inch in the
Safety Complex, made closets into offices, and have done everything they could. They can’t do it
on the cheap end. When they occupied the building in 1994, it was originally retrofitted from a
printing company to a PD. Then they rented the upstairs space to the ATF instead of utilizing it
for the department. They’re bursting at the seams and there’s nothing else they can do to take
any more inches of that property. They like the location. They want to be along the Route 101
corridor, maybe Wallace Road or maybe Jenkins. He appreciates the effort and the conversation
and another committee. Trying to sell it to the public, he has a hard time finding out what they’re
doing. If they don’t do anything every year, maybe put money aside in a big Town-wide facilities
plan and have money in there so when they are ready to take over a business or buy a piece of
property they have the money to do it. All buildings are in need. They need it because of the safety
for their officers and safety for the community. They’ll be professional and give them the best
service there is no matter what building they are in. They are always told it’s not a good time, but
it’s never going to be a good time. He wished they could get a forecast and a plan in place that
they know they’re going to strive on. He pointed out that since they moved into that building in
1994, they’ve built a middle school and a high school. They haven’t built anything for public safety.
He thought it was time that they did something with the public safety building.

5. FIRE DEPARTMENT CURRENT STATUS AND NEEDS PRESENTATION

Chief Fraitzl noted that Deputy Chief Dubowk would be the point person for since he would likely
not see a project of this magnitude completed during his tenure, despite having designed and
built two fire stations in his career. Deputy Chief Dubowk would see this project through. Chief
Fraitzl shared that he had lived in Town for all but 10 years of his life, but he worked full-time for
the Town during that 10-year span, giving him a deep investment the Town and the Department
for over 40 years. This has been a topic since he joined the department in the 1980s. The Town
had spent money on countless plans and studies and designs for both Police and Fire, but they
remained in a spin cycle and just keep going around and around. He agreed with Mr. Lianza's
previous comments that housing was severely impacting them, though he noted this was a
statewide and national issue beyond their ability to control. They growing and they were dealing
with things they’ve never dealt with before on a daily and weekly basis.


Chief Fraitzl stated that they were doing 350 times the call volume of 1994, running 3,000
ambulance calls a year, and have a pressing need to put a fourth ambulance into service, at least
as a ready reserve, because of the call volume. He felt some direction as to where they are going
would be a big help. They will help them to where they want to go. They just need to know if they
are talking staying in the current location or move to one or more stations. He noted that to cover
the Town according to national standards would require three fire stations to reach every home
within a four to six-minute response time. He didn’t believe that was realistic due to a lack of
available land. They need at least two stations, because of exponential growth on both sides of
Town. There were projects coming up on the South River Road corridor and the west side of
Town. He didn’t know the outcome of a recent sale pending of 88 acres on New Boston Road. It
scares him to think of what that could be. They need to act and secure land and they can give
them an idea of what that would look like from an operational standpoint for the Fire Department.
The current location no longer provides the best protection for the community.

Chief Fraitzl stated that as of last week, they were over 120 calls ahead of last year year-to-date,
projecting over 130 additional calls for the year, mostly medical calls. A 13-person shift is a
national standard. If they are talking about two stations, they need direction to tell them what they
would need to be able to do that. It’s not realistic in the current facility without some radical
changes. They’ve outgrown the Safety Complex as it’s being currently used and they need to do
something to relocate one or the other agency in order to be able to have future growth capacity.
Dave Gilbert referred to the call volume chart, estimating that 70 to 80 percent of calls were
medical. Chief Fraitzl confirmed it was 78 percent. Chair Levesque noted that other towns weren’t
quite as high, 60 to 65 percent. As fire organizations, she wanted to know what they saw as what
has to happen to address that and what they would like to see from governing bodies. Chief Fraitzl
agreed that 78 was high and that the high call volume is driven by assisted living and nursing
home facilities. They have 798 beds compared to the city of Manchester's 600 beds. They are
198 more beds than the largest city in NH. Despite Manchester having 14 facilities to Bedford's
seven, Bedford has 198 more beds. That is part of their demand and part of their 15-18% over
other communities.

Councilor Greazzo wanted to know how many fire stations Manchester had. Chief Fraitzl replied
10. They don’t run their own ambulances; they do run fire apparatus on those calls. Bedford also
sends an engine on moderate or higher acuity calls, automatically committing five people (half
the shift) to the first call.

Chief Fraitzl answered Chair Levesque that there was no simple answer. They have staff that are
running from call to call. They have statutory requirements to complete medical reports within a
maximum of 24 hours. Crews often have two to four ambulance reports to write without a space
to work and a space to work quietly. How they handle that and the stress that comes with that,
they try to improve the quality of the environment they’re in when they’re not in a high stress
environment. When they’re doing their reports they have a quiet area. He didn’t think there was
a way to reduce those calls. He and the manager have met with some of the nursing home staff
from around the area. The global healthcare system is broken beyond repair, There is not enough
EMS services, ambulances, to meet the demands of the State. This isn’t unique to NH. It’s around
the country. They don’t make money on their ambulances. They lose money significantly.

Chair Levesque asked if strategies were being floated at regional or national conferences. Chief
Fraitzl replied there is no magic bullet. He shared that non-fire-based EMS communities are
folding their ambulance services into fire department to continue to run the ambulance service.
He spoke with a private ambulance owner and they are assisting municipal and private services
that cannot get staffing. He noted one nearby community is losing its municipal ambulance service
at the end of the year and being absorbed by its fire department. They have an upcoming meeting
with Goffstown, Hooksett, Londonderry, and Manchester Fire to discuss the mutual aid demands
Manchester is placing on those communities. He noted Bedford brings in mutual aid frequently,
sometimes multiple times a day.

Chair Levesque observed that the burden has fallen to the Fire Department to figure out. Chief
Fraitzl stated that was in most communities around the country. There were very few third party
municipal or quasi-municipal agencies providing EMS to communities. They are fortunate to be
able to hold their own for the time being, but they’re barely keeping their chin above water.

Mr. Gilbert stated the Town has taken on too much risk. One of the problems with this group is to
get the public to understand that they don’t touch or see or need the Police or Fire every day, but
when they do, they want them there when they want them there. They have to plan for that and
have the resources and the people and the equipment. People didn’t see that, because they
haven’t used it. They have to protect themselves against the risk or bad things are going to
happen. He asked if the department had updated its heat map. Chief Fraitzl said they had not, as
it hasn’t changed a whole lot. Covering the Town with two stations has no simple solution, but he
could not justify building a station on Pulpit Road due to low call volume. Mr. Gilbert noted he lives
on Pulpit Road and. Mr. Gilbert reiterated that the heat map shows the action is at 101, South
River Road, and 114. He recalled former Chief Hunter's fear of reaching people on higher floors
in apartment buildings. Chief Fraitzl stressed that getting there in a timely fashion with the right
equipment is a critical issue and a liability. Mr. Gilbert thought it was educating the public on what
the issues are and what the risks are.

Councilor Bator shared that when he speaks to people at the gym or golf course, they agree new
facilities are needed but lack insight into how outdated they are and aren’t sufficient enough for
the capacity they need them to. Mr. Gilbert added that people do not pay attention because the
Town has been raising this issue for years. Chief Fraitzl invited anyone to take a tour of the Fire
station between 8:00 AM and 4:00 PM to see the reality of the working conditions, warning they
are at a critical point for public safety.

Chair Levesque noted that the board must balance needs with affordability to propose something

them with we agree that we need this, but we may feel that we can only pass this. They don’t
know what that is yet. She didn’t think there was any reason to go forward if they didn’t think they
could get something passed. She asked hypothetically, if the Police stayed and Fire left for two
stations (east and west), did he see one as a primary, one as a secondary. Chief Fraitzl explained

6. REQUEST FOR QUALIFICATIONS

Mr. Sawyer introduced the draft Request for Qualifications (RFQ) for a project manager, noting
that Chair Gail Levesque and Assistant Town Manager Jeanne Walker had prepared the
document. There were a few typographical errors needed correction, such as ensuring
consistency by changing instances of "RFP" to "RFQ." He stated the document would be
published on the 15th once the committee was ready to proceed.

Councilor Bator stated he had reviewed the document and found it acceptable, subject to the
mentioned cleanup. Mr. Sawyer explained that the intent was to keep the RFQ simple and allow
bidders to be open-ended in their presentations, focusing on their qualifications and experiences
rather than requiring them to be overly specific. It was qualification-based rather than price-based
at this stage, aimed at identifying who was available to assist with the program. If they find the
most qualified candidate who would offer the best working relationship for the Town, then they
would negotiate a contract with the selected firm to proceed with the work.

Chair Levesque clarified that the RFQ makes it clear that the project journey would run through
to completion, but the Town would initially only contract the first phase. They need to know the
qualifications to be able to support them and guide them through the whole journey, while
remaining clear that they weren’t signing a contract for the whole thing. They’re just going to
engage for the first phase. She thought Ms. Walker did a good job pulling this together. She
wanted to know how it did it get advertised. They had identified four or five firms with experience
building municipal facilities, preferably in New Hampshire. Mr. Sawyer responded that the RFQ
would be posted on the Town website. They could also advertise in the classified ad section of
the Municipal Association. It’s a limited number of regional businesses that do this work and they
could make them aware of the posting.

Chair Levesque pointed out that it listed a due date of November 5th. Mr. Sawyer confirmed that
the RFQ would be posted on the 15th with responses due on November 5th. He suggested that
the committee, or a subcommittee review the responses, noting that the scoring system
developed by Chair Levesque and Mr. Walker made sense.

Councilor Bator stated that the timeline was tight, but acknowledged the committee was operating
under a tight timeframe overall. Mr. Sawyer stated that the timeframe was reasonable for the
types of businesses responding, particularly since there were no major holidays during that
period. Chair Levesque stressed the importance of moving forward quickly to secure a guide for
the project, warning that without one, the committee might slow down and have to repeat work.
Mr. Sawyer emphasized the need for experts to advise on costs, scheduling, and specific space
                     Bedford Facilities Committee – October 8, 2026 – page 9

capabilities, and HVAC requirements. He stated a preference for utilizing current industry
professionals rather than attempting to guesstimate construction costs based on past projects
and adding multipliers. Mr. Sawyer then asked if the committee wished to proceed by consensus
or make a formal motion to correct the typos and post the RFQ.

              MOTION by Mr. Lianza to go ahead forward with this and post it. Seconded
