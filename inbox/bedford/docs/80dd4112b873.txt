III. Approval of August 27, 2026 Meeting Minutes

    Chris Bandazian. Vote 3-0-3 (abstain – Kash, Dumont and Ali).

IV. Chubbuck Road Solar Facility

   Ms. Walker provided an update on the Chubbuck Road Solar Facility, which went online on May 28.
   The Town Council agreed to sell its renewable energy credits (RECs) to Community Power. Ms.
   Walker reported that the town generated 122 RECs through the end of June and will receive a
   quarterly check of $4,270 from Community Power New Hampshire. She explained that RECs
   represent the environmental benefit of generating power without fossil fuels, which is separate
   from the Eversource credits for the energy itself. In response to Mr. Kenneth Kash's question, Ms.
   Walker confirmed that Eversource checks are received monthly. She noted that production to date
   has exceeded predictions, though she cautioned that days are getting shorter. Mr. Kash suggested
   writing an article for the Bedford Bulletin newspaper to share the progress and anecdotes about the
   facility's environmental impact, offering to draft the piece. Mr. James Dumont suggested waiting
   until June to capture a full year of data. Mr. Ali and Mr. Kash emphasized the importance of tracking
   issues, such as a previous inverter outage and cloudy weeks, to accurately address any resident
   questions regarding lower production months.

V. Community Power

   Ms. Walker noted that Community Power is in a holding pattern until its next rate setting. Mr.
   Bandazian added that the rate setting is expected around December. He mentioned that
   Peterborough applied a surcharge of approximately one penny to build reserves, which accumulated
   funds for municipal projects. While unsure if Bedford would support a similar measure, Mr.
    Bandazian suggested a potential round-up approach, noting that the timing is flexible and not tied
    to the budget or town meeting.
VI. C-PACER Program

    The Commission discussed the C-PACER program, which was recommended to the Town Council last
    month. Ms. Walker reported that the presentation to the Town Council has been delayed to
    November 18 to accommodate the schedule of the C-PACER representative. Mr. James Dumont
    suggested informing the local Chamber of Commerce to build support among commercial property
    owners. Mr. John Bator requested a detailed briefing on the program before the November
    meeting, and Mr. Bandazian agreed to coordinate a time. Mr. Bandazian explained that adoption is
    straightforward under a town council form of government. He clarified that the town does not act as
    a guarantor; loans are handled by outside servicing and are tied to the property via a lien that is
    superior to other liens other than the town's tax lien. This structure commands better financing
    rates and encourages developers to invest in energy efficiency.

VII. Reports of Members and Committees

    a. School projects progress and planning – No news or on-going projects. It is budget season

VIII.      Old Business

    a. NH Saves – Vice Chair Bandazian noted that the Town Hall Energy Audit will take place in
       December.

    b. CENH Updates – Vice Chair Bandazian noted he is registered for the Local Energy Conference and
       inquired about the town's two complimentary tickets. Ms. Walker explained that one ticket
       became available after John Russell indicated he could not attend. Mr. Ken Kash stated they had
       no conflict and volunteered to attend. Ms. Walker agreed to update the ticket registration and
       send the information.

    c. Diesel Grant – Mr. James Dumont presented information on the EPA's Diesel Emission Reduction
       Act (DERA) program, which offers up to 60 percent funding for eligible projects with maximum
       awards around $3.5 to $4.5 million. He explained the eligibility requirements, noting that the
       Transfer Station backhoe likely qualifies but risks losing eligibility if run to the end of its useful
       life. Mr. Campin suggested speaking with Department of Public Works Director Brian Desfosses,
       who maintains the equipment inventory. Ms. Walker reminded the Commission that the Town
       previously pursued a DERA grant, but the Town Council directed staff to turn it down and
       required permission for future applications, citing concerns over the requirement to destroy the
       engine block of the replaced vehicle. Mr. Dumont confirmed the destruction requirement and
       stated they would contact Mr. Desfosses to review the equipment list and discuss approaching
       the Town Council.

        d. Other – None.

IX. New Business
        a. Future Solar Projects
        b. Future Battery, Future Storage Projects

            Mr. Bandazian stated there are no future solar projects to discuss. Ms. Walker provided a
            year of Eversource bills for review. Mr. Bandazian plans to analyze the meters with the
            highest demand charges to evaluate peak shaving opportunities and potential battery grants.
            Mr. James Dumont inquired if the town has a capital reserve fund for grant matching. Ms.
            Walker confirmed no such fund exists, and Mr. Bandazian noted that the budget locks the
            town in with little latitude. The discussion shifted to electric vehicles (EVs) and charging
            infrastructure. Ms. Walker stated the town has no immediate plans to purchase EVs,
            preferring to wait for the technology to mature. Mr. James Dumont highlighted that
            switching a police vehicle to an EV would require significant unbudgeted funds and that the
            motorized-equipment capital plan currently lacks funding for the necessary DC fast chargers.
        c. Other - None.
