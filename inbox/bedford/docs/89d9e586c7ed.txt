TOWN OF BEDFORD
                        FACILITIES COMMITTEE MEETING MINUTES
                                   September 24, 2026
                                  10 Meetinghouse Road

1. ROLL CALL: A meeting of the Bedford Facilities Committee was held on Thursday, September
24, 2026, 10 Meetinghouse Road. Participating were Town Councilors Gail Levesque (Chair), John
Bator (Vice Chair), Phil Greazzo, and resident members Dave Gilbert, Chris Swiniarski, Tom Lianza,
and Ray Pieczarka. Also present was Rick Sawyer, Town Manager and Jeanne Walker, Assistant
Town Manager. Committee member Chris Petzke was absent.

The meeting began at 5:30 PM.

2. PLEDGE OF ALLEGIANCE – Led by Chair Levesque.
3. PUBLIC COMMENT – None.
4. PUBLIC WORKS CURRENT STATUS AND NEEDS PRESENTATION

Brian Desfosses, DPW Director presented a conceptual slide show detailing the current state
and future needs of the Town's DPW facilities. He stated that there was no single right answer
for the facility upgrades, noting that the final direction would depend on testimony from other
departments, the committee's preferences, the Town Council, and the taxpayers.

Councilor Bator asked for clarification on whether the $12 million represented potential savings.
Mr. Desfosses clarified that $12 million is the total value of the equipment currently sitting
outside, not a direct savings figure. Councilor Bator noted that putting the equipment undercover
would extend the useful life of the assets.

Chair Levesque asked how many vehicles are currently stored under cover. Mr. Desfosses
explained that the facility has three mechanical bays and a rear drive-through area, but the
drive-through is heavily utilized for storing pallets, signs, and voting setups, making vehicle
parking difficult. The Town's wash bay is also located in that area. The welding shop is also
there, and while one or two vehicles are sometimes parked under the sand shed roof, very little
equipment is kept undercover on a daily basis.

Chair Levesque asked Mr. Desfosses to clarify what he meant by "undercover" storage and
whether he was assuming a temperature-controlled area. Mr. Desfosses stated that dry, out-of-
the-sun storage is a great start to prevent UV damage, but heated storage provides significant
operational benefits. Heated storage would allow staff to preload trucks with salt ahead of a
storm, ensuring warm, snow-free vehicles are ready for immediate deployment. Currently, staff
must clear snow and ice, warm the trucks, start the loader, and load the salt during the storm,
delaying response times. He estimated that only two to three trucks could fit in the existing
highway garage if they were not displaced by broken equipment needing the mechanic shop.


Mr. Desfosses shared an example of the Dover Highway Shed, which features a drive-through
facility with angled parking, suggesting a similar, though less tall, structure could be added to
the back of the Bedford highway garage. He also recommended adding one or two more
mechanic bays to ensure a 50-year service life, noting the shop currently fixes every piece of
Town equipment with only three bays.

Chair Levesque clarified the proposal to demolish the building for parking and rebuild near the
pool, and Mr. Desfosses confirmed that sharing the pool's utilities and bathrooms made sense.

Councilor Greazzo asked for the parcel size of the current parks facility and suggested looking it
up on the Geographic Information System (GIS). Mr. Gilbert asked if the back portion of the
facility was a separate building or an attachment. Mr. Desfosses confirmed it is a single
attached building that can be accessed through the bay doors.

Chair Levesque asked what variables Mr. Desfosses used to generate his cost estimates,
questioning if they were based on square footage or inflation applied to the Harriman report. Mr.
Desfosses stated it was a combination of those factors and discussions with counterparts in
other towns. He cautioned that adding to an existing steel structure, as he experienced with the
DOT Route 101 Manchester shed upgrade, triggers requirements to bring the entire facility up to
modern fire, electrical, and plumbing codes, which can cost nearly as much as building a new
facility from scratch.

Chair Levesque confirmed the Highway Garage plan involved keeping and renovating the
existing structure. Mr. Desfosses wants to build an administrative section in the front of the
building and a longer rear section for vehicle parking. Expanding into the tree line would disturb
the electrical piping for the Veeder-Root system connected to the gas pumps, which would be
difficult but doable. Chair Levesque noted the existing garage is a two-story building; Mr.
Desfosses responded yes, but the 2nd offices are inadequate and akin to "camping."

Councilor Bator asked if any other towns had recently renovated or rebuilt their facilities. Mr.
Desfosses noted Guilford completely rebuilt their facility for $12 million. Mr. Gilbert recalled that
Merrimack had completed a new facility recently. Councilor Greazzo noted that the Salem
Police Department renovated their DPW building for $4 million to serve as temporary police
headquarters before turning it back over to DPW. Chair Levesque pointed out that the $4 million
only covered making it suitable for the police, and it was unclear how much more DPW had to
spend once they moved back in. She asked how Bedford's fleet and road miles compared to
other towns. Mr. Desfosses explained that Portsmouth has half the lane miles but an enormous
facility because an excavation contractor sold them a large area of land. He noted Portsmouth
has 12 sidewalk machines compared to Bedford's two, and uses smaller trucks for narrow city
roads. Bedford has a larger population and twice the road miles as Portsmouth on a smaller
budget. Bedford utilizes all its equipment and could benefit from a second ten-wheeler for road
construction pipe work, as six-wheelers have smaller beds better suited for plowing and salt.

Chair Levesque asked for a comparison of the incremental value between an unheated shelter
and a heated building, noting the high cost and low salvage value of the vehicles. Mr. Desfosses
explained that unheated shelter provides substantial value by preventing UV damage and wear,
but heated space is necessary for operational efficiency. Chair Levesque requested that Mr.
Desfosses research different structural options and price ranges to help the committee identify
                    Bedford Facilities Committee – September 24, 2026 – page 4

the most cost-effective "sweet spot" for investment. Mr. Desfosses agreed to conduct the
research and report back.

Mr. Gilbert asked exactly how many vehicles require heated storage during winter operations,
suggesting the entire 48-vehicle fleet might not need it. Mr. Desfosses estimated that during a
storm, he uses about 18 six-wheelers, the ten-wheeler, and needs two reserve six-wheelers
ready in case of breakdowns, meaning roughly half the fleet requires heated storage. He noted
the loader is parked in the cold sand shed, which is acceptable as long as it is not covered in
snow. He also pointed out that keeping vehicles heated indoors would save electricity, as the
diesel trucks currently rely on glow plugs plugged in all winter to prevent the fuel from gelling.
Chair Levesque asked if it would be more efficient to build one large, heated building or
separate heated and unheated buildings. Mr. Desfosses agreed to give that layout
consideration.

Councilor Bator asked how Mr. Desfosses would characterize the scale of his proposal. Mr.
Desfosses described it as very reasonable and sympathetic to taxpayers, stressing the
importance of protecting the Town's equipment to increase its longevity. He suggested one
large building is generally superior. Site constraints might dictate the final design.

Chair Levesque announced that the Committee's next meeting would be scheduled for October
8th at 5:30 PM.
