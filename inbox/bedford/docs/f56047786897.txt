TOWN OF BEDFORD
                                   ENERGY COMMISSION MINUTES
                                       September 24, 2026

A meeting of the Bedford Energy Commission was held on Thursday, September 24, 2026 in the Town
Meeting Room, 10 Meetinghouse Road.

I. Roll Call: Chair John Russell opened the meeting at 7:00 PM.

Present: John Russell (Chair), Chris Bandazian (Vice Chair), John Bator (TC Liaison), Bill Foote (SB
Liaison), Matt McLaughlin, Chris Campin and Jeanne Walker (Assistant Town Manager).

Absent: Michael Strand (TC Alternate), James Dumont, Nadir Ali, and Ken Kash.

II. Pledge of Allegiance

III. Approval of August 27, 2026 Meeting Minutes

    MOTION by Chris Bandazian to approve the August 27, 2026 meeting minutes. Seconded by Bill
    Foote. Vote 6-0-0.

IV. Chubbuck Road Solar Facility

   Chair Russell noted that the facility is producing well and according to Gemini predictions, could
   generate 1.2 gigawatts of power, ahead of estimates. Ms. Walker reported that the town received
   its first two checks from Eversource. The first check for $18,000 covered June and July production,
   and the second check for $14,687.96 covered production from July 24 to August 23, totaling
   132,019 kilowatt hours. Walker noted that production exceeded site performance estimates for July
   and August, despite a brief period in June where one inverter was down.
   Councilor Bator asked if the numbers being provided by Eversource are being checked and Ms.
   Walker confirmed she is comparing Eversource's meter data with the facility's own meter data. Mr.
   Foote asked if the checks were net of usage. Ms. Walker clarified that the checks are strictly for net
   metering production and do not subtract the usage from the town's 51 meters. However, it was
   noted that the town used about 113,000 kWh during the same period it produced 132,000 kWh.
   Mr. McLaughlin pointed out that Eversource buys power at a lower rate of $0.10/kWh than it sells it
   for at $0.22/kWh, and the solar production only offsets the supply side, not demand or distribution
   charges.
   Ms. Walker also provided an update on the Inflation Reduction Act tax credit, stating the town
   expects a direct rebate of approximately 26.5 percent of the project costs, which will be used to pay
   down the bond. The construction cost was approximately $2.8 million, plus an additional $1 million
   spent previously to bring three-phase power to the transfer station, which the town will attempt to
   include in the tax credit application.
   Chair Russell asked the audience if they had any questions. Ken Rousseau, 55 Hilltop Drive, asked
   about the warranty on the replaced inverter and future replacement costs, noting that these costs
   will affect the project's payback period. He requested that the town provide a dollars-and-cents
   breakdown of the project's profitability, comparing bond costs against Eversource checks, after six
   to twelve months rather than just reporting kilowatt hours. Ms. Walker explained that the
   comparison should be against the supply side of the invoices as it provides the apples-to apples
   comparison. He also asked aboutthe status of the three-phase compactors at the landfill. Ms.
   Walker and Vice Chair Chris Bandazian explained that the compactor project is delayed while the
   Department of Public Works considers a potential redesign of the entire transfer station layout. The
   money for the compactors has not been bonded although it was approved when the warrant article
   for the solar facility was passed (2023). It is possible, dependent upon costs and available funding
   that a new transfer station layout would require additional bonding, which would require an
   additional warrant article.
   Vice Chair Bandazian requested that Ms. Walker provide twelve months of electric bills for the
   town's meters. He stated he wants to prioritize locations with the heaviest demand charges to
   identify opportunities for peak shaving or peak management, such as installing a battery at the pool.
   Ms. Walker agreed to provide the data.

V. Community Power

   Vice Chair Bandazian noted that the commission had its Community Power presentation last month
   and there was nothing new to add on the topic.
VI. C-PACER Program

   Vice Chair Bandazian reported on reaching out to James Key Wallace at the New Hampshire Finance
   Authority, regarding the Commercial Property Assessed Clean Energy (C-PACER) program. Mr.
   Wallace indicated the program is going well with other communities opting in. Vice Chair Bandazian
   cited a recent $22.3 million multi-unit housing project in Lebanon financed through C-PACER and
   recommended moving forward with the program, noting it would benefit local businesses. He stated
   that the administrative burden on the town can be assigned away through the financing process.

   MOTION by Chris Bandazian to recommend to the Town Council that they proceed with
   implementing the C-PACER program. Seconded by John Russell. Vote 6-0-0. MOTION PASSED.

VII. Reports of Members and Committees

   a. School projects progress and planning – Mr. Foote reported that Eversource, via Clean Energy
      New Hampshire, reached out to the school district regarding a transformer replacement
      program. Peter Woodbury School is looking at replacing four interior transformers dating from
      before 2000, along with kitchen walk-in freezer optimization and LED lighting upgrades, all
      potentially funded by Eversource. Foote also reported that the district's bulk energy buy contract
         expired, and they have signed up for a community solar project expected to yield $26,000 to
         $30,000 in annual savings.

VIII.         Old Business

     a. NH Saves – Vice Chair Bandazian noted that the Town Hall Energy Audit will take place in
        December.

     b. CENH Updates – Vice Chair Bandazian reminded the Commission that the Clean Energy NH
        (CENH) Local Energy Solutions Conference would be held on November 12, 2026 at the
        Doubletree in Manchester. It was determined that John Russell and Matt McLaughlin will be
        given the two free tickets included in its membership.

     c. Diesel Grant – Vice Chair Bandazian and Chair Russell discussed Diesel Emissions Reduction Act
         (DERA) grants and reviewed an email regarding federal and state money available to replace diesel
         equipment with electric alternatives. They discussed reviewing the town's Capital Improvement Plan to
         see if upcoming purchases, such as loaders, skid steers, or stationary generators, could dovetail with a
         DERA grant application.

          d. Other – None.

IX. New Business

          a. Future Solar Projects
          b. Future Battery, Future Storage Projects

              Chair Russell opened a brainstorming session for future projects. He suggested looking into
              combining roof replacements on schools with solar installations and exploring ground-
              mounted solar on town-owned land. Mr. Bandazian noted that most town-owned parcels
              are problematic due to conservation easements, size constraints, or abutter veto rights,
              though a new parcel on South River Road might have potential. Russell suggested keeping an
              eye on new, smaller wind generation technologies and battery storage advancements. Mr.
              Foote expressed skepticism about the thermodynamic efficiency of battery storage and
              hydrogen, arguing instead that micro-nuclear reactors are a more reliable and efficient
              future solution for baseload power. The commission discussed adding nuclear and other
              alternative energies to the future new business agenda.

          c. Other - None.

X.      Reminders
