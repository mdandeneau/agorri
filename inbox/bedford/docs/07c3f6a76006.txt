        1. Rundlett Hill Road – Speeding

Carole Lewis, 73 Rundlett Hill Road, addressed the Board regarding safety and speed concerns on
her street. She detailed heavy traffic during morning and evening commutes, as well as during the
day, noting that cars and trucks travel at high speeds and even pass each other on the hill. She
stated that six or seven neighbors estimated vehicles travel at 70 mph between Worthley and the
crest. She recounted a hit-and-run involving a retired couple from Colorado who had moved to be
near their grandchildren; the man was hit while walking in front of the white farmhouse on
Rundlett, thrown approximately 60 feet, and suffered severe bodily injuries, ultimately leading the
couple to sell their home. She also described a crash this summer where a driver experiencing a
medical emergency drove from Worthley into the yard of the white farmhouse, noting that cars
have historically hit that house and porch. She pointed out that if the car had gone straight, it would
have struck the left side of the gray ranch where a woman was sleeping in her bedroom. She
emphasized that a new group of children requires bus stops, including four families with little ones
and some preteens. She recounted an incident two weeks prior where she pulled out of her

driveway midway between Worthley and the crest. A car flew past her, partly on her neighbor's
grass, and by the time she reached the crest, the car was already turning right onto Donald Street.
She noted the car had passed a school bus; a young girl from four houses up the hill confirmed the
bus's yellow lights were on, and the car was gone by the time they turned red. Regarding
construction near the old Chartrand house where people park, that driveways across from house
two and house four nearly face the new development's stone road, and she was told there would
also be an exit and entrance on the other side. She asked the Board to imagine a school bus, multiple
driveways, and cars coming down the hill at 50 to 70 mph. There was no public meeting she knew
of regarding how the development would work, noting the property was voted down years ago.
Describing the recent water line installation, she stated that at approximately 7:10 a.m. on the first

Chair Desfosses explained that the signage described is a typical three-sign lane-closure package
used when closing a lane of a road, which includes Road Work Ahead, One Lane Road, Be
Prepared to Stop, and usually a flagger symbol. Noting that Ms. Lewis had used her allotted 10
minutes, Chair Desfosses asked if she had a specific request or if her comments were advisory.

Ms. Lewis requested a "Hill Blocks View" sign similar to the one near Ann Remus's house, noting
that Town sign staff told her many people do not pay attention to signs, referencing the rotary by
Market Basket. She also requested an occasional police presence or a speed feedback sign. She
mentioned St. A's runners on the hill, noting her 51-year-old daughter was involved in one of three
past incidents, and that she recently encountered a pack of runners coming up the hill despite them
not being supposed to be there. She added that construction signage was absent for about two days
before crews added enough signs for cars to slow by the crest, concluding that she did not know
the exact answer but that the road is dangerous.

Councilor Levesque shared that a neighbor coming down Rundlett toward Donald Street at dusk
was blinded by the light the construction crew used to illuminate their excavation. The neighbor
did not see a worker dressed in black until she was right on top of him, and then saw the flagman,
warning him he was going to get killed standing in the middle of the road. The worker replied they
were doing the best they could. She suggested checking how the crews conduct construction to
ensure safety, adding that the crew was digging with a backhoe when her neighbor passed.

Chair Desfosses clarified that three separate crews worked at the site: The Town crew resetting
catch basin frames and grates, the developer's excavation company installing a water line and
trenching across the road well into the evening hours, and a tree crew that removed all the trees at
the site. He guessed that Councilor Levesque's neighbor had encountered the water line crew.

Chief Douidi thanked Ms. Lewis and acknowledged that enforcement is partly a police issue. He
declined to discuss the hit-and-run as it remains under investigation and is not definitively known
to be a speed issue. He stated the road is a cut-through from Manchester to Bedford with a 30 mph
speed limit, and police have placed the radar trailer there numerous times to count cars. He noted
speeding is one of the biggest complaints across the Town's 30 square miles and statewide, but

Mr. Kash asked about speed bumps, noting that Europe uses cameras and speed bumps, but U.S.

would not be in place in winter for obvious reasons, but summer use could be considered and he
would research it. Mr. Kash cited the removable hard rubber bumps used on the back way into the
Super Walmart area, stating they mitigate speeding even if they do not completely deter it. Chair
Desfosses noted that introducing them would likely lead many neighborhoods to request them in

Mr. Thomas asked Chair Desfosses to review the signage on Rundlett Hill for possible

VIII. New Business

     1. Preston Field Parking/No Parking

Chair Desfosses read correspondence from Brad Boutin, Assistant Director of Maintenance for the
SAU. He reported that staff at McKelvie Intermediate School (MIS) had expressed concerns that
public parking along the Preston Field fence blocked the view of traffic on County Road for
vehicles exiting the MIS parking lot. Chair Desfosses explained that the Department of Public
Works had installed delineators covering approximately the first 30 feet from the McKelvie right
turn out to prevent parking in that immediate area. The formal request was to establish an
enforceable no-parking zone with signage, which would be forwarded to the Town Council so that
police could issue tickets to violators.

Councilor Levesque asked whether the no-parking restriction would cover the entire fence line or
just two or three car lengths to allow for visibility. Chair Desfosses confirmed the intent was to
restrict just two or three car lengths to provide adequate sight distance. He noted that the location
was a popular place to park, and sightline issues primarily arose when visitors squeezed additional
cars into the end of the line once the area filled up.

Mr. Thomas proposed establishing a no-parking zone for three cars, estimating the distance at
about 30 feet, to improve sight distance for turning vehicles. Mr. Kitsis pointed out that 30 feet
equated to only about a car and a half, suggesting the restricted distance should be increased to 50
or 60 feet. Chair Desfosses advised wording the restriction to allow for clear sight distance
generally, which would enable him to provide an exact measurement to the Town Council later.
Chief Douidi emphasized that for enforcement purposes, the zone must be clearly delineated with
signs marking the exact boundaries, ensuring officers could definitively know whether a fourth or
fifth parked car was ticketable.

Mr. Thomas revised his proposal to establish a no-parking area at the entrance with a distance to
be determined. Chair Desfosses specified that a 400-foot sight distance was required, meaning the
no-parking length should be whatever distance was necessary to achieve that 400-foot visibility.
Mr. Thomas added that the length should be determined at the Public Works Director's discretion.

     2. Beals Road Bridge – Speeding Complaint

Chair Brian Desfosses introduced a written speeding complaint from Dannielle Tischler, an abutter
of the Beals Road Bridge Project, and read her letter into the record. Ms. Tischler wrote that traffic
has increased since the road reopened, as it serves as the main route to the Merrimack Transfer
Station. Speeding was prevalent before the project but has increased because the straightened road
makes it easier for motorists to ignore the speed limits, which she noted are posted on both sides
of the town lines. She observed speeding during the early morning, after school by teenagers, after
work, and at night. She recounted an incident where she and her daughter were at a four-way
intersection and a driver went through at over 40 miles per hour. She added that drivers use the
road as a bypass from Route 101. Acknowledging that police can only be present on any given
shift and that Town resources are limited and prioritized, she offered that she and her neighbor,
Chuck, are willing to allow officers to sit in their driveways. She suggested utilizing "This Is Your
Speed" mobile units and potentially involving the Town of Merrimack. The Bedford School
District stopped having her daughters walk to a local bus stop because it was unsafe, changing
their pickup and drop-off location to her house.

Chief Douidi stated that the Police department took the complaint, investigated it, completed an
incident report, and had already conducted directed patrols in the area a few times. He explained
that isolated incidents, such as a driver running a stop sign, inevitably occur when officers are not
present. He did not believe Beals Road had a specific speeding problem, noting that speeding
occurs townwide and drivers naturally do not speed when police are present. He confirmed that
the department had spoken to the complainant and followed up on the issue.

Chair Desfosses asked if there was a specific action item for the complaint. Chief Douidi replied
that the action item would be continued directed patrols. He also suggested purchasing more speed
feedback signs, referencing the ones currently placed on Ministerial Road and Chubbuck Road.
He proposed potentially buying more pairs of the signs using end-of-year budget funds and
suggested discussing the purchase off the record for future placement. He noted that the flashing
lights on the signs serve as a minor deterrent, though drivers eventually become accustomed to
them. He added that, in his experience, speeders are of all ages, not just high school students.

     3. South River Road – Removal of no right turn on red light
                Highway Safety Committee Minutes – October 27, 2026 – Page 6


Chief Douidi stated that the Police department had received calls regarding traffic backing up on
South River Road. He explained that the original concern with the intersection was that northbound
traffic taking a left onto Meetinghouse Road might become clogged, but he did not see that
happening. He believed that allowing a right turn on red would keep the heavily trafficked South
River Road moving. Currently, drivers were not permitted to take a right turn on a red light.
Mr. Thomas added that minutes of traffic movement were being lost on South River Road. He
thought the change was a good idea and asked where the intersection was exactly. Chair Desfosses
explained it was the right slip ramp from South River Road at Meetinghouse Road that allows a
quick right turn onto Route 101. Chief Douidi further described the location as the southbound
approach past Carrabba's going into the overpass, noting there were now two lanes available to
take a right onto Meetinghouse Road. Mr. Thomas pointed out the presence of the two lanes, and
Chief Douidi added that drivers currently could not take a right on red.

Chair Desfosses stated that the signage had already been changed. He provided the history of the
intersection, noting that Market & Main had funded the expansion of the right-turn bay, and a
second bay was subsequently installed with the DOT. He explained the initial concern was that
right-turning drivers would compete with northbound drivers on South River Road turning left,
with a difference in speed causing right turners to fail to yield to the left turners. He felt this was
an unwarranted concern and noted the "no turn on red" signs had been replaced with "stop here on
red" signs to clearly show drivers where they are supposed to stop. Drivers could then stop, yield,
and move on, noting that there were issues on the South River Road corridor where traffic queued
up, sometimes all the way down the road. The intention was to keep traffic moving by allowing
the permissible right turn after stopping and yielding to oncoming traffic.

Mr. Gagne suggested adding a "yield on red" sign. He traveled the intersection daily and expressed
concern that free-flowing southbound right-turning traffic entered the intersection without
yielding, preventing northbound left-turning traffic from clearing. Chair Desfosses asked if the left
turners were unable to move through. Mr. Gagne explained that right-turning drivers on the slip
ramp took advantage of the time, which prevented northbound left turners from clearing the
intersection. Chair Desfosses emphasized that drivers still needed to stop and yield on the slip
ramp and should not proceed unless the intersection was clear, asking if Mr. Gagne was seeing this
behavior. Mr. Gagne confirmed he saw it every morning, noting that busy drivers simply glided
through the intersection. If those drivers properly yielded to the westbound left-turn traffic, the
typical morning queue of four or five cars going up Meetinghouse Road would have a chance to
clear. Chair Desfosses remarked that drivers were interjecting themselves into that four or five car
queue. Chief Fraitzl added that he had seen drivers on a couple of occasions fail to stop entirely,
making the right-hand swing on red and keeping going despite the signs.

Mr. Kitsis pointed out that the State had an unwritten practice of prohibiting rights on red at double
right turns. This specific intersection added a lane, allowing for free movement. He had personally
broken the law by taking a right on red there when no cars were coming, highlighting that this
created an enforcement issue because many drivers ignored the old signage since the turn was safe.
He expressed gladness that changing the signs was being considered to avoid an enforcement issue,
and he asked about the State's position. Chair Desfosses responded that he had reached out to DOT
District 5 and they had no concerns at all. Mr. Kitsis concluded that the sign could likely be
removed. Chair Desfosses reiterated that the sign had already been removed and replaced, and
stated he would investigate the yielding issues Mr. Gagne had described.

     4. Meetinghouse Roundabout

Chair Brian Desfosses read a communication from Lawrence Cheetham, who suggested posting a
sign instructing westbound traffic on Meetinghouse Road entering the roundabout to use their
directionals, noting that 90 percent of that traffic bears right. Cars entering from the opposite
direction would appreciate knowing they do not have to ride their brakes until they are certain a
westbound vehicle is actually staying on Meetinghouse Road heading toward Route 101. He also
suggested posting a second directional use sign for eastbound traffic exiting onto Meetinghouse
Road. He explained that because regular commuters expect 90 percent of the traffic to stay on
course, the 10 percent that behaves unexpectedly creates the potential for near collisions. He
recommended observing the dense traffic flow at 4:30 p.m. to see cars braking their way in and
out of the roundabout.

Councilor Levesque observed that most people do not know how to use a roundabout, noting that
drivers approaching from Gault do not even slow down. She has learned to read the traffic and
anticipate other drivers because people do not follow standard rules. Mr. Thomas recommended
sticking with conventional roundabout signage, though he suggested adding a slow-down sign if
there was an opportunity to do so.

Mr. Gagne asked what the inserts on the pictures indicated. Chair Desfosses stated the complainant
was indicating the flow of traffic and proposing a large white sign encouraging drivers to use their
directionals. He expressed concern with the proposal, arguing it could create an unrealistic
expectation that other drivers will actually use their directionals, only for them to fail to do so.

Mr. Kash stated that he uses his directional but emphasized that roundabout navigation is an
educational issue. He explained that roundabouts are a standard way of life in other countries,
citing the Netherlands where bicycles rule and bicycle paths are sometimes wider than cars.
Americans do not grow up learning about roundabouts in driver's education, though the
intersections have become popular across the nation in large suburbs and small communities over
the last 10 to 15 years. Drivers aged 70 and older never learned how to navigate roundabouts unless
they had traveled abroad.

Mr. Thomas asked whether there were many roundabout accidents. Chief Douidi replied that
crashes and complaints are very rare.

Chief Douidi agreed with leaving the intersection as it is, arguing that the Town already has too
many signs and that drivers must take responsibility for their own navigation. He was taught at a
young age never to trust another driver's directional, as people frequently leave them on
inadvertently.

Councilor Levesque noted that she grew up with rotaries in the Chelmsford area and teaches young
drivers that the vehicle already in the rotary has the right of way and cannot be interrupted. She

Chief Douidi that having too many signs ultimately makes them meaningless.

IX. Committee Members’ Comments or Concerns

      1. School Transportation Coordinator

Chair Desfosses noted that the role appeared likely to be eliminated or reduced as part of the school
budget, which would create a vacancy on the committee. The current coordinator had not attended
meetings during his tenure. He suggested that this vacancy presented an opportunity to reach out
to the school district to see if an alternative representative would like to serve, or if the seat should
potentially be filled by another member of the public.

Chief Douidi emphasized that school representation remains important for the committee. The
school has the authority to change and move bus stops, a topic they had discussed just that morning.
Until the position is formally filled, the committee has an obligation and responsibility to contact
the school directly to ensure their input is included whenever necessary. Chair Desfosses shared
that he had spoken with the Town Manager, who indicated that the transportation coordinator's
duties would likely be shifted, at least in part, to another individual. Mr. Desfosses stated the
committee would need to identify that person and gauge their interest in joining the committee.
Mr. Thomas suggested that the school district likely has its own safety committee, and he
recommended that a member of that group should be appointed to serve on this committee.
