TOWN OF BEDFORD
                         FACILITIES COMMITTEE MEETING MINUTES
                                      October 22, 2026
                                   10 Meetinghouse Road

1. ROLL CALL: A meeting of the Bedford Facilities Committee was held on Thursday, October 22,
2026, 10 Meetinghouse Road. Participating were Town Councilors Gail Levesque (Chair), John Bator
(Vice Chair), and Phil Greazzo and resident members Dave Gilbert, Tom Lianza, Chris Swiniarski,
and Ray Pieczarka. Also present was Rick Sawyer and Jeanne Walker, Assistant Town Manager.

The meeting began at 5:30 PM.

2. PLEDGE OF ALLEGIANCE – Led by Mr. Pieczarka.

3. PUBLIC COMMENT – None.

4. UPDATE ON REQUEST FOR QUALIFICATIONS–OWNER PROJECT MANAGER

Ms. Walker reported that the RFQ was posted online on the 15th, with proposals due back on
November 5th. She sent notifications to several vendors the Town had been speaking with or had
found, and noted the RFQ was also posted on the Town website. Several vendors responded that
they were interested and thanked her for the notification. She received two inquiries containing
questions that were already addressed in the RFQ

Chair Levesque asked whether it was communicated when a decision would be made. Ms. Walker
replied that they were not told a specific date, but told them they would be inviting a short list of
candidates for interviews.

Chair Levesque stated that proposals are due on November 5th and the committee meets on
November 12th, so they could probably look at some of them, but it was a short amount of time to
digest all of it. They don’t meet again until December 10th. She was concerned about how much
time would lapse and then running into the holidays. She suggested the Committee use the
November 12 meeting to set a decision timeline, communicate it to respondents, and potentially
consider a special meeting to discuss the proposals. Ms. Walker stated that they could make the
decision on how they would like to interview the firms, such as during the day or in the evening,
how many firms to shortlist, and whether everyone reviews all proposals or a subcommittee
reviews them, with everyone then interviewing.

Chair Levesque stated that the RFQ contains a ranking process and how they’re going to rank
them. They’re going to want to interview at least the top couple. She suggested reserving
December 10th for interviews. Councilor Bator suggested establishing a small subcommittee to
review the proposals and pare down the list, with the whole committee conducting the subsequent
interviews. He volunteered to be part of that subcommittee. She would like to see Councilor Bator
involved due to his financial background. She also suggested Mr. Gilbert and Mr. Pieczarka for
the subcommittee because of their history with the facilities, and thought that would be helpful
looking through the RFQ’s. Mr. Swiniarski mentioned he might participate but clarified he was not
volunteering. Chair Levesque clarified that the subcommittee would only handle the initial
screening to condense the list and take a first stab at ranking, likely narrowing the field down to
four or five firms. Councilor Bator reiterated his position that the whole committee should
participate in the interviews. Ms. Levesque then named Councilor Bator, Mr. Gilbert, and Mr.
Pieczarka as the subcommittee to review the RFQ responses as they come in.

5. DISCUSSION ON POLICE, FIRE AND DPW PRESENTATIONS–REVIEW OF
   DEPARTMENT NEEDS

Councilor Greazzo stated that the presentations were good, very involved, and in-depth. They
laid out all the deficiencies and needs. He was satisfied knowing the information provided exactly
what the Committee was trying to address. Councilor Bator added that the presentations were
consistent with his own observations during his opportunity to tour the facilities.

Mr. Pieczarka mentioned the historical information available. He referred to the 2024 safety
complex proposal that was not approved, noting it included a whole layout for a Police department,
which was a great starting point so they didn’t have to start from scratch. The layout showed
common areas such as utilities, a visitor entrance, and interview rooms, alongside a fire
substation. If they remove the Fire substation, there was still 34,000 square feet for the Police
department. Some common areas might shrink, utility rooms, boilers, and heating systems would
still be required, so the footprint would not shrink considerably. He suggested asking the Police
Department to review that layout. Subsequent to that, when they were working on the Fire
substation plan, that added a central Fire station with Building, Planning, and DPW. That was
more conceptual than the 2024 detail, but the layout totaled 35,285 square feet. The current safety
complex is approximately 35,000 square feet, so they’ve got a 35,000-square-foot building that
isn’t big enough. They’ve got conceptual plans showing a new central Fire department and offices,
plus another layout that showed the 2024 South River Road Police department that wasn’t
approved, but they have layouts to work from and the challenge is figuring out how the pieces fit
together. Chair Levesque noted that there are layouts with previous designs, mentioning that
Harriman had provided many. Mr. Pieczarka replied that Harriman's work was almost ten years
old, whereas the newer layouts were more recent and showed current numbers that the
Committee could build upon. The problem was finding land to do it. Chair Levesque didn’t think
they were trying to start at ground zero.

Mr. Swiniarski mentioned the discussion of a potential swap of Town land for State land. He
wanted to know if they had a plan to gain traction on the swap, as it had seemed like a very good
possibility. Chair Levesque confirmed there were some plans but stated that the discussion would
be kept to a non-public session for the time being, because they were in the early stages of that.

Chair Levesque thought the presentations were complete and well thought out, and appreciated
that they attached numbers to their needs and found the information consistent with the facility
tours. She thought all departments presented at the same level of detail, which she found helpful.

6. PROS AND CONS OF RELOCATING FIRE OR POLICE DEPARTMENTS

Mr. Sawyer to displayed a slide to frame a discussion on the pros and cons of facility options.
Chair Levesque clarified she was not seeking a decision tonight, but wanted to outline the first
major choice: who stays at 55 Constitution and who leaves.
                      Bedford Facilities Committee – October 22, 2026 – page 3


Chair Levesque asked the committee for additional thoughts on pros and cons regarding the
scenario of the Police staying in the current building.

Tom Lianza stated that whatever is done to 55 Constitution, personnel will have to move, and he
recommended starting by deciding where they will go. He described a process from the high-tech
industry, which involves ignoring details initially, developing a plan and a time map, focusing on
priorities, and circulating multiple plans until consensus forms. Mr. Lianza noted that planning and
the approval process cross three or four silos in Town, and moving those personnel would open
up space in existing buildings. He proposed securing short-term commercial leases of five to ten
years for services that do not require full rebuilds or the costly radio infrastructure needed by the
Public Works, Police, and Fire. He emphasized that moving those specific facilities is a huge
expense. Move the people you have to move and move them as quickly as possible. He
expressed doubt that the public wants to construct a new building like they did in Salem, but
believed voters would support leasing commercial space over a five-year plan.

Chair Levesque pointed out that this could be an advantage of the Police staying at 55
Constitution, as Police staff could be relocated into a temporary office building, whereas it is very
difficult to temporarily house the Fire department. Mr. Lianza added that other occupants could
also be moved out of the building. Ultimately that’s going to happen. More buildings, renovations,
and extensions are needed. He stated his first priority would be finding space for office and
administrative staff who do not require loading docks. Inspection staff still require a customer-
facing location, meaning there will be build-out costs for areas where the public submits plans
and for basic security functions. Chair Levesque stated that depending on the design, part of a
building could be renovated and staff brought back, though some renovations may require
vacating the building entirely. Mr. Lianza advised the Town to move people once in an orderly
fashion and then evaluate the available space. Councilor Bator added that if the Police stay at 55
Constitution, Fire space would likely be part of the renovation, meaning Fire personnel would
need a place to go. He suggested it would be better to decide first who goes where, noting that
finding temporary space should be the easier problem to solve.

Mr. Lianza recalled a prior report about buying at least four properties around Constitution, but
didn’t think voters currently have the stomach to buy buildings. He suggested voters might support
a five-year plan showing how the Town will be built out, noting that presenting such a plan does
not require a warrant article and would demonstrate the Town is making legitimate moves to save
money. Chair Levesque thought if the police stay, the first step should be to build a Fire station
so when it’s done, all Fire personnel can leave 55 Constitution. Councilor Bator suggested that,
at a high level, the Town should first build a new facility for whichever department makes the most
sense, and then renovate 55 Constitution second, possibly over a five-year period.

Chair Levesque then outlined the scenario where the fire department stays at 55 Constitution.
Councilor Greazzo responded that a new Police facility would not necessarily need Town space,
as the Town space at 55 Constitution is currently attached to Fire. Mr. Lianza added Planning.
Councilor Greazzo stated that Planning works with the Building department on the Fire side. Chair
Levesque clarified that the Town Office footprint would be sufficient. Councilor Greazzo stated
that discussions with the Town Manager indicated that moving the Fire department would provide
additional Town Office space for departments tied to their ancillary duties. Chair Levesque
acknowledged that this would free up Town Office space.

Chair Levesque noted a central Police location and a substation site would still be needed. She
asked whether a South River Road substation, with 55 Constitution serving as headquarters,
would satisfy residents' desire for a west-side presence.

Councilor Greazzo stated the main takeaway is that two buildings are needed either way. He
suggested that a South River Road substation would allow the current station to focus full-time
on central and west Bedford instead of being continuously drawn east, a benefit residents may
not have considered. He added that the desire for a west-side presence might reflect voters
wanting a central or western footprint before agreeing to a South River location. Chair Levesque
noted that both chiefs have said they will go wherever directed, though each has a preference.
They’re looking for something that’s least disruptive, because temporarily housing either
department could be disruptive to them and the Town. Councilor Greazzo emphasized that the
Fire department definitely cannot be temporarily housed.

Chris Swiniarski clarified that "West Bedford" does not mean the far west near the Amherst line.
Chair Levesque specified the Wallace corridor, and Mr. Greazzo suggested a location somewhere
along Route 101, but not the north end of 101. Mr. Swiniarski stated it would be insane to build in
the least populated area of Town, though he acknowledged it is hard to convey to voters that
building in one location improves response times elsewhere. Mr. Gilbert noted that crews would
be "twiddling their thumbs" out west where he lives, pointing out that the heat map shows where
problems actually arise. Calls are not generally west of Wallace. Most activity occurs on South
River and on Route 101 east of Wallace. That’s where you need Fire. The Police have four patrol
zones and could be anywhere. Fire needs to be where the action is going to be and where it’s
proven they have more calls. Mr. Swiniarski noted that feedback indicated voters want police
stationed near schools. He found this odd since schools are dispersed around the Town. Mr.
Gilbert stated that they also have resource officers. Mr. Swiniarski didn’t think it was a good idea
and didn’t think police needed to be near schools. He didn’t see the upside to that, but
acknowledged it was a public relations hurdle because the sentiment was common.

Mr. Pieczarka didn’t think it was about response time for the Police, it’s about space. The Fire
department has a need for space, but it’s more importantly response times. That’s why he feels
two new buildings for the Fire department would greatly improve response time versus a new
Police station doesn’t help Fire with response times.

Mr. Gilbert stated that while both departments face safety issues, he believes the Police issues
outweigh the Fire issues citing space, bringing people in, interviewing and detaining people, the
sally port, weapons and evidence storage. Councilor Bator mentioned structural problems, noting
the building uses drywall rather than concrete or Kevlar-reinforced walls. Mr. Gilbert referred to
the facility as a "ticking time bomb." Chair Levesque added that the Police building must be public
facing whereas Fire personnel leave the building on a call. They have people in emergency
situations coming into that building.

Mr. Gilbert raised the possibility of using other properties on Constitution. Chair Levesque cited

45 and 47 Constitution next to the Safety Complex. Ideally, space could be leased there if needed.
Mr. Gilbert said that would solve many of these problems. Mr. Lianza suggested a couple of
years. Right now they have overcrowding everywhere, and once a move is decided, the Town
should say this is a five-year maximum procession, reevaluate what space they have in Town,
and prioritize what’s getting rebuilt over what period of time, stressing that space must be freed

Mr. Lianza asked whether the Town had received survey feedback on what voters want aside
from the people that don’t want it at all. Chair Levesque stated an initial survey received about
500 responses, with comments including a desire for a presence on the west side. She explained
that at the March election, voters dropped a ticket in one of four buckets.


Ms. Levesque noted the first option received 25 percent and the do-nothing option received 25
percent. Across the first three options, 75 percent essentially said to do something and stop
kicking the can. She acknowledged that about 20 to 25 percent of residents will always oppose
new taxes. The east and west presence option received a lot of positive feedback. The flyer they
handed out in September yielded conversational feedback, with some residents opposing new
taxes and others wanting the project done. The Committee is looking for what’s going to pass.

Mr. Lianza asked what majority a warrant article needs, and Mr. Sawyer confirmed a bond
requires 60 percent. Mr. Swiniarski proposed a warrant article that authorizes the Town to replace
current bonds that are falling off with new bonds with specific dates. Chair Levesque stated that’s
essentially what they proposed. Councilor Greazzo stated that was the handout in September.
Mr. Swiniarski added make that the actual warrant article, so it’s not about specific locations or

They would have a budget to work with. Chair Levesque stated that was the approach with the
survey done at the polls. Mr. Swiniarski stated that he was talking about something a little different.
No one wants taxes to go up, so they’ll be the availability to issue bonds without taxes going up.
The idea is to get a vote authorizing that exact issuance allocated to facilities. Chair Levesque
stated that that was a large part of the conversation. They’re working with members of the
Communications Committee on a possible November survey stating the bond approach: laying it
out over about 20 years and pulling bonds as needed, similar to the roads project. Mr. Swiniarski
stated that if they could get a budget authorized without raising taxes without having details hang
up the vote. Councilor Bator stated that they want to raise whatever the number is without giving
specifics and then they’re going to come up with a detailed plan. Mr. Swiniarski noted prior warrant
articles at $30-plus million and $8-something million failed. Councilor Greazzo stated that it was
due to too much detail. The only facilities measure that ever passed authorized bonding up to $4
million for a Fire station without specifics. He agreed that 75 percent of residents on the survey

say to fix facilities, but agreeing on what that solution is, they’re never going to get everybody in
Town to agree on that and that’s why they haven’t gotten anywhere. Not everyone gets to make

asked for $20 million to repair the roads. He asked how much the retiring bonds total. Councilor

issued as three separate bonds. One has already been paid off. Mr. Swiniarski stated the vote

mathematically say there won’t be any increase in taxes. It may be softened because of spreading
it out over multiple years She explained that bond debt declines like an amortization table, so the
freed amount is only the remaining payment, not the original principal cost. She noted bonding
minimizes the tax increase but may not keep taxes flat. Mr. Gilbert didn’t think they could attached

agreed the bond carries no conditions. The concern is securing the revenue currently paying debt
for the new debt service. Chair Levesque clarified that they are going to wait until the bond debt
is done before they add on more bond debt. Mr. Swiniarski agreed that was part of it, but the main
goal was securing the money. If they paid off all their bonds, they’re not going to lower taxes in

that the money had to be allocated for facilities or it goes in the budget and gets used for

Chair Levesque thought this was another fork in the road from a decision point on whether to
pursue a March warrant article. If they’re going to take the high-level approach, a March article is
possible, whereas waiting for a fully developed design would require another year's wait. She
thought it took away from the urgency of what they’re seeing to go do something. Even when they
start, a new building is two or three years from occupancy. If they kick the can for another year,
that’s where they are. If they put a warrant article, and it gets voted down, it doesn’t mean you
can’t come back with a more baked design. She would rather see a warrant article even if it
doesn’t dot all the I’s and cross all the T’s. Councilor Greazzo stated a warrant article is needed
every year until something gets done. If they say no, they say no, but if you never ask, they’re
never going to get it and it needs to be gotten. You have to ask every single year at every single
election until people realize they better pay for it now, because if they had paid for it when it was
$4 million, they wouldn’t be facing $30 million today. Chair Levesque stated that they tried to
address the concerns they got back on the survey as far as site, location, and what things are

the blow, but there will be a cost, and three-quarters of residents say to fix the facilities.
