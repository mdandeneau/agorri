                         Representative Reports – Richard Doucette
   Richard shared a photo of the plaque that will hang in the new recycling building to thank
    and recognize funders and helpers along the way.
   Richard provided Commissioners a detailed recycling specifications list. The Transfer
    Station will be accepting #1 PET clear/see-through drink bottles, two types of #2 plastic
    (natural/whitish and colored), cardboard, aluminum, tin, glass, and mixed paper. An
    abbreviated version of the recycling specifications will be included on a flyer and made
    available on the Tamworth Exchange, Town website, and onsite. The NH Recycles
    website will also have three videos showing what happens to various recyclable materials
    in NH, including cardboard and plastic.
   Kerryn asked about styrofoam and batteries. The Tamworth Recycling Project, a
    volunteer organization, currently manages styrofoam recycling twice a year. The Transfer
    Station staff are hoping to have enough space in the new building to accept styrofoam
    more regularly. The Transfer Station is now accepting lithium batteries, and they hope to
    eventually accept button and rechargeable batteries as well. Lithium batteries were noted
    as a particular fire hazard; he noted that Casella waste management loses one facility per
    year in the US to a lithium battery related fire.
   The “Swap Shop” idea originated with Transfer Station employees, but there are concerns
    that if it is too widely publicized, people may leave items that are broken or should
    otherwise be disposed of. The "Can Shack" may be repurposed for the Swap Shop, with
    close supervision needed.
   Richard raised concerns to make Town-owned conservation land easier for the public to
    find on the Town website. He noted that it currently takes several steps to reach the map
    through the Conservation Commission website. Discussion included updating the map
    and improving trailhead information.

                                     Managed Lands & Trails
      The Fire Tower has been repainted and was still free of graffiti as of Thursday last week.
       Discussed adding a small, polite “No Graffiti Please” and “Volunteer Maintained” sign
       and a QR code that links to the TCC webpage, where panoramic photographs, peak
       identification, and historic information can be provided. Consider periodic graffiti
       removal, potentially in the spring and fall, rather than allowing graffiti to accumulate.
       Lucy will work on new panoramic photo contact information and will coordinate with
       others for the website. Ned will look for the files of the last panoramic photos.
      Continue working toward clearer signage, maps, and QR codes at managed trails. Maps
       should remain available in a format that does not depend on cell service; a physical map
       or large trailhead display may be useful in addition to a QR code.
