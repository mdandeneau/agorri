 1. Request for a Variance Extension (Two)
    680 Portsmouth Avenue (Map R17, 62: Commercial A Zone)
    Owner/Applicant – Lang & Sons, LLC
    The owner/applicant is seeking a two-year extension of a pair of Variances that were granted by the
    Zoning Board allowing the creation of an additional lot with frontage of 68.09 feet and a lot width of
    69.08 feet. The Variances were granted in November 2024 and will expire in November 2026.

Attorney Kevin Baum (Hoefle, Phoenix, Gormley & Roberts, LLC), representing Lang and Sons, addressed the
Board; also present were the Lang brothers. They were requesting a two-year extension to a pair of Variances
to allow the creation of an additional lot.

Variance Background: The Variances were granted in November 2024 for reduced frontage of 68.09 feet and
a lot width of 69.8 feet. The Variances were scheduled to expire in November 2026.

Extension Request: Attorney Baum explained that the family needed additional time to complete and file a
formal subdivision plan. Survey and testing issues took longer than expected, and the death of two family
members further delayed the process. The applicants intended to submit the Planning Board application by
the end of 2026 but requested the extension as a precaution. Attorney Baum stated that zoning and
surrounding conditions had not changed since the approval in 2024.

C. Hussey opened the hearing to public comments. There being none, he closed the public hearing and
returned to the Board for further discussion.

MOTION: G. Page moved to grant the two-year extension for the two Variances granted in November 2024.
This extension will expire in November 2026. Second – J. Eno; all in favor. MOTION CARRIED

 2. Request for a Variance
    1139 Portsmouth Avenue (Map R11, 12: Residential Zone)
    Owner/Applicant: Richard Hussey
    The owner/applicant is seeking a Variance for a 21-foot x 22-foot garage which will extend 8 feet
    into the 20-foot setback. The Greenland Zoning Ordinance Article IV – Dimensional Requirements,
    Section 4.1 – General Requirements, Subsection 4.1.1, and Section 4.2 – Table of Dimensional
    Requirements, Item ‘f’ – Side Yard Minimum requires a 20-foot setback. The total lot size is 14,140
    square feet (.32 acres).

C. Hussey recused himself for this application.

Present were Richard and Eleanor Hussey. R. Hussey, owner/applicant, was requesting relief for a 21-foot by
22-foot garage that will extend 8 feet into the required side yard setback. The proposed two car garage will
remain at approximately the same height as the existing structure and would not be used as a residence.

Property Constraints: R. Hussey explained that the well limited placement on the side, the septic system
limited expansion behind the existing garage, and the front of the property had a significant elevation drop.
Relocating the driveway or well would create a substantial cost. It was noted by the Board that the lot
predates current larger lot-size and setback requirements.

Abutter Status: The Board asked R. Hussey if the neighboring property owner was aware that the garage
would be approximately 6 feet from the property line and could potentially affect the hedge between the
properties. R. Hussey stated that the current neighbor was aware and had no objections. It was noted that
the Town Office had not received any objections.

L. Schwab opened the hearing to public comments. There being none, L. Schwab closed the public hearing
and returned to the Board.

Height Discussion: G. Sims questioned if the approval of the setback Variance would allow a future owner to
build up to the 35-foot maximum height allowed by the Zoning Ordinance. After a lengthy discussion, Board
members agreed that height is separately regulated and the applicant was not proposing additional stories
to the proposed garage.

Approval Criteria: The statutory criteria was reviewed, which included public interest, consistency with the
intent and spirit of the Ordinance, hardship arising from the well, septic system and site elevations, and the
absence of a fair and substantial relationship between the setback requirement and the property’s specific
conditions.

MOTION: G. Page moved to grant the Variance to allow a 21-foot x 22-foot garage to extend 8 feet into the
20-foot setback at 1139 Portsmouth Avenue (Map R11, 12: Residential Zone), according to the plan submitted
by James Verra & Associates, dated July 29, 2026, Job 26-2076. Second – J. Eno; all in favor. MOTION CARRIED

C. Hussey joined the meeting.

3. Approval of Minutes

RCIM Jurisdiction: Members revisited a prior application involving the RCIM Overlay District and clarified that
the Board had voted on two Variances: the building footprint Variance was approved (vote: 3 to 2); the open
space Variance was denied. A third requested Variance was not voted on: it was determined that the matter
was permitted under the RCIM Overlay District and belonged at the Planning Board.

Minutes Accuracy: Several members questioned the statement in the draft minutes from Tuesday, July 21st,
that the Board had agreed to prepare formal questions for the Town Attorney. Members recalled the
discussion and informal agreement; however, there was no clear formal vote authorizing legal work or
expenditure.

Legal Counsel Discussion: The initial confusion about RCIM jurisdiction led some participants to consider
requesting guidance from the Town Attorney. The issue was clarified through research by Board members;
the legal request was not needed. The Board discussed whether a formal vote would have been required
before requesting counsel or incurring Town expense.

Approval of Minutes Continued: The recording from the meeting was needed to determine if a consensus
was reached. Members were asked to review the recording before revisiting the disputed language in the
minutes.

MOTION: L. Schwab moved continue approval of the minutes from the meeting on Tuesday, July 21, 2026, to
the next meeting. Second – G. Sims; all in favor. MOTION CARRIED

4. Other Business

Certified Mailing Fee Increase: The Administrative Assistant recommended increasing the certified mailing
fee to $15 per letter to reflect increased postage costs. The Planning Board had increased their mailing fee
to $15 the previous month. The Board considered consistency with the Planning Board and the convenience
of using the same fee structure for applicants.

MOTION: L. Schwab moved to increase the applicant cost for certified mailings to $15 per letter, effective
with the applications due Wednesday, November 25th for the December meeting. Second – G. Page; all in
favor. MOTION CARRIED

Applicant Information: G. Sims questioned if applicant information was available to the Board. The
Administrative Assistant stated that she would check on adding a form to the application similar to the
Planning Board’s.

5. Adjournment

MOTION: J. Eno moved to adjourn at 7:14 pm. Second – G. Page; all in favor. MOTION CARRIED
