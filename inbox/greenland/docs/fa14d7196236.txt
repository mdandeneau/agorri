 3. New
    Site Plan Review
    143 Post Road (Map U2, 2: Residential Zone)
    Owner: City of Portsmouth
    Applicant: Albert Pratt, City of Portsmouth Water Division
    The City of Portsmouth Water Division is designing a water treatment facility, which includes a
    36-foot by 54-foot treatment building adjacent to the existing Greenland well house. The existing
    access road will be improved to a 20-foot wide paved drive and will include drainage
    improvements.

Al Pratt, City of Portsmouth Water Resource Director, presented the application for a proposed water
treatment facility at 143 Post Road. Also present from Underwood Engineers: Tom Page, Zach Sullivan
and Adam Mayes.

MOTION: J. McDevitt moved to accept the application for 143 Post Road (Map U2, 2: Residential Zone) as
complete. Second – F. Catapano; all in favor. MOTION CARRIED

PFAS Treatment: A. Pratt explained that the Greenland well supplies up to 450 gallons per minute directly
to Greenland’s public water system. The PFAS is at 4.2 parts per trillion, which is slightly higher than the
federal limit of 4.0 parts per trillion that takes effect in 2029. The new facility will use two granular
activated carbon vessels to remove PFAS from the water.

Site and Building Design: The proposed treatment building is approximately 36 feet by 54 feet, and
approximately 25 feet high. The design includes a masonry veneer, steel structure, horizontal metal
siding, limited exterior lighting, process piping, metering, chemical systems, and a security fence. A lower
roof section and architectural details were discussed to reduce the building’s visual mass.

Access, Utilities, and Drainage: The project will include clearing trees and widening the Post Road
entrance, improving and re-graveling the existing access road, widening the turning radius for trailer
deliveries during construction, and potentially replacing the City water main to Post Road. Utility poles
may be relocated, and a new three-phase electrical service is planned. Drainage improvements include
filtration swales and a basin designed to receive initial carbon-media backwash water, floor drain flows,
and emergency vessel-relief discharge.

Water and Backwash Questions: Responding to questions, A. Pratt and T. Page explained that the
backwash water is treated water from the system. Only fine carbon particles, not the main carbon media,
will settle in the infiltration basin and infiltrate slowly. PFAS will remain retained in the carbon media and
removed when the media is replaced. The existing well from 2018 is being evaluated as a backup well. If
approved, both wells could provide up to approximately 900 gallons per minute for a limited period.

Community Concerns and Conditions: Pam Gentile, 161 Post Road, and Kass McInnis, 147 Post Road,
voiced concerns about culvert capacity and maintenance, road widening, drainage, lighting visible from
homes, tree removal, landscaping, traffic speed, rec field traffic, and utility company damage. A. Pratt
and T. Page responded that the road and culvert would be restored or extended as needed, construction
would include regular inspections and materials testing; trees will be flagged before removal. Tree
clearing will occur between October 01st and April 14th due to bat restrictions.

Fire Lane/No Parking: It was noted that the 20-foot access road is for Fire Department use and can be
posted ‘No Parking – Fire Lane’, which is enforceable by the Police Department. Vehicles use this access
road and idle when lining up to drop off and pick up students at Greenland School.

Lighting: The applicant agreed to look at correcting the existing light that shines toward abutting homes.

Review by Town Engineer: J. McDevitt questioned if Altus Engineering should review the plans. Some
members were concerned it could delay the project by months. A. Pratt stated they would like to start
the bid process in October. It was determined that the plans did not have to be reviewed by Altus
Engineering; Underwood Engineers would be doing regular inspections during construction.

MOTION: H. Droesch moved to approve the Site Plan Review for 143 Post Road, Map U2, 2: Residential
Zone, for the City of Portsmouth’s Greenland Well PFAS Treatment Facility, according to the plan
submitted by Underwood Engineers, Project No. 3047, dated July 22, 2026, with the following conditions:
tree flagging – all trees to be removed are to be marked/flagged in advance of removal; lighting – the
applicant is to supply a lighting fixture cut sheet, and all fixtures should be downward-directed with cutoffs
so that light is not visible from the side; traffic calming – two speed tables are to be placed on the access
road: one past the entrance of the pump house/treatment facility, and the other approximately 300 feet
farther up the road; limited tree trimming: tree trimming along the access road is to be limited to what is
necessary, south where the abutter’s pines are located, and north where the road widens; landscaping: a
landscaping plan that includes a buffer that will grow in within a reasonable time frame, between the
building/fence and the access road on the north side; construction oversight: regular on-site inspections
during construction to verify the road, culvert, drainage and related work follow the approved plans. This
plan is consistent with the Town’s Site Plan Review Regulations, Subdivision Regulations and Zoning
Ordinance. Second – J. McDevitt; all in favor. MOTION CARRIED
