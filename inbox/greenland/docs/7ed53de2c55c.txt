2. Mailbox Damage During Winter Maintenance Policy

The Board of Selectmen adopted a Mailbox Damage During Winter Maintenance Policy that will establish
clear guidelines for addressing mailbox damage that may occur during snowplowing and winter road
maintenance operations. Under this policy, the Town may repair, replace or reimburse residents up to a
maximum of $85 per incident caused by direct damage with Town or authorized contractor snow removal
equipment. Damage caused by snow or ice being pushed or thrown by plows would not be covered.
Residents are responsible for ensuring their mailboxes are properly installed and maintained. Mailbox
damage must be reported to the Town within five business days to be considered under this policy. The
full policy can be found on the Town website.

MOTION: Z. Pike moved to adopt the Mailbox Damage During Winter Maintenance Policy, effective
immediately. Second – H. Droesch; all in favor. MOTION CARRIED

     III. OLD BUSINESS

   1. Fire Department Job Descriptions

   Chief Stanchina had submitted Fire Department job descriptions to the Board at the meeting on Monday,
   September 21st, and discussion was continued. Chief Stanchina requested the job descriptions be
   discussed at this meeting.

   Purpose and Scope: The job descriptions fill a gap; the Fire Chief position was previously the only formally
   documented position. The job descriptions establish basic qualifications, expectations, and accountability
   categories for future policies and procedures.

   Employee Classifications: Employees who qualify for either call firefighter or the part-time firefighter
   classification may review the descriptions and choose the classification that best reflects their relationship
   with the department. Part-time employees are expected to meet a future minimum hours requirement,
   while firefighters and EMT’s are expected to respond to a specified number of eligible off-duty calls.

   Pay and Scheduling: Compensation between the call and part-time classifications would not change when
   certification levels are the same. Part-time work is generally as needed unless a recurring shift is
   separately arranged, while call personnel respond as needed. Existing 24-hour rotating shift expectations
   are addressed in the relevant descriptions.

   MOTION: Z. Pike moved to approve the Fire Department job descriptions as submitted by Chief Stanchina.
   Second – L. Malloy; all in favor. MOTION CARRIED

 VII. BUDGET REVIEWS

1. Approval of Selectmen’s Budget

The Board reviewed the Town Administrator’s proposed reductions and department requests. P. Stanley
left the meeting at 7:00 pm.

Overall Budget Options: The department draft represented approximately a 14.4% increase. TA Tucker’s
reductions brought the overall increase to approximately 7.7%. The first year fire truck payment could
reduce the comparable increase to approximately 6%.

Comparable Baseline: TA Tucker explained that the prior year budget included one-time or unusual
transactions: the $528,000 Fire and Ambulance fund transaction and $350,000 from the Unreserved Fund
Balance for roads and capital reserve funds. Removing those items created a comparable baseline
intended to align with how the Department of Revenue Administration would view the budget.

Tax and Benefits Pressures: The Board reviewed historical tax-rate changes and noted that education
related increases were outside of the Board’s control. Savings were identified from a lower NH Retirement
System employer rate, insurance reductions based on claims history, and reductions in Library
maintenance estimates. The Library budget was reduced from approximately 9% to 6.4% after they
reviewed their repair estimates.

Budget Target: The Board discussed whether to use a percentage target or evaluate individual line items.
Chief Stanchina requested that the fire and ambulance budgets be combined, bringing their increase of
no more than 6.8%, or $1,485,474.79 and still maintain the required service levels.

 Fire and Ambulance Staffing: The Board examined call-volume data and proposed reducing overnight
  staffing. Chief Stanchina explained the operational and safety consequences of reducing the third on-
  duty responder. The Board kept the full staffing option.

Call Data Analysis: TA Tucker reviewed dispatch data from January 2025 through October 2026.
Approximately 68% of calls occurred during the 10 am to 10 pm period and 32% during the night shift; the
average was approximately one call per day. One proposed savings option removed the third part-time
responder for two overnight shifts per week, which would reduce the budget by approximately $35,717.

Response Capability: The Board questioned what would happen if two on-duty personnel were
committed to an ambulance. Chief Stanchina explained that two people are required for an ambulance;
fire, vehicle-crashes, and similar incidents may require the ambulance and fire truck to deploy separately
so the crew retains access to protective equipment and specialized apparatus. Mutual aid is available;
however, the first local response remains important.

Staffing Standards: Chief Stanchina explained that a four-person engine crew was the national minimum
standard; the department currently operates below that level. Three personnel provide greater capability
than two because firefighting tasks such as moving hose, rescue operations, pump operation, and
maintaining an outside safety team cannot be performed effectively by a single responder.

Two-In/Two-Out: For immediately dangerous environments, the OSHA two-in/two-out principal requires
two personnel inside and two outside for rescue support. With only two people arriving on a fire truck,
one may have to operate the apparatus and remain outside, leaving only one person available to enter.
The third responder improves the ability to make a rescue attempt while awaiting additional personnel.

Board Direction: Board members stated that emergency service safety should not be reduced solely to
achieve a budget percentage. The full staffing option was selected by the Board. Chief Stanchina was
asked to identify other reductions to keep the combined Fire and Ambulance budget at or below
$1,485,474.79.

 Fire Department Budget Reductions: Chief Stanchina and the Board reviewed line-by-line reductions.

Training and Equipment: The Board discussed reducing fire training funds while preserving the ability to
train personnel, including potential EMT training for otherwise unqualified call firefighters. Chief
Stanchina proposed reducing the protective equipment or training line to approximately $13,000 while
maintaining core training capability. Chief Stanchina explained that firefighter courses were less
expensive than ambulance-related courses.

Technology Purchases: Chief Stanchina proposed reducing telephone and professional service costs by
limiting the number of iPads purchased in the current budget and potentially funding the devices through
the Fire Inspection Revolving Account. The iPads are intended to support inspections, vehicle integration
with the dispatch CAD system and improved response time data. TA Tucker was asked to verify whether
the revolving account could legally cover the purchases or related service costs.

Fuel and Vehicle Use: The Board considered retaining approximately $15,310 for fuel because the current
usage and diesel price uncertainty made a lower amount unreliable. Chief Stanchina explained that
sending a pickup instead of a fire engine to accompany an ambulance could reduce fuel use, but the
change could limit access to fire equipment and would require further analysis of mileage, secondary calls
and operational risk.

Other Line Items: Reducing general station supplies, uniforms, and ambulance administrative expenses
was discussed. The ambulance administrative line had a $1,000 budget; only $36 had been spent. The

Health Programs: Eliminating or reducing new mental health and NFPA physical programs was

 Fire Truck and Ambulance Fleet Planning: Chief Stanchina presented replacement and consolidation
  options for fire apparatus and ambulances. The Board favored further evaluation rather than
  immediately pursuing a major new purchase. They requested additional information on resale,
  financing and timing.

Tanker Replacement: The 2009 tanker carries 3,000 gallons but no longer matches the Town’s staffing
model because it cannot reliably carry the available crew or all equipment needed for first response in
parts of Town. A new pumper-tanker with a four-to-six-person cab and integrated equipment storage was
estimated at approximately $1.4 million, with a potential four-year delivery period and payment generally
due when delivered.

Used Vehicle Option: Chief Stanchina told the Board that a three-year-old pumper-tanker from another
New Hampshire community may be available for between $650,000 and $700,000. Chief Stanchina had
not completed the research and was asked by the Board to determine whether the seller would hold the
vehicle until the Town could address warrant article and financing requirements.

Fleet Consideration: Chief Stanchina proposed selling Engine 2 and Tanker 1, and purchasing one vehicle
that better matches current operations. Estimated resale values were between $280,000 and $310,000
for the tanker; $320,000 to $385,000 for Engine 2; and $80,000 and $100,000 for another engine under
consideration. TA Tucker noted that sale proceeds could not simply be assumed in a warrant article and
may require separate handling before replacement vehicles are available.

Future Procurement: Chief Stanchina suggested that a 2027 project could include a department working
group, a formal specification, an RFP, and earlier pricing. The Board agreed that additional information
was needed before committing to a purchase.

Ambulance Reserve: Two ambulances are used on a rotating basis to extend the newer vehicle’s service
life and preserve backup coverage for maintenance, special events, and overlapping calls. The Ambulance
Revolving Fund was projected to receive approximately $100,000 to $120,000 annually. Delaying
replacement of the other ambulance could allow between $900,000 to $1 million to accumulate before
the newer unit reached its projected replacement point.

Lease Payment Funding: Chief Stanchina stated that the Ambulance Revolving Fund could support the
fire truck lease payment for the current year. He cautioned against using the account to cover that cost
indefinitely because the payment would consume essentially all annual revenue.

MOTION: Z. Pike moved to forward the combined Fire and Ambulance budget at an amount not to exceed
$1,458,474.79 (6.8%). Second – L. Malloy; all in favor. MOTION CARRIED

Uniform Funding: Chief Bertogli explained that the department expects to outfit at least one new
employee and potentially a second. He noted that aging jackets and related equipment would need to be
replaced. Although the proposed uniform reductions were considered manageable, Chief Bertogli noted
that the timing of hiring could require additional funding next year.

Taser and Camera Costs: The Board supported shifting or restructuring taser and body camera payments
to reduce the five year cost from approximately $50,000 to around $33,000 while maintaining the
program. The change was discussed as a way to reduce the current tax impact while continuing to fund
future replacement needs.

Fuel Budget: Chief Bertogli explained that current fuel spending understated normal operations because
staffing and vehicle use had been reduced for part of the year. The fuel amount was approximately
$22,226.40 based on projected gallons and state pump pricing; exposure to further price increases was
discussed.

Future Office Request: Chief Bertogli stated the department was compiling data to support a future
request for a tenth officer. The position was intended to improve patrol coverage, vacation coverage, and
staffing. The Board requested comparative information from surrounding Seacoast and Rockingham
County communities as well as the expected personnel and equipment cost.

MOTION: Z. Pike moved to forward the Police Department budget to the Budget Committee in the amount
of $1,316,033.25. Second – H. Droesch; all in favor. MOTION CARRIED

 Road Maintenance Funding: Road conditions, prior spending, use of Capital Reserve Funds, and fuel
  price pressures were reviewed. Two highway line items were increased by a combined $60,000 to
  avoid reducing road work below the prior year’s level.

Road Conditions: TA Tucker and the Board discussed two roads that were considered severe enough to
require substantial attention; other roads were deteriorating but not at the same level. Bayside Road was
identified for reclamation and paving; Church Lane remained subject to property issues; Moulton Avenue
and Eagle Court were identified as additional problem areas.

Prior Spending: Approximately $400,000 was previously spent from the highway line and another
$100,000 for patching, crack sealing, repairs and striping. $350,000 was transferred from the Unreserved
Fund Balance for roads, making comparisons difficult because it was not part of the normal recurring
budget.

Capital Reserve Use: It was noted that Road Capital Reserve Funds are intended for significant repairs and
emergencies, including unexpected failures (example: Alden Avenue). The Board agreed that reserves
should not be reduced too far: emergency road work and winter related costs remain unpredictable.

MOTION: Z. Pike moved to increase line 01.4312.492 (Road Maintenance) to $100,000 and line
01.4319.001 (Other Highway, Streets & Bridges) to $400,000, a total increase of $60,000. Second – H.
Droesch; all in favor. MOTION CARRIED

Funding Increase: The discussion linked part of the funding strategy to potentially using the Fire and
Ambulance Revolving Fund for the fire truck lease payment, freeing general budget capacity for roads.
