 I. APPOINTMENT

1. Cemetery Trustees

The Fred Dobler, on behalf of the Cemetery Trustees, presented the Board of Selectmen with three bids
for their proposed expansion project.

Expansion Scope: F. Dobler explained that the expansion would include grading the property adjacent to
the Transfer Station by removing approximately 10 feet of gravel, adding loam, and seeding the area. The
initial site work is expected to take one year, with fencing, irrigation, landscaping and columbariums
addressed over the following two or three years.

Bid Comparison: Three quotes were obtained from Severino, Knipstein, and Bourassa. The most favorable
quote was received from Bourassa. They offered up to 800 tons of gravel toward the expansion and
possible help with roadway work. The Trustees cited Bourassa’s prior connection to the Cemetery as an
additional consideration for the offer.

Site and Stormwater Review: The Board questioned whether lowering the elevated gravel area would
affect the runoff or require a stormwater review for the MS4. It was noted that the finished area would
remain seeded rather than paved and runoff may not materially change; the existing survey and any
applicable stormwater requirements will be reviewed.

Work Conditions: Before equipment arrives on the site, the Board of Selectmen want to have the
contractor’s schedule, access route, equipment staging area, cleanup responsibilities, notice
requirements, working hours, and safeguards for continued access to Krasko Field and nearby facilities
documented. F. Dobler will coordinate with Bourassa and report back to TA Tucker. The Board suggested
outside guidance could be used to help draft those terms.

 III. OLD BUSINESS

1. Road Program Update

TA Tucker informed the Board that the Alden Avenue reclaim and paving was delayed by rain and
contractor scheduling; the tentative start date is the week of October 19th, weather permitting.
Notification letters were sent to businesses in Autumn Pond Park. The patch on Waters Edge should
follow directly afterward.

Indus finished the road up to the Transfer Station with a heavy sealer treatment intended to hold it
together and avoid reclamation next year. Additional work was done near the Police Department. A
different contractor will address parking lot areas at the Police and Fire Departments.

The Town normally spends $450,000 to $550,000 a year on roads but has fallen behind. Several roads will
need reclamation in 2027. The Board was asked to consider a preventative maintenance program which
would require drawing down the Road Capital Reserve Fund or a bond. TA Tucker advised the Board to
be ready to decide before the budget is finalized at the meeting on Monday, November 02nd.

2. Other Old Business

There was no ‘Other Old Business’ to discuss.

 IV. NEW BUSINESS AND ADMINISTRATIVE

1. Mailbox Winter Maintenance Policy

The Board reviewed a draft of the proposed Mailbox Winter Maintenance Policy which has been reviewed
by Steve Whitley, Town Attorney, and Justin Gamester, Piscataqua Landscaping. If approved by the Board,
there will be a public hearing on Monday, November 02nd.

Policy Purpose: The proposed policy would put winter mailbox maintenance practices in writing even
though the Town is not legally required to repair or reimburse damaged mailboxes.

Reimbursement Options: A range of possible approaches was discussed, including no reimbursement to
a limited amount based on the cost of standard materials. The proposed policy is intended to avoid paying
for unusually expensive or customized installations, such as granite mailbox posts.

Temporary Mailboxes: Piscataqua Landscaping kept several standard mailbox and post assemblies
available that could be installed temporarily after damage. That allows the resident to maintain mail
service until permanent repairs could be made in warmer conditions.

Expected Volume: The estimated number of affected mailboxes is 10 in a typical year and was closer to
20 last winter due to the number of storms. It was noted that damage can result from direct contact with
snowplows or from snow being pushed against mailboxes.

Next Steps: There is a specific reporting period included in the proposed policy. The Board questioned if
a reimbursement policy could create unintended claims. Clarification will be made prior to the public
hearing on Monday, November 02nd. The Board was asked to submit questions or recommendation for
revisions before that meeting.

VII. BUDGET REVIEWS

1. Library

Adam Schlager presented a more detailed 2027 Library budget that breaks the former repairs and
maintenance line (reduced from $8,500 to approximately $2,500) into contracted-service lines such as
HVAC, elevator inspections, copier, and security. Personnel increases reflect a 3% cost-of-living
adjustment and additional weekly hours for two staff members, driven by attendance growth to 700
people a month for programs and services. Other items included a new magazine vendor, adult
programming exceeding its allocation, in-house promotional items, re-barcoding about 1,400 items after
forming a lending cooperative with Lane Memorial (Hampton) and Wiggin Memorial (Stratham) libraries,
replacing two failing 13-year-old children’s learning stations with lower-cost alternatives, and e-resources
rising about 33% because of digital licensing costs. Board members noted the newly itemized contracted
services net to an increase of about $7,350 and asked the library to provide prior-year actual invoices
(including HVAC) and identify reasonable reductions, with a preference for keeping department increases
closer to 5% than 10%. The overall library increase was approximately 9%.

2. Fire Department

Chief Stanchina presented a reorganized 2027 budget, his first for the department, that moves expenses
out of generic lines and adds recurring costs not previously budgeted (callback pay, off-shift training, full-
time holiday hours, comp time, and potential advanced-EMT pay increases).

Ambulance Maintenance: Ambulance mechanical maintenance was moved from the fire budget into the
ambulance budget. The ambulance repair line includes the renewal and expansion of the Stryker
preventative-maintenance contract in the amount of $8,497.10 for cardiac monitors, mechanical CPR
equipment, stretchers and related devices. This included three items that were not previously been
covered.

Training and Compensation: The proposed training increase supports two initial EMT trainees, two
advanced EMT trainees, and reestablishing CPR/AED instructor capacity. The proposed wage increase is
3%. Callback pay, off-shift training, full-time holiday hours, comp time usage, and potential advanced-
EMT certification increases are not explicitly budgeted rather than absorbed informally.

Personnel Structure: The department currently has eight full-time employees excluding the chief and
approximately 17 part-time employees, with part-time staffing fluctuating. Callback pay applies when off-
duty personnel are asked to return for serious or prolonged incidents, typically when a station would
otherwise be uncovered for about 20 minutes or more.

Overtime and Stipends: Overtime is anticipated to decrease by approximately $12,172 based on
operational calculations, with full-time callback and training costs remaining in overtime and part-time
costs assigned to straight-time categories when possible. The stipend line will decrease because the
former chief now serves as a part-time deputy employee; the remaining amount supports longevity
payments for three eligible employees.

Mobile Data Devices: The department plans to replace outdated vehicle iPads and equip seven vehicles,
including ambulances, engines, a tanker, utility vehicle and command vehicle. The devices support
mapping, situational awareness, incident and patient reports. They will also have access to the sheriff’s
computer-aided dispatch system, allowing crews to see call notes, updates statuses, reduce radio traffic
and improve incident-time data.

Radio Purchase: The Board authorized spending up to approximately $3,383 from the Fire and Ambulance
Revolving Fund to replace an outdated vehicle radio before the December 15th trade-in deadline. The
reduced cost auxiliary radio is approximately #3,381.61 before the $500 trade-in, reducing the earlier
quote of $6,312.46 and reducing the proposed communications budget.

Vehicle and Equipment Standards: The budget follows annual testing requirements identified by NFPA
and ISO rather than the previous practice of testing some equipment every other year. It also plans for
eight turn-out gear sets annually to ease future costs. Grants will be pursued for protective equipment
and vehicle replacement where possible.

Hydrants and Cisterns: The department proposed approximately $10,350 for dry hydrant and cistern
maintenance, inspections and investigation of out-of-service infrastructure. Chief Stanchina said a full
condition assessment will begin after the winter. That assessment may indicate that one or two dry
hydrants should eventually be replaced with cisterns, which could cost more than $100,000 each and
require capital planning.

Uniform Program: The proposed uniform increase completes a multi-year effort to provide adequate
clothing and footwear rather than requiring employees to purchase items themselves. The preferred
process uses a department approved online uniform shop with individual spending limits.

Medical Wellness: A new medical expense line includes pre-employment physicals and a proposed annual
confidential mental-health wellness visit for each department member, plus additional visits for major life
events or critical incidents. The stated rate is $175 per routine visit and $200 for a critical incident visit.

Physical Screening Options: Chief Stanchina presented three levels of annual medical screening: a
comprehensive package including an NFPA physical, fitness assessment, and mobile comprehensive
ultrasound; a lower cost option without fitness assessments is available at a cost of $23,908; and an NFPA
physical only option would cost $14,548. The state cancer screening program does not replace the
complete physical and fitness assessment. The current department practice generally provides the NFPA
physical only at hiring.

Fire Budget Reduction and Capital Strategy: Chief Stanchina was asked to provide clearer options for

Reduction Target: Board members indicated that department increases should be closer to 5% than 10%

Revolving Fund Use: The Board discussed using the Fire and Ambulance Revolving Fund for limited vehicle

weakening long-term reserves.

Hydrant Funding: Members questioned whether the full $10,000 hydrant and cistern allocation was
needed before the department completed its condition assessment. Chief Stanchina stated the amount
could potentially be reduced by a few thousand dollars but recommended retaining most of it because
maintenance needs and possible equipment access costs are not yet known.

Capital Reserve Revenue: The Fire Inspection Revolving Account was reported at approximately
$32,542.41, with projected annual revenue of approximately $13,350. The balance in the Ambulance
Revolving Fund is $618,664.60 and is projected to receive roughly $120,000 annually. Chief Stanchina
cautioned that recurring deposits would not support using $128,000 every year for fire vehicle
replacement.

Future Vehicle Replacement: The Planning Board’s Capital Improvement Plan identified $1.4 million for a
tanker replacement and $480,000 for an ambulance replacement. Custom fire vehicles can take roughly
three to four years to build; Chief Stanchina will discuss further with the Board at the next meeting.

Financing Structure: Chief Stanchina will email the Board alternative options and the effect each choice
will have on individual line items and total percentages. The Board will review those materials before
deciding which components to retain, reduce or phase in.
