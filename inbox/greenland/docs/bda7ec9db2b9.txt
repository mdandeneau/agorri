 III. OLD BUSINESS

1. Alden Avenue Update

Alden Avenue and Waters Edge: A complete reclaim and paving is scheduled during the week of October
12th, weather permitting. Notices have been sent to businesses on Alden Avenue. The Town expects to
patch Waters Edge after Alden Avenue to extend the life of the severely damaged lower-traffic section.

Road Prioritization: It was noted that several roads are in poor condition and patching some of those
roads would waste funds when reclamation was imminent. Officials plan to walk the roads and establish
priorities for next year using the existing priority list and available funding.

Preventative Treatments: The Board discussed several preventative treatments including micro surfacing,
crack sealing, and milling to reduce moisture damage and potentially extend road life by eight to ten years.
Roads with alligator cracking require more substantial work than basic crack sealing.

Funding Strategy: Approximately $550,000 has been spent on roads this year, which includes $350,000
from the end-of-year reserve balance. Similar funding may not be available next year. The Board will
have a more detailed discussion of the amount needed, including through possible warrant articles, and
whether other financing options should be considered.

2. Other Old Business

There was no ‘Other Old Business’ to discuss.

 IV. NEW BUSINESS AND ADMINISTRATIVE

1. MS-535: Financial Report of the Board

TA Tucker explained that the annual MS-535 can only be completed after the audit was finalized. All
required signatures must be obtained and the form uploaded to the DRA portal before the tax rate can
be set. The auditor expects the 2025 audit to be completed before the end of the year; there are two
additional levels of review. TA Tucker informed the Board that the MS-4 deadline for stormwater
requirements had been met.

2. Tax Rate Setting

The tax rate is typically set in mid-October. The Board of Selectmen agreed to remain available for a last
minute meeting when DRA calls TA Tucker with the tax rate.

3. Other New Business

Speed Control: There was a brief discussion about the best way to control speed in Town. The availability
and cost of temporary rubber speed bumps will be researched.

Chicken Ordinance: S. Toth asked the Town revisit the possibility of a Chicken Ordinance so locally
collected fines could remain in Greenland. Complaints about chickens roaming neighboring properties
was discussed. It was noted that a local ordinance could not mirror or conflict with state law. The relevant
statutory provisions and RSA language must be reviewed before the proposal could be advanced.

 VII. BUDGET REVIEWS

➢ Police Department: Chief Bertogli presented a proposed budget increase of approximately $127,000.
  The increase was primarily due to new body-worn cameras, tasers, training, and a new technology
  contract. Chief Bertogli also requested adjustments for staffing, overtime coverage, cybersecurity,
  vehicles, fuel and uniforms.

Wages and Overtime: Chief Bertogli proposed a 3% wage increase for full-time and part-time staff, which
is consistent with regional cost-of-living increases ranging from approximately 2.8% to 3.5%. Overtime
coverage was increased from 25% to 35% to support double coverage as staffing returns to full strength
and officers face increased call demands.

Recruitment: The budget includes a $10,000 sign-on bonus if the anticipated hire is not completed by the
end of December. One position has been filled and another candidate was being pursued.

Technology and Cybersecurity: Approximately $24,834 was included for the Police Department to change
to NESSIT for server management, cybersecurity monitoring, email protection and related support.

Continuous monitoring and incident response will be provided as well as the ability to isolate
compromised accounts. Chief Bertogli will confirm whether Microsoft 365, Barracuda and other
subscriptions remain separate costs.

Contract Classification: The $54,000 contract for tasers, body worn cameras, training, and supplies will
be a separate line item so the public can understand the cost. TA Tucker will contact MRI regarding a line
item number.

Additional Operating Costs: Vehicle maintenance had a modest increase. Fuel was budgeted at
approximately 440 gallons per month; the price increase was from $3.40 to $3.80 per gallon. Office
equipment decreased due to tables and chairs being purchased this year.

Willow: The Board questioned Willow’s retirement timeline. Chief Bertogli explained that support and
therapy dogs do not have a fixed retirement age. Service generally continues until the dog can no longer
perform the work. Willow is approaching seven years old, had a prior leg injury and surgery funded by
donations. She has remained active in school visits and was recently recertified for approximately two
more years. The budget for Willow was reduced by half after researching handler and support costs, and
averaging the request against comparable needs.

Additional Officer: Chief Bertogli proposed adding one full-time officer, bringing the department to 10
full-time employees (including the Chief) which would improve the patrol element and scheduling. The
last position filled was in 2017; the School Resource Officer was added in 2020 and is primarily assigned
to the School. The estimated annual cost of an additional officer is approximately $70,000 to $74,000 plus
benefits, with the potential to reduce overtime costs.

Union Question: A question was asked about whether 10 employees would trigger collective bargaining.
Chief Bertogli explained that supervisory positions with disciplinary authority would be excluded from the
union. The Board agreed the department would remain below the threshold and that bargaining-unit
status would require a separate process.

➢ General Government: TA Tucker reviewed general government, which includes elections, financial
  administration, insurance, and other costs. She recommended continuing MRI support for financial
  operations.

Administrative Staffing: Wages increased and include a larger adjustment for administrative finance
responsibilities and the bridge between the Town and MRI. $10,000 was allocated for scanning and
digitizing documents on a part-time basis.

MRI Financial Support: TA Tucker recommended continuing with MRI for accounts payable, payroll,
budgeting, and financial guidance. MRI also works directly with the auditor, provides auditor access and
supports the audit on site. The combined cost for accounts payable, payroll and their annual fee is
approximately $92,300. The Board acknowledged that the cost was less than hiring a full-time finance
manager.

Actuals and Estimates: TA Tucker stated that the 2026 actual figures were incomplete. Transactions were
posted through September 20th; the lower actuals did not necessarily indicate underspending. Insurance
figures were estimated pending updated information.

Treasurer Compensation: The proposed Treasurer’s stipend was questioned if it aligned with comparable
towns. TA Tucker will contact surrounding communities with similar workloads and populations to
compare stipends and mileage practices before the amount is finalized.

Software and Supplies: The General Government budget includes numerous software and subscription
costs, with monthly technology expenses ranging from $5,000 to $6,000. A higher estimate was included
to avoid a shortfall.

Records Preservation: Greenland has been notified that the Town received Congressional Directed
Spending funds for digitizing roughly 40 years of historic books. However, funding is on hold due to the
federal budget not being fully passed. If funds are received, the program could cover the estimated
$160,000 preservation project; the amount requested by the Town Clerk for records preservation remains
in the budget.

Elections: The Town Clerk purchased additional VotingWorks ballot equipment and poll pads using
available election funds. The 2027 budget includes reprogramming and maintenance costs for the
expanded equipment inventory.

Potential Special Election: Two elections are included in next year’s budget: the regular Town election
and a possible special election for a new fire station. It was clarified that the fire station vote was unlikely
to be ready for the March election and could occur later in the year, depending on community feedback
and project timing.

Building Inspector: The current Building Inspector is budgeted for approximately 25 hours per week and
continues with monthly training. The additional Building Inspector will remain on the books through
December for limited backup coverage. The budget reflects the transition from the higher hourly rate to
the current Inspector’s regular schedule.

Cemetery Operations: The Cemetery budget declined significantly due to an allocation of $48,000 that
covered major landscaping in 2025. TA Tucker explained that the proposed future expansion of the
cemetery is expected to use funds from the Capital Reserve Fund. The Cemetery Trustees are expected
to present updated quotes for fencing, landscaping and irrigation at an upcoming Board of Selectmen
meeting.

Solid Waste: Costs were managed below prior estimates, including a $7,000 savings because brush
chipping was not needed this year; chipping is expected in 2027. The recent Household Hazardous Waste
event in Exeter had 37 participants from Greenland, which is comparable to participation in Portsmouth’s
event. The estimated cost of the new process was $3,400 rather than the $17,000 (plus) cost of
participating with Portsmouth.

Transfer Station Building: The water intrusion in a shed near the trash compactor at the Transfer Station
was discussed. A concrete coating functions as a vapor barrier; a dehumidifier and pump are in place.

Health and Welfare: Health administration costs were estimated below $30,000 because fewer local
testing events occurred; costs could increase if mosquito or other testing is required. Welfare spending
remains at $500 and is handled on a case by case basis. Portsmouth handles welfare for Greenland.

Recreation and Library: Recreation uses revolving accounts for program fees and advertising; no major
project has been identified. The pickleball court condition has not yet been evaluated. The Library will
present its budget at the meeting on Monday, October 19th.

Patriotic Flag Replacement: The Patriotic line remains at $1,000 for replacement flags and repairs. Recent
storms damaged some flag brackets and several Town of Greenland flags. TA Tucker explained that some
of the Town of Greenland flags were too short and had been secured with temporary ties and were
damaged during inclement weather.

Field Maintenance RFP: The Town has not yet issued an RFP for field cleanup that was brought to the
Board’s attention by the Conservation Commission. The work may be proposed through a warrant article
or in the Conservation Commission’s budget. The appropriate funding route will be determined after the
RFP process begins.

Next Budget Reviews: The Library and Fire Department will review their budgets at the next Selectmen’s
meeting on Monday, October 19th.

VIII. OTHER BUSINESS

1. Acceptance of Board of Selectmen Resignation

S. Toth has submitted his resignation from the Board of Selectmen. He will be relocating to the Boston
area. The Board reluctantly accepted his resignation.

MOTION: Z. Pike reluctantly moved to accept the resignation of Stephan Toth from the Board of
Selectmen, at the conclusion of tonight’s meeting. Second – H. Droesch; all in favor. MOTION CARRIED

2. Appointment of Selectman

The Board had met Ted Irving, Barrett Farm Road, on several occasions and felt he would be a good fit.
Dennis Malloy, Van Etten Drive, suggested making the appointment conditional on the appointee running
for the position in March. Board members declined to mandate that, noting it would not be enforceable
and hoped that he would choose to run.

MOTION: H. Droesch moved to appoint Ted Irving as a Selectman to fill the vacancy created by Stephan
Toth’s resignation, his term to expire in March 2027. Second – Z. Pike; all in favor. MOTION CARRIED

T. Irving will need to be sworn in by the Town Clerk before the next meeting on Monday, October 19th.

3. Election Day Scheduling

Election Day is Tuesday, November 03rd. Selectmen are required to be present; please contact the Town
Clerk with your schedule. Set up will be Monday, November 02nd after 3:00 p.m.
