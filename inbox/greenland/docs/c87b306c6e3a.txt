 2. Continued
    Conditional Use Permit
    1575 Greenland Road (Map R20, Lot 13: Commercial District B, RCIM District)
    Owner/Applicant: 1575 Greenland Road LLC
    A Conditional Use Permit is required in the RCIM District to allow the Motor Vehicle Sales/Leasing
    use and work in the Wetland Protection Zone at the Consolidated Communications site.

Attorney John Bosen (Donahue, Tucker & Ciandella), representing the applicant, addressed the Board.
Also present were members of their development team: Neil Hansen (Project Engineer, Tighe & Bond),
Tom Dennis (Tighe & Bond) Bryce Johnson (Architect), Mark Williams (General Manager, BMW Stratham),
Brad Johnson (Group 1 Automotive), and Kelsey Cross (1575 Greenland Road).

Proposed Redevelopment: The applicant was proposing to replace the approximately 50,000 square foot
former Consolidated Communications building on an 8.5-acre commercial site with an approximately
46,000 square foot BMW dealership. The project was characterized as an adaptive redevelopment of an
existing developed property. The redeveloped property will include approximately 50% open space,
upgraded landscaping, reduced impervious surface, improved stormwater controls, employment
opportunities, tax-base growth and no anticipated demand for additional students in the school system.

Conditional Use Criteria: Attorney Bosen stated that the proposal satisfies the RCIM criteria because it
supports district goals, concentrates commercial development along an established transportation
corridor using existing infrastructure, provides a positive fiscal impact, and is suitable for the site. The
Board drew a distinction between the Conditional Use Permit decision, which determines whether a
dealership may operate in the zone, and the subsequent site plan review. It was noted by the Board that

and other site-specific effects.

Operational Questions: Operating hours are generally 7:00 am to 7:00 pm, Monday through Thursday;
7:00 am to 6:00 pm, Friday and Saturday; and closed on Sunday. Service bay doors will open briefly and
close automatically. Noisy work and vehicle wash operations would occur inside the building. Vehicle
carriers will unload on the site rather than on the adjacent road. There is no intercom at the dealership.

Stormwater and Wetland Controls: Neil Hansen, Tighe & Bond, explained the proposed stormwater
treatment, detention and wetlands protections, include the removal of impervious surface from the 25-
foot buffer and maintenance requirements for the treatment system.

Treatment System: Stormwater will be collected through site catch basins and routed through
underground filtration units before entering the detention system. N. Hansen described the filtration as
functioning similarly to a large Brita filter, with catch basin hoods and sediment settling providing
additional oil, sediment and pollutant control.

Detention Capacity: The detention system will contain approximately 90 concrete chambers, each
approximately 8 feet by 14 feet, and 5 feet high. The system will hold treated stormwater and release it
gradually through a weir with progressively larger openings during larger storm events, reducing peak
discharge to the surrounding wetlands.

Site Drainage Changes: The existing site, which is relatively flat, will allow runoff to sheet-flow towards
wetlands on both sides. Runoff will be collected across the site and directed toward the front of the
property. This will require greater storage capacity while producing a lower discharge rate than existing
conditions.

Maintenance Requirements: The proposed project includes an operations and maintenance program.
The filtration units are expected to be inspected quarterly during the first year and twice annually
thereafter, with cleaning or replacement as needed. Observation ports will allow inspection of sediment
buildup, which can be removed by vacuum truck along with sediment from catch basins.

Wetland Buffer: All impervious surface will be removed from the first 25 feet of the wetland buffer. The
Board questioned whether the parking area could be pulled back farther; N. Hansen explained that the
layout had been tightened to balance the buffer protection with the inventory storage requirements of
the dealership.

Abutter Concerns: Keegan Pafford, 405 Portsmouth Avenue, and Debbie Ludington, 407 Portsmouth
Avenue, abutters, questioned how vehicle storage, service operations, lighting, noise, traffic, and
landscaping would avoid detrimental impacts on nearby residences. Board members explained those
concerns would be addressed in greater detail during the Site Plan Review process. The applicant assured
the Board that they were committed to working with the Board and the abutters regarding screening,
lighting, landscaping, and related protections. D. Ludington stated that the language in Section 28.3
(Conditional Use Permit (CUP) Criteria) needed to be clarified. She felt that section could be clearer for
the impacts on residential abutting property.

Conditional Use Permit Decision: After public comment and Board discussion, the Conditional Use Permit
for the BMW dealership within the RCIM District was approved.

       3. New
       Site Plan Review
       1575 Greenland Road (Map R20, Lot 13: Commercial District B, RCIM District)
       Owner/Applicant: 1575 Greenland Road LLC
       The owner/applicant is proposing to redevelop the existing commercial property at the
       Consolidated Communications site for a Motor Vehicle Sales/Leasing dealership. A 46,000 square
       foot modernized building footprint and open space of 50% on the existing site is proposed.

The Board continued discussion of the proposed project for 1575 Greenland Road.

Incomplete Submission: Altus Engineering’s letter of November 17, 2026, stated that the materials
provided were insufficient to confirm a complete application. It was noted that Altus Engineering had
received only the drainage report and site plan rather than a full submission package. A complete package
will be provided to Altus Engineering.

Abutter Screening: The Board noted that a clearer plan showing the location of nearby residences was
needed and should show how the building, parking, service doors, lighting, fencing and landscaping would
affect the abutters. The project team will provide additional information and stated that fencing along
the western and southern property lines could supplement landscaping.

Buffer and Landscaping: Members of the Board stated that a portion of the parking area appeared to
extend approximately two feet or three feet into the required 25-foot buffer. The applicant will need to
revise the layout or otherwise address the encroachment. Additional screening will need to be provided,
including trees along Greenland Road and Alden Avenue consistent with the applicable planting
requirements.

Traffic and Emergency Access: This project will require a NHDOT permit. The traffic study that was
submitted indicated that approximately 80% of traffic is expected to turn right from the site, while 20%
will turn left. The Board requested detailed turning-movement plans showing that fire trucks and vehicle
carriers can circulate on site and unload internally. A traffic engineer will be at the next public hearing.

Utilities and Drainage Infrastructure: The project will require redesign and reconstruction of the existing
septic system and approval from the NH Department of Environmental Services. An Alteration of Terrain
permit will be needed for stormwater work. The culvert under Alden Avenue has lost its bottom and will
need replacement; they will coordinate with the Town regarding related road repairs.

Architectural Approach: Bryce Johnson, project architect, presented the architectural approach and
explained how the design would balance BMW’s branding preferences with the Town’s requirements.
BMW prefers a modern, largely gray and white building with substantial glass and metal. The Town’s
standards require roof articulation, varied massing, and materials that reflect natural materials. The
submitted design uses stepped and varied facades, masonry, wood-like materials and vertical board and
batten style elements rather than corrugated metal. The Board reviewed requirements allowing wood,
stone, brick, architectural concrete masonry, and metal products that convincingly simulate natural
materials, excluding cheap plastic-like finishes. The front showroom will use high-performance glazing.
The final design would verify compliance with the minimum glazing requirement. The Board’s preference
was the design submitted by the architect.

Lighting Controls: Exterior lighting will be shielded and directed downward, remain within the property
boundaries, and avoid wall-washing. Low-level security lighting will be used after hours. The photometric
details will be reviewed further during the Site Plan Review.

Mechanical Equipment: Rooftop HVAC equipment is planned behind a tall parapet or upper building mass
to reduce visibility from surrounding roads and properties. Visibility studies will be conducted and
screening will be provided if any equipment remains visible.

Service Noise: Compressors, fluids, service activity, and the car wash tunnel will be enclosed with the
building; service doors will be used intermittently and designed to close quickly. No body shop painting
is planned.

Vehicle Wash and Environmental Operations: The car wash will use a reclaim tank to recirculate
approximately 80% of its water. Remaining wastewater will be captured in a holding tank and pumped
out as needed, generally every three weeks. It will not be discharged to the stormwater system or septic
system.

Fuel Tank Removal: The underground fuel tank shown on the existing plan is not part of the proposed
operation and will be removed.

EV Charging: Electric vehicle charging stations are planned.

MOTION: F. Catapano moved to continue the Site Plan Review of 1575 Greenland Road (Map R20, Lot 13:
