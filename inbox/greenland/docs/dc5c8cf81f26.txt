 I. APPOINTMENTS

1. Conservation Commission

Thompson Brook Trail Funding: Kathleen Babin-Johnson, Conservation Commission Chairman, requested
that the Board of Selectmen authorize expending the remaining funds from the 2024 warrant article for
Thompson Brook Trail maintenance.

Remaining Balance: K. Babin-Johnson stated that the warrant article expired at the end of December
2026. The remaining balance in the warrant article was $1,382.29; Thompson Brook Trail proposed
maintenance expenses totaled $1,267.25. Planned uses: mowing of the top field ($450), mowing of the
main field ($750), and repair of the bridge ($67.25). The remaining balance will be approximately $115.
It was clarified that the motion was made to ‘not exceed’ the amount in the warrant article.

MOTION: Z. Pike moved to authorize the expenditure from the 2024 Conservation Commission warrant
article not to exceed $1,382.29 for Thompson Brook Trail maintenance. Second – H. Droesch; all in favor.
MOTION CARRIED

2. Winnicut River Project: Bob Dion, Chairman

B. Dion presented a summary of the Winnicut River Project community charrette, remaining Great Bay
2030 grant funds and their proposed next steps.

Project Outcomes: B. Dion explained that the project addressed traffic and safety concerns on Rt. 33,
pedestrian and bicycle access, conditions at Rolston Park, recreation access, and Winnicut River water
quality. The charrette on October 16th and 17th included Plan New Hampshire staff, Town officials, public

Remaining Grant Funds: The project received $75,000 from Great Bay 2030; the Town of Greenland

 III. OLD BUSINESS

1. Fire Department Vehicle Replacement: Chief Stanchina

Chief Stanchina proposed purchasing a three-year-old 2023 Rosenbauer Commander Pumper Tanker from
the City of Keene to replace and consolidate existing apparatus. Chief Stanchina distributed his proposal
to the Board.

Replacement Strategy: Chief Stanchina explained that the 2009 Mack tanker is aging and no longer meets
current staffing and response needs. The Rosenbauer Tanker would consolidate the 2016 Engine 2 and
the 2009 Tanker 1. The fleet would be reduced from three large apparatus to two, while replacing the
two aging vehicles with one combination apparatus.

Vehicle Sale Proceeds: Garage Technologies estimated that the vehicles could be sold for approximately
$618,000 to $718,000 which includes market values and suggested listing prices after applicable fees. The
proceeds would go to the General Fund and increase the Unreserved Fund Balance. Chief Stanchina noted
that the strategy could be plus or minus $50,000 depending on the actual sale and purchase price.

Proposed Replacement Vehicle: The 2023 Rosenbauer Commander Pumper Tanker is from the City of
Keene, New Hampshire. Chief Stanchina reviewed the original manufacturer specifications and drawings,
and confirmed that the vehicle would fit in the station. An in-person inspection would involve several

2. Annual Approval of Policies

There are four policies that the Board reviews and approves on an annual basis: Fraud Prevention and
Fiscal Management, Fund Balance, Investment Policy – Town Treasurer, and Indemnification Policy. TA
Tucker found no material changes last November. It was noted that the Indemnification Policy dates back
to 1976.

MOTION: Z. Pike moved to approve the policies as presented by the Town Administrator. Second – H.
Droesch; all in favor. MOTION CARRIED

3. Other New Business

• Police Department Replacement Vehicle: Chief Bertogli requested the Board authorize the use of
Police Department Special Detail funds to replace a department vehicle that is approximately 10-years
old. Chief Bertogli will research grants for a new radio unit. It was noted that there is over $100,000 in
the Special Detail fund.

Vehicle Condition: Chief Bertogli reported that the vehicle used primarily for details remained in the fleet
for an additional year but was reaching the end of its useful life. Continued repairs were expected to
become costly.

Replacement Cost: The estimated cost for a replacement vehicle and full outfitting was between $65,000
and $70,000. The vehicle would be purchased through the state bid process; the final outfitting cost was
unknown. The Board established a spending threshold rather than approve a specific vehicle.

MOTION: P. Stanley moved to authorize Chief Bertogli to spend up to $80,000 to replace the department’s
roughly 10-year old vehicle used mainly for details. Second – Z. Pike; all in favor. MOTION CARRIED

 VII. BUDGET REVIEW

1. Budget Committee’s Recommendations

TA Tucker stated that the Police Department, based on Budget Committee recommendations, increased
$3,000. Roads were cut by $150,000 and the Mosquito Control line was reduced to $0. TA Tucker also
informed the Board that MRI inadvertently had not included the line for the Trustees of Trust Fund
stipend, which was $1,500; L. Malloy abstained from this discussion. TA Tucker noted that she had found
two other lines that were left off and has requested that MRI review everything to make sure nothing else
was missed.

MOTION: Z. Pike moved to approve the Town budget in the amount of $8,164,650 and move to public
hearing on Tuesday, January 12, 2027. Second – H. Droesch; all in favor. MOTION CARRIED

Budget Process: There was a discussion about continuing the year-round, incremental review process
with more data distributed earlier. It was noted that reorganizing the accounting lines into more accurate
categories improved transparency. The additional cleanup of budget classifications and capital
improvement planning would continue.

Upcoming Budget Work: It was noted that the School budget session would be held on Saturday,
December 05th, at 8:30 am in the Town Hall Conference Room. Z. Pike, the Selectmen’s Rep on the Budget
Committee, requested an alternate from the Board of Selectmen be appointed to that meeting. The Board
was reminded that warrant articles will be discussed at the meeting on Monday, November 30th.

Comments: Members of the Board and those in attendance at this meeting commented that this was the
best budget process yet. Department heads were prepared and did a great job. The Town Administrator
and Jarrod, techie in residence, did a phenomenal job putting the budget and presentations together; the
visual spreadsheet allowing everyone to see how changes affected the bottom line was very helpful.
