 III. PUBLIC MEETING

1. Adoption of Infrastructure/Roadway Moratorium Policy

The Infrastructure/Roadway Moratorium Policy restricts excavation or pavement disturbance on newly
paved Town-owned roads for three years, with exceptions for emergencies (example: water main or utility
emergencies). The intent is to prevent premature road failure caused by cuts that let ice and water into
the pavement. Restoration costs are not set at a fixed escrow amount; applicants would pay “reasonable
costs” that are determined with engineering support and that amount can be adjusted over time. This
policy will apply to Town-owned roads only; State-owned roads have a separate State escrow program.
The Town Attorney has reviewed the policy.

MOTION: S. Toth moved to adopt the Infrastructure/Roadway Moratorium Policy, effective immediately.
Second – H. Droesch; all in favor. MOTION CARRIED

2. Adoption of Administrative Policy – Use of AI

The Administrative Policy – Use of AI applies to employee use of AI. It prohibits entering sensitive or
confidential information into AI systems. There was a discussion about limiting employees to use only an
AI system provided by the Town rather than personal systems; however, no action was taken. It was
noted that AI tools and risks are changing quickly and the policy may need further revisions in the future.
The Town Attorney has reviewed the policy.

MOTION: S. Toth moved to adopt the Administrative Policy – Use of AI, effective immediately. Second –
Z. Pike; all in favor. MOTION CARRIED

 V. OLD BUSINESS

1. 2027 Budget Survey Results

The Board gave Jarrod Lord a ‘huge thank you’ for the amount of time spent on the 2027 Budget Survey
and its results. 415 responses were received through the website, in the mail, or dropped off at the Town
Office. Results are a non-statistical snapshot rather than a binding direction.

Survey Methodology: Approximately 1,700 surveys were mailed to households in Town. In addition,
surveys were available on the Town website and other forms of outreach. Of the 415 responses, 25%
were handwritten, which indicates that mailed information remains important for some residents. Survey
results are not statistically representative but provide an indication of current priorities for residents.

Data Preparation: J. Lord scanned the submitted responses and generated percentages and graphs. AI
was used to summarize the findings, including readable written comments verbatim (with punctuation
and capitalization).

Resident Understanding: Misconceptions were clarified by the Board: the Town does not own the Parish
House, the relative size of the Library funding, and the status of the Fire Department as full-time rather
than a volunteer department. The Board noted that the survey indicates where additional explanations
are needed, including the rationale for a new fire station and the consideration of regional service options.

 VI. NEW BUSINESS AND ADMINISTRATIVE

1. Seabrook Station Graded Exercise

The Board was informed that on Wednesday, September 23, there will be a Seabrook Station graded
exercise.

Resignation: The Board was also informed that the individual currently serving as the Emergency
Management Director and Health Officer will be resigning from both appointed positions due to starting
a new job. He has requested a prorated Health Officer stipend through September.

Interim Exercise Coverage: Chief Stanchina agreed to coordinate the graded exercise on Wednesday,
September 23rd, with assistance from the Town Administrator. Chief Stanchina and TA Tucker have met
with Homeland Security personnel; they indicated the exercise could proceed. The Board emphasized
that future exercise notices must be communicated promptly through the Town until a new Emergency
Management Director is appointed.

Exercise Requirements: The graded exercise assigns personnel to functions such as transportation and
radiation coordination, as well as coordination of fire, police, the school liaison, emergency operations
center, and notification acknowledgement. Selectmen are not individually required; however, every
operational role must be staffed. The Town typically conducts two practice exercises followed by the
graded exercise.

Role Distinctions: TA Tucker clarified that the Emergency Management Director coordinates the
emergency exercises and response functions. The Health Officer handles public health complaints such
as unsanitary conditions. The roles are separate even though one individual previously held both. The
Emergency Management Director stipend is $8,400 annually and is partially supported through Seabrook
funding; the Health Officer stipend is $1,800 annually and is funded through the Town budget.

2. Police Department Budget Item

Chief Bertogli, Police Department, proposed a five-year Axon contract that included body cameras,
evidence storage, redaction software, tasers and training upgrades. He noted that one year of the
contract would be paid in advance at a cost of $54,000; a price increase was expected in January.

Contract Costs: The proposed five-year package totals approximately $270,000, or $54,000 annually. The
cost in January is anticipated to increase to between $292,000 and $297,000. Due to staffing vacancies,
the first year can be funded from the existing budget.

Contract Flexibility: The Axon contract includes an escape clause that will allow the Town to discontinue
payments if voters do not fund future years; however, the vendor would retrieve the equipment. Body
cameras and evidence storage remain continuing operational needs. The Board discussed budgeting them
as recurring expenses rather than treating the contract as a one-time obligation.

Technology Upgrades: The Axon package includes newer tasers and training equipment that includes
virtual reality components, camera refreshes, expanded storage and improved redaction tools. The
upgraded redaction system can process specified blurring and audio-muting tasks in bulk rather than
requiring staff to watch hours of footage manually.

Translation Capabilities: The proposed camera system includes a translation module covering
approximately 56 languages. The Police Department has experienced increased interactions with people
who do not speak English. They have previously paid for telephone or external translators, at
approximately $300 per incident.

Payment Options: The Board discussed paying one or two years in advance. Axon does not offer a
discount for early payment. Z. Pike suggested that paying one year could possibly save some money to
help lower the tax rate. Future annual funding will be addressed through the budget, with the option of
a warrant article.

MOTION: Z. Pike moved to approve paying the Axon contract for the first year in advance in an amount
not to exceed $54,000. Second – S. Toth; all in favor. MOTION CARRIED

3. Police Department Grant

Chief Bertogli reviewed the Highway Safety Grant and speed trailer with the Board.

Grant Amendment: The original grant amount was $11,900 for additional traffic enforcement. The
amendment adds $15,941.25 toward a digital speed trailer, for a total of $27,841.25.

Local Match: The trailer costs approximately $22,000. The grant will cover 75% and the Town must
provide a 25% match ($5,313.75). There are sufficient funds in the Police Department budget for the
match.

MOTION by S. Toth: The Select Board, in a majority vote, accepted the terms of the Highway Safety Grant
as presented in the amount of $11,900 and the amended grant terms in the amount of $15,941.25 as
presented in the Grant Agreement Amendment Notice for an adjusted total grant limitation of
$27,841.25. The Town Administrator, Tara Tucker, is authorized to sign all documents related to the
NHOHS grants. Second – Z. Pike; all in favor. MOTION CARRIED

Documented Positions: The proposed job descriptions cover existing call, part-time and full-time
firefighter and EMS positions, as well as full-time lieutenant, full-time captain, and call Deputy Chief roles.
Chief Stanchina noted that no positions were created; these formalize the structure that is in place.

Job Description Content: Each description identifies duties, accountability, supervision, equipment,
workplace health and safety requirements, and many other department requirements and
responsibilities. The intent of the job descriptions is to support future policies for each employment
classification.

Staffing Recall: When a crew leaves Town for mutual aid and may be unavailable for a length of time, the
department can issue a staffing recall requesting that off-duty personnel return to man the station. Part-
time and call personnel may be eligible; call firefighters are generally expected to return when available;
part-time personnel respond when specifically requested.

Review Before Adoption: The Town Attorney has not reviewed the job descriptions. Town staff will
compare them with the existing personnel policy. Board members may submit questions to Chief
Stanchina or the Town Administrator before adoption.
