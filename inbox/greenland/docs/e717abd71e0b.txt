1. Town Center Overlay District

The Planning Board held an informational meeting to review the latest Town Center Overlay District
proposal. The eight residents that were present voiced concerns about density, notification, traffic,
setbacks, parking, environmental impacts, design controls, and district boundaries. Revisions were
suggested by residents and the Board answered questions. Among those residents present were: Debbie
Ludington (407 Portsmouth Avenue), the resident at 14 Pinewood Circle, Jean Eno (Shore Drive), Richard
Pimpis and Laurie Prince (517 Portsmouth Avenue), Peter Durant (31 Post Road), Susan Loveland (459
Portsmouth Avenue) and Robin Hughes (115 Tuttle Lane).

Overlay Purpose: Members of the Board explained that the intent is to support a village-oriented concept
with small lots, mixed residential and commercial uses, and more detailed architectural standards.
Property owners who do not use choose to use the overlay option remain subject to the existing
underlying zone.

Proposal Status: The Town Center Overlay District is still in the draft stages. If revisions are made based
on comments and concerns, another informational meeting would be held before any recommendation
is made by the Board to include the overlay district on the March ballot. It was noted by the Board that
there would not be a vote at this meeting to approve or reject the proposed overlay district.

Resident Concerns: Debbie Ludington (407 Portsmouth Avenue) objected to the reduction of the
minimum lot size from 60,000 square feet to 10,000 square feet, frontage from 200 feet to 70 feet, the
elimination of the open-space requirement, small setbacks, potential 36 foot to 38 foot building heights,
traffic impacts, and limited protection for nearby single-family homes. D. Ludington also commented that
the language should be clearer. Other residents raised concerns about preserving wooded views, the scale
of development, parking, smaller setbacks, and potential taller building heights. Jean Eno (Shore Drive)
suggested the Board may want to revisit the proposed map for the overlay district; H. Droesch responded
that shrinking the overlay district would defeat the purpose. Peter Durant (31 Post Road) told the Board

Opt-In Process: The Board clarified that opting into the overlay was voluntary; however, once an owner
or developer chooses to use their increased density or mixed-use provisions, the project must comply
with the design criteria and conditions of the overlay. A property owner may also propose a change with
retail on the bottom and residential space above; that would need to be reviewed by the Board and
subject to applicable public hearing and approval procedures.

Existing Regulations: The Board explained that the current Site Plan Regulations include detailed
requirements for parking, landscaping, lighting and related protections; the proposed Town Center
Overlay District does not repeat every existing rule. Applicants must comply with those requirements;
the Board may impost project specific conditions regarding the neighbors, access, wells, driveways,
mailboxes and connectivity as well as related impacts.

Design and Connectivity: Richard Pimpis, 517 Portsmouth Avenue, specifically requested stronger
standards for setbacks, parking, signage and lighting. The Board acknowledged that the overlay
information did not clearly include those issues, explaining that some are covered by existing Site Plan
Regulations. They agreed to consider clear requirements, including connecting sidewalks and avoid
isolated sidewalk segments as development occurs incrementally.

District Boundaries: The Board clarified that the proposed Town Center Overlay District is not the same
as the existing Residential-Commercial-Industrial-Mixed Use overlay (RCIM). The map has undergone
several revisions in response to public feedback and to make the boundaries more readable.

Environmental Review: Susan Loveland, 459 Portsmouth Avenue, questioned if the Conservation
Commission would review impacts to wildlife, wetlands and greenery. The Board responded that any
projects involving conservation related matters are referred to the Conservation Commission for written
feedback and is considered by the Planning Board during reviews.

Village Character: There was some support among residents of the overlay to enable small scale walkable
village services. It was explained that current 60,000 square foot lot requirements would not allow the
existing variety of lot sizes and building types in the village to be recreated. The overlay was intended to
preserve that through controlled design rather than permit unrestricted development.

Public Outreach: Residents requested Town-wide notification because changes could affect traffic,
schools, fire and police services, infrastructure and voters throughout Town. The Board noted that
approximately 70 property owners within the proposed district were notified; a Town-wide notification
would be cost-prohibitive. Residents were directed to the Town website, meeting agendas, minutes, and
social media.

Next Steps: No action was taken on the proposed overlay district by the Board. Comments will be retained
for further discussion. Revisions are possible based on concerns of residents.

3. Topics for the October Public Hearing

Applications for the public hearing on Thursday, October 15, 2026, include: Moulton Avenue
development, retail units at 1460 Greenland Road (pad next to Target), and the proposed BMW dealership
at the former Consolidated Communications Building located at 1575 Greenland Road.

Moulton Avenue Development: M. Fougere informed the Board that the Moulton Avenue project has
increased from 8 lots to 10 lots. Members questioned whether changes to the application constituted
the need for a new application requiring re-notification. The issue remained subject to further procedural
review by M. Fougere. It was noted that the Conservation Commission did a site walk with the engineer
and applicant. Their findings will be included in the packet for the public hearing. Also to be discussed
will be the traffic calming concern of an abutter.

BMW Dealership – 1575 Greenland Road: They will be before the Board with a Conditional Use Permit
only and plan to be back with the full plan set in November. The applicant will be going to the
Conservation Commission in November.

Retail Units – 1460 Greenland Road (Pad Adjacent to Target): They are proposing to divide the space into
two retail units, with Restaurant Depot in the larger side.

It was announced that the meeting will start at 6:00 pm rather than 6:30 pm.

6. Other Business

 M. Fougere reviewed the state mandated zoning amendments with the Board due recent legislation.

Childcare Requirements: Childcare facilities serving 30 or less children are to be allowed by right in
residential zones. In addition, childcare centers shall be allowed in the Industrial Zone within an existing
operation by right. The Table of Uses and Section 3.7.4 of the Zoning Ordinance will need to be amended
and on the March ballot.

Multi-Family Housing: Multi-family uses must be allowed by right in all commercial zones, including zones
without public water. The Board may require some or all of the ground floor to remain commercial. The
regulations must be amended and on the March ballot.

Open Space Conservation Subdivision: Section 26.3.3 of the Residential Open Space Conservation
Subdivision Development Zoning Ordinance must be amended to allow utilities and septic systems in open
space. Wells and drainage are currently allowed. The amendment will be on the March ballot.

Workforce Housing: M. Fougere stated that the existing workforce housing incentive may not be viable
and proposed revisions to better satisfy the statutory requirement that municipal regulations not
unreasonably hinder workforce housing. There was a brief discussion that workforce housing can involve
multi-family development and should be addressed in both subdivision and multi-family provisions,
including commercial zones.

Road Length Limits: State law prohibits municipalities from limiting the length of subdivision roads or the
number of homes served by a road. Greenland’s current 1,000 foot maximum road length must be
removed by April 2027, with road design and safety controlled through applicable NFPA requirements and
other existing standards. This is a Subdivision Regulation and does not go to ballot vote.

 Sewer and TIFF District Feasibility Study: The Board reviewed updated costs and revenue projects for
the proposed sewer district. The 2012 Phase One Plan was financially inadequate under current
estimates. The Board agreed that an independent engineering review should examine alternative routes,
phasing, pump station requirements and connections to nearby systems before further decisions are
made.

Updated Cost Estimate: The 2012 estimate was approximately $8.9 million. The updated estimate is
approximately $28 million, which included $20 million for construction, $2 million for engineering and
design, permitting and construction administration, a contingency of $4 million and other additional
related costs.

Revenue Gap: It was estimated that development in the proposed area would produce approximately $60
million in assessed value and almost $800,000 in annual revenue; approximately $1.8 million per year is
needed to cover the project. The Board concluded that it was not financially feasible without changes.

Alternative Routes: The Board questioned whether the 2012 route remains appropriate because sewer
infrastructure has expanded to Pease; the current plan requires extending sewer more than a mile
through Portsmouth.

Design and Phasing: The Board discussed whether a revised plan could reduce the number of pump
stations by placing sewer deeper, avoid a costly pump station, or phase the project differently. It was
noted that Phase One is the largest phase and reaches the Dunkin’ Donuts area and serves multiple
neighborhoods, which may be the reason the overall plan is difficult to finance.

Independent Review: M. Fougere contacted an independent engineering company to obtain a
preliminary cost for an independent study. The purpose of the study is to reassess the route, phasing,
costs, and practical alternatives in light of changes since 2012. The study was not authorized at this
meeting; a proposal will be reviewed once the potential cost is known.
