Bob Dion opened the Planning Board meeting at 6:00 p.m. A roll call was taken. It was announced that a
quorum was present, and the meeting was being recorded and live streamed.

Bob Dion, as Chairman, appointed M. Adams and P. Dion as voting members.

1. Projects of Regional Impact

M. Fougere explained that the proposed use of the pad adjacent to Target by Restaurant Depot plus future
retail is expected to generate roughly 5,000 to 6,000 fewer trips per day than the approved supermarket.
Weekend traffic would decrease from approximately 8,000 trips to 2,000. He noted that the site borders
Portsmouth and a regional impact finding would have required tabling the application and notifying the
Regional Planning Commission as well as the City of Portsmouth as an abutter. It was stated that the
original approval had gone through the regional notification process.

MOTION: H. Droesch moved that the proposed use of the pad adjacent to Target was not a project of
regional impact. Second – M. Adams; all in favor. MOTION CARRIED

3. Continued
   Subdivision of Land
   Moulton Avenue (Residential: Map R9, Lot 8K-1)
   Owner/Applicant: Michael Gill
   The owner/applicant is proposing to subdivide a 39.12 acre parcel into 8 residential lots. Lots 1
   through 7 will be greater than 60,000 square feet and greater than 45,000 square feet of
   contiguous upland. Lot 8 will be 26.3 acres.

Steve Haight – President, Civilworks NE, and representing Michael Gill, applicant, addressed the Board.
Also present were Jase Gregoire – Project Engineer (Civilworks NE), and Michael Gill, applicant.

Subdivision Layout: A revised plan for a 10-lot subdivision was presented. The proposal is to subdivide
the 39-acre lot into nine residential lots that will be accessed from a cul-de-sac off Moulton Avenue; Lot
10 will remain a residual parcel of more than 20 acres with access off Post Road. It was clarified that Lot
10 will not use the new subdivision road and will avoid wetland impacts.

Added Residential Lot: The plan changed from eight lots to ten lots because a portion that was previously
included with Lot 10 was determined to be a viable building lot. A lot line was added rather than changing
the cul-de-sac, road design, drainage, or size of the other lots.

Wetland and Conservation Review: The proposed wetland buffer impact is approximately 1,500 square
feet to 1,584 square feet near the detention pond and treatment area. The Conservation Commission
reviewed the 10-lot plan and unanimously recommended approval of the Conditional Use Permit. They
also did a site walk of the property.

Fire Protection: S. Haight told the Board they were proposing individual residential sprinkler systems
rather than a cistern for the development. Chief Stanchina indicated that each system can be designed
using a qualifying well, municipal water, or a small storage tank, generally around 300 to 350 gallons,
supplying approximately 30 minutes of flow. An easement will be retained for a future cistern. Any change
from sprinklers to a cistern will require returning to the Planning Board.

Moulton Avenue Traffic and Neighborhood Concerns: Abutters voiced concerns about the proximity of
the road to existing homes. Tammy and Scott Perkins, 48 Moulton Avenue, and Jack and Sandy Colliton,
45 Moulton Avenue, objected to the placement of the road in the right-of-way close to the existing homes.
Their concerns included headlights shining through bedroom windows and loss of neighborhood
character. S. Perkins requested that the road be shifted, even it meant that the number of lots would be
reduced.

Sight Distance Waiver: S. Haight stated that the proposed road location would provide 167 feet of sight
distance and maximize visibility at the roadway’s high point. The Regulations refer to 400 feet; S. Haight
compared the design with typical residential road standards of roughly 150 to 200 feet. The Board
clarified that the requested relief would be a Subdivision Regulation waiver rather than a Zoning
Ordinance Variance.

Drainage and Maintenance: Residents questioned where the runoff from the first 100 feet of road would
flow and whether the existing downstream drainage facilities could handle additional water. S. Haight
explained that the road is crowned and graded toward a road swale within the Moulton Avenue right-of-
way; no catch basin is proposed at that end. The stormwater operation and maintenance manual,
Alteration of Terrain permit, and HOA documents identify maintenance responsibilities; the Town
Engineer and an environmental monitor will inspect construction.

Construction Oversight: It was clarified that Altus Engineering will inspect construction under an escrow
funded account funded by the applicant. An environmental monitor will conduct weekly or storm event
inspections under the Alteration of Terrain permit. A pre-construction meeting will review drainage,
erosion control, grading, and related concerns with the contractor, Town staff and engineers.

Traffic Data: Chief Bertogli stated that police data collection on Moulton Avenue recorded approximately
1,800 vehicles over eight days in 2023 and approximately 2,600 vehicles over a recent comparable period.
There was slightly more traffic traveling uphill towards Post Road. Of that count, 19 vehicles exceeded
the 33 mile per hour enforcement threshold on a 25 mile per hour road. The highest recorded speed was
40 miles per hour.

 4. New
    Site Plan Review
    1460 Greenland Road (Map R21, Lot 44B: Commercial District C, RCIM District)
    Owner/Applicant: New England Development
    The owner/applicant is proposing two retail spaces in the area initially approved for a 70,500
    square foot grocery store. One space will be approximately 49,000 square feet and the other is
    approximately 11,000 square feet, for a total area of approximately 60,000 square feet.

Paul Cincotta (New England Development), a representative from Vanasse Associates, Doug Reynolds (LJB
Engineering) and Larry Cohen (Restaurant Depot) were present.

Proposed Use: P. Cincotta presented an amended site plan to replace the previously approved Stop &
Shop on the pad adjacent to Target. The revised plan shows Restaurant Depot occupying a 60,000 square
foot portion of the pad and a future retail space of 11,000 square feet. The retail space will not be built
out until a tenant is identified; the tenant will need to return to the Planning Board for a review of
architectural plans.

Existing Infrastructure: The previously approved constructed site improvements including the parking lot,
access from Rt. 33, site lighting, landscaping, stubbed water, sewer, gas and electric utilities will be used.
The proposal does not change the parking layout, number of spaces, roadway access, or broader grading
and drainage configuration.

Traffic Analysis: Vanasse Associates estimated the proposed uses would generate roughly 75% fewer trips
than the approved supermarket use. The traffic study evaluated the signalized shopping center entrance
and found that signal timing, lane configuration, storage, queuing and intersections remained acceptable;
movements operated at a Level of Service ‘D’ or better. NH DOT has not completed its review.

Operating Hours: L. Cohen stated that Restaurant Depot’s hours are generally 7 am to 6 pm during the
week, with shorter weekend hours. Delivery and trash pick-up will occur within the operating hour
framework. The existing approvals on the previously approved plan will be added to the amended site
plan. The existing sound barrier along the back of the center will remain in place.

Site Safety Conditions: The Board required review of the sight distance and traffic flow at the internal
entrance and exit, including vegetation removal or trimming, clearer signage and refreshed pavement
markings. It was noted that striping or markings along Rt. 33 must be approved by NH DOT. They will
evaluate whether a merge or directional sign would clarify the two-lane approach.

Other: It was noted that New England Development will make a $10,000 contribution to the Town’s sewer
study. Two waivers were approved for the redevelopment project: the project uses a site already
developed and existing municipal infrastructure, which eliminates the need for high-intensity soil
information and test pits.
