1.​ Conceptual Review for 1471 Greenland Road 140 Unit Apt Complex ​
   Attorney John Bossi introduced the applicant, Wakefield Investments, Inc., which
   owns the near-10 acre property located at 1471 Greenland Road, bordered by the
   railroad to the north and Pickering Brook to the south. Michael Schelzi of Wakefield
   Investments, Jones & Beach Engineer Paige Libbey, and certified wetland scientist
   (CWS) Brenden Walden were also present.​
   ​
   The conceptual review discussion for this 140-unit rental apartment project began
   with Ms. Libbey reviewing potential extensive wetland impacts, with “just over 1 acre
   of direct impact,” and “just over 2 acres of buffer impacts.” Said development would
   require a wetlands permit from the New Hampshire Department of Environmental
   Services (NHDES), as well as a permit from the Planning Board. She stated that the
   property “would be serviced by Portsmouth Water and Sewer, [that] there is currently
   water down at the Ocean Road intersection that they [would] have to bring up the
   road.” [They’d] need to coordinate with the city of Portsmouth for the sewer. “That's
   still an ongoing piece that will take a while to resolve because of the situation with
   the two towns needing to come up with an intermunicipal agreement for the sewer.”​
   ​
   CWS Walden, who conducted a wetland delineation late summer last year, outlined
   the wetlands and wetland buffers he identified that would be impacted: a large
   wetland system that begins near the railroad and encompasses the 4-acre upland
   area, and a separate, isolated wetland in the central portion of the property. “For the
   most part, both wetlands consist of mature forested wetlands. As the larger wetland
   approaches the brook, it transitions from forested wetland to scrub-shrub wetland,
   then to emergent wetland, and finally to riverine habitat along the brook.” He
   conducted a subsequent vernal pool assessment in the spring of this year. He
   observed several areas of standing water on the site but said he did not identify any
   primary or secondary indicators of vernal pools in those areas.​
   ​
   He also explained there had been a preliminary application meeting in which
   mitigation for the proposed road impacts were discussed. He said the most
   straightforward mitigation approach would be [the State’s] Aquatic Resource
   Mitigation fund. He said their primary objectives were to “demonstrate avoidance
  and minimization of wetland impacts on-site and to keep the total impacts below one
  acre, which would help them avoid the requirement for an off-site alternatives
  analysis.”​
  ​
  Ms. Libbey offered a couple of additional points. She had not mentioned stormwater
  management because the project is still in the preliminary conceptual phase and
  they have not yet developed a detailed stormwater management plan. As they move
  forward, they will need to design a system that meets both the Town’s standards and
  the State’s Alteration of Terrain requirements. And, given the proximity of the
  development to the wetlands and the need to provide adequate stormwater
  treatment, they would likely need to incorporate enhanced treatment measures. One
  option would be porous pavement. Further, following up on CSW Walden’s
  comments regarding the consolidation of impacts, they “have focused the proposed
  impacts on areas with ‘lower wetland functions and values.’”​
  ​
  Ms. Libbey said the [road] access impact is largely unavoidable. The proposed
  impacts at the rear of the site, she said, “have been concentrated around the
  isolated wetland and a narrow extension of the larger wetland complex; our goal is to
  reduce the overall wetland impacts to less than one acre by eliminating or reducing
  some of the proposed impacts within this portion of the larger wetland complex.”​
  ​
  Chair Kathleen then opened the Board to questions.​
  ​
  Jean said she would like to detail some math she did, for the sake of viewers, on this
  proposal’s impervious surfacing. She provided the following numbers:​


Site component                                   Estimated area


Building footprint                               140,000 sq. ft.


Parking spaces                                   66,000 sq. ft.


Road and circular access area                    8,000 sq. ft.


Total estimated impervious area                  215,000 sq. ft.


Total site area                                  421,890 sq. ft. (9.68 acres)​
                                                 ​

  Jean asked if the applicant was familiar with the term ‘impervious surfacing’, and
  familiar with how many communities surround the Great Bay Estuary. Jean reported
  52. She asked the applicants if they were familiar with the PREP, the Piscataqua
  Region Estuary Partnership (Ms. Libbey confirmed), and the decades of work PREP
   has spent in educating those 52 communities on the impacts of impervious impacts,
   how hundreds of scientists over those years have determined there’s a tipping point
   for serious water quality decline in our watersheds, and subwatersheds, along with
   habitat decline and loss of biodiversity. That tipping point is 10%. She asked the
   applicants if they knew what their % was. They did not.​
   ​
   Using the very conservative numbers from the applicant’s plan, Jean pointed out that
   their proposal hit 51% imperviousness. The entire 3.61 acres of upland will be
   entirely consumed with imperviousness.​
   ​
   Jean asked the applicant to provide a copy of the wetlands evaluation, and for an
   opportunity for the CC to walk the site. ​
   ​
   Cindy pointed out the road’s width of 22’ being inadequate for the fire department’s
   ladder truck, and that ultimately there would be even greater impacts to the wetlands
   to accommodate the fire department’s needs. Egress onto Route 33 would be
   hampered by right-turns only and potentially lead to even more wetland impacts to
   make accommodations for people living at this complex.​
   ​
   Kathleen expressed concern for potential impacts caused to the wetlands if there
   were to be an auto accident on the access road, what the spill contamination might
   be, or [if there were] a fire in the building and chemicals being sprayed on the
   building, how would the applicant handle all that chemical going into the wetlands?
   Ms. Libbey said she did not know at this point.​
   ​
   Alice asked if they had given any thought to conserving the land instead of
   developing it. They had not.​
   ​
   The applicant was asked about a sitewalk, and a joint walk with the PB will be
   sought. ​
